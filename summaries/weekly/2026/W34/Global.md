**The Iran Conflict and Oil Markets**
Trump's pivot to economic warfare on Thursday marks an implicit acknowledgment of the military campaign's limits after nearly six months of conflict, with US resources stretched and elevated gasoline prices an increasing domestic political liability ahead of midterm elections. Brent nonetheless held below $93 as covert Persian Gulf flows — still running at meaningful volumes despite vessel attacks — and Chinese refiners' reluctance to absorb the oil-price premium have partly capped upside. Analysts remain divided on whether tightened sanctions can extract concessions that months of force have not, and China's role as Iran's largest oil customer makes Trump's threat as much a test of the US-China relationship as further pressure on Tehran — a tension Washington has so far shown limited appetite to confront directly.

**The Global Bond Selloff**
Barclays attributes the synchronized rise in long-end yields to three compounding forces: widening deficit outlooks across major economies, a roughly $1 trillion wave of AI-related hyperscaler debt flooding markets, and a shifting buyer base as foreign holdings of US Treasuries fell $72.1 billion in June — led by Japan and China — the third monthly decline in four months.
