Daily Brief

Wednesday, September 16, 2026

US

  • The Federal Reserve is virtually certain to hike the federal funds rate by 25 basis points today — the first increase since 2023 — with interest-rate swaps pricing a 94% probability, a threshold that has historically proven reliable.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield rose to its highest level since 2007, briefly topping 5%, with cumulative upward pressure driven by an 80-basis-point rise year-to-date and growing expectations of additional hikes beyond today.
  • Brent crude closed at $108.75, its highest since May, with WTI near $104.85, sustaining inflation concerns that have complicated the Fed's communication calculus.
  • The Senate blocked the Clarity Act — a landmark digital asset market structure bill — with bipartisan opposition sending Coinbase down 10% and Bitcoin lower.

Monetary Policy

The FOMC meeting dominates the session. Chairman Kevin Warsh has spent months signaling inflation resolve — invoking language of "impatience" and pledging to "not hesitate to act" — and financial markets have effectively removed the option of inaction. Goldman Sachs, which shifted to a 25bp hike forecast only after Friday's CPI print, acknowledged the move lacks a strong fundamental justification given energy-driven, potentially transitory inflation dynamics, but concluded the credibility cost of a hold outweighs the economic rationale for patience. The more consequential unknown is Warsh's forward guidance: markets are pricing two additional hikes with an 80% probability of a third, and the dot plot, which Warsh has historically downplayed, will be scrutinized for any signal of how deep the cycle runs. Trump's public opposition to rate increases, through adviser Peter Navarro among others, places Warsh in a difficult position — appearing dovish would buoy equities but risk driving long yields further above 5%, into territory last seen in the mid-2000s.

Equity and Credit Markets

The S&P 500 has gained 10.7% year-to-date despite the rate backup, sustained by a substantial upward revision to forward earnings — driven largely by AI-related capital expenditure — that has partially offset multiple compression. Financial conditions remain accommodative by historical standards. The Russell 2000 has underperformed over the past month, declining more than 6% from its recent peak as rate sensitivity weighs on leveraged small-cap balance sheets, though year-to-date outperformance versus the S&P 500 provides a buffer. ---

Asia

  • Japan's export growth remained robust in August, with shipments rising 19.3% year-over-year, above the prior trend, led by semiconductors and chip manufacturing equipment; imports gained 28%.
  • China's credit growth weakened materially in August, with PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng explicitly framing the deceleration as deliberate policy rather than a demand shortfall — a significant rhetorical shift.
  • SK Hynix resolved its union dispute, with approximately 57% of members approving a revised wage agreement splitting profit-sharing evenly between cash and equity.

China Macro and Credit

The PBOC's public positioning on credit deserves attention. Governor Pan's article in the Communist Party's Qiushi journal — an unusually formal venue for economic communication — characterized the August slowdown in aggregate financing as structurally desirable, citing the economy's reduced reliance on debt-intensive industries such as property relative to high-tech sectors. Whether this framing reflects genuine policy intent or manages expectations around weak demand remains to be seen, but it appears to signal that a large-scale credit stimulus is not imminent. Separately, China's gasoline and diesel inventories are declining sharply as domestic refining struggles to offset Middle East supply disruptions, raising the possibility of renewed restrictions on fuel exports.

Markets and Corporate

The South Korean Kospi gained 1.4%, supported partly by relief around the SK Hynix labor resolution and broader optimism around the potential US-China trade truce ahead of the Xi-Trump summit. ByteDance founder Zhang Yiming became Asia's wealthiest individual at over $105 billion, reflecting AI-driven valuation gains. CATL dropped approximately 10% over two sessions in Shenzhen on competitive concerns around potential production cutbacks. Trip.com gained 3.9% in Hong Kong after earnings beat estimates. ---

Europe

  • UK 30-year gilt yields reached their highest level since 1998, approaching 6%, as inflation accelerated to a five-month high in August, driven by energy costs, ahead of Thursday's Bank of England meeting.
  • The Bank of England is widely expected to hold rates Thursday, but markets are pricing four additional hikes over the following 12 months.
  • EU Commission President von der Leyen proposed Canada as the bloc's first associate member during her State of the Union address, with Prime Minister Carney in attendance.

UK Rates and Fiscal Credibility

The UK's strategy of reducing long-maturity gilt issuance — adopted to stabilize markets following the Liz Truss-era dislocation — appears to have failed to contain borrowing costs. Thirty-year yields have continued to rise since the 2022 episode, and market participants describe the situation as increasingly precarious given the compounding cost of refinancing at elevated rates. UK diesel and petrol prices are now at their highest since 2022, feeding directly into August's above-consensus CPI print and reinforcing the market's hawkish repricing of the Bank of England's trajectory.

Climate and Infrastructure

The UK's Climate Change Committee concluded that Heathrow's proposed third runway is "not currently compatible" with the country's legally binding net zero target, estimating that expansion could increase aviation emissions by roughly 7% by mid-century. The CCC proposed a government levy to fund sustainable aviation fuel and carbon capture — mechanisms it acknowledged are largely unproven at commercial scale — that would raise a return ticket to New York by approximately £400 by 2050. The tension between growth-oriented infrastructure investment and statutory climate commitments remains unresolved. Separately, von der Leyen cited a "summer of truth" on climate following 660,000 hectares burned across Europe, announcing a new climate insurance alliance and resilience framework. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's central bank is expected to cut the Selic rate today, extending its easing cycle against a backdrop of intensifying political uncertainty ahead of the October 4 presidential election first round.
  • Polls show Flávio Bolsonaro and incumbent President Lula statistically tied, with corruption allegations — centered on the Banco Master scandal — complicating both candidates' positioning.

Brazil Political Economy

The Brazilian election dynamic is sharpening into a contest defined less by economic platforms than by competing credibility deficits. Flávio Bolsonaro has concentrated his campaign on the Lula government's proximity to Justice Alexandre de Moraes and the Banco Master banking fraud — the same fraud in which police are now investigating Bolsonaro's own financial relationships, including potential money laundering. The convergence of both leading candidates on the scandal underscores a broader governance challenge. The central bank's expected rate cut today proceeds against this backdrop of political uncertainty, with monetary easing providing some near-term macro support but longer-run fiscal credibility contingent on the election outcome. Argentina presents a separate illustration of sub-national fiscal fragility, with at least one opposition governor reported to be issuing local currency instruments — a development that tests the boundaries of Milei's market liberalization framework. ---

Global

  • US naval operations enforcing the Strait of Hormuz blockade have cost approximately $7.1 billion since January, representing more than 30% of total US Navy warship deployment and running at roughly $32.5 million per day.
  • Brent crude closed near $108.75 as Saudi Arabia pivoted to route additional oil volumes through the Strait of Hormuz after pro-Iranian militia drone strikes forced closure of the kingdom's East-West pipeline to its Red Sea coast.
  • The US and China are in active discussions ahead of a Xi-Trump summit next week on tariff reductions covering roughly $30 billion in trade, including American energy and agricultural products, with Treasury Secretary Bessent meeting Chinese counterpart He Lifeng this weekend.

Energy Markets and Hormuz

The Hormuz disruption is now reshaping global LNG trade structurally, not merely cyclically. With approximately one-fifth of global LNG supply effectively trapped inside the Persian Gulf — and no timeline for normalization — buyers across Asia are accelerating efforts to secure US supply. At the Gastech conference in Bangkok, industry participants privately acknowledged that LNG traffic through Hormuz may not fully normalize even after a ceasefire, driving a durable reorientation toward US Gulf Coast exports. Long-term Henry Hub-linked contracts are currently delivering LNG to Asia at roughly $8 per million BTU against spot prices near $30, a differential that creates powerful incentives to lock in supply but is drawing in rising construction costs and competition from data center power demand. Saudi Arabia's rerouting of crude through Hormuz following the East-West pipeline shutdown increases its own exposure to the conflict zone it had previously avoided.

US-China Trade and AI

The prospect of a pre-summit trade truce extension tempers, but does not eliminate, the structural tensions between the two economies. China has continued narrowing the AI capability gap with the US despite export controls, and the Trump administration's meeting with Anthropic to discuss AI safety risks reflects a broader uncertainty about how aggressively to manage technology diffusion. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is reportedly attending Trump's state dinner with Xi — an illustrative signal of how deeply AI competition is embedded in diplomatic interactions. The WTO has characterized this as the most consequential moment for international commerce in eight decades.
