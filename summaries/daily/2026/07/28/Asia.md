• South Korea's Kospi plunged 11% on July 28, triggering multiple trading halts — the index's largest single-day decline in recent memory — as Samsung and SK Hynix each fell more than 13%.
• A report that a Chinese state-backed company has begun mass-producing immersion deep-ultraviolet lithography machines fueled fears of rising competitive capacity and a potential flood of global chip supply.
• Bank Indonesia Governor Perry Warjiyo abruptly resigned for "personal reasons," shocking investors who had met with him in Singapore just days earlier with no indication of his departure.

**Semiconductor Selloff**
The proximate trigger for Asian equity losses was a confluence of AI-spending anxiety and China's reported chipmaking advances. SK Hynix has now fallen approximately 47% from its all-time high set in June, erasing market value comparable to SpaceX — this despite expectations for record quarterly earnings on Wednesday. The dynamic illustrates a sentiment shift: investors are no longer buying the dip in AI-adjacent hardware stocks, instead interpreting each data point as a reason to reduce exposure. Japan's Nikkei 225 and Taiwan's Taiex each fell roughly 4%. A Taiwanese court detained an Nvidia employee as part of a chip-smuggling probe, one of at least eight such investigations across four jurisdictions — adding regulatory overhang to sector sentiment.

**Indonesia Political Risk**
Warjiyo's abrupt departure from Bank Indonesia introduces policy uncertainty at a sensitive juncture. His resignation — characterized as for "personal reasons" — raised immediate questions about political interference in monetary policy under President Prabowo, and the rupiah came under pressure. The central bank had been navigating a delicate balance between supporting growth and defending the currency; continuity of that approach is now in question.
