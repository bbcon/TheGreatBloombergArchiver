All Change

Once every few years, Europe’s policy circles get consumed with the ultimate 
guessing game: who will become the next head of the region’s central bank?

Working out Kevin Warsh’s chances 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-30/trump-administration-prepares-for-warsh-fed-chair-nomination?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 of clinching the Federal Reserve chair this year required watchers to attempt 
to inhabit the mind of US President Donald Trump. That’s challenging enough, 
but the European equivalent is arguably even trickier, because it forces anyone 
motivated enough to try to fathom the strategies and poker hands of several 
leaders all at once.

President Christine Lagarde’s hints of an early exit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/lagarde-rebuffs-queries-on-future-at-ecb-while-plugging-her-book?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 from the European Central Bank had already kicked off a season of deduction 
and prediction on a successor. But the resignationannounced last week 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/schnabel-will-leave-ecb-board-early-for-senior-role-at-imf?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 of her colleague, Isabel Schnabel, to join the International Monetary Fund, 
means there’s now a real impetus for the region’s governmentsto get a move on 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/lagarde-succession-race-at-ecb-is-heating-up-as-schnabel-exits?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
, and a gossip-fueled parlor game of high-level divining to match.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-25/greece-pm-on-shipping-diesel-eu-fiscal-rules-ecb-video?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
Three top jobs are essentially in play. Aside from those two already 
mentioned, Chief Economist Philip Lane will end his own stint at the ECB in May.

In 2019, during an even more complex round of horse-trading between European 
Union leaders, French President Emmanuel Macronbrought Lagarde 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-07-02/lagarde-to-succeed-draghi-as-ecb-chief-amid-weakening-economy?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 into the equation at the last minute to solve an impasse with Germany. Her 
candidacy as the first female ECB chief trumped any other.

This time is probably easier, with two clear contenders for the top job: Bank 
for International Settlements chiefPablo Hernandez de Cos 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/knot-would-be-perfect-fit-at-ecb-for-many-nations-dutch-pm-says?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
, a Spaniard, and former Dutch central bank PresidentKlaas Knot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/knot-would-be-perfect-fit-at-ecb-for-many-nations-dutch-pm-says?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
.

Even then, the three-dimensional chess 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-27/four-ecb-scenarios-sketch-a-succession-plan-for-post-lagarde-era?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 of EU decisions requires Paris and Berlinto cut a deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/ecb-jobs-will-be-part-of-grand-bargain-greek-premier-says?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 on candidates for the other two slots. No-one can exclude either a surprise 
outcome for any or all of the positions in play, or an extended stalemate that 
could last for months.

In truth, it’s much less taxing for any forecaster to just stick to monetary 
policy right now. WithSpanish inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/spanish-inflation-unexpectedly-hits-5-backing-higher-ecb-rates?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 having just hit 5%, andnumbers this week 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/euro-zone-s-inflation-rate-is-about-to-reach-highest-since-2023?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 likely to show the fastest euro-zone price growth since 2023, another rate 
hike is on the way.Lagarde’s testimony 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-28/ecb-s-lagarde-says-higher-yields-to-slow-growth-and-inflation?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 to lawmakers on Monday pared investor bets in October, but a hike in December 
is widely predicted.

After that, given how a lot depends on energy-price increases that hinge on 
the conflict in the Middle East, the path of policy is anyone’s guess — as is 
who will actually be making the decisions.

Need-to-Know Research

Many parallels have been drawn between the current artificial intelligence 
investment surge and the 1990s technology boom. Tax revenue gains that sharply 
shrink theUS budget deficit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-2026-budget-gap-hits-1-96-trillion-with-a-month-left-to-go?cmpid=BBD092926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9388>
 won’t be one of them, according to Standard Chartered economists Dan Pan and 
Steve Englander.

“The current US tax system collects little corporate tax,” with such revenues 
dropping thanks in part to cuts made in the Republican tax bill last year, Pan 
and Englander wrote in a recent note. “Average corporate tax revenue rose by 
0.5% of GDP to 2.1% of GDP in the second half of the 1990s. But this is 
unlikely to be repeated.”
Note: NFP is nonfarm payrolls
Today, income and payroll taxes make up 80% of revenue, but an ageing 
population and less immigration could prove to be headwinds for growth in such 
revenue, the duo wrote. “A fundamental shift in the US tax structure may be 
needed” to spur revenue from faster-growing segments of the economy if deficits 
are to be addressed, according to the Standard Chartered economists.
