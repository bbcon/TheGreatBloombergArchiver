We’re watching India’s biggest-ever share sale this week. Jio’s $3.1 billion 
IPO drew strong demand, particularly from institutional investors,defying the 
drag of a weeklong selloff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/jio-s-record-ipo-draws-strong-demand-defying-india-market-rout?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10580>
 in Indian equities last week.

It may have raised a record amount of funds for billionaire Mukesh Ambani’s 
digital and telecommunications giant 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-06-19/ambani-s-jio-could-be-india-s-biggest-ipo-video?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10580>
, but it proved to be far less lucrative for the bankers handling the deal. The 
company is paying about 1.96 billion rupees ($20.7 million) in fees to the 19 
banks. That amounts to about 0.65% of the issue size,trailing the fee pools of 
several previous offerings in India 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/jio-s-record-india-ipo-falls-short-of-record-payday-for-bankers?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10580>
.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/jio-s-record-india-ipo-falls-short-of-record-payday-for-bankers?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10580>
Fierce competition among investment bankers in India has compressed fees, 
particularly for marquee deals. Those firms will have to get their payoff in 
other ways: league-table rankings, bragging rights and credentials that help 
win the next big pitch.

—Stephanie Phang

. The crude will be held in the Jurong Rock Caverns, in a petrochemicals and

enhances the resilience of the Saudi crude oil supply chain in Southeast Asia, 
as well as contributes to Singapore’s energy security and resilience in the 
event of supply disruptions,” he said.
