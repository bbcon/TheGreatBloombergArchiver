China tries shock and awe

China’s top economic officials are talking up openness in Hong Kong. Back on 
the mainland, authorities are showing investors just how active they remain in 
steering markets.

As Vice Premier He Lifeng pledged support for a free and open economic 
environment in Hong Kong 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/china-s-he-says-will-support-free-open-economy-in-hong-kong?cmpid=110326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10898>
, policymakers in Beijing were deploying an array
of measures aimed at influencing asset prices. The central bank moved to cool 
the yuan’s longest winning streak 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/offshore-yuan-weakens-after-pboc-moves-to-cool-currency-rally?cmpid=110326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10898>
 in three decades, while regulatorsrevived stock-market rescue tools 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/china-to-boost-use-of-stock-support-tools-to-stabilize-market?cmpid=110326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10898>
 and took steps to encourage more share buybacks.

The flurry of intervention highlights the balancing act Beijing faces as it 
seeks to attract global capital and promote the yuan internationally while 
retaining firm control over markets at home.

While the yuan weakened, today’s effort fell flat in the stock market. The CSI 
300 Index dropped 0.2% and the tech-heavy Star 50 tumbled 3.8%. (Some of the 
same tools, rolled out in 2024, had sparked a huge rally.)

The stimulus came after a meeting between officials and securities firms, 
banks and investors to discuss slumping stocks. Chinese shares have been 
weighed down by a weak economy and doubts over AI earnings.

The CSI 300 fell 8.5% in October, its biggest monthly drop in more than a 
decade, and has declined a record 12 straight weeks.It’s fallen 13% this year 
even as markets in most of the rest of the world have rallied.

“Overall I think it’s an effort to stabilize its financial markets, but it has 
a clear long-term perspective rather than an immediate reward,” said Anna Wu of 
Floodlines Research. “A real turnaround needs demand-side fiscal support that 
reaches households, a steadier property market, and evidence that earnings, 
especially in AI and tech, can justify valuations.” —Winnie Hsu

Buoyant Bitcoin

Since the record cyber theft against the world’s largest crypto exchange last 
week, traders bracing for the worst have instead watched Bitcoindefy gravity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/bitcoin-s-resilience-after-binance-hack-masks-market-jitters?cmpid=110326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10898>
.

The cryptocurrency is changing hands at almost $77,000 today. It’s down 
roughly 6% from Thursday’s high before Binance disclosed the $6.3 billion hack. 
The response from Binance, which promised from the start to cover all losses, 
helped cushion the decline.

Bitcoin was already under pressure from a selloff in risk assets, so its 
relative buoyancy underscores how differently traders are treating the incident 
than the 2022 collapse of the exchange FTX. In that episode, Bitcoin fell 19% 
over three days as Sam Bankman-Fried’s company tumbled into bankruptcy.

“The market treated this as an operational shock, not a systemic one,” said 
Kelly Ye at Talos. —Suvashree Ghosh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUE9GK4qUHo/suvashree-ghosh?cmpid=110326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10898>
