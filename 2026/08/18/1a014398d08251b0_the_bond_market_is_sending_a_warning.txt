Investors are increasingly demanding a higher premium 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/bond-rout-sends-long-term-borrowing-costs-to-highest-in-decades?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7839>
 for long-dated government bonds on concern inflation is above target and 
fiscal deficits are hard to contain.

Yields on 30-year US Treasuries rose to 5.33% this week, the highest since 
2007, while Frenchborrowing costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/traders-short-french-bonds-as-fight-over-budget-set-to-escalate?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7839>
 hit the loftiest since 2008 and their German peers traded at 2011 levels. 
Equivalent gilt yields are approaching 6% in the UK.

Bonds aren’t pricing in a crisis — yet. Short-term debt is largely untroubled 
— the UStwo-year yield 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/treasuries-gain-as-weak-retail-sales-data-erode-rate-hike-bets?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7839>
 has barely moved in the past month — and MSCI’s world equity index is near a 
record. But the tumble in 30-year US bond prices is forcing asset allocators to
rethink portfolio mixes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-08-18/the-30-year-itch-comes-for-bonds-and-brazil?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7839>
 as long-dated Treasuries lose their haven tag and doubt rises over the US’s 
debt levels.

Yardeni Research said the market is becoming more concerned about the surge in 
borrowing by hyperscalers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/yardeni-warns-bond-vigilantes-stirring-as-us-yields-near-5?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7839>
, while questioning whether the Federal Reserve will remain sufficiently 
vigilant on inflation if oil prices climb.

“We aren’t pushing the panic button,” strategists led by Ed Yardeni wrote in a 
note. “However, we are closely monitoring whether the bond vigilantes might do 
so.”

Yardeni pointed to the summer of 2023, when US yields surged from 4% to 5% in 
just a few months. That level ultimately proved attractive to buyers, and the 
firm says there may be a similar buying opportunity ahead.

Still, the increase in yields reinforces the passing of the post-global 
financial era, one where governments could borrowwith little fear 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/germany-is-set-to-sell-30-year-bonds-at-highest-yield-since-2011?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7839>
 of penalty and rates were low. Where yields go from here — and how investors, 
central banks and governments respond — will be crucial to watch.

“Hovering around this 3% level is probably what people are looking at for the

Addressing the costs of tighter monetary policy for mortgage holders, Lane 
said the ECB must prioritize curbing the pace of price increases to avoid 
inflation staying “too high for too long.”
