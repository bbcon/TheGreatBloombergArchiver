The demise of the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty has also spurred 
development of new weapons. From 1988 to 2019, it prohibited the US and Soviet 
Union/Russia from building missiles with ranges of 500 to 5,500 kilometers.

That also meant no US allies pursued such weapons. Then the first Trump 
administrationpulled out 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-10-26/putin-offers-nato-missile-inspections-in-bid-to-reboot-treaty?cmpid=BBD073026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7164>
 of the treaty, opening the door.

And there’s probably never been a single conflict involving as many of the 
weapons

 as the Iran war. Tehran has launched about 2,000 since it began in February, 
causing massive damage throughout the region and eroding stockpiles of 
hard-to-replace interceptors.

These wars, which also involve tons of drones and small, cheap cruise 
munitions, have dominated the spotlight. So you’d be forgiven for not noticing:

 * South Korea’s huge Hyunmoo-5 ballistic missile unveiled at the end of 2024, 
whose size implies a range of thousands of kilometers

hypersonic glide vehicle
 * A long-range strike program launched by six European nations late last year
