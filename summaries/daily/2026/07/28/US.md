• No major US macro data releases on July 28; the Federal Reserve meeting this week is the dominant focus, with Citadel Securities calling for a surprise rate hike.
• S&P 500 futures fell 0.1–0.3% and Nasdaq 100 futures declined 0.8–0.9% as AI spending fears spread from Asian equity markets into US tech.
• Washington imposed new blanket tariffs of at least 10% on 60 trading partners — markets barely reacted, a sign of "tariff fatigue" following Liberation Day.
• Major hyperscaler earnings — Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Apple — are due Wednesday through Thursday, with capex scrutiny the central investor concern.

**Monetary Policy**
The Federal Reserve meeting this week carries unusual uncertainty. Citadel Securities publicly argued that a surprise rate hike would strengthen Chairman Kevin Warsh's inflation-fighting credibility, with its head of macro strategy noting that "the market may once again be underestimating the extent of the hawkish shift at the Fed." No consensus has formed around such a move, but the possibility alone is shaping rates markets, with the US 10-year Treasury yield at 4.62%.

**Technology and Markets**
US equity futures weakened ahead of the open, with chip names under broad pressure: Nvidia fell 1.3%, AMD 4%, and Micron 5%. The more consequential story, however, is this week's hyperscaler earnings. Alphabet's prior-week results — which included cloud revenue surging more than 80% but free cash flow turning negative for the first time since its 2004 IPO — set a cautionary template. Investors are increasingly unwilling to reward revenue growth funded by ever-expanding capex, with the five largest hyperscalers planning roughly $740 billion in combined AI investment for 2026. Microsoft now trades at approximately 19 times forward earnings, well below its 10-year average of 27.
