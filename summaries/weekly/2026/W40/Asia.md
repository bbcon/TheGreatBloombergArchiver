• China's manufacturing PMI returned to expansion in September for the first time since June, a modestly positive signal, though August retail sales growth was "barely above zero."

• The Bank of Japan's September meeting summary offered no signal of imminent rate action; the yen weakened on the release, extending import cost pressure.
• Overseas investors sold $2.1 billion of Indian equities and $1.1 billion of sovereign bonds in September as rising oil prices and global yields eroded carry attractiveness.

**China: Stimulus Signal Without Structural Resolution**
Beijing's stimulus package — the largest policy intervention in two years — combined mortgage subsidies for lower-priced homes, a 200 billion yuan expansion of central bank relending quotas for technology and infrastructure, and signals of additional fiscal measures including interest-rate subsidies and deployment of unused local government bond quotas. Goldman Sachs described the measures as "more significant as a policy signal than as a near-term growth impulse"; Barclays called it "a nudge, not a game changer." The structural diagnosis is unchanged: China's constraint is demand for credit, not its cost. August industrial output rose 5.2% YoY, but the median product category contracted 1.8%, retail sales growth was negligible, and broad government expenditure fell 3.5% in the first eight months. August industrial profits rising only 4.2% YoY — the weakest since a decline last November — confirm that the AI-and-commodities-driven recovery has not transmitted into broad domestic consumption. Equity markets fell to more than one-year lows during the month. The tariff-reduction list agreed following the Trump-Xi summit covers roughly $60 billion in two-way trade but excludes soybeans, which Beijing appears to be preserving as political leverage ahead of US midterm elections.

**RBA, India, and Regional Capital Flows**
The RBA's unanimous 25bp hike to 4.60% retained an explicit tightening bias, noting that "upside risks to inflation are materialising." The decision exerts additional pressure on Australia's housing market, where Sydney high-end values have already fallen 10.7% over the past year. India faces a compound external shock: September saw $2.1 billion in equity outflows and $1.1 billion in sovereign bond sales, the rupee was among Asia's worst quarterly performers, and the 10-year government bond yield touched a two-year high. A monsoon delivering its weakest precipitation since 2015 is pressuring rice yields and raises food inflation risk, complicating the RBI's calculus at a moment when a $133 billion domestic liquidity surplus is already pushing toward a more hawkish posture. India's solar manufacturing sector faces additional headwinds from preliminary US tariff duties of 126%, while Chinese export controls on battery technology and withdrawal of Chinese engineers from Indian electronics operations introduce supply-chain fragility.

**Japan and Korea**
The BOJ's September meeting summary contained no indication of growing support for an October rate hike, effectively dashing near-term hawkish expectations that had been building on a former official's comments earlier in the week. The yen weakened on release. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each fell approximately 5% Monday, tracking global semiconductor weakness. Nidec used its first major CEO appearance since an accounting scandal — which erased a third of its market value — to outline a restructuring toward AI and semiconductor segments.
