“Somebody has got to pay”

By Alastair Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQvVwWuhxcQ/alastair-marsh?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>

It’s widely accepted that taking carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere will be 
essential to mitigate the worst effects of climate change.

As world leaders gather in New York for the United Nations General Assembly 
and the annual Climate Week NYC, a critical question remains unanswered:Who 
will pay to remove the CO2? 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/the-un-says-carbon-removal-is-necessary-who-will-cover-the-cost?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>

Carbon removal can’t offer the economic upside of technologies such as 
electric vehicles, said Adair Turner, co-chair of the Energy Transitions 
Commission, a coalition of executives from companies including ArcelorMittal 
SA, HSBC Holdings Plc and Shell Plc.

“When you get to removals, it’s never gonna be a win-win,” Turner, also the 
former head of Britain’s financial regulator, said in an interview. “You’re 
doing it to take emissions out, and so somebody has got to pay.”

Scaling up carbon dioxide removal is among four major environmental ambitions 
that have fallen short, in a world that’s blowing past targets for limiting 
global temperature rise, according to a report from the Energy Transitions 
Commission.

The UN said earlier this month that the world will likely surpass the 2015 
Paris Accord’s goal of limiting global warming to 1.5C above pre-industrial 
levels within the next few years, with current policies putting warming on 
track for roughly 2.6C.

Pricing climate

By Coco Liu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AU-LNn94_HY/coco-liu?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>

Debt markets today are treating the risks associated with rising temperatures 
a lot more seriously than they did just half a decade ago, according to Sarah 
Kapnick, global head of climate advisory atJPMorgan Chase & Co. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/JPM:US?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>

“I have seen a shift in understanding on the fixed-income side about how to 
start pricing in climate from the physical standpoint, and that’s a big shift 
from five years ago to today,” Kapnick said in an interview.

The comments follow a summer of extreme heat that hit Europe’s commodities 
markets particularly hard, asrivers dried out 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/germany-weighs-options-as-low-water-levels-trigger-price-surges?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
, supply chains were disrupted andcrops stunted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/europe-s-seared-pastures-spark-animal-feed-crunch-as-prices-soar?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
. Economists havewarned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/heat-morphs-into-a-major-economic-shock-for-an-unprepared-europe?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
 that the summer of 2026 will leave a meaningful dent on gross domestic 
product, while theEuropean Central Bank 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2539Z:GR?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
 hasacknowledged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-04/ecb-warns-of-impact-of-heat-waves-on-inflation-economic-growth?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
 the clear inflationary impact of weather shocks.

Against that backdrop, Kapnick says it’s fixed-income investors that have 
become the “first part of the market to really price in climate.” They’re now 
“looking at climate and trying to integrate physical risk into their modeling,” 
with their approach “becoming more sophisticated in the last two years.”

El Niño confirmed to be record-breaking

By Mary Hui 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXDwzJNHmiM/mary-hui?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
,Brian K Sullivan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AF4NRCMRVn8/brian-k-sullivan?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
 andLauren Rosenthal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXCV-bQx5Dk/lauren-rosenthal?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
Floods in China, on Sept. 4.Photographer: Lyu Ming/China News Service/China 
News Service/VCG/Getty Images
This year’s El Niño is the strongest ever recorded 
<https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/blog/2026/record-breaking-el-nio-surpasses-previous-benchmarks->
, the UK Met Office said Tuesday. The event has exceeded previous records 
several weeks before its expected peak, which shows “the enormity of this El 
Niño,” said Adam Scaife, head of long range prediction at the forecaster.

“Scientists are still assessing whether the impacts will be in proportion to 
its magnitude,” he said. “But we are already seeing expected effects worldwide.”

A strengthening El Niño is stirring up a dangerous mix of typhoons and 
hurricanes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/el-nino-and-heat-fuel-destructive-storms-across-pacific-ocean?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
 across the western Pacific, fueling storms that have killed hundreds, 
disrupted trade and caused billions of dollars in damage. Another storm neared 
Japan on Monday and more could be possible in the region through November.

Pacific cyclones have caused more than 230 deaths since April, media reports 
show, while natural disasters — including three back-to-back storms — caused an 
estimated $8.5 billion in direct economic losses in China in July alone, 
according to government statistics.

On the other side of the Pacific, California declared a state of emergency 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/california-declares-emergency-as-el-nino-threatens-winter-mayhem?cmpid=BBD092226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9181>
 as a strengthening El Niño raises the threat of damaging storms, widespread 
flooding and mudslides across the state this fall and winter. Governor Gavin 
Newsom directed state agencies to assess infrastructure, including drainage 
systems, and speed up projects meant to protect highways and bridges from 
flooding and coastal erosion.
