No good options

France’s political and social woes keep getting worse, and the fallout is 
being felt in markets. After a weekend ofviolent unrest 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/french-violence-raises-alert-as-protests-flare-across-europe?cmpid=101926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10255>
, thenation’s sovereign bonds are sinking 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/france-s-bonds-drop-as-rioting-heaps-pressure-on-government?cmpid=101926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10255>
 and the CAC 40 is the worst-performing European stock index.

Protesters took to the streets over the rising cost of living, and rioters 
used the marches as an opportunity to loot stores and burn government buildings.

The government has little room to maneuver. A mountain of debt and a budget 
deficit among the largest in the euro area mean France cannot simply increase 
spending to appease the public.

Investors are taking fright, demanding that the country pay up to borrow. The 
yield on France’s 10-year bond is up 8 basis points to 4.88%, among the highest 
in Western Europe. The yield premium over German bunds has widened, pushing 
further into territory associated with the euro-area debt crisis.

It’s stocks, too. The CAC 40 is down 3.7% this year, while the Stoxx Europe 
600 Index has gained 8.1%.

There’s no sign of a respite. Parliament in coming weeks will have to debate 
and pass a 2027 budget that’s sure to contain bitter medicine in the form of 
spending cuts and higher taxes. Reaching a compromise appears difficult, and 
could prove even harder with a presidential election six months away.

“While the violent protests are not representative of the vast majority of the 
people, the developments over the weekend nevertheless underscore the delicate 
balancing act the government is facing between appeasing the people and the 
markets,” said Erik Liem at Commerzbank. —Phil Serafino

Buckle up

Before last Wednesday, the S&P 500 had gone 53 sessions without a 1% drop 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/option-traders-brace-for-vix-surge-as-ai-jitters-jolt-market?cmpid=101926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10255>
, the longest stretch since January 2020. Bulls chalked the low volatility up 
to optimism over AI and earnings growth.

Then the mood changed. The index dropped more than 1% on both Wednesday and 
Friday, and a measure of expected future volatility jumped at the end of the 
week as investors questioned the durability of the AI boom.

For many traders, it’s becoming more a question of when, rather than if, the 
AI selloff spills over into broader liquidation. Meanwhile, the anxiety is 
helping US Treasuries start toreclaim their traditional role as a haven 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/bond-market-s-big-short-bet-gets-wakeup-call-as-ai-trade-wobbles?cmpid=101926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10255>
.

capable of providing the hedge investors have been missing for much of the last 
few years,” said Masahiko Loo at State Street Investment Management. —Phil
