China’s factory activity expanded in August after three straight months of 
slowdown, a private survey showed, indicating resilience among the country’s 
export-oriented firms despite a broader deceleration in the economy.

The RatingDog China manufacturing purchasing managers index rose to 51.5 from 
50.9 in July, according to a statement on Tuesday. It’s been above the 50 
threshold separating expansion from contraction for nine months, thelongest 
upswing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/china-manufacturing-gauge-surprises-with-faster-august-expansion?cmpid=BBD090126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8307>
 in five years.

Official figures released Monday showed factory activity fared better 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/china-factory-activity-edges-up-in-hopeful-sign-for-economy?cmpid=BBD090126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8307>
 than expected in August while staying in contraction for a second month. That 
uptick suggested manufacturers remainsupported by exports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/china-makes-cars-so-fast-that-it-s-starting-to-worry-regulators?cmpid=BBD090126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8307>
 and pockets of strength in technology-related industries.

Related: China Tells Carmakers to Keep Price War Away From Export Markets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/china-tells-carmakers-to-keep-price-war-away-from-export-markets?cmpid=BBD090126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8307>

The reports are “signaling some improvement in the economy,” according to 
Chang Shu and Eric Zhu of Bloomberg Economics. “The stronger print in 
expansionary territory for this export-oriented gauge in August reflects a 
number of external tailwinds: robust AI-related demand, firmer growth in major 
economies and rising prices of tech products.”

Chinese manufacturers saw their new orders rise for the 15th straight month in 
August, the longest such stretch since 2018, according to the RatingDog survey.

GDP Momentum

China’s economy showed further signs of losing momentum in July. Gross 
domestic product was expanding at an annual rate of roughly 4% at the start of 
the third quarter, according to Goldman Sachs, down from 4.3% in the previous 
quarter and below the government’s 4.5% to 5% growth target.
