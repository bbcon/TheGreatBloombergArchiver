**Oil, Iran, and the Hormuz Constraint**
The oil market's reaction to renewed US-Iran military speculation is sharp and consistent with a risk premium that has been building since hostilities began. At $117 per barrel, Brent reflects both the physical disruption to Persian Gulf shipping and the optionality premium embedded in a conflict whose resolution timeline remains opaque. BP reported its highest profit in 18 years on soaring refining margins; ExxonMobil and Chevron similarly beat expectations. The energy windfall for Western majors stands in direct contrast to the $468 billion in additional global fuel costs estimated since the war began, with the US accounting for over $160 billion — the largest national burden in both absolute and per-capita terms.

**US Natural Gas Consolidation**
The US natural gas sector is undergoing rapid structural consolidation, driven by LNG export terminal buildout along the Gulf Coast and AI data center power demand. EQT's $12.8 billion all-stock acquisition of Range Resources — restoring its position as the largest US producer — is the latest in a series of deals that have eliminated several major independent names. The strategic logic is integration: combined pipeline, reserves, and trading infrastructure that allow producers to compete for long-term contracts with large industrial and technology consumers, reducing spot market exposure. Further consolidation among smaller Appalachian and Permian operators appears likely given the competitive dynamics around contract origination.
