Bond Market Angst

Financial markets have a sometimes-unpredictable tendency of cottoning on to a 
certain narrative, as someone with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s decades 
of hedge-fund experience is in position to know well. (Read our featurehere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
)

Recent news had — and still has — the potential to craft a worrying narrative 
with regard to the US bond market, the world’s largest. First, Federal Reserve 
Chairman Kevin Warsh’s press briefing following the US central bank’s divided 
decision to forgo an interest-rate hike stoked concern among investors. The 
worry was that for all Warsh’s tough talk on inflation, hewasn’t rushing to 
deliver 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/bond-rout-sends-warning-to-warsh-that-tough-talk-is-not-enough?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
.

Next up: Japan. With the yen sliding to a 40-year low last month, Tokyo 
authorities were increasingly looking at intervening. That typically entails 
selling Treasuries in order to produce the dollars to purchase yen. And that’s 
not ideal for the US bond market, given that Japan is the largest foreign 
holder, with over $1 trillion of Treasuries.

Bessent moved to snuff out both of those narratives. He intervened with Japan 
on July 31 to prop up the yen, and suggested that Tokyo make use of a Fed 
facility that wouldavoid outright sales 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/japanese-yen-intervention-why-bessent-wants-fed-to-expand-fima-backstop?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
 of Treasuries. He also took to the airwaves and social media to defend Warsh’s 
new communications strategy.

Wednesday brought another bit of bad news: total US public debt surpassed the 
$40 trillion threshold 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
 for the first time. The kind of milestone that can focus attention on the 
rapid increase in federal borrowing needs.

Hours before that data point was released, Bessent’s department unveiled a 
surprise expansion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
 of the Treasury’s program of buying back outstanding securities. Its 
open-ended guidance stated officials will “at least double” purchases of 
10-year to 30-year debt. Those operations will retire costly, high-yielding 
Treasuries and effectivelyreplace them 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/treasury-s-potentially-limitless-buybacks-cloud-t-bill-outlook?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
 with cheaper short-dated bills, strategists said.

So instead of a triple-whammy of Fed credibility concerns, Japanese exodus 
from Treasuries and an eye-popping figure for US debt, the narrative — for now 
— is one of Bessent’s dedication to bring down borrowing costs to help theUS 
economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/dollar-risks-becoming-biggest-loser-from-bessent-s-bond-buying?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
.

It’s a case of “a bond-vigilant administration” mounting resistance against 
the so-called bond vigilantes, Guneet Dhingra, head of US rates strategy at BNP 
Paribas, wrote in a note with his colleagues. (The vigilantes of course refers 
to the theoretical investors demanding higher yields from the indebted 
borrower.)

In a view shared by many 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/-the-treasury-is-watching-bessent-s-buybacks-jolt-bond-market?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
 market participants and economists, the BNP team viewed Bessent’s latest move 
as insufficient to thwart pressure on Treasuries. That risks sustained 
headwinds on key parts of the economy, including the housing market, as 
highlighted in this newsletterWednesday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-19/us-housing-is-a-buyer-s-market-trouble-is-there-aren-t-many-buyers?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
.

Need-to-Know Research

The UK Treasury would be as much as £32 billion ($43.3 billion) better off 
each year if Britain’s government-run health service had kept pace with its 
pre-pandemic productivity gains, according toanalysis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/prize-worth-32-billion-if-uk-tackles-nhs-efficiency-crisis?cmpid=BBD082026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7933>
 of official figures.

The data shows a 26% surge in resources used by the National Health Service 
from 2019-2025 as successive governments tried to turn around ailing frontline 
services. However, NHS output was up just 19% over the same period.

It leaves productivity lagging well behind pre-Covid levels despite some 
improvement in recent quarters. If productivity had continued to grow at the 
pace seen during the 2010s, the government could be spending £31.5 billion less 
each year on health while achieving the same level of service that it currently 
provides. Alternatively, the UK could be spending that money to improve the NHS 
or other public services.

“We could spend the same amount, but spend it more efficiently and 
effectively,” said Tera Allas, co-chair of the Health Foundation’s NHS 
Productivity Commission, who analyzed the numbers. “I can’t help but look 
around and see that there’s a ton of ways in which that could be done. Those 
tons of ways don’t easily match up with the reality of political economy.”
