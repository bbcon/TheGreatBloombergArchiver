Pageantry, pandas and pancetta did most of the heavy lifting at this week’s 
summit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-xi-trump-state-visits/?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
 between the leaders of the world’s two biggest economies.

Donald Trump broke with precedent to greet Xi Jinping on the tarmac at Joint 
Base Andrews, a rare gesture that offered Beijing the kind of great power 
optics it prizes.
Trump and Xi at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, US, on Tuesday.Photographer: 
Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images
Xi brought some soft power of his own, announcing two pandas for the Atlanta 
Zoo. And a state dinner — featuring crispy pancetta, seabass and a toast with 
the same American sparkling wine served during Richard Nixon’s historic 1972 
visit to China — offered another display of warmth.

Even against already-low expectations of concrete outcomes, there was little 
to show 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/trump-xi-summit-goes-big-on-pomp-but-small-on-substance?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
 for the spectacle.

The two sides discussed artificial intelligence, with Xi proposing continued 
dialogue 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/trump-xi-kick-off-state-visit-amid-tensions-over-ai-trade?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
 on its risks and benefits. But there was no sign of any new mechanism for 
managing AI.

On Taiwan, Xi raised the pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/why-taiwan-is-the-most-volatile-issue-for-us-china-relations?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
, urging Trump to “oppose” independence — stronger language than Washington’s 
longstanding position that it “does not support” it.

The trade truce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/trump-xi-buy-time-on-trade-with-neither-able-to-afford-a-fight?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
 got another two months, but even that extension was shorter than some had 
expected.

The lack of deliverables may miss the point. After a year in which tariffs 
soared and China demonstrated its rare-earth leverage, both presidents appeared 
determined to keep the relationship from veering back toward confrontation.

The splendor, in some ways, was the substance: a demonstration that the 
world’s superpowers can still talk even whenthey agree on little 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/what-is-the-thucydides-trap-and-why-did-xi-raise-it-with-trump?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
.

There were plenty of reminders of how fragile their détente remains, not least 
with US-made F-35 fighter jet parts somehow stuck in China.

The leaders have two more potential opportunities to meet this year, at Xi’s 
APEC in November, followed by Trump’s G20.

Either summit would offer another chance to deliver more than pandas and pomp 
— or to simply keep the train from falling off the rails.— Rebecca Choong 
Wilkins

Chart of the Day

After each of his blood donations in Romania, Ionut Nedelea logs onto his 
brokerage account to retrieve his financial reward: a chance to buy Romanian 
government bonds offering more than 7% — a full percentage point above the 
going rate available to most retail investors. The blood-for-bonds program is 
one of the world’s mostunusual debt-financing campaigns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/romania-blood-bonds-target-retail-investors?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>

And Finally

Despite Trump’s campaign to economically isolate Cuba, US exports to the island
are booming 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-trump-us-cuba-trade-boom/?cmpid=BBD092526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9317>
, with cargo ships making more than 200 trips this year carrying fuel, food, 
vehicles and other goods. Washington is allowing US trade to expand even as 
sanctions squeeze Cuba’s other trading partners in an effort to make the island 
increasingly economically dependent on the US, essentially taking the place of 
the Soviet Union during the Cold War and Venezuela more recently.
