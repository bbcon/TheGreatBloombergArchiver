Rivalry Partnership

Formula One is now a much safer sport thanks to modern guard rails to absorb 
shocks when cars crash, bigger run-off areas to avoid collisions and stronger 
debris fencing to protect crowds when accidents do still happen.

Much the same is now happening with US-China economic relations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/bessent-hails-very-successful-china-talks-on-ai-threats-trade?cmpid=BBD092126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9121>
.
Scott Bessent and He Lifeng during their meeting in New York, in this photo 
released by the Treasury Secretary on social media on Sept. 20.Source: Scott 
Bessent/X
The latest news on that front came, when US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent 
lauded “very successful” talks with his Chinese counterparts in New York on 
artificial intelligence, trade and investment. The two sides agreed to set up a 
dialogue on AI aimed at reaching a common understanding on goals and threats, 
he told reporters after hours of discussions wrapped late Sunday.

Speaking next to him, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the two 
sides have “operationalized” a Board of Trade that can “create a kind of 
ballast for the relationship.” A list of “non-sensitive 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/trump-xi-talks-could-mean-6-billion-of-us-natural-gas-for-china?cmpid=BBD092126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9121>
” items would likely include consumer and low-tech goods from China, and energy,
agricultural 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/china-s-selective-crop-buying-tests-us-trade-truce-before-summit?cmpid=BBD092126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9121>
 and medical products from the US, he said.

“There is a world where you have a relatively small subset of American and 
Chinese goods that can be traded in a balanced way, that are non-sensitive, 
that in the future, as trade actions come up, we can consider them as a class 
unto themselves,” Greer said.

While neither side publicly said whether their tariff truce will be extended, 
the mood music appears to be pointing that way ahead of US President Donald 
Trump’s summit with Xi Jinping in Washington later this week.

On their own, the AI dialog and the Board of Trade development may appear 
incremental. But for the world’s two biggest economies, which remain fierce 
geostrategic competitors, their significance lies in the improved guardrails 
and runoff areas that are increasingly being embedded into the rivalry.

That’s getting closer to a scenario I referenced in this newsletter back in 
2023 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2023-05-12/world-economy-latest-you-say-supply-chains-i-hear-counter-china?cmpid=BBD092126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9121>
 from‘Destined for War’ author Graham Allison that’s worth revisiting.
“One possibility is a ‘rivalry partnership,’ in which they are both fierce 
rivals and intense partners. This concept often occurs in business. Apple and 
Samsung are fierce competitors in smartphone markets, but Samsung is the 
biggest supplier of parts for Apple. That’s an uncomfortable situation, but who 
said life should be comfortable?”
The rivalry will go on. But for the world economy, a safer race is a win for 
all.

Need-to-Know Research

The twin threats of war and weather are driving up Asia’s food prices — an 
established trigger forinstability in the region 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/asia-risks-unrest-as-el-nino-war-threaten-food-supplies-consultancy-says?cmpid=BBD092126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9121>
, according to a study by consultancy Verisk Maplecroft. Key agricultural 
inputs like fuel and fertilizer are still in short supply due to the Middle 
East conflict, while weather extremes linked to a powerful El Niño are further 
threatening harvests across the region.
Blend of civil unrest, food insecurity, agricultural reliance spells trouble 
for some Asian markets, according to a report by Verisk Maplecroft.Source: 
Bloomberg
Over half of countries that are at high- or very high-risk in Verisk’s Food 
Security Index also sit within the two highest categories of its predictive 
Civil Unrest Index. Those include India, the Philippines and Indonesia, where 
protests have already broken out in recent weeks.

“The data suggest if food price and availability pressures intensify, and 
governments are seen to respond ineffectively, unrest and political instability 
could emerge in these higher-risk markets,” the report said.
