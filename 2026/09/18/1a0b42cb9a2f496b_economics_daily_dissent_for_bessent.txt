Defying the House

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says he’s now “the house 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-dares-traders-to-bet-against-yen-i-am-the-house-now?cmpid=BBD091826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9003>
” when it comes to currency markets, daring traders to bet against his 
“insight” into what Japanese monetary officials will do after his rare joint 
intervention to support the yen.

But that “insight” may not extend to board members Toichiro Asada and Ayano 
Sato, both handpicked by Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, who is known for her 
wary stance on rapid interest-rate hikes that might slow the economy.

In Tokyo Friday, Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda delivered for the house 
with a well-telegraphed25 basis-point increase 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/boj-hikes-rates-at-fastest-pace-since-1990-as-inflation-persists?cmpid=BBD091826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9003>
 that marked the sixth hike under his leadership — the most by any BOJ chief in 
at least half a century.

But Asada and Sato voted against the move, leading to a split decision that 
saw Japan’s currency weaken after the decision.
via Bloomberg via Bloomberg Economics; Photos: Bloomberg, Bank of Japan
This was the first BOJ board meeting since Japan and the US jointly intervened 
to support the yen following the bank’s July gathering, the first coordinated 
action to support the yen by the two countries since 1998.

“I am the house now, so when we intervene with the Japanese yen, I have pretty 
good insight into what the Japanese, what the Bank of Japan is going to do, 
what Japanese policymakers are going to do,” Bessent said at an event in Texas 
last week. “And you can bet against me if you want.”

While intraday traders who did bet against Bessent and the yen on Friday may 
have made a profit, the currency remains well off its pre-intervention lows.

Japan spent a record $96.4 billion from July 30 through Aug. 26 in a campaign 
enhanced by US support. The intervention is seen as having narrowed the 
political space for Takaichi’s government to resist further tightening, 
according to an overwhelming majority of economists surveyed by Bloomberg.

It’s unclear whether Asada and Sato’s dissenting votes will act as a brake on 
plans to raise Japanese rates going forward. But to Bloomberg Economics’s Taro 
Kimura, it sent a “dovish signal”.

“Pro-stimulus Takaichi may not be comfortable with a rapid pace of hikes from 
here. The yen’s mild drop on the news underlined the dovish surprise relative 
to expectations in the market,” Kimura wrote in a note, availablehere <> to 
Bloomberg clients.
