Friday, September 11, 2026

US

  • US CPI data due at 8:30 a.m. ET is the pivotal release of the day; consensus estimates not yet confirmed, but the print will likely determine whether the Fed hikes rates at its meeting next week.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield climbed ~18–20 basis points this week to approximately 4.94–4.95%, approaching the psychologically significant 5% level for the first time since 2023.
  • US diesel prices breached $6 per gallon for the first time on record, raising the prospect of an additional energy-driven inflation pulse.
  • Trump's midterm convention in Dallas concluded with little apparent electoral momentum, as vulnerable Republicans stayed away and approval ratings stood at 33%.

Inflation and Monetary Policy

The day's macro focal point is the August CPI report. Traders currently assign roughly a 70% probability to a Fed rate hike at next week's meeting, while a majority of economists still expect rates on hold — a divergence that underscores genuine uncertainty about the policy path. Inflation has run above the Fed's 2% target for five consecutive years, and the latest oil-price surge threatens to widen that overshoot. US diesel crossing $6 per gallon — a historic first — will compound goods and freight inflation in coming months. Treasury Secretary Bessent dismissed concerns about the bond market's trajectory, characterizing Treasuries as being in "very good shape," though a sustained move above 5% on the 10-year would materially raise borrowing costs across mortgages, corporate credit, and government financing ahead of the November midterms.

Markets

Equities steadied ahead of the inflation print, with S&P 500 futures up 0.6%. Oracle gained 6.4% after cloud revenue beat forecasts, a positive read-through for AI-linked capital spending. The rate-equity nexus remains the key tension: survey data suggests a 10-year yield sustained above 5.00–5.25% could catalyze a 10% equity correction, though near-term direction depends almost entirely on today's CPI outcome. ---

Asia

  • China's EV and hybrid share reached 65% of new-car sales in August, nearly reaching the government's 2030 target of 70% four years early.
  • China's trade surplus reached $806 billion year-to-date through August, on pace to surpass the 2025 record of $1.2 trillion.
  • The Nikkei 225 fell 1.9% and South Korea's Kospi dropped 1.8%, reflecting the combined pressure of rising global yields and elevated oil prices.

Energy Shock and Macro Pressure

Oil above $100 poses a differentiated but broadly negative shock across Asia. Japan, South Korea, and India face steeper import bills and renewed inflationary pressure at a moment when domestic price dynamics are already sticky and policy space is constrained. One economist estimates every $10 increase in Brent adds meaningfully to Indian CPI while shaving approximately 15 basis points from growth. Governments in Indonesia and Thailand may face renewed pressure to subsidize fuel costs, straining already-stretched fiscal positions.

China: AI Infrastructure and Trade

China's "dongshu xisuan" strategy — routing coastal AI workloads to data centers in energy-abundant western regions such as Inner Mongolia's Ulanqab — is accelerating. BloombergNEF projects these hinterland hubs will surpass coastal megacity computing capacity around 2028, effectively doubling national data center capacity within five years. The contrast with the US, where data center development faces political backlash and state-level freezes, is drawing explicit attention in Washington. Meanwhile, China's expanding trade surplus and the approaching Trump-Xi summit — provisionally anchored around a September 24 state dinner — are converging into a high-stakes negotiation over tariff structures. Barclays estimates China faces an effective US tariff rate of 23%, against a 7% average for other major partners. ---

Europe

  • The ECB delivered its second rate hike since the outbreak of the Middle East war this week, with President Lagarde calling the move a "no brainer"; markets are pricing a further hike as early as October 29.
  • UK GDP growth beat expectations in July, with AI-linked activity cited as a contributing driver — a positive contrast to France, which cut its GDP forecast and acknowledged it will miss its deficit target in 2026.
  • European natural gas prices are on track for their biggest weekly gain since July as Middle East conflict disrupts LNG flows and the continent works to refill pre-winter inventories.

ECB Monetary Policy

The ECB's hawkish turn reflects two distinct pressures: Europe's acute exposure to energy import costs, which amplifies the Middle East conflict's inflationary pass-through far more than in the US; and institutional memory of the 2022 episode, when the ECB was widely criticized for being late to tighten despite inflation exceeding 10%. Officials have acknowledged that their own forecasts, which assumed average oil below $90 per barrel, are already stale given crude trading well above $100. Markets are pricing approximately three additional hikes over the next year, though ECB officials have signaled that pace may be overdone. Separately, Lagarde's departure timeline remains uncertain, with her memoir scheduled for January publication fueling speculation about an exit well before her term concludes in October 2027.

UK vs. France Divergence

Britain's July GDP print was characterized as a beat, with AI-sector activity cited as an emerging growth contributor — a noteworthy early signal if sustained. France presents a sharply contrasting picture: the government has abandoned its deficit reduction target for 2026 as growth disappoints, a development ECB President Lagarde described as "financially dangerous" in comments directed at far-left spending proposals. ---

Global

  • Brent crude traded between $103 and $107 during the week — on track for its largest weekly gain since July — as Houthi forces advanced toward the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and tanker attacks resumed in the Gulf.
  • The IEA's latest monthly report cut both global oil supply and demand forecasts, warning that "further demand reductions may be required" and that the supply shortfall will be larger and more prolonged than previously estimated.
  • The 10-year US Treasury yield approached 5% while a gauge of global yields hit its highest level since 2007, with German, Australian, Japanese, and New Zealand yields all surging in tandem.
  • The BRICS summit in New Delhi brings together Xi Jinping (his first India visit since 2019), Vladimir Putin (his first in-person BRICS appearance outside Russia since the Ukraine invasion), and host Narendra Modi — a convergence reflecting shifting alignments as US tariff pressure and war reshape global trade flows.

Oil Markets and the Strait of Hormuz

The Iran war, which began in February, initially shocked markets above $120 per barrel before improvised logistics — backup pipelines, clandestine shuttle runs, reduced Chinese imports — allowed prices to nearly retrace to the $60s by July. That partial normalization has now reversed. Renewed Houthi advances toward Mokha and the Bab el-Mandeb, combined with fresh tanker attacks in the Gulf, have pushed Brent back above $100. The IEA notes Saudi Arabia has been forced to cut production to its lowest level since the 1990 Gulf War. The cumulative supply destruction is now large enough that the IEA acknowledges the gap may only close through demand destruction — a stagflationary dynamic with implications for every major economy. US diesel at a historic $6-per-gallon reflects this squeeze at the retail level.

Global Monetary Policy Divergence

The ECB is hiking aggressively into an energy shock. The Fed faces a binary decision next week contingent on today's CPI print, with market pricing and economist consensus in rare disagreement. The Bank of Japan and other Asian central banks are navigating oil-driven inflation with limited fiscal room. The result is a global tightening impulse occurring unevenly, with the bond market providing its own verdict: a global yield gauge at its highest since 2007 signals that the repricing of rate expectations is far from complete.
