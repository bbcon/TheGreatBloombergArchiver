Stocks versus gravity

US stocks just notched their first record since August. But to Jim Paulsen, 
the pressure from $100 oil, 5% Treasury yields and an elevated dollaris yet to 
be felt in a major way 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/paulsen-sees-a-15-s-p-500-drop-as-oil-rates-and-dollar-rise?cmpid=100726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9770>
.

Paulsen, a 40-year-plus investing veteran and longtime bull on US stocks, says 
prior instances when the three factors simultaneously weighed on the S&P 500 
have preceded a drop of as much as 15% in the next three to five months. A 
repeat would send the index down for the year for the first time since 2022.

To Paulsen, while blockbuster earnings and enthusiasm around AI keep 
supporting the S&P 500, investors will feel the effect of rising yields, dollar 
and oil prices soon.

“We’re going to have a good little gut check,” Paulsen said. “Gravity may soon 
bring its undefeated reputation to both the economy and the stock market.” —
Geoffrey Morgan

Taiwan’s AI opportunity

As equity investors enter the final stretch of a pivotal year that’s propelled 
South Korea and Taiwan to the forefront of the global AI trade,the latter is 
emerging as the stronger bet 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/taiwan-dethrones-korea-atop-global-markets-as-ai-trade-widens?cmpid=100726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9770>
.

The Taiex Index beat South Korea’s Kospi by about 23 percentage points last 
quarter, the widest margin since the turn of the century. Earnings upgrades 
also outpaced those for its Korean peer for the first time since March 2025.

Driving the optimism are Taiwan’s deeper linkages across the AI supply chain 
and a more upbeat earnings outlook.

Korea’s market leadership, meanwhile, remains heavily concentrated in 
chipmakers Samsung and SK Hynix, leaving it more vulnerable as doubts emerge 
over how much longer the memory upcycle can run. (Investors there also absorbed
big losses from a speculative mania 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/korean-investors-suffer-1-7-billion-losses-from-leveraged-etfs?cmpid=100726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9770>
 this year.)

“The distinction for us is not simply the amount of AI exposure, but the 
nature of the earnings supporting it,” said Vikas Pershad at M&G Investments. 
“Taiwan offers a broader and deeper opportunity set. Its earnings come from 
volume and that makes its earnings upgrades broader and stickier. Korea’s 
earnings, in the near term, are coming from price.” —Abhishek Vishnoi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASxY3iVjhAY/abhishek-vishnoi?cmpid=100726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9770>
 andWinnie Hsu
