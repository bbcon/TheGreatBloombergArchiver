Global bond angst

Longer-maturity bonds are at the epicenter of investor angst about everything 
from inflation to the debt-laden AI boom — andgovernments are paying the price 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/bond-rout-sends-long-term-borrowing-costs-to-highest-in-decades?cmpid=081826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7833>
.

Sovereign borrowing rates are surging around the world. Yields on 30-year US 
Treasuries rose to the highest since 2007 this week, whileFrench borrowing 
costs hit the loftiest since 2008 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/traders-short-french-bonds-as-fight-over-budget-set-to-escalate?cmpid=081826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7833>
 and their German peerstraded at 2011 levels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/germany-is-set-to-sell-30-year-bonds-at-highest-yield-since-2011?cmpid=081826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7833>
. Equivalent yields are approaching 6% in the UK and similar-maturity Japanese 
ones are close to their all-time high.

Setting aside domestic factors, investors are fretting about the risks of 
persistent inflation, profligate governments and changes in market structure 
and demographics that are weighing on demand for bonds.

It’s a maelstrom for finance ministers, many of whom are shifting their 
debt-issuance toward shorter tenors where yields are lower.There’s only so much 
room for maneuver, though, as they adjust to a world where they can no longer 
lock in financing costs for decades at rock-bottom rates.

In US Treasuries specifically, investors are becoming more concerned about the 
surge in borrowing by hyperscalers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-17/ai-debt-sales-are-keeping-us-treasury-yields-high?cmpid=081826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7833>
, said strategist Ed Yardeni. They’re also questioning whether the Fed will 
remain vigilant on inflation if oil prices climb again, he says.

“We aren’t pushing the panic button,” his firm said in a report today 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/yardeni-warns-bond-vigilantes-stirring-as-us-yields-near-5?cmpid=081826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7833>
. “However, we are closely monitoring whether the bond vigilantes might do so.”

The anti-AI stock

As investors have rediscovered their appetite for all things AI, Apple shares 
have paid the price 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/apple-s-anti-ai-position-suddenly-becomes-stock-market-baggage?cmpid=081826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7833>
.

The stock is down 10% from its July 28 record following the iPhone maker’s 
disappointing earnings. Meanwhile, investors have snapped up shares of 
perceived AI winners Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet as their results assuaged 
fears about overspending on the technology.

Apple, which has opted to power its AI services through partnerships and avoid 
the costly arms race for computing infrastructure, is among the 25 worst 
performing stocks in the S&P 500 over the past three weeks. Meanwhile, the 
Nasdaq 100 is up 8%.

“Not spending looks smart when you’re worried about spending, but now that the 
AI trade is going strong, we can’t say that it’s reaping the rewards the others 
are,” said Clayton Allison, an Apple shareholder at Prime Capital Financial. —
Ryan Vlastelica
