 andthe dollar is weakening 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/dollar-risks-becoming-biggest-loser-from-bessent-s-bond-buying?cmpid=082126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=7995>
. Suddenly, chatter about the debasement trade is back.

Scott Bessent’s announcement this week that the US Treasury will step up its 
bond repurchases in a bid to bring long-term yields lower sparked a new round 
of dollar selling.

The thinking is that, while Bessent and Donald Trump want to bring long-term 
bond yields down,the buyback news is further evidence they aren’t yet ready to 
do the hard work of reducing the US budget deficit.

Instead, the administration and Congress are pursuing a path that will fuel 
inflation and weaken the currency, which ultimately will reduce the real value 
of the debt. That’s a case to buy relatively scarce, hard assets like gold.

“Markets are primed for dollar debasement to resume and — as Japan shows — it 
can be next to impossible to stabilize a currency once it enters a devaluation 
spiral,” Robin Brooks, a former Goldman Sachs currency strategist,wrote on 
Substack <https://robinjbrooks.substack.com/p/the-debasement-trade-takes-off> 
yesterday. “The US is playing with fire with this buyback.”

For now, the talk is largely just that. The Bloomberg dollar index, for 
example, is only down 1% this year. Gold and Bitcoin are far away from their 
previous highs, and have been deeply unpopular trades.

The gold trade lost momentum

Europe’s surprising rally

European stocks started trading this morning on the brink of their longest 
losing streak in a decade. But looking past the seven-day decline for the Stoxx 
Europe 600 Index, equities are racking up another solid year.

Corporate earnings have blown past analysts’ estimates, an improving economic 
outlook has reassured investors and Europe has a broad swath of industrial 
companies that are benefiting from the AI boom.

Market forecasters have consistently underestimated the strength of the rally, 
andthey’re still playing it cautious 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/goldman-jpmorgan-are-among-most-bullish-on-europe-taking-stock?cmpid=082126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=7995>
: In aggregate, they expect the Stoxx 600 to end the year little changed from 
where it is now, a Bloomberg survey shows.

“Europe has done much better than almost everybody would have expected at the 
outset of this year,” said Sharon Bell at Goldman Sachs. “There’s been so much 
attention on a handful of companies in the US and Asia that I just don’t feel 
Europe has had its proper due.” —Sagarika Jaisinghani
