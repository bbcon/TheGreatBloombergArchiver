Panda Diplomacy

At a dinner during Richard Nixon’s historic trip to Beijing in February 1972, 
first lady Patricia Nixon mentioned her fondness for pandas to Chinese Premier 
Zhou Enlai. Mao Zedong’s right-hand-man soon gifted Ling-Ling and Hsing-Hsing 
to the American people, and “Panda Diplomacy 
<https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/history-giant-pandas-zoo>” was born.
Mao Zedong, left, shakes hands with Richard Nixon in Beijing in 1972.Source: 
Keystone/Getty Images via Hulton Archive
Ling-Ling and Hsing-Hsing remained the only two Pandas on US shores through 
the rest of the 1970s as the two sides inched toward formal diplomatic 
recognition, the 1980s as early commercial ties formed and into the mid 1990s. 
Then something of a Panda boom took off, accelerating after China’s entry into 
the World Trade Organization in late 2001 to peak at 15 by 2015.

That was 11 years ago, when Xi Jinping made his last state visit to the US and 
his then host President Barak Obamadelivered a toast 
<https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/reliable-source/wp/2015/09/25/full-transcript-president-obamas-toast-at-the-chinese-state-dinner/>
 at the state dinner in which he entreated the Chinese and American people to 
“work together like fingers on the same hand in friendship and peace.”
Xi Jinping shakes hands with President Barack Obama in 2015.Photographer: Pete 
Marovich/Bloomberg
Less than 14 months after that speech, Trump was elected president, with his 
America First, tough-on-China rhetoric. Tariffs ensued. Panda numbers plunged. 
At one stage in 2024 there wasn’t a panda to be seen in the US.

Now, the pandas are back 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/two-pandas-to-arrive-in-atlanta-in-a-few-days-xi-says?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
.

“In a few days, two pandas, Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, will come to their new 
home in Zoo Atlanta and meet with the American people,” Xi said in a speech at 
the White House during his first state visit to the US in 11 years.

In a relationship where symbols often reveal more than words, the panda news 
isn’t without significance. As we explored inMonday’s newsletter 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-21/trump-xi-summit-prep-gathers-pace-as-officials-make-headway-on-ai-trade?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
, the world’s two biggest economies remainfierce strategic rivals 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/what-is-the-thucydides-trap-and-why-did-xi-raise-it-with-trump?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
 but are placing guardrails around that competition.
Xi and Trump shake hands on Thursday.Photographer: Annabelle 
Gordon/CNP/Bloomberg via Consolidated News Photos
Still, gaps between the two 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/trump-xi-kick-off-state-visit-amid-tensions-over-ai-trade?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
 remain. Trump wants to keep AI “exactly where it is” while XI seems open to 
guard rails. Xi wants to “leverage respective strengths” — a predictable 
approach for a leader who likes to look for “win-win” outcomes.

Trump said Xi and he had made “tremendous strides on the issues facing our two 
countries,” referring to efforts to expand access to the Chinese market for US 
farmers and ranchers under the newly created Board of Trade.

Meantime, Xi issued his most explicit call for Trump to change the US’s stance 
on Taiwan, urging him to “oppose” independence for the self-ruled island that 
Beijing sees as its own territory.

While the visit may have underwhelmed in some aspects

Chang Shu

<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVK6e_X298w/eric-zhu-economist?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>

Need-to-Know Research

The 2020s are bound to go down as the decade of supply-chain shocks. 
Economists have had to come to grips with shortages of everything from 
semiconductors, crude oil, high-bandwidth memory and fertilizers to, now, 
diesel. Morgan Stanley analysts havetaken a look 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/gasoline-to-cost-more-if-us-diesel-ban-hits-morgan-stanley-says?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
 at that chain.

US retail prices of diesel recently topped $6.50 a gallon for the first time 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/us-diesel-tops-6-50-a-gallon-as-wars-worsen-global-fuels-crunch?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
, driven by two main factors, analysts including Martijn Rats wrote in a note 
this week. Major refining capacity in both the Middle East and in Russia has 
effectively become unavailable for global markets due to the two current main 
wars. The surge in prices has now spurred debate over a potentialUS export ban 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/trump-backed-export-ban-could-initially-lower-diesel-prices?cmpid=BBD092526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9301>
.
Sources: UN Comtade, JODI, Eurostat, Morgan Stanley Research
One of the likely side effects of any such move would, counterintuitively, be 
higher gasoline prices, the Morgan Stanley team warned. That’s because as 
domestic supply of diesel rose, prices would fall and refiners would likely 
trim crude processing, which means a net drop in gasoline supply, they said. 
Elsewhere, diesel prices would climb, with Europe most exposed, they added.
