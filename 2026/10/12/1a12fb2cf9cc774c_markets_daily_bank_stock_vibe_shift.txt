Bank stocks try to escape AI scare trade

US bank stocks have been battered in recent months by the AI “scare trade” — 
the idea that the widespread adoption of agents will undermine the customer 
inertia that helps lenders retain low-cost deposits.

Yet with the benchmark KBW Bank Index down roughly 13% since August, some 
analysts think the narrative is overdone. Wall Street is poised to deliver 
another strong quarter, and the stocks’ cheapnesscould be a win for investors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/goldman-sachs-gs-poised-to-lead-wall-street-s-19-billion-stock-trading-haul?cmpid=101226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9973>
.

“We see this selloff as an attractive entry point,” said Morgan Stanley 
analyst Manan Gosalia.

If there is a vibe shift, if could start with equity trading desks. Wall 
Street’s biggest banks are expected to unveil a quarterly stock-trading haul of 
nearly $19 billion when the firms report earnings this week.

Yet investors will need to be selective. “There’s likely a wider dispersion 
this quarter between the winners and losers,” Wells Fargo analyst Mike Mayo 
said.

Potential stumbling blocks: Higher rates are weighing on fixed-income trading, 
and they threaten to add more volatility to banks’ balance sheets. The pipeline 
of M&A deals has also emerged as a concern. —Denitsa Tsekova

The week ahead

The main economic event is the release of US CPI data on Wednesday. Higher 
gasoline prices probably led to quicker overall inflation in September, even as 
underlying price pressures moderated. That second bit is crucial. Cooler 
underlying inflation reduces the urgency for the Fed to raise rates this month.

Elsewhere, IMF meetings in Bangkok are likely to draw attention, with global 
finance chiefs and central bankers — including Kevin Warsh and Christine 
Lagarde — set to speak.
