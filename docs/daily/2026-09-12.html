Energy and Industrial Policy

The DC Circuit Court of Appeals rejected the Energy Department's use of emergency powers under the Federal Power Act to extend the operational life of Consumers Energy's J.H. Campbell coal plant in Michigan, a 1,420-megawatt facility that was scheduled to retire in May 2025. The court found that successive 90-day emergency orders — six in total, the most recent issued August 14 — exceeded the statutory authority Congress intended. The ruling has broader implications: the administration has applied the same legal mechanism to fossil fuel plants elsewhere across the country, and those arrangements may now face similar legal exposure. The administration has also directed hundreds of millions of dollars in federal support toward coal plant life extension, suggesting the policy will persist through alternative channels even as the emergency-powers route is constrained.

Critical Minerals and Seabed Policy

The administration's effort to establish US licensing authority over deep-sea polymetallic nodule extraction in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone has attracted a wave of largely undercapitalized entrants pivoting into the sector. Commercial and technological viability remain highly uncertain — independent assessments, including a 2025 Rand Corporation study, suggest commercial viability is unlikely within 15 years — and the legal framework conflicts with International Seabed Authority jurisdiction. ---

China: Innovation and Political Economy

Analysis from ChinaTalk founder Jordan Schneider offers a measured corrective to prevailing pessimism about China's technological trajectory under Xi Jinping's consolidation of power. Schneider acknowledges that the political environment imposes a real discount factor on entrepreneurial activity — regulatory reversals and legal risk are not trivial — but argues the comparison to Soviet-era stagnation is misplaced. Chinese universities have materially improved research output and talent quality, and the scale of the domestic market continues to serve as a structural enabler for technological development. The more substantive constraint, in Schneider's framing, is the alignment of private-sector dynamism with state priorities, which tends to deepen as firms scale. Whether that alignment crowds out genuine innovation or merely redirects it remains an open and consequential question for assessing China's long-run competitiveness.

Geopolitics

On Taiwan and broader regional security, Schneider characterizes Xi as operating within a self-imposed risk threshold, suggesting that full-scale military action against Taiwan remains below the threshold Xi has set for the PLA in the near term. China's alliance structure — limited to Russia and North Korea — contrasts with the US network of treaty allies across the Indo-Pacific, a structural asymmetry that complicates straightforward assessments of Chinese military dominance. ---

Brazil: Political Economy

Brazil's electoral dynamics are shifting meaningfully, with Bolsonaro's political positioning strengthening despite continued legal proceedings that have defined his post-presidency period. A tightening race introduces material uncertainty around fiscal policy continuity, trade posture, and the trajectory of institutional relationships with the central bank and judiciary. Markets will be attentive to whether the legal proceedings accelerate or stabilize, as the outcome bears directly on Bolsonaro's eligibility and the credibility of the opposition's positioning.

Frontier Sovereign Credit

Senegal's debt restructuring proposal, which carries implied bondholder losses of approximately 60%, represents a significant stress event for frontier sovereign credit. The scale of the proposed haircut, if realized, would rank among the more severe restructurings in recent African sovereign debt history and may prompt reassessment of risk premia across comparable frontier issuers. Contagion pressure will depend on whether the restructuring is perceived as idiosyncratic to Senegal's fiscal position or symptomatic of broader vulnerabilities across sub-Saharan frontier borrowers who accessed international capital markets during the low-rate era. ---

Resource Governance and Multilateral Tension

The US administration's pursuit of unilateral deep-sea mining licensing in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone — an area under ISA jurisdiction — exemplifies a broader pattern of the current administration asserting resource claims outside established multilateral frameworks. Forty-six ISA member states have called for a moratorium on seabed mining, creating a legal and diplomatic overhang that complicates the commercial pathway for any operator seeking internationally recognized title to extracted minerals. The episode sits alongside trade and technology policy as an area where US unilateralism is generating structural friction with international institutions.

Climate and Energy Transition

Indonesia's wildfire season is intensifying under an extended dry period, with hotspot counts exceeding 5,000 and transboundary haze posing public health and economic risks across Southeast Asia. Separately, Northern California faces targeted power cuts this weekend due to elevated wildfire conditions driven by high winds and low humidity — a recurring operational stress for Western grid managers that underscores the intersection of climate volatility and energy infrastructure reliability.
