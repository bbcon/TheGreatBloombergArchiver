Pricing AI risk

Investors considering Anthropic’s planned mega-IPO are grappling with an 
unusual, and more than a little scary, conundrum 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/anthropic-ipo-investors-struggle-to-put-a-price-on-rogue-ai-risk?cmpid=100926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9921>
: How to value a company that some fear could help wipe out the human race?

AI researchers are having a public debate about potential AI-caused 
extinction, with some pegging the probability at 10% or more. Anthropic CEO 
Dario Amodei hasweighed in 
<https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier> on risks too, 
outlining concerns that humans could lose control of AI systems, which could be 
misused for cyberattacks or bioterrorism.

Observers have scoffed at the idea that a company could be held liable for 
some of these more dire scenarios. The immediate question, with Anthropic 
seeking to go public as soon as the middle of November, is how would you even 
quantify that risk in an IPO disclosure?
Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, arrives for a meeting on AI at the White House.
Photographer: Aaron Schwartz/Bloomberg
Here’s one idea on how to address the issue:

Slow Ventures partner Sam Lessin has proposed the US government should force 
AI labs to put up $1 trillion in cash in escrow to pay for the future potential 
cleanup, similar to how regulators demanded after the 2008 financial crisis 
that banks should hold more capital to cover losses. Even that may not be 
enough, he said.

“The liabilities might be a $2 trillion or $4 trillion risk to society, even 
before you consider the mass-extinction they talk about, making the companies 
worth less than zero overall,” Lessin said.

Yet with little direct legal precedent for AI agents going rogue, investors 
considering buying into Anthropic face what amounts to a binary choice:Accept 
the risk that the company may be on the hook for some of the most stark AI 
scenarios, or don’t.

The latter seems most likely. After all, SpaceX pinned its aspirations on AI 
and its shares are trading more than 20% above their June IPO offering price. —

China bonds become source of cheap money

China is letting a wave of cheap money flow across its borders, turning the 
country into arare source of low-cost funding 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/chinese-bonds-become-source-of-cheap-money-for-global-borrowers?cmpid=100926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9921>
 for foreign borrowers.

Regulators have approved the proceeds from more than 40% of panda bonds sold 
so far this year to be taken offshore, the highest share on record, according 
to data compiled by Bloomberg. Panda bonds are yuan-denominated bonds issued in 
mainland China by overseas borrowers.

Subdued inflation and a weak economy have pushed rates lower as borrowing 
costs rise elsewhere, while Beijing is also loosening its grip on cross-border 
flows as it seeks a bigger global role for the yuan.

“Panda issuers are simply finding borrowing costs in the onshore renminbi 
market increasingly competitive versus those elsewhere,” said Dariusz Kowalczyk 
of Banco Bilbao Vizcaya Argentaria.
