Australia is boldly hiking as the central bank seeks to bring inflationary 
pressures under control.

The Reserve Bank’s board lifted the cash rate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/australia-raises-key-rate-to-15-year-high-to-restrain-inflation?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9462>
 to 4.6% from 4.35%, as widely expected, taking rates to the highest level in 
about 15 years. The nine-member policy committee voted unanimously to deliver 
its fourth hike this year, saying firms are experiencing cost pressures and 
either increasing prices or looking to do so.

“Since the previous meeting, some of the upside risks to inflation are 
materialising,” the policy board said. “The board will continue to do what it 
considers necessary to bring inflation sustainably back to target, including 
increasing the cash rate target further if needed.”

Traders lifted bets on a further RBA rate hike, with swaps pricing a 56% 
chance of a follow-up move in November. Tuesday’s tightening will weigh further 
on Australia’s housing market, which is already ina downturn 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/one-of-the-world-s-longest-housing-booms-is-cracking-in-sydney?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9462>
. The question now is how far the RBA will need to go to tackle inflation at a 
time when consumers are already feeling the squeeze.
