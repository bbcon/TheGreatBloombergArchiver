Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
senior reporter Peter Millard looks at the newest names in Venezuelan oil.

Amid the flurry of dealmaking around Venezuela’s battered energy industry, the 
country’s next generation of oil barons is beginning to emerge.

Some are familiar, such as US oil major Chevron Corp. which stole the show on 
Wednesday with a new$7 billion dea 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/chevron-to-spend-7-billion-on-doubling-its-venezuelan-output?cmpid=BBBXT090226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8436>
l. Others look rather different from what went before.

The Latin American country was once one of the world’s biggest crude 
suppliers. Decades of dominance by major US and European oil companies cracked 
initially due to nationalization. It was later undone almost completely under 
socialist Presidents Hugo Chávez and Nicolás Maduro.

Maduro’s capture and removal by US forces in January signaled a reset. 
President Donald Trump now wants to revive production using private capital 
rather than American taxpayers’ money.

While some big, established producers have demurred, other more risk-tolerant 
investors are stepping in.

Some of the names to emerge so far have solid experience, such as closely held 
Hunt Oil Co. of Texas and Colombia’s billionaireGilinski family 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/billionaire-gilinski-geopark-said-to-be-near-venezuela-oil-deal?cmpid=BBBXT090226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8436>
.

Chevron, the one US oil producer that stayed the course during the Chavez and 
Maduro years, has just finalized a deal that will double its production in the 
country, adding two giant oil fields in the Orinoco Belt.

But others either have little track record in oil — Brazil’s billionaire 
Batista brothers, for example — or none whatsoever: Bloomberg reported Tuesday 
that Coinbase co-founder Fred Ehrsam is seeking totake control 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/coinbase-co-founder-seeks-fields-in-venezuela-oil-shakeup?cmpid=BBBXT090226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8436>
 of at least three Venezuelan oil fields.

There’s also Alejandro Betancourt, a controversial Venezuelan tycoon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/us-stands-by-oil-deal-with-venezuelan-energy-mogul-betancourt?cmpid=BBBXT090226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8436>
 with diverse interests who deftly navigated Chavez and Maduro to become the 
Trump administration’s fixer on the ground.

The US government has agreed to take a direct stake in Betancourt’s North 
American Blue Energy Partners, which says it’s already producing about 200,000 
barrels a day. The new company is targeting 1 million barrels a day, almost 
matching the entire current national output. How such an enormous expansion 
would be financed isn’t clear.

The profile of some of these would-be oil barons reveal two features of 
Trump’s handling of the situation.

First, some of the winners in the scramble for Venezuela’s black gold have 
personal connections with the Trump administration.

Second, the US president is impatient. With midterm elections looming and high 
fuel prices becoming a liability, hewants progress right now 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/trump-s-energy-chief-says-venezuela-poised-to-double-oil-output?cmpid=BBBXT090226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8436>
.

Wildcatters and their ilk tend to move more quickly than lumbering oil majors. 
Whether they can follow through on their promises remains to be seen.

—Peter Millard, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

European Union carbon prices are likely to edge lower in September as cooler 
temperatures reduce power demand and curb utility emissions, according to 
BloombergNEF. Higher allowance supply may further loosen the market balance. 
The main upside risk comes from renewed buying ahead of the September 
compliance deadline and investment funds rebuilding positions after cutting 
exposure in August.
