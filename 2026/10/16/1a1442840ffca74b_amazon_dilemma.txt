The construction of a highway in the middle of the Amazon rainforest lays bare 
the tensions at the heart of Brazil’s presidential race.

For many in the Brazilian Amazon, the paving of a 550-mile dirt road is a 
godsend, promising the first modern land link from one of the country’s poorest 
regions to more prosperous points south.

Others worry that deforestation will follow the roadworks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-15/brazil-election-amazon-highway-br-319-shows-high-climate-stakes?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
, as settlers arrive to clear land and plant crops or raise cattle.
Workers improve the BR-319 highway in Realidade, Amazonas state, Brazil.
Photographer: Victor Moriyama/Bloomberg
The dilemma is reflected in President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s platform, as 
he tries to reconcile conserving the rainforest with improving living standards 
and opportunities for residents through development projects like building the 
highway.

It’s a balance that the leftist leader appears to be getting right: He led his 
opponent, Flávio Bolsonaro, there in the Oct. 4 first round by 48.2% to 45%, 
even as hetrailed the conservative senator nationally 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/brazil-election-what-s-at-stake-in-the-oct-25-presidential-runoff?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
.

Bolsonaro has yet to detail his policy plans. 

What’s clear is that he supports opening more of the Amazon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/brazil-s-bolsonaro-promises-a-sharp-pivot-on-climate-and-the-amazon?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
 to farmers. At a recent campaign stop in the region, he vowed to bring more 
development — including paving roads — to the rainforest.

Deforestation surged under his father, former President Jair Bolsonaro.

Flávio Bolsonaro led after the first round 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/bolsonaro-lula-set-to-face-off-in-brazil-runoff-datafolha-says?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
 of voting across Latin America’s largest economy. Yet latest polling suggests 
the incumbent may beclosing the gap 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/bolsonaro-lula-in-tight-race-ahead-of-brazil-runoff-poll-shows?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
 ahead of the Oct. 25 runoff.

That’s as Lula’s government unveiled a proposal this week to forgive debt owed 
by so-called micro-entrepreneurs, and won Senate approval of legislation to
shorten the workweek 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/brazil-election-senate-approves-bill-to-shorten-workweek-in-boost-to-lula?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
 without reducing pay.

With some 2 million voters in a country of 200 million-plus deciding the first 
round in favor of Bolsonaro, both candidates are scrapping for every ballot.

What the outcome might mean for the Amazon will become clearer in 10 days. —

Next week’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meeting in Hong Kong will be the 
city’s highest-profile assembly of policymakers in years. For China, the 
gathering is an opportunity to showcase a Hong Kong that has emerged from what 
officials call a transition “from chaos to order.”It’s a turnaround from 2014 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/hong-kong-s-apec-comeback-showcases-a-city-remade-by-beijing?cmpid=BBD101626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10151>
, when China last chaired APEC and abruptly moved the event to Beijing as 
threats of pro-democracy demonstrations brewed. Twelve years on, much of the 
opposition has been jailed, driven into exile or disbanded, and the mass street 
protests that once defined the city’s political life have all but vanished.
