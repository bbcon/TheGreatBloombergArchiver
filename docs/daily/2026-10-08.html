Daily Brief

Thursday, October 08, 2026

US

  • FOMC minutes confirmed unanimous September rate hike and signaled another increase by year-end, with Fed officials describing policy as "not restrictive or only mildly restrictive."
  • 10-year Treasury yields reached 5.36% intraday before settling near 5.28–5.35%, extending the year's bond selloff; Goldman Sachs forecasts a decline to 4.75% by year-end.
  • PepsiCo cut its earnings outlook, citing slower-than-expected North America recovery amid higher costs and consumer pressure.
  • Consumer confidence (Conference Board) fell to a 12-year low; one-year inflation expectations (NY Fed) rose to 3.9%, a three-year high, even as retail sales data beat.

Monetary Policy

The September FOMC minutes, which showed all 19 officials backing the rate hike unanimously, reinforced market expectations for at least one additional increase before year-end — currently priced at 96.4% probability. The minutes noted that "several participants" viewed current policy as not yet restrictive, and that "a number" saw a higher rate path as warranted under their modal outlooks rather than merely as risk management. Fed Governor Waller separately acknowledged flexibility on timing. Goldman Sachs forecasts 10-year yields declining to 4.75% by year-end, arguing underlying inflation pressures remain contained; Bank of America projects 5%, but flags that the balance of risks skews toward higher rates.

Earnings and Markets

Q3 earnings season begins Tuesday with the large banks. Deutsche Bank's Bankim Chadha projects 34% S&P 500 EPS growth this quarter versus 27% consensus, setting up another round of above-average beats. Goldman Sachs notes AI infrastructure beneficiaries account for over 50% of index EPS growth, with hyperscaler capex up 116% year-on-year. The S&P 500 slipped 0.2% as elevated oil prices and bond yields weighed. PepsiCo's guidance cut was a rare negative data point, signaling that cost pressures and a bifurcated consumer remain genuine risks even as aggregate spending holds up. The 30-year mortgage rate hit 7.38%, the highest since Trump's return to office, adding to affordability stress ahead of November midterms. ---

Asia

  • Samsung Electronics Q3 operating profit rose nearly ninefold year-on-year but missed elevated expectations; shares fell 2.4% in Seoul.
  • TSMC Q3 revenue jumped 51%, a positive signal for AI infrastructure demand sustainability, though ADRs slipped 1.1%.
  • Singapore's Straits Times Index tumbled 3.5%, its worst session since April 2025, after JPMorgan warned surging long bond yields would hurt Southeast Asian bank earnings.
  • India's rupee approached a record low near 96.97 per dollar, with RBI intervention drawing down FX reserves by $51 billion in the four weeks through October 2.

Corporate Earnings and AI Demand

Samsung's near-ninefold profit surge — impressive in absolute terms — still fell short of sky-high market expectations inflated by relentless AI infrastructure spending, illustrating the execution risk embedded in current valuations. TSMC's 51% revenue gain offered a more constructive read on the sustainability of the AI capex cycle. Yet investor reaction to both results was muted, weighed by concerns about whether AI investment commitments — many financed with debt — can be honored if demand falters. Tencent is separately considering an offshore bond offering of up to $5 billion to fund AI-related expansion.

China Stimulus and Currency

China's pre-Golden Week stimulus package drew skeptical assessments from major banks. JPMorgan characterized it as "state-guided and supply heavy," while Morgan Stanley warned it could only "backstop the growth floor" without breaking the deleveraging trap. Property sales remain down over 13% for January through August, and the stimulus contained minimal consumption support. China is on track to meet its 4.5%–5% growth target, but primarily by reinforcing already-strong export and high-tech sectors. Separately, China's central bank rejected claims that the yuan is undervalued as EU-China trade talks opened in Beijing. ---

Europe

  • French 10-year OAT yields rose to 4.94%, with French government bonds handing investors a 4.6% loss year-to-date; nearly €215 billion of French corporate bonds now trade tighter than sovereign debt — an 18-fold increase since January 2026.
  • Germany more than doubled its 2026 growth forecast, driven by robust exports and a surge in government spending.
  • The Stoxx Europe 600 fell 0.9%; Stoxx 600 bank index posted its largest two-day decline since March, with Société Générale shares down roughly 25% over two months.

France as the Fiscal Stress Bellwether

France has emerged as the primary indicator of sovereign stress in developed markets. Political gridlock and fiscal deterioration have eroded confidence in French government debt to the point where corporate bonds from multinationals like L'Oréal are now perceived as safer by the market. Prime Minister Lecornu's decision to release diesel reserves reflects the political difficulty of implementing austerity as student protests mount over education funding. Marine Le Pen has simultaneously moved to displace Jordan Bardella atop the National Rally's 2027 presidential campaign, adding political uncertainty. The Bank of France governor warned that higher borrowing costs are a global challenge facing all countries.

EU-China Trade and German Policy

The EU's trade chief Maros Sefcovic opened two days of Beijing negotiations taking a notably more assertive posture, with safeguard measures on Chinese hybrid vehicle imports on the table. Separately, Chancellor Merz's cabinet blocked Cosco Shipping's proposed 80% acquisition of Hamburg freight forwarder Konrad Zippel, citing supply chain resilience and strategic dependency risks — a signal that German policy on Chinese investment is hardening despite strong bilateral trade ties. Germany's upgraded growth forecast provides some fiscal cushion, though its climate finance spending fell more than one-fifth to €4.75 billion in 2025, below its €6 billion annual pledge. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's Flávio Bolsonaro secured backing from four center-right parties ahead of the October 25 presidential runoff against incumbent Lula, consolidating his coalition.
  • Chinese auto brands captured 25% of Brazil's new-car market in September, up from approximately 9% for full-year 2025, raising concerns about domestic industry employment.
  • Cuba's communist government remains in place despite nine months of tightening US sanctions and a de facto oil blockade, with the private sector serving as an inadvertent economic stabilizer.

Brazil Political Economy

The October 25 runoff between Flávio Bolsonaro and Lula is tightening as Bolsonaro consolidates center-right support, though questions about governing experience persist. The Chinese automotive surge in Brazil — 17% market share year-to-date, spiking to 25% in September — has become a flashpoint. Stellantis' South American president publicly warned of job risks, echoing the broader "China Shock 2.0" narrative taking hold across emerging markets. Brazil notably declined to sign the US-led joint statement on excess manufacturing capacity, reflecting its continued hedging between Washington and Beijing.

Cuba and US Hemispheric Policy

The Trump administration's Cuba strategy reflects a tension between maximum-pressure rhetoric and tactical pragmatism. While sanctions and the oil blockade have squeezed the government, US goods continue flowing to Cuba's nascent private sector — including solar panels that help offset chronic blackouts — creating inadvertent dependency on Washington. The administration has added banking restrictions on Cuban entrepreneurs as a political concession, but a military operation against Havana appears deprioritized given the ongoing Iran conflict and approaching midterms. Cuba's deputy foreign minister signaled no expectation of post-election easing. ---

Global

  • WTI crude rose to $90.37 (+2.4%) and Brent reached $104.40 (+4.2%) after reports the White House asked the Pentagon to plan Iran strike options executable before the November midterms.
  • The Strait of Hormuz recorded ten attacks in the first week of October, compared with 16 in all of September, yet crude shipments are approaching prewar levels as freight economics attract risk-tolerant tanker operators.
  • Global merchandise trade growth for 2026 is now forecast at 4.6%, upgraded from a 3.6% January projection, with AI-related goods demand the primary driver.

US-Iran Conflict and Energy Markets

The reported White House request for Pentagon strike planning against Iran — potentially before the midterm elections — represents a material escalation risk that has directly driven the latest oil move. Yet the energy market's operational response to seven months of conflict has been notable: supertankers are being drawn into the Gulf rather than repelled, with shipowners earning up to $40 million per round trip. Middle Eastern producers are slashing prices to compete for buyers willing to take on shipping risk. Saudi Arabia is formalizing long-term contracts for loadings outside Hormuz. The market has adapted, but the risk premium remains real — and a direct US strike would test that resilience acutely.

Global Monetary Policy and Bond Markets

The synchronized rise in global bond yields is exerting a measurable drag. Bloomberg Economics estimates a 0.2%–0.3% negative GDP impact for the US, UK, and eurozone by end-2027, with the drag building to 0.5% in the US by 2029. The cause of the yield rise matters: if driven by stronger demand, partial offsets exist; if energy-driven, the hit compounds. France's fiscal stress is the visible fault line in European sovereign debt. Oil shipping costs have surged, with direct implications for goods inflation globally and further complicating central bank path-of-least-resistance calculations heading into year-end.
