AI Vulnerabilities

Just months ago, enthusiasm over the artificial intelligence boom was such 
that concerns about how this was all going to get financed appeared limited. 
Even Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent observed that debt issuers seemed “almost
yield agnostic 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-26/artificial-intelligence-investment-now-competes-with-governments-for-capital?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
” because of their belief in future high returns on investment.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh said Wednesday he was a doubter all along. In his 
press conference after the central bank decided toforgo a rate hike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/fed-holds-fire-as-warsh-telegraphs-more-hikes-without-saying-it?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
, he alluded to a summer narrative about how the US central bank was “going to 
raise rates or talking about raising rates, but it’s not going to have any 
effect on AI because the returns that investors are expecting are so different.”

“I was skeptical of that view,” he said. “Interest rates get in the cracks. 
Interest rates affect directly or indirectly almost everything.”
Kevin WarshPhotographer: Al Drago/Bloomberg
And what’s happened since September — when the Fed raised rates for the first 
time in three years — Warsh asked. Market participants are now 
“discriminating,” he said. “You’ve seen credit spreads for certain assets widen 
out,” and for others, financial conditions have tightened, but not 
significantly.

He was referring to a selloff in the shares and debt this month of a number of 
AI cloud-computing providers, including CoreWeave. (Read more about thathere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/coreweave-rout-sends-warning-sign-to-rest-of-the-stock-market?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
 andhere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-17/coreweave-woes-risk-shutting-down-ai-junk-market-credit-weekly?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
.) Bessent, for his part,played down 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/bessent-plays-down-concerns-over-ai-financing-fed-rate-policy?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
 the concerns shortly after Warsh’s press briefing concluded.

After the Fed held off 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/fed-holds-rates-says-more-data-will-help-next-decision?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
 on raising rates, saying that more data will help the next decision, Diane 
Swonk, chief economist at KPMG, speculated whether the AI wobbles indeed might 
have been part of the calculation. “Is there something else the Fed is worried 
about,” she said on Bloomberg TV. “Are they worried about breaking something, 
with some of the fragilities we’ve seen in the AI boom, in the issuance of 
debt?”
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-16/fed-has-started-a-rate-hiking-cycle-swonk-says-video?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
Warsh, as in July, declined to spell out exactly why the Fed held off on 
Wednesday. That leaves it to others to say whether tightening too fast amid the 
AI market volatility was a concern. What is clear is how vital Warsh views this 
tech boom. Earlier this month, he reiterated that it’s “probably themost 
consequential change 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/warsh-boosts-market-expectations-of-fed-hold-in-october?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
” to the economy in his lifetime.

He also said, during the IMF Bangkok meetings, the AI path may be “bumpy.” 
Perhaps the Fed holding off at least until December might avoid too many bumps.

Need-to-Know Research

The US Treasury chief regularly states that not only is America dominant in AI 
today, but its grip will even tighten in coming years — predicting the country 
will have80% of global computing power 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/bessent-blasts-us-hyperscalers-for-failure-to-tout-ai-benefits?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
 by 2028. An alternate scenario has now emerged.

China is well placed to lead the world by 2030 in the large language models at 
the core of AI, aBloomberg Intelligence report 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/china-set-to-lead-in-ai-models-other-key-areas-by-2030-bi-says?cmpid=BBD102926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10706>
 showed this week. The study predicts China will be ahead in eight out of 17 
key industries by then, including LLMs. In the other categories crucial to the 
AI race — producing the hardware needed to run it, from chips to optical 
networking components — Chinese firms are mostly behind now but will be 
competitive by 2030, the report said.

“The US and China are on track for joint technology leadership in AI by 2030, 
with neither country likely to achieve overall supremacy,” BI analysts led by 
Francis Chan wrote. American models may maintain a narrow lead at the frontier, 
while “China’s expertise in software optimization leaves it well positioned in 
lower-cost AI implementation,” they wrote.
