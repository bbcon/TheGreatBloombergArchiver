Bessent vs the markets

Scott Bessent’s extraordinary campaign to bend markets to his will is kicking 
up a notch.

Late yesterday, he challenged traders to test his resolve 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-dares-traders-to-bet-against-yen-i-am-the-house-now?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
 on boosting Japan’s currency. And today, the US Treasury will unveil how much 
it will spendin a first round of expanded bond buybacks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-s-fever-quelling-debt-buybacks-put-wall-street-on-edge?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
, part of Bessent’s plan to keep the lid on US bond yields and cool“this fever 
that was building” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-says-buyback-move-was-aimed-at-quelling-market-fever?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
 in the market.

Bessent’s words yesterday:

On the yen: “I am the house now, so when we intervene with the Japanese yen, I 
have pretty good insight into what the Japanese, what the Bank of Japan is 
going to do, what Japanese policymakers are going to do,” he said. “And you can 
bet against me if you want.”

On the surge in bond yields: “My job is to try to push things back towards 
equilibrium,” the Treasury secretary said. “I don’t believe that I can change 
the equilibrium price. But nothing’s ever in equilibrium.”

History is littered with examples of policymakers challenging the market’s 
judgment on asset prices and losing. In his hedge fund career, Bessent himself 
played a key role in one such episode, providing research on the UK economy 
that underpinneda successful bet against the pound in 1992 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-08-11/treasury-secretary-bessent-on-tariffs-deficits-trump-s-economic-plan?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
 by George Soros and Stanley Druckenmiller, his bosses at the time.

Druckenmiller, in a Wall Street Journal opinion piece last month on Bessent’s 
bond market moves, renderedhis judgment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/druckenmiller-bessent-s-early-mentor-calls-bond-buys-a-mistake?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
: “Governments defending prices against fundamentals always lose.” —Phil

The bull case on stocks

To skeptics, the bull case for stocks looks shakier by the day, with US long 
bond yields near two-decade highs and oil prices back to $100 a barrel. Yet the 
bulls have a powerful argument: Earnings just keep getting better.

More analysts have raised rather than cut US estimates for a 21st straight 
week,the longest run of upgrades in five years 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/us-earnings-upgrades-are-on-their-longest-run-in-five-years?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
, a Citigroup index shows. In emerging markets, estimates are rising atthe 
fastest pace on record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/emerging-market-profit-estimates-climb-at-record-pace-on-ai-boom?cmpid=090926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8654>
. In Europe, they’ve ticked higher for 19 weeks in a row.

“Micro, not macro, drives stocks,” said Marija Veitmane at State Street Global 
Markets. “I continue to see strong earnings upgrades boosting investors’ 
appetite for stocks, and I don’t think this is euphoria and ‘head in the sand’ 
behavior.” —Phil Serafino
