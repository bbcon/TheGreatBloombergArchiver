• The Bank of England raised interest rates unanimously for the first time in more than three years, with consumer price inflation running close to 5%.
• The Stoxx Europe 600 and FTSE 100 were on pace to close at all-time highs, carried by cyclical rotation following the US election outcome.
• France faces a nationwide strike and ongoing budget deadlock, extending more than a month of protests over the cost of living with elections six months away.

**Bank of England Policy**
The BoE's unanimous decision to raise rates marks a material shift after an extended pause, responding to CPI that appears to be stabilizing near 5% — well above the 2% target. The unanimity of the vote signals that the committee assessed the inflation picture as sufficiently entrenched to warrant action, even as global growth signals remain mixed. The move places the BoE alongside a broader tightening trend, though the pace and terminal rate remain uncertain given the UK's particular exposure to energy cost pass-through and wage dynamics.

**French Political and Fiscal Stress**
France's political situation is deteriorating ahead of elections. Major unions from public and private sectors have called a nationwide mobilization, with the Yellow Vest movement and student protesters joining organized labor in a broadening coalition of discontent. Parliamentary budget negotiations remain deadlocked, and President Macron's critics are actively seeking to exploit public frustration over the cost of living. The political calendar — six months to elections — reduces incentives for compromise and raises the probability that fiscal uncertainty persists into 2027.

**Markets**
European equities rallied in sympathy with the US post-election move, with cyclicals outperforming and the rotation away from semiconductor-linked names benefiting more diversified continental benchmarks. UBS confirmed approximately 200 redundancies in London as Credit Suisse integration nears completion, spanning wealth management and investment banking divisions. Meta raised €9 billion in a euro bond debut that drew record demand, suggesting credit appetite for high-quality issuers remains robust despite elevated rates.
