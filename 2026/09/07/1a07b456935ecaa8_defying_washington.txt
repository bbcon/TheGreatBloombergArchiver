As the US puts the squeeze on countries around the world, whether through 
imported inflation from the Iran war or tariff pressures, Malaysia is 
considering making a stand.

The Southeast Asian country is seriously evaluating 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/malaysia-eyes-huawei-chips-for-ai-project-despite-us-warning?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8610>
 using Huawei’s AI hardware as the backbone of a 2 billion ringgit ($494 
million) initiative intended to give the country greater control over national 
data, according to people familiar with the matter. If Malaysia does move 
forward with Huawei chips, it would mark the first known instance of a foreign 
government officially opting for Chinese AI accelerators over American ones.

Huawei has been working to improve 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-18/huawei-unveils-new-ai-chip-tech-to-rival-nvidia?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8610>
 the performance of its AI chips in the face of years of US restrictions on 
access to foreign production tools, and is finding traction in China. DeepSeek
plans to deploy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/deepseek-plans-big-huawei-ai-chip-order-to-power-new-data-center?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8610>
 at least 160,000 of Huawei’s top accelerators at a massive data center it’s 
building in Inner Mongolia. Huawei isalso vying 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/huawei-egypt-ai-ascend-chips-test-us-tech-diplomacy-nvidia-amd-microsoft?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8610>
 to build an AI data center for the Egyptian government, prompting Trump’s team 
to work on a counterbid.

US officials have explicitly warned the world last year that use of such chips 
could violate US export regulations. Malaysia may present a particular 
challenge: it has one of the world’s fastest-growing AI data center ecosystems 
due in part to projects from American firms, making it an important market for 
the likes of Nvidia, Microsoft and Oracle.

Trump seemingly persuaded South Korea 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/south-korea-paving-way-to-send-troops-to-hormuz-reports-say?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8610>
 to consider deploying a naval support ship and troops to the Strait of Hormuz, 
after the US leader abruptly ordered the Pentagon to scale back annual joint 
military drills with the ally last month. Whether he can apply such pressure to 
Malaysia remains to be seen.

Credit risk: Bathla Group, the insolvent Australian builder that’s shaken the

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/bathla-s-2-5-billion-property-debt-crisis-faces-fresh-tests?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8610>
 to stay afloat for a couple of weeks as it rushes to make headway repaying

The company’s representative, the administrator Teneo, agreed to the emergency

creditors. The financing is about A$4 million, a person familiar with the 
matter said, an amount that was discussed with several private lenders last 
week. Teneo declined to comment on the size of the deal.

 that the nation’s regulator had warned about in private lending, particularly
