• S&P 500 futures retreated 0.3%–0.6% while the Nasdaq 100 fell roughly 1%; MGM Resorts slumped 10% after Barry Diller abandoned acquisition plans.

**Economic Data**
Wednesday's S&P Global composite PMI delivered a clear upside surprise, printing at its best level since the post-pandemic reopening boom. The result validated last week's contentious Fed decision to raise overnight rates and reinforced the case for additional tightening. Half of public companies flagged supply constraints in recent earnings calls, per Bloomberg's Orange Book, with specialized labor shortages, elevated freight costs, and Middle East-driven fuel prices all cited as drags that would have been worse still absent these headwinds.

**Rates and Fiscal**
The bond selloff accelerated meaningfully. With growth running hot and oil persistently near $100, real yields have driven the entire move higher — inflation breakevens remain anchored — but the trajectory is placing direct pressure on government borrowing costs. The Institute of International Finance cautioned that Treasury Secretary Bessent's reliance on short-term bill issuance and debt buybacks constitutes "financial engineering" that risks creating only an "illusion of stability." The US fiscal deficit exceeded $40 trillion last month. Thirty-year mortgage rates returned above 7%.
