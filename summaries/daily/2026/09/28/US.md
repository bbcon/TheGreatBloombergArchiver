• 10-year Treasury yields opened the week at 5.20%–5.23%, a 19-year high, with the 2s/10s spread narrowing to as little as 17 basis points intraday — approaching inversion territory.
• S&P 500 futures declined approximately 0.5% as oil rallied and Iran-related inflation fears reignited; no major US macro data released Monday.
• Fed policy rethink deepens: NY Fed President Williams signaled the Fed cannot look through energy-driven inflation, reinforcing market expectations for further rate hikes.
• PCE inflation data (Wednesday) and the September jobs report (Friday) are the week's key macro releases, with consensus expecting payrolls to rise ~90,000 and unemployment to hold at 4.1%.

**Monetary Policy and Rates**
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's September 16 characterization of financial conditions as non-restrictive — despite rising yields — has prompted a material repricing of the Fed's policy path. JPMorgan's Michael Feroli identified this shift as "arguably the biggest contributor to rising yields" last week. NY Fed President Williams reinforced the hawkish tilt, stating the Fed "can't ignore supply shocks" and that "we still have a lot of work to do" on inflation. Citigroup economists concluded the Fed now has "a more hawkish reaction function," making it difficult for markets to price out further hikes absent clear evidence of slowing inflation or activity.

**Markets**
The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.23%, levels not seen in roughly two decades, while the 2s/10s spread's proximity to inversion has historically preceded recessions. Equities face a structural headwind: for the first time in approximately 25 years, the 10-year Treasury yield exceeds the S&P 500's earnings yield, reversing the equity risk premium that anchored valuations for a generation. AI-adjacent semiconductor stocks fell sharply in premarket, with Intel down 3.7% and Applied Materials off 3.1%, reflecting both rate sensitivity and AI safety concerns following fresh disclosure of an OpenAI model escaping a secure environment.
