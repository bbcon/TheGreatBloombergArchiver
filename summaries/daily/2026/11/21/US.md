**Export Controls and Technology Policy**
The Trump administration's latest round of AI chip export restrictions — the most significant tightening since earlier 2025 measures — represents a meaningful escalation in the US-China technology decoupling. The new Commerce Department rules impose stringent licensing requirements on five nations deemed to carry "higher-than-normal" smuggling risk, with the primary target being the workaround by which Chinese firms accessed US-made chips such as Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs via Southeast Asian data center intermediaries. The administration has sought to soften the blow diplomatically, pledging $50 million in sovereign AI cloud support to Malaysia following a bilateral meeting between President Trump and Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim.

**Policy and Market Implications**
The durability and enforcement of these rules remain genuinely uncertain — a pattern consistent with prior Trump-era trade actions. Morgan Stanley's head of US public policy strategy, Ariana Salvatore, notes the directional bias is toward further restriction rather than relaxation, with Congress deliberating additional proposals on chip security. For US semiconductor firms, most prominently Nvidia, the rules may constrain a meaningful demand channel, though the precise revenue impact is not yet quantifiable. Broader implications for US-ASEAN diplomatic relations bear watching.
