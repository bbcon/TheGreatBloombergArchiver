The case for a year-end rally

Anthropic’s IPO is giving a glimmer of hope to equity bulls that the painful 
tech stock selloff might be over. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/the-path-to-a-year-end-rally-looks-clearer-taking-stock?cmpid=111926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11584>

“The strong demand for the IPO is a welcome reminder that the AI theme is well 
and truly still alive,” said Jai Malhi at Vanda Research. “Now that we have 
seen the positioning de-rating in tech, this provides a compelling opportunity 
for dip buyers.”

The bull case is that tech stocks are cheaper than they’ve been in six years, 
and earnings continue to be robust. Add in that oil prices dropped 20% in 
November and Nvidia once again delivered on profits, and some traders say 
there’s an argument for a quick reversal in stock prices.

The selloff in the past few weeks has been nothing short of dramatic. More 
than 10% has been wiped off the Nasdaq 100 Index in the wake of Jensen Huang’s 
departure and the Democrat sweep of the US midterm elections.

“We do think the recent selloff has created a number of buying opportunities,” 
said Jefferies strategist Mohit Kumar. “However, 2027 is going to be about 
differentiation even within the tech world and the easy money from buying 
anything is over.” —Michael Msika 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AP7hp2DanV8/michael-msika?cmpid=111926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11584>

Political risk hits the euro

The euro’s tumble to an 18-month low against the dollar shows few signs of 
easing, as expectations mount that next year’s election in a politically 
divided and fiscally stressed France willweigh further on the currency 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/french-risks-traders-betting-euro-slide-currency?cmpid=111926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11584>
.

Morgan Stanley and Commerzbank predict the euro will slip to $1.08 next year, 
and speculative bets on a weaker currency are at the highest in more than six 
years.Political unrest in France, and concerns about its ability to pass a 
budget, pushed the euro below $1.10 this week, bringing declines since late 
August to 6%.

Traders have shunned the euro due to worries that the two frontrunners in the 
election —far-right leader Marine Le Pen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/france-s-le-pen-proposes-individual-investments-in-pensions?cmpid=111926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11584>
 andhard-left Jean-Luc Mélenchon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-18/french-election-jean-luc-melenchon-is-wild-card-for-voters?cmpid=111926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11584>
 — would worsen France’s fiscal outlook. The country’s budget woes are also 
raising questions about how far the ECB can raise rates.

“Political risks are shifting back against the euro in 2027,” said Thu Lan 
Nguyen of Commerzbank. —Naomi Tajitsu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVtlmblMDyc/naomi-tajitsu?cmpid=111926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11584>
