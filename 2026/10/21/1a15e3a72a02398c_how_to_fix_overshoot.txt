Limiting overshoot needs major emissions curbs

By John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>

The world can no longer keep global warming below 1.5C and must now focus on 
minimizing its overshoot, the United Nations said ahead of next month’s COP31 
climate talks in Turkey. The US retreat from the fight is making it even harder.
The Mer de Glace glacier in the Mont Blanc Massif, French Alps, on Sept. 23.
Photographer: Jeff Pachoud/AFP/Getty Images
Countries will have to cut CO2 emissions by nearly a fifth by the end of the 
decade compared to 2025 levels to achieve the “lowest possible” overshoot, the 
UN Environment Programme said in its annual emissions gap report, published on 
Wednesday.

The UNEP defined that scenario as limiting temperatures to 1.7C to 1.8C above 
pre-industrial levels and returning global mean warming to 1.5C by the end of 
the century. It described the necessary emissions reductions as “immediate, 
deep, rapid and sustained.”

The report sets out potential paths forward after the UN first reported last 
month that exceeding the 1.5C threshold envisaged by the Paris Agreement in 
2015 is nowvirtually guaranteed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/un-says-global-warming-will-cross-1-5c-soon-hit-at-least-1-8c?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
.

Each fraction of a degree of warming above pre-industrial levels raises the 
risk of extreme weather events, harm to global food production and rapid 
inflation.

The US exit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-27/us-exits-paris-agreement-what-trump-s-withdrawal-means-for-climate-change?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
 from the Paris Agreement has been particularly damaging to global climate 
efforts. While the UNEP report found that collective G20 emissions growth 
slowed last year, America’s emissions increase of 133 megatons of CO2 “far 
exceeded” that of any other country. COP31 host Turkey was second, with an 
increase of 24.4 megatons.

A US climate target announced 
<https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-the-united-nations-climate-change-conference-glasgow-united-kingdom>
 in the final weeks of the Biden administration, which was projected to cut 
emissions by one gigaton by 2030, has now been omitted from the UNEP’s 
calculations. One gigaton is equivalent to a year’s output from the country’s 
entire vehicle fleet.

While global greenhouse gas emissions last year were similar to 2024 levels, 
“it is too soon to conclude that global emissions are plateauing or peaking,” 
the report said. Those resulting from the use of fossil fuels climbed, but were 
offset by a decrease in emissions from land use and forestry.

Global leaders and climate negotiators are set to meet for COP31 in the 
Turkish city of Antalya in early November to discuss how to combat warming and 
adapt to its impacts.

UK approves North Sea gas field

By Jess Shankleman 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASoVYP9ojQ0/jess-shankleman?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
 andChloe Chaplain 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYT_iQhuPgc/chloe-chaplain?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>

The UK government approved the Jackdaw gas field, a contentious decision that 
risks alienating some of the left-leaning voters Prime Minister Andy Burnham 
has attracted back to Labour.
The Jackdaw gas field.Source: Adura
Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh gave the green light on Wednesday, reviving 
a fossil-fuel project that was previously quashed by a Scottish court over its 
climate impact. She said the field may start supplying gas by the end of this 
month.

The decision ends years of wrangling over a project that’s become a 
battleground in the push to net zero. Approving Jackdaw will anger some on the 
soft left of the Labour Party, who helped Burnham to become Prime Minister in 
July. But others will welcome it. US President Donald Trump has put sustained 
pressure on Britain’s Labour government to allow more drilling, while unions 
have pressed for the project on jobs grounds and right-wing parties have said 
it’s necessary for energy security and tax revenues.

By Coco Liu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AU-LNn94_HY/coco-liu?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
 andAlastair Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQvVwWuhxcQ/alastair-marsh?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>

After successfully disrupting Oracle Corp. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/ORCL:US?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
’s data center plans in New Mexico, environmental activists say they’ve now got 
a blueprint for waging similar campaigns against hyperscalers across the US.
Construction work at Oracle Corp.’s Project Jupiter data center in Doña Ana 
County, New Mexico, in August.Source: Oracle Corp.
Mariel Nanasi, executive director of New Energy Economy (NEE), says Oracle’s 
handling of water and air permits opened the door for environmental nonprofits 
to challenge the company’s plans in court, and created a template for other 
activists to replicate.

The battle is far from over. On Oct. 15, 12 Republican state attorneys general 
asked 
<https://ago.wv.gov/article/attorney-general-mccuskey-leads-coalition-backing-pipeline-approval-keep-gas-moving-and>
 federal regulators to allow construction of a gas pipeline that Oracle needs 
to power its data centers. And Oracle has sought toadjust 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/terminal/TMZDZJBNG4XS?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
 its approach to address environmental concerns. But Nanasi says the activists 
she represents will keep fighting until Oracle is forced “to walk.”

The company’s setbacks have reverberated through markets. Last month it 
emerged that Oracle hadcited force majeure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/oracle-cites-force-majeure-to-shield-itself-on-controversial-data-center?cmpid=BBD102126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10358>
 as it sought to deal with the fallout of permitting hurdles. News that it was 
trying to shield itself from soaring expenses tied to potential delays then 
triggered a broad-based slump in the shares of hyperscalers including Oracle, 
as well as their vendors.

A spokesperson for Oracle said the company remains “committed to developing 
Project Jupiter responsibly and protecting New Mexico’s air and water.” The 
company respects the public’s “right to raise concerns and we have responded 
with meaningful improvements to the project’s design.”

Ben Green <https://fordschool.umich.edu/faculty/ben-green>, assistant 
professor of Information and Public Policy at the University of Michigan, says 
the activists fighting data centers “have achieved far more than I ever 
expected them to.” As a result, it’s now getting “harder and harder for local 
officials and politicians to just ignore the voices of dissenters.”
