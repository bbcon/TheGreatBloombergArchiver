After a torrid week for global tech stocks, Tuesday brought a modicum of 
relief. A return of positive sentiment for technology companies — and afall in 
oil prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/brent-crude-falls-below-90-a-barrel-for-first-time-since-august?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11216>
 — helped fuel thebiggest two-day gain 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11216>
 in global stocks in more than three months. South Korea’s chip-heavy Kospi 
Index jumped more than 4% to be the top performer, followed by key gauges in 
Japan and Taiwan.

Underpinning Tuesday’s ebullient mood was chip kingpin TSMC, which saw growth 
in its monthly salesaccelerate to 60% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/tsmc-taiwan-semiconductor-manufacturing-revenue-earnings-ai-chip-demand?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11216>
. Its shares finished at a one-week high.

But the durability of the market rebound remains in question, with regulatory 
risks now layered on top of concerns that were already weighing on the sector — 
stretched valuations, soaring capital spending and uncertainty over the payoff 
from massive AI investments.

SoftBank Group underscored those concerns when it reported its first net loss 
in almost two years 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/softbank-group-earnings-loss-after-ai-worries-hit-tech-stocks-masayoshi-son?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11216>
 after swallowing a $3.7 billion writedown related to non-refundable advance 
payments made to secure equipment and manufacturing capacity for its Ohio 
data-center campus.

Japan’s Rapidus Is Said Likely to Miss Mass Production Deadline

Japanese government-backed startup Rapidus has fallen behind schedule on its 
high-stakes pledge to mass produce cutting-edge chips by March 2028 and may no 
longer be able to make that deadline. The startup, which has received billions 
of dollars’ worth of public funds, has yet to successfully produce a key type 
of advanced memory that’s an essential component of all processors, people 
familiar with the matter said.

China’s Global Auto Export Dominance Comes at a $270 Billion Cost

China’s automakers are exporting more cars than ever — and destroying billions 
of dollars in their own market value along the way. Even as exports hurtle 
toward 10 million vehicles this year, the country’s automakers have lost around 
$270 billion from their peak late last year. The divergence points to a problem 
at the heart of China’s manufacturing boom: conquering overseas markets may not 
be enough to offset punishing competition at home.
