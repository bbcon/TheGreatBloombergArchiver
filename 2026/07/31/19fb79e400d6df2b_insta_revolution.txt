In India, it turns out the revolution was digitized.

One of the most notable aspects of the “Cockroach Janta Party” protests that 
swept the country was the young demonstrators’total command 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/inside-modi-s-decision-to-cave-to-india-s-cockroach-protesters?cmpid=BBD073126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7216>
 of the power of social media.

Wielding Instagram reels like Molotov cocktails, the Gen-Z protesters hurled 
blow after blow at the government of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, in the end 
forcing a rare retreat that included the sacking of a sitting minister.

They in effect beat Modi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-07-24/cjp-student-protest-tests-modi-s-rising-india-vision-as-youth-seek-jobs?cmpid=BBD073126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7216>
 at his own game.

India may be poor, but it has the Internet connectivity — and the mobile-phone 
penetration — of a much richer country. Modi’s Bharatiya Janata Party grasped 
that years ago, and used its early adroitness with popular platforms like X 
(then Twitter) and WhatsApp to cultivate support all the way down to the 
village level.

In so doing, it won election after election.

These last few weeks, Modi’s social-media machine started to look a bit old 
and creaky.

Instagram — not X or WhatsApp — powered the activists 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/india-s-instagram-loving-cockroaches-outwit-modi-online?cmpid=BBD073126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7216>
 who used the photo-swapping platform to share viral videos, organize logistics 
and pass around reels of baton-wielding police charges.

There were memes, there was dancing. Instagram helped imbue the movement with 
a joyful vibe.

All the while, the campaign faced scant government interference online save 
for sporadic Internet shutdowns.

The BJP’s digital counter-programming was confined mainly to its more familiar 
turf of X, practically oblivious to the upheaval blossoming just an app away.

Already, Modi and his party are wising up to their digital failings.

Shortly before sacking Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, Modi posted a 
selfie-style video to Instagram that quickly racked up hundreds of millions of 
views. Bloomberg News has reported that BJP officials are pushing leaders to 
post more reels on Instagram and be more visible in touting Modi initiatives.

The “Cockroach” party clearly had the initiative in the digital realm. Now 
comes the test of whether it can keep it.— Dan Strumpf 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWkviDlXMgI/dan-strumpf?sref=LlL4jr17&cmpid=BBD073126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7216>
