It’s not how things were meant to go.

Had they already won 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/egypt-sees-us-iran-crisis-bridgeable-as-it-pursues-mediation?cmpid=BBD072826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7061>
 the war, the two leaders planned to convene in Jerusalem in April for Israel’s 
Independence Day. They hoped to unfurl Middle East peace treaties flowing from 
the fall of the Iranian regime. Trump would have received the Israel Prize, and 
Netanyahu would have been all but assured of reelection later this year.

Instead, they’re meeting in a Washington clouded by the funeral of Senator 
Lindsey Graham, the dean of pro-Israel hawks on Capitol Hill.

Other regional powers sense his need for deals.

Saudi Arabia may get US-engineered atomic energy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/trump-signs-nuclear-sharing-deal-with-saudi-arabia-in-policy-shift?cmpid=BBD072826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7061>

Trump indicated he’ll let Turkey have advanced US F-35 warplanes over 
Netanyahu’s objections.

Syria is gaining US sanctions relief, and Lebanese calls for a repositioning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/israel-withdraws-troops-from-parts-of-lebanon-in-test-of-truce?cmpid=BBD072826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7061>

 in Israeli polls, is twofold: to remind Trump of their common interest in 
seeing the war through, and keep Israel nimble atop the shifting sands of the 
president’s Middle East ventures.

adversaries had never been stronger and the Pentagon wasn’t keeping up. Before 
he entered government, the billionaire was co-CEO of Cerberus Capital 
Management, a private equity firm overseeing $70 billion of assets. In 
Feinberg’s rise to the Pentagon’s No. 2 job, former Cerberus executives have
