.

Long regarded as a country defined by Buddhist traditions, social harmony and 
low levels of everyday violence, Thailand also has one of Southeast Asia’s 
highest rates of civilian gun ownership.

Despite relatively strict laws on paper, weak enforcement and regulatory 
loopholes have helped make firearms widely available.

Decades of military influence and repeated coups have further helped entrench 
a gun culture in parts of Thai society.

Thailand has experienced a string of mass shootings in recent years, including 
a 2020 attack by a soldier in a mall that killed about 30 people and a 2022 
rampage in a daycare center by a former police officer in which some 37 people 
including two dozen children died.

A Bloomberg investigation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2023-us-made-gun-exports-shootings-violence-sig-sauer/?cmpid=BBD081226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7639>
 the following year found that Thailand’s so-called welfare gun program, which 
allows police and local officials to buy discounted firearms, helped bring 
hundreds of thousands of weapons into the country. Many were illegally resold, 
fueling corruption and violent crime.

Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul ordered the suspension 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/thailand-halts-gun-program-tightens-laws-after-school-shooting?cmpid=BBD081226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7639>
 of the controversial program at a cabinet sitting yesterday, while halting the 
issuance of new firearm permits and further tightening gun-control laws.

The government pledged draft legislation within 60 days to overhaul Thailand’s 
firearms law, increase penalties for gun-related offenses and require owners to 
renew permits every three years. It designated school safety a national 
priority.

It all comes too late for one local official, shot dead by a former lawmaker 
on Monday in Nonthaburi, a province bordering Bangkok where the school shooting 
happened. —Randy Thanthong-Knight

And Finally

In the opening salvo of the US-Israel war on Iran, Supreme Leader Mojtaba 
Khamenei was hurt in the same Israeli airstrike that killed his father and 
predecessor, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. The extent of his injuries are unknown, 
but Khamenei’s prolonged absence from public life has left a vacuum in which 
disputes between Iran’s political factions have becomemore pronounced and 
seeped into the open 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/iranian-factions-squabble-over-war-aims-as-khamenei-looks-to-assert-control?cmpid=BBD081226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7639>
. Whereas his father often played the role of referee as the ultimate decision 
maker on all matters of state, it’s not clear that Khamenei commands that 
authority yet.
