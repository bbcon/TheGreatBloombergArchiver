Welcome to Next China, your weekly dispatch detailing where the nation stands 
and where it’s headed. In this edition, Allen Wan unpacks the purge of a rising 
political star. We also look at the latest test of the China-US trade truce, 
and the buzz around a poetry-reciting Pakistani astronaut.

Hello, this is Allen Wan in Shanghai.

China was stunned this week when one of its rising political stars got caught 
up in President Xi Jinping’s anti-corruption purge.

Chen Jining, Communist Party chief of Shanghai and a Politburo member, is 
being investigated “for suspected severe violations of discipline and laws 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/china-probes-politburo-member-and-shanghai-chief-chen-jining?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
,” effectively removing him from office. State media didn’t say what Chen had 
allegedly done wrong.

Xi’s anti-graft crusade has rumbled on for more than a decade, taking down top 
military leaders and party officials alike. Still,Chen’s downfall was surprising
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/xi-unleashes-china-earthquake-with-shock-ouster-of-rising-star?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 given his sparkling resume and position as a business-friendly figure who ran 
Shanghai, which has a GDP bigger than nations like Belgium and Sweden.

Chen was a relentless advocate for Shanghai. When Elon Musk visited a few 
years ago to check in at Tesla’s Gigafactory, Chen greeted him todiscuss 
increasing investment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-06-01/musk-asked-to-boost-investment-in-shanghai-by-top-party-official?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.

Chen’s Rolodex of visitors includes many of the world’s rich and powerful, 
such as JPMorgan Chase’s Jamie Dimon, Apple’s Tim Cook and former US Secretary 
of State Antony Blinken.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/xi-unleashes-china-earthquake-with-shock-ouster-of-rising-star?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
Chen Jining with Chuck Schumer during a meeting in Shanghai in 2023.
Photographer: Allen Wan/Bloomberg
I’ve met Chen a few times. During a particularly stormy time in US-China 
relations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/china-dismisses-us-tariff-prospect-as-political-maneuvering?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 in 2023, I was invited to attend a small meeting with a visiting delegation of 
American senators led by then-Majority Leader Chuck Schumer.

Besides telling Chen that the US sought competition and not conflict, Schumer 
raised the issue of fair competition for American companies in the country. 
Chen came across as friendly and hospitable, easing any possible tensions.

My most recent encounter with Shanghai’s party chief came during this year’s 
World AI Conference 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/xi-vows-to-make-ai-for-all-in-debut-at-china-s-top-tech-summit?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
. Chen used the annual event to champion an ambitious effort to turn Shanghai 
into a hub for the tech Xi sees as critical to China’s economic future. Think 
AI, chips and biomedicine.

Chen had an impressive track record that suggested he would rise even further 
inthe ruling Communist Party 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-china-policy-guide/?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.

He once served as president of Tsinghua University — the nation’s most 
prestigious institution of higher education. He also led the environmental 
ministry, and was formerly Beijing’s mayor.

The Shanghai party boss role has often served as a springboard to high office. 
Xi himself led the city in 2007 after the downfall of Chen Liangyu in a 
corruption scandal. Just months later, Xi won a promotion to the party’s elite 
seven-man Standing Committee, putting him on course to become the country’s 
leader.

We may never know what triggered Chen’s demise, such is the opacity of the 
Chinese government’s inner workings. Xi is preparing for a congress in the 
second half of next year that is likely to hand him another five years in 
office — and the political maneuvering seems to be getting tumultuous.

Last month, a key figure in the military’s fight against graft, Zhang Shengmin,
won promotion to the Politburo 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/china-names-vice-military-chair-as-new-politburo-member?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/china-probes-politburo-member-and-shanghai-chief-chen-jining?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
It is possible Chen Jining overreached. He recently told visitors he expected 
to join the party’s national leadership, according to a Bloomberg Economics 
note citing sources.

“Such self-promoting statements could have fed Xi’s suspicions about his 
ambitions, particularly if he interpreted Chen’s corporate relationships and 
growing popularity as an emerging independent power base,” wrote Neil Thomas, 
senior geoeconomics analyst for China.

Chen also represented a group of technocrats who were promoted under Xi’s 
predecessors but are now falling out of favor.

One investment banker in Shanghai said the financial sector saw the rise of 
such engineers, scientists and economists as a sign that the nation’s economic 
governance was turning more professional. Chen’s downfall dealt a heavy blow to 
that view, added the person, who asked for anonymity to speak privately.

Corruption does remain a problem in China. Top graft busters have warned that 
dirty officials shouldn’t expect a “safe landing” regardless of their rank, 
contributions or whether they’ve retired.

According to Transparency International’s 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index, 
China ranked 76th out of some 180 countries and territories, an improvement of 
just four places from 2012, when Xi took the party reins.

The takeaway for foreign businesses from Chen’s downfall is that they should 
avoid building strategies around political personalities, said George Chen, a 
partner at The Asia Group, an advisory firm based in Washington.

“Officials can rise unexpectedly and disappear just as quickly” but the system 
remains in place, he wrote. “Understanding a particular official can be useful. 
Understanding how China’s political and regulatory system works is 
indispensable.”

Keeping It Interesting

It’s safe to say that there’s never a dull moment in China-US ties.

No sooner had the friendly scenes from Xi hosting President Donald Trump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-20/where-china-the-us-and-everyone-else-stand-after-historic-apec?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 in the southern city of Shenzhen last week faded than observers of the world’s 
most important bilateral relationship had to recalibrate their thinking all 
over again.

For one thing, there was the matter of Jensen Huang returning as Nvidia’s CEO 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/trump-jensen-huang-ceo-second-term-at-nvidia?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
, striking a somber tone as heapologized to employees 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/nvidia-s-huang-to-host-all-hands-meeting-a-day-after-his-return?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.

Huang has been one of the few people outside Trump himself who seemed able to 
bridge the widening gulf between Beijing and Washington. The AI champion was 
also a proponent of giving China access to high-end Nvidia chips, endearing him 
to tech policymakers in Beijing.

Of course, China had dismissed allegations that a woman Huang had an affair 
with was one of its spies, saying the charges were nothing more than “smears.”
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/huang-s-return-followed-unprecedented-intervention-by-trump?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
Jensen Huang, Nvidia’s CEO.Photographer: SeongJoon Cho/Bloomberg
The development with Huang on its own might have left China-US ties looking 
even better this week than last, butthere was more to come 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/china-halts-critical-minerals-ban-to-maintain-truce-with-us?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.

Huawei, the nation’s largest technology company, was found guilty in a court 
in New York of multiple criminal charges 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/huawei-found-guilty-of-us-criminal-charges-in-landmark-case?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
, including money laundering, a conspiracy to steal trade secrets and 
defrauding banks about its business in Iran and North Korea.

While that ruling on its own probably wasn’t going to affect the trade truce 
set to expire in January, Trump once again threatened towallop China with 
tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/us-targets-china-eu-with-new-tariffs-after-overcapacity-probe?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 after months of US inquiries intomanufacturing capacity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/trump-riles-trade-partners-with-tariffs-that-undercut-china-1?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.

The new 20% levy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/trump-tariffs-tracker/?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 is within the bounds of the trade truce, but the threat of a 40% rate that’d 
take effect if negotiations break down would very likely be far more than 
Beijing would tolerate.

Any miscalculation in either side’s approach could quickly complicate talks, 
particularly with a more hawkish US Congress pressing for tougher export 
controls on China.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/us-partners-respond-to-tariffs-with-anger-protest-and-diplomacy?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
And Beijing signaled it’d stand its ground in another trade fight, saying in 
response to the tariff announcement that it “reserves the right totake 
necessary measures to resolutely defend its legitimate rights and interests 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/trump-s-china-tariffs-raise-threat-of-new-test-for-trade-truce?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.”

Beijing, of course, has significant tools for hitting back at Trump for any 
new tariffs or tech controls. This week it extended apause on export curbs for 
some critical minerals 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/china-halts-critical-minerals-ban-to-maintain-truce-with-us?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 used to make advanced products like semiconductors and military hardware. (It 
also put inan order for 50 Boeing planes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/boeing-wins-7-4-billion-order-first-major-china-sale-in-years?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
, the biggest such purchase in years.)

But the restrictions and other tools — like export controls on rare earths — 
could easily be put into force in January should talks on extending the trade 
truce falter.

This week was a reminder, fresh on the heels of the Shenzhen summit, that 
China-US ties remain fragile and will be tested often. It also showed that the 
two sides have a lot of heavy lifting to do to keep their trade ceasefire 
intact when they meet next month at the G20 in Miami.

Behind the Great Firewall

A weekly look at the big water cooler news in China.

The Shenzhou-24 spacecraft docked with China’s space station this week but 
much of the buzz on social media was focused less on the mission and more on 
the food preferences and language skills of one of its astronauts.

That crewman was Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a Pakistani air force pilot who became 
thefirst non-Chinese astronaut to visit the Tiangong station 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/china-space-program-to-fly-pakistan-pilot-as-its-first-foreigner?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
. Serving as a payload specialist alongside commander Tang Hongbo and flight 
engineer Wu Yi, he became the first Pakistani to orbit Earth.

His place on the mission underscores China’s close ties with Pakistan, seen 
also in thefinance and business sphere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/china-backed-pakistan-exchange-prepares-to-launch-stock-options?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
. The fact both those nations have a rivalry with India was not lost on Chinese 
internet users.

“Indian netizens claim that Pakistan’s access to the Tiangong space station is 
a handout from China and it is merely hitching a ride rather than a reflection 
of its own capabilities,” said one Weibo user with 2 million followers. “Indian 
netizens are envious.”

For what it’s worth, Pakistan’s modest space program dates back to the 1960s, 
and has mostly focused on satellite development, communications and remote 
sensing.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/china-space-program-to-fly-pakistan-pilot-as-its-first-foreigner?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
Muhammad Zeeshan Ali at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center on Wednesday.
Photographer: Andy Wong/AP Photo via AP Photo/Andy Wong
The mission was another step forward for Beijing’s ambitious space program 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/china-us-space-programs-compete-to-get-to-moon-then-mars?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
. Over the past several years it has landed a crafton the far side of the moon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-25/china-s-spacecraft-completes-historic-mission-to-moon-s-far-side?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 andput a rover on Mars 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-04-25/china-s-mars-rover-fails-to-wake-up-after-choking-on-red-planet-s-dust?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
.

China plans to send astronauts to the moon by 2030 and is competing with the US
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-moon-race-explained/?cmpid=BBD112726_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11858>
 to build data centers in space.

The nation completed the Tiangong (meaning Heavenly Palace) in 2022 after a 
decade of planning in the aftermath of being shut out of the US-led 
International Space Station. Some 29 different astronauts have now visited it.

Despite Ali studying Mandarin intensely for just months, Weibo users were 
impressed with his language skills and love of Chinese culture, which includes 
calligraphy and food like Kung Pao chicken.

Videos of the astronaut singing a Chinese friendship song and reciting Tang 
dynasty-era poems went viral.

“Ali honored his homeland with the tenacity he displayed over the past several 
months in mastering Chinese,” said a Weibo user with almost 6 million followers.
