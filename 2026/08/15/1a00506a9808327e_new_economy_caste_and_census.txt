India’s mammoth census 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-01/india-begins-first-census-in-15-years-will-count-1-4-billion-people?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
, which kicked off in April, is more than just the world’s largest population 
count. The latest survey — the first in a decade and a half — also has the 
potential to redraw some of the political bargains that have shaped the nation 
for decades.

Census 2027 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/india-census-can-new-population-and-caste-count-help-fix-inequality?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
 will be the first since India’s independence to count by caste across the 
entire population, with the last such survey dating back to 1931. It could 
provide the clearest picture yet of how deeply one of India’s oldest social 
hierarchies still shapes economic opportunity and political power.

Caste sits at the heart of India’s affirmative-action system and remains a 
powerful force in politics. While Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s government had 
resisted opposition demands for a count, advocates argued that collecting caste 
data alongside demographic and socioeconomic data is essential to target 
welfare and addresspersistent inequalities 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-05-08/a-caste-census-will-reveal-india-s-worst-kept-secret?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
.

Modi’s Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) had been wary of emphasizing caste 
divisions, which could undercut the broad Hindu coalition it has worked to 
build. His decision to yield on this point likely reflects the BJP’s efforts to 
strengthen its appeal among disadvantaged communities after losing its 
parliamentary majority in 2024.

Recent student protests 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/cockroach-janta-party-what-is-it-how-it-ousted-india-s-education-minister?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
 have showcased growing anxiety among young Indians over intense competition 
for quality education and jobs. The census may surface tensions over who should 
benefit from caste-based affirmative action and how heavily eligibility should 
continue to depend on caste.

At its founding — 79 years ago today — India faced the question of how to 
address deep-rooted social inequality. Framers of theconstitution 
<https://www.legislative.gov.in/constitution-of-india> embedded safeguards for 
historically disadvantaged “Scheduled Castes” and “Scheduled Tribes” through 
reservations in public education, jobs and political representation.

In the 1990s, the national government extended reservations to “Other Backward 
Classes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/india-census-can-new-population-and-caste-count-help-fix-inequality?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
,” or OBCs. At the federal level, quotas now broadly comprise 27% for OBCs, 
22.5% for “Scheduled Castes and Tribes” and 10% for “Economically Weaker 
Sections,” which was added more recently.

Yet India has no authoritative national count of the OBC population. A 2011 
socioeconomic survey that collected caste information through an open-ended 
question produced over 4 million caste and sub-caste descriptions that proved 
difficult to classify. The full caste data wasnever released 
<https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/ht-explainer-why-2011-caste-data-was-not-made-public-101724995302387.html>
.
Parliament House in New Delhi. India’s rapid growth has sharpened questions on

Part of the problem is that the caste system is extraordinarily granular. 
Broad administrative categories encompass hundreds of communities and 
sub-groups with widely varying levels of income, education and political 
influence.

The available evidence nevertheless suggests that caste identity continues to 
shape social and economic mobility.Affirmative action <> has expanded access to 
education and public jobs, but large disparities remain in wealth, occupations 
and upward mobility, both between broad caste categories and within them.

As India marks its Independence Day, it can point to remarkable achievements. 
The nation has held together with an enormous, diverse population, lifted 
hundreds of millions out of poverty and is making an increasingly credible 
claim to global power. But itsrapid growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-27/india-s-growth-may-outpace-current-estimates-after-data-overhaul?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
 — while raising aspirations — has also sharpened questions on how the gains 
are distributed.

A clearer outlook on caste and societal disadvantage could have significant 
political and policy consequences. It may reveal how benefits have percolated 
within broad categories, and if relatively dominant subgroups have captured a 
disproportionate share. Newly visible groups may demand expansion or 
redistribution of entitlements — potentially catalyzing new political 
coalitions.

Much will depend on the execution of the census. Opposition parties already 
are warning that inconsistent enumeration or an unwieldy classification system 
could produce numbers too noisy to be useful, reducing the effort to a symbolic 
exercise.

The census will be consequential for other reasons as well. The count will 
inform the potentially transformative exercise of redrawing parliamentary 
constituencies and allocation of seats among states, which have been frozen for 
decades.

While India’s northern states have continued to grow and account for a larger 
share of the population, wealthier southern states have brought fertility down 
to around or below replacement rates. That’s fueled fears that a redistribution 
based solely on population could weaken the South’s relative political 
influence — effectively penalizing states that succeeded in implementing 
population-control programs.

Finding a new federal bargain that reconciles political representation with 
the different demographic and economic trajectories of Indian states poses a 
major test for Modi. His attempt to expand the size of the lower house of 
parliament by roughly 50% and reserve athird of the seats for women 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-04-21/india-s-decades-long-political-truce-is-over?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260815&utm_content=7565>
 failed to pass earlier this year.

Having dramatically transformed itself over eight decades, India wants to make 
the leap from lower-middle income tohigh income status 
<https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/who-moves-up-and-why--a-closer-look-at-the-new-world-bank-group->
 during the next two. The census will likely trigger debates and negotiations 
across regions and social groups that may reshape the political architecture 
for this next phase. The test for India’s institutions is whether they can 
absorb the political claims and coalitions that emerge, and do so without 
losing momentum on the policy reforms and execution needed to keep the economy 
growing.
