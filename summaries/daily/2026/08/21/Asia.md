• China's July activity data landed well short of expectations — industrial production, retail sales and fixed-asset investment all "fared far worse than expected," with economists at BNP Paribas, Macquarie and Pantheon saying growth is now running below the lower end of Beijing's 4.5%-5% annual target.

• Samsung plans to return up to $79 billion to shareholders and SK Hynix announced a $28.6 billion buyback, funded by record AI-driven chip profits, even as both continue heavy capex commitments that leave little cushion if AI spending slows.
• Unitree Robotics' Shanghai IPO raised 6.1 billion yuan and shares surged 460% on debut, though the offering drew skepticism over valuation given humanoid robotics remains commercially unproven.
• North Korea launched roughly 10 short-range ballistic missiles that flew about 300 kilometers, prompting a coordinated response call among the US, Japan and South Korea.

**China's Slowdown and Policy Response**
Beijing's response to the weak July data has been measured rather than aggressive: officials are prioritizing "full play" of existing policy tools over major new stimulus, a stance underpinned by resilient AI-linked exports (electronics output rose more than 19%) and political caution ahead of an October Communist Party meeting. Bloomberg economists Chang Shu and David Qu expect a near-term lull in fresh policy signals until late September, when the adequacy of current fiscal deployment against the annual growth target should become clearer.

**Korean Tech and Capital Markets**
Samsung and SK Hynix face intensifying pressure to share AI-driven windfall profits with shareholders even as capital expenditure rises sharply — SK Hynix's spending is up 50% to at least $31 billion this year — a dynamic that could leave both companies exposed should AI-related chip demand eventually cool. Separately, Nvidia is in early talks with Korean AI chip designer Rebellions over a potential partnership, investment or acquisition.
