Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporters Rajesh Kumar Singh and Kanupriya Kapoor explore how India is turning 
kitchen waste into biofuels to help reduce its dependence on costly crude.

India is funneling more of its leftover frying oil 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/india-s-biofuel-mandate-spurs-rush-for-waste-cooking-oil-to-fuel-trucks-to-jets?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
 into fuel tanks, driven by an urgent need to cut its massive crude import bill.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s government is promoting energy made from urban, 
industrial and agricultural waste to help meet growing demand andcut reliance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/how-controversial-biofuels-are-booming-on-energy-security-push-in-india-brazil?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
 on overseas supplies.

As part of this push, international flights leaving India are required to use 
<https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2255021&reg=3&lang=1> a 1% 
blend of sustainable aviation fuel starting in January. That will rise to 5% by 
2030 alongside a similar biodiesel target forroad transport 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-05/modi-s-biofuel-push-comes-under-pressure-as-motorists-protest?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
.

The cheapest feedstock for this fuel is used cooking oil, often carrying the 
scent of yesterday’s fried chicken and fish fritters. Yet only a fraction of 
what is produced is collected.

That’s sparked a rush by companies like M11 Energy Transition Pvt. to ramp up 
volumes across a fragmented supply chain. In the megacity of Bengaluru, auto 
rickshaws plyclogged roads 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-09-27/india-s-tech-boom-has-fueled-bangalore-s-growth?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
 to gather gallons of used cooking oil from restaurant kitchens.

It’s poured through metal strainers and recorded in an app. Later in the day, 
it’s collected at the local M11 depot, which receives about 4 tons a day.
Collectors filter cooking oil before loading tubs onto auto rickshaws in the 
Indian city of Bengaluru.Photographer: Samyukta Lakshmi/Bloomberg
While these efforts may be small-scale, bigger players are taking notice. 
State oil majors Hindustan Petroleum Corp. and Bharat Petroleum Corp. sought a 
combined 83,000 tons of used cooking oil in tenders that close this month, 
according to government documents.

The moves are also part of a broader Asian push into biofuels, with Singapore 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/world-first-green-flight-levy-in-singapore-adds-to-rising-costs-of-air-travel?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
 and South Korea planning to introducesustainable aviation fuel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/europe-widens-lead-in-cleaner-jet-fuel-as-us-asia-fall-behind?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
 requirements starting in 2027. Collection networks are expanding in Malaysia, 
Vietnam and Indonesia, while China is alreadya major supplier 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/china-s-used-cooking-oil-ships-to-us-as-war-drives-biofuel-boom?cmpid=BBBXT102726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10615>
 of used cooking oil to more mature biofuel markets abroad.

Most of M11’s biodiesel currently heads to Europe and the US, but India will 
become an increasingly important buyer. The company plans to double its number 
of depots to 50 by year-end, adding households, food manufacturers and 
industrial kitchens to its collection routes.

And with crude prices kept high by war in the Middle East, the need to find 
more fuel at home will only become more pressing.

—Kanupriya Kapoor and Rajesh Kumar Singh, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Liquefied natural gas deliveries <bbg://news/stories/TNK6SSN3N095> to Japan 
fell by 1.4 million metric tons during Oct. 1-25 compared with the same period 
a year earlier, while South Korean imports rose by 0.9 million metric tons. 
Japan entered autumn with ample inventories after a milder-than-normal summer 
limited storage withdrawals, reducing the need for purchases. In contrast, 
South Korea has been stepping up imports to rebuild stockpiles that lag their 
five-year seasonal average.
