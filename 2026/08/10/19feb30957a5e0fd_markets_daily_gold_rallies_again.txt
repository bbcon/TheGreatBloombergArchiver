Gold revival

Gold prices have come back to life in recent days, underpinned by a return of 
ETF buyers.

Total bullion holdings in exchange-traded funds have increased by 24 tons 
since July 20, the fastest pace since early April. Central-bank purchases had 
already picked up, but the rebound only took hold onceETF inflows resumed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/chinese-dip-buying-bolsters-gold-as-prices-find-floor-at-4-000?cmpid=081026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7542>
.

The metal last week jumped 7.3%, its biggest weekly gain since late January 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/gold-holds-gain-after-soft-us-jobs-data-eases-rate-hike-concerns?cmpid=081026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7542>
, and an index of US mining stocks surged the most in a decade. The gains were 
fueled by signs of progress towards a deal on the Strait of Hormuz and a 
surprise contraction in the US jobs market.

Both make it less likely that the Fed will raise interest rates next month to 
keep inflation in check. Higher rates reduce the relative attractiveness of 
metals, which provide no yield.

Yet bullion is still a long way from its pre-war peak above $5,400 an ounce in 
January, when a speculative frenzy gripped gold and silver markets. The threat 
of higher interest rates helped undo that rally.

The ETF and central bank buying may be a harbinger of further gains. The 
financial world looks largely as it did before the Iran war: heavy government 
debt loads, huge budget deficits, a massive non-bank credit industry and 
rampant leverage. The reasons for owning gold therefore have not changed, and 
demand is beginning to reflect that. —Tatiana Darie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASS6kq2J56U/tatiana-darie?cmpid=081026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7542>

The week ahead

The consumer price index report on Wednesday is likely to show that prices 
ticked up only marginally last month after falling in June for the first time 
in six years, a welcome tempering in war-driven inflationary pressures.

That may help alleviate some of the anxiety at the Fed after three officials 
dissented on July 29 in favor of raising interest rates.
