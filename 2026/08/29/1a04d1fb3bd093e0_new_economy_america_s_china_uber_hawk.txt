When it comes to news headlines these days, references to the US Federal 
Communications Commission generally have apolitical 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/fcc-queried-stations-about-coverage-plans-before-trump-s-speech?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 orculture war 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-10/brendan-carr-s-fcc-tv-repeal-is-only-speeding-up-broadcast-s-end?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 context — something not unusual to an agency that decades ago was at the 
center of debates overvulgarity 
<https://constitutioncenter.org/blog/george-carlin-and-the-supreme-court> in 
programming.

Perhaps less-well recognized is that the regulator is emerging as a key 
instrument in Washington’s efforts to “de-risk,” or in some cases outright 
decouple, US supply chains from China. This shouldn’t come as a surprise: Since 
before the FCC’s creation, the US national security apparatus (and especially 
the Navy) was paying close attention to devices that transmit and receive 
signals over the air.

When the US entered World War I, the Navy took over 
<https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2016/summer/russell-willson> 
the nation’s radio stations, and it went on to play an active role in nurturing 
US wireless technology — including the establishment of the iconic Radio 
Corporation of America (RCA). When the FCC was set up in 1934, subsuming a 
number of predecessor agencies, among its foundational charges was “the purpose 
ofnational defense <https://uscode.house.gov/statviewer.htm?volume=48&page=1064>
.”
The Tuckerton Wireless Tower 
<https://www.radiomarine.org/point-to-point/tuckerton> in New Jersey. 
Originally built by a German concern, it was taken over by the US Navy during 
World War I and later sold to RCA.Photograph: Bettmann/Getty Images
Perhaps with this in mind, current FCC Chairman Brendan Carr has elevated the 
agency’s focus on China, setting up a national security panel to “leverage the 
full range of the commission’s regulatory, investigatory and enforcement 
authorities.” The stated aim is focused on countering the Chinese Communist 
Party, Carrsays <https://www.fcc.gov/fcc-council-national-security>. And given 
the regulator polices everything with commercial connectivity, from cables and 
broadcast wavelengths to cellular, satellite, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, it has 
considerable scope to do so.

Since late last year, FCC announcements on the subject have come fast and 
furious. They’ve included controls on imports offoreign-made drones 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-22/fcc-blocks-foreign-made-drones-from-future-us-use?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 and components, advancedrobotic devices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 andpower inverters 
<https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESowEB6zswFXj58itvhkKwyWnHxX8GbhK3bT7oQAl2mTDFvuiQUrTjQefRzddI7Gu_w0bDjjObS0NchBWhFY2C7Fv1YWdWUuKUmlU4KNEastT2d71NvzkpGmvJKoFWlFymLyRF-hx6mUY-4LdWNYbxk2NToDF_RyKfveux5dkoaPyvHuuwosqqhS7kSdd9dpKYEpR6wBXQfoEDhgq047YFFJ2iJR1D>
 (used in battery storage systems and solar panels). And a potential US ban is 
looming onoptical transceivers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-s-official-newspaper-warns-us-over-expanding-tech-curbs?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 — components that transmit data at high speeds within and between data centers.

“The FCC is a critical gatekeeper for national security, as it controls entry 
points into the US communications ecosystem through its authorization and 
licensing authority,” Evercore ISI analysts led by Sarah Bianchi wrote in a 
note earlier this summer. In opening new fronts in US-China tech restrictions, 
it’s “driving the administration’s most hawkish actions against China,” they 
said.

The Trump administration, as with the Biden administration before it, says the 
goal of various trade, investment and regulatory measures related to China 
isn’t todecouple <>. But when it comes to some areas, that now appears to be 
the exact aim. Indeed, Goldman Sachs Group Inc. economists refer to an “ongoing 
trend of tech decoupling” between the world’s two biggest economies.

China’s leadership is keenly aware of the situation. Beijing responded 
forcefully when the US Department of Commerce last Septembermoved to bar 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-29/us-hits-sanctioned-firms-subsidiaries-drawing-chinese-rebuke?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 sanctioned Chinese companies from using affiliated units to buy restricted US 
tech. China unleashedbroad curbs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-09/china-tightens-exports-of-rare-earths-and-related-technology?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 on exports of rare earths and related magnets,threatening to cripple 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-10/asml-other-semiconductor-firms-brace-for-fallout-from-china-s-rare-earths-curbs?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 global output of crucial items including semiconductors. The Trump 
administrationquickly backed down 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-30/us-to-suspend-china-sanctions-expansion-for-a-year-beijing-says?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
.

Unlike the Commerce Department’s so-called entity list, the FCC’s restrictions 
are “facially neutral,” says Tobin Marcus, head of US policy and politics at 
Wolfe Research. While bans on imports of new types of drones and advanced 
robotics “end up targeting primarily Chinese companies, there’s plausible 
deniability,” he says. Using this mechanism is a relatively new idea, he adds, 
saying that it stems from2019 legislation <>.
A Chinese-made SZ DJI Technology Co. drone flies over New York City.
Photographer: Mark Kauzlarich/Bloomberg
FCC restrictions are also mainly prospective, targeting new types of devices 
rather than goods already flowing into the US. So that removes any immediate 
sting for Chinese producers. (Still, Washington is also implementing other 
steps, such as100% tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/trump-imposes-100-tariffs-on-certain-drones-countering-china?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 on some drones and components.) Beijing’sresponses 
<bbg://news/stories/T7Q58EKJH6V4> to FCC announcements so far have largely 
amounted to stern warnings, though this month China did stiffen its export 
controls on drones as other parts offour sets of countermeasures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-hits-back-at-us-with-tighter-drone-export-curbs-sanctions?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 to recent US moves.

FCC bans are triggered by national security agencies, such as the Departments 
of Defense or Homeland Security. But under Carr, who first joined the agency in 
2012, the FCC has proven more than just a conduit for others parts of the 
administration. He’s “someone who just knows his agency,” Bianchi said in an 
interview this week. To be effective, “you need a task force to get this stuff 
through the building.”

Among the FCC’s other actions has been stepping up scrutiny of testing labs. 
Before products such as smartphones, drones and routers can legally be sold in 
the US, they must be tested by a lab recognized by the FCC. Last year, the 
agency moved towithdraw recognition 
<https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-takes-action-bad-labs-apparently-controlled-china>
 for seven labs it said are “owned or controlled by the Chinese government” and 
failed to renew four others. The idea is to shift testing to labs located in 
the US and allied nations.

The FCC has beefed up enforcement as well, launching “Operation Clean Carts 
<https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-415082A1.pdf>” to press online 
retailers to remove items covered by previous bans. Carr alleged in a statement 
last October that the Chinese Communist Party “has engaged in a multi-prong 
effort to insert insecure gear into Americans’ homes and businesses.”

“National security has been his priority for years, and he’s brought that 
focus to the chairmanship,” Carr adviser Adam Chan said on the ChinaTalkpodcast 
<https://www.chinatalk.media/p/fccs-adam-chan-on-the-new-robot-rule> this month.
Brendan CarrPhotographer: Al Drago/Bloomberg
Will all these efforts spark domestic US production? This newsletter has 
showcased analysis that suggests import barriers alonedon’t guarantee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2025-09-20/lessons-from-how-the-us-built-a-manufacturing-base-new-economy?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 that outcome.Federal spending 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2025-04-05/us-industrial-golden-age-requires-federal-support-new-economy-saturday?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260829&utm_content=8162>
 combined with multiple domestic competitors can help achieve it, but it’s not 
clear those elements are in place.

“This will be more successful if you try to incentivize” US production, 
Bianchi said. Also unclear is the degree to which the US will welcome the 
import of products made in allied nations. South Korea could, for example, be a 
considerable source of robotics output, she suggested.

As time goes on, the FCC’s actions over the past year will have increasing 
influence. That’s because, while they don’t apply to previously developed 
models, those become obsolete before long in any case. With inverters, the next 
generation comes along every two-to-five years, according to Nomura Holdings 
Inc. analysts Frank Fan and Donnie Teng. That said, the FCC can allow 
exemptions, and that process isn’t crystal clear, Bianchi said.

Chan contends the FCC’s market signals are already working, claiming that 
billions of dollars in domestic investment and plans for US production have 
followed the agency’s moves on drones and Wi-Fi routers. “So I do think this is 
something industry can adapt to,” he said.

ChinaTalk’s Jordan Schneider pressed Chan on what else is coming. Chan 
responded: “My to-do list is definitely very long.”
