• October ISM Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.5, beating consensus and reaching its highest level since late 2021, while October ADP private payrolls also beat estimates — constructive data offset by severe equity market turbulence.
• Democrats swept both chambers in Tuesday's midterms, flipping at least seven Senate seats and securing 232 House seats, a result that significantly exceeded pre-election expectations and introduced meaningful near-term uncertainty around AI capital expenditure.
• The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% on Thursday — its worst session since 2020 — wiping $2.3 trillion in market value as AI regulation fears overwhelmed post-election relief; futures recovered 1.3% by Friday morning.
• Gold rose above $4,000 per ounce on Tuesday, gaining 3.1%, as markets began pricing fiscal restraint under political gridlock and reduced oil-driven inflation risk.
• October nonfarm payrolls, expected at 65,000, were due Friday as the critical labor market read of the week.

**Manufacturing and Labor Market**
October's ISM Manufacturing index rose one point to 55.5, posting its best production reading since late 2021 with new orders remaining firmly expansionary. ADP private payrolls similarly beat consensus, suggesting the labor market retains momentum into year-end. These prints offered a constructive macro anchor during a week dominated by political and market turbulence, though October nonfarm payrolls — consensus at 65,000, more than double September's pace — represented the definitive weekly data point and remained pending at time of writing.

**Monetary Policy and Fiscal Risk**
The Fed emerged as an institutional beneficiary of the election outcome. Senate Democratic control neutralizes Trump's ability to populate the Fed board with loyalists beyond the single scheduled vacancy, and the attempted removal of Governor Lisa Cook loses practical force under the new Senate arithmetic. Chair Kevin Warsh, confirmed by the narrowest margin in Fed history, will need to cultivate the incoming majority. Market expectations for further rate hikes faded materially following the equity selloff, with Governor Williams signaling a data-gathering posture. The more consequential medium-term risk is fiscal: with the debt ceiling roughly $800 billion below the $41.1 trillion limit and the X-date projected in early-to-mid 2027, a divided government faces both a December 11 funding deadline and a borrowing limit breach — an outcome Goldman Sachs analysts assess as likely to produce an inflationary "win-win" spending deal boosting both defense and non-defense outlays.

**AI Correction and Equity Markets**
The midterm results introduced immediate downside risk to AI-driven capital expenditure, the primary driver of recent GDP acceleration. Goldman Sachs estimated more than 45% of planned US data center capacity sits in states where newly elected Democratic governors campaigned on construction restrictions. Texas alone — representing approximately 25% of total planned capacity — elected its first Democratic governor since 1990. The Thursday selloff was indiscriminate: Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, and memory names all fell sharply before recovering 4–9% in Friday premarket trading. ING moved to sell a $500 million data center loan portfolio, an early signal of credit market repricing. Exceptions included Airbnb, which gained 12%, and Cloudflare, up 14%, on strong earnings. The equal-weighted S&P 500 diverged meaningfully from the Nasdaq throughout the week, confirming the correction remains sector-specific rather than macro-driven.
