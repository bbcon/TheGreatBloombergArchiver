• European equity markets reflected global tech weakness, with Philips falling 11% in Amsterdam on margin pressure in its medical-imaging division and Barclays dropping its most in over a year on earnings that lagged US peers.
• Unilever and Mercedes-Benz offered contrasting bright spots: Unilever rose 6% after lifting its full-year outlook, while Mercedes gained 3.9% after beating profit expectations despite a China-driven volume decline.
• Massive wildfires in France and Spain continued for a second week, with more than 300,000 people evacuated; both governments are preparing emergency financial aid packages.

**Corporate Earnings**
Mercedes-Benz's resilient profit performance — which beat consensus — was driven by cost discipline rather than volume, as deliveries fell due to weakening Chinese consumer demand amid the prolonged property downturn. The result illustrates a broader tension facing European automakers: margin defense through restructuring while confronting structural demand erosion in their most important growth market. Barclays, by contrast, reported earnings that disappointed relative to US investment banking peers, reflecting a share of dealmaking activity that has not kept pace with the Wall Street recovery.

**Wildfire Emergency**
France and Spain are preparing emergency-zone declarations and financial support packages for wildfire-affected regions. Madrid's regional government announced an initial €30 million aid package. French Finance Minister Lescure committed insurer coverage for relocation costs for the more than 200,000 evacuees. Officials warned that a new heat wave forecast for this week could render containment efforts unpredictable. MSCI's CEO Henry Fernandez used the moment to underscore climate risk's growing materiality to asset valuations, noting that "rising temperatures are now a source of risk and return in any investment."
