The fight over AI chips is moving beyond the chipmakers. The US government is 
investigating 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/us-probes-apex-logistics-over-alleged-nvidia-ai-chip-smuggling?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>

chips to China, people familiar with the matter said, in what may result in the 
first action against a transportation company for allegedly participating in 
the illegal semiconductor trade.

Even though Washington imposed export controls in 2022 over fears the 
technology could benefit Beijing’s military, this is a significant widening of 
Washington’s enforcement perimeter: from who makes the chips to who moves them.

Nvidia, meanwhile, is pushing further into the AI stack. According to news 
reports, the company is nearing a roughly $13 billionacquisition of Hugging Face

, the widely used platform for sharing AI models, extending adealmaking spree

Nvidia isn’t the only chipmaker racing to keep up. Japan’s Kioxia plans a new 
chip fab 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/kioxia-plans-new-chip-facility-in-japan-to-meet-ai-memory-demand?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>
 with partner Sandisk to produce higher-end NAND for data centers, sources 
said, as surging AI workloads drive a global memory crunch and push up costs 
across the tech industry. The pair will seek Japanese government subsidies for 
the expansion, joining a capex race that is drawing in Samsung and SK Hynix as 
Big Tech locks up supply years in advance. The bet is that AI demand stays hot. 
The risk, as ever in memory, is building into the next glut.

The oil squeeze at Hormuz is easing, not because the conflict has, but because 
Gulf producers are getting better at working around it. Sources said Kuwait and 
Qatar haverestored crude exports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/kuwait-and-qatar-add-to-growing-oil-flows-getting-through-hormuz?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>
 to about 70% of pre-war levels, joiningthe UAE 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/uae-s-adnoc-defies-hormuz-risks-to-keep-crude-flowing-to-global-market?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>
 and Saudi Arabia in using costly “shuttle” trades to move barrels through the 
strait and transfer them to other vessels beyond it. Some 7 to 8 million 
barrels a day are now making it out, roughly double mid-July levels, helping 
pull Brent back to around $87 from above $120 in late April.

Even as Gulf producers find ways to keep oil moving through Hormuz, Washington 
islooking for new ways 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/us-aims-to-revive-civil-war-era-court-to-claim-iran-oil-as-prize?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>
 to tighten the screws on Iran. The Justice Department is preparing to revive 
prize courts, a largely dormant system of maritime law last used more than a 
century ago, to speed the seizure of Iranian tankers and cargo and allow the US 
to sell the oil, sources said. Proceeds will flow to the Treasury. The move 
could strengthen the blockade and help offset the cost of the war, but it is 
almost certain to face legal challenges, not least because modern US courts 
have virtually no experience with the system.

India’s central bank is stepping up its defense of the rupee as higher oil 
prices put renewed pressure on the currency, sources said. Flush with $73 
billion of fresh dollar inflows since June, the Reserve Bank of India has 
shifted from stepping in mainly during sharp currency swings tointervening more 
routinely 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/rbi-is-showing-up-whenever-rupee-weakens-in-intervention-shift?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>
 whenever the rupee weakens. It spent$7 billion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/inr-usd-india-central-bank-sold-about-7-billion-to-defend-rupee-on-friday?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8234>
 in a single day last month. The pressure hasn’t disappeared: higher oil prices 
are widening India’s current-account deficit while a narrowing rate gap with 
the US is pulling capital away. But the new strategy appears to be buying some 
calm, with near-term rupee volatility close to a 10-month low.
