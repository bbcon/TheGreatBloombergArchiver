There is a well-known scene In Paul Verhoeven’s 1987 movie RoboCop, where a 
character drily says “Oooh. Guns, guns, guns!” when two rival factions suddenly 
point weapons at each other, including at him. Right now feels a bit like that, 
only with the US administration and bonds.

The US 30-year bond yield has been above 5% for 55 days, the longest stretch  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/us-30-year-bond-enters-september-on-its-worst-stretch-since-2006?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
since 2006. Yields have moved higheron concern over 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 the size of America’s growing fiscal deficit, with public debt increasing by a 
third in less than five years to top $40 trillion. Rising yields increase the 
cost of servicing that debt load: the US government recently sold 30-year bonds 
at thehighest interest rate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/us-braces-for-30-year-bond-auction-at-highest-yield-since-2001?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 in a quarter century. Few expect Washington to act unless forced to by a 
market crisis.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has made attempts to rein in 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 long-term borrowing costs. Like his efforts to shore up Japan’s yen, the 
impact has beenshort-lasting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/bessent-tells-japan-officials-rate-hikes-are-needed-nhk-says-mthudgie?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
. Yet he has expressed little concern about rising yields,saying on 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/bessent-talked-with-druckenmiller-touts-us-debt-outperformance?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 CNBC Monday “the US bond market is the most resilient in the world” and 
arguing that Treasuries are outperforming other major bond markets.

The US can afford to be relaxed about the bond market because of its reserve 
currency status 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-02-28/europe-and-china-can-only-dream-of-rivaling-dollar-new-economy?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 and the lack of risk-free alternatives, meaning countries and institutions 
have to hold Treasuries regardless of levels. Yet the government is 
increasingly competingwith tech firms 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/pimco-says-too-much-too-fast-ai-debt-is-stoking-bond-yields?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 issuing bonds to fund their massive AI spending plans. And the Reserve Bank of 
Australia has reduced its US dollar holdings as part of a broader move by 
central banks to cut exposure under an unpredictable White House, the 
Australian Financial Review reported Tuesday.

Bond traders and the administration are in a standoff, with neither side 
backing down. Time to rewatch that movie.

What You Need to Know Today

It’s not just the US. Global bond yields climbed back to the highest level 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/australian-benchmark-bond-yield-jumps-to-level-last-seen-in-2011?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 in almost two decades as rising oil prices fueled inflation concerns and 
investors ramped up expectations for interest-rate hikes.

The rate on 10-year Japanese government notes touched 3% for the first time 
since 1996, UK 30-year yields reached the highest since 1998 and the 10-year 
Treasury rate hit levels last seen January last year. The yield on a Bloomberg 
gauge of global sovereign bonds advanced for a fourth straight session on 
Monday, rising to 3.72%, the highest since mid-2008.

Global bonds have been under pressure for months, with worries over elevated 
government spending in markets like Japan, the UK and the US prompting 
investors to seek higher compensation to own longer-maturity debt. Data Tuesday 
showed Euro-area inflation quickened to thehighest level 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/euro-zone-inflation-jumps-to-highest-in-almost-three-years?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 in almost three years.

“The direction of travel is going to be higher yields from here,” said Laura 
Cooper, global investment strategist at Nuveen.

Fast fashion, slow IPO: Shein struggled in its Hong Kong debut after a 
years-long process to listing that valued the company at a fraction of what it 
was once worth and still left investors questioning its value.

The shares fell as much as 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/shein-set-to-debut-in-test-of-investor-demand-for-consumer-plays?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 10% before closing little changed from its IPO price. Shein’s shares saw heavy 
selling in early trading, with bids hit three times as often as offers were 
lifted. Its debut has come at an awkward time: the fast-fashion retailer’s peak 
growth years are behind it just as tariffs, regulatory pressure and 
intensifying competition are making its next phase harder.

within reach of younger families.

Separately, South Korea plans to issue a near-record

 222.8 trillion won ($162.7 billion) of bonds in 2027 as Lee pushes his 
expansionary fiscal agenda.

World tour: Chinese leader Xi Jinping is setting off on what’s shaping up as 
his busiest travel schedule in more than seven years, partly in a bid to 
convince nations thatBeijing offers better 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/xi-s-busiest-travel-itinerary-in-seven-years-is-all-about-trump?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260901&utm_content=8387>
 global leadership than the US.

This month, Xi is visiting Kyrgyzstan and Egypt, and is widely expected to 
attend a BRICS summit hosted by Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi. When those 
visits are done, he’s set for a late September stop in Washington, where he’ll 
meet President Donald Trump in a bid to keep the two nations’ trade truce in 
place. The last time Xi was as active abroad was mid-2019, when he visited five 
countries.
