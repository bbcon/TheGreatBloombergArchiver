**Growth and Monetary Policy**
The eurozone GDP beat is the period's most consequential data point for the region, suggesting domestic demand has remained more resilient than feared despite elevated energy costs and Middle East-related supply disruptions. The Bank of England's split vote mirrors the analytical tension visible in global bond markets: persistent inflation pressures argue against easing, while cost-of-living stress on households — who are drawing down pandemic-era savings to sustain spending — argues against prolonged tightness. British retail sales and card spending have held up, though the savings buffer's durability is uncertain if energy prices remain elevated.

**Corporate**
Shell offered the clearest European corporate read of the period, more than doubling quarterly profit year-on-year despite gas production falling roughly a third due to Qatar war-related outages. Record refinery utilization and strong trading revenues more than offset lost production — a pattern echoed at TotalEnergies, Eni, and Glencore, whose trading arm is tracking toward one of its best years. Adidas fell 17% in Frankfurt after World Cup-related spending compressed margins below expectations.
