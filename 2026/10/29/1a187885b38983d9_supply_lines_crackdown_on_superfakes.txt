Have you ever been convinced by a great-looking “dupe” or “replica” product by 
somebody you follow on social media, or wondered if the ad you just clicked on 
was legit?

You’re not alone. The US Trade Representative recently closed the comment 
docket <https://www.regulations.gov/document/USTR-2026-0529-0001> for its 2026 
Review of Notorious Markets for Counterfeiting and Piracy, which focuses this 
year on “high-quality counterfeit trademark goods known as superfakes or 
superclones.”

The USTR’s Notorious Markets List, or NML, is typically aimed at foreign 
actors and marketplaces outside the US that undermine American businesses. But 
recently the USTR has been asked to take a closer look at US-based tech giants 
and their marketplaces, too.

Last year, without officially adding them to the list, the USTR noted the 
increasing popularity of social media influencers who review, promote and share 
links to counterfeit luxury products, using platforms like TikTok, Facebook and 
Instagram.

“As with other markets listed in the Notorious Markets List, stakeholders 
raised concerns with the high volume of counterfeit products available in these 
marketplaces, fraudulent advertisements and popular influencers promoting 
counterfeit goods, and ineffective enforcement measures to limit counterfeit 
sales and deter repeat offenders,” the USTR said in its2025 report 
<https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/2025%20Notorious%20Markets%20List%20(final).pdf>
.

Read More: Vinted Pledges to Crack Down on Looted Goods After French Riots 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/vinted-faces-influx-of-suspected-looted-goods-after-french-riots?cmpid=BBD102926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10564>

Recently, secondhand marketplace Vinted agreed to increase the policing of its 
listings after seeing an increase of items suspected to have been looted during
riots in France 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/french-consumer-confidence-hits-lowest-since-2013-after-riots?cmpid=BBD102926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10564>
, the country’s finance ministry said. Several other online platforms including 
Amazon.com, eBay, Meta and TikTok agreed to share data with French law 
enforcement and scour listings for items with security tags still attached.

‘Tip of the Iceberg’

That’s just one example of how quickly stolen and counterfeit goods can find a 
buyer. Anew report 
<https://www.tracit.org/the-landscape-of-lawlessness-online.html> from the 
Transnational Alliance to Combat Illicit Trade, or TRACIT, details how the 
explosion of a largely unregulated, global web of online marketplaces has 
created vulnerabilities for brands — and myriad risks for consumers.

“Today, counterfeiting is just the tip of the iceberg,” the report said 
<https://www.tracit.org/uploads/1/0/2/2/102238034/landscape_of_lawlessness_online.pdf>
. “Criminal activities have deepened to include the unauthorized sale of 
regulated products, the laundering of stolen goods, the circumvention of 
consumer health and safety laws, and the sale of products that violate product 
safety regulations.”

In rebuttal comment <https://www.regulations.gov/comment/USTR-2026-0529-0045> 
filed Oct. 22, Meta highlighted the artificial intelligence tools it’s 
deploying to combat illicit trade, and said calls for its platforms to be 
included on the USTR’s Notorious Markets List were misleading.

“The fact that Meta’s services reach beyond the United States does not 
transform Meta Platforms Inc. — a US-headquartered company — into a foreign 
actor,” the company said. “We strictly prohibit counterfeiting, piracy and 
other forms of IP infringement on Facebook, Instagram, Threads, WhatsApp and 
our other services.”

Self-Policing

In the absence of robust laws governing online marketplaces, several US-based 
platforms tout their own policies on seller-verification requirements, 
licensing obligations or enforcement procedures.

Enforcement of those terms is inconsistent, though, and sales of counterfeit 
goods is only a small part of the picture, according to Jeff Hardy, 
director-general of TRACIT.

In the meantime, TRACIT is developing scorecards for individual platforms 
based on what they say they’re doing to keep illicit goods off their 
marketplaces. The first grade will likely be issued toIndiaMART 
<https://www.indiamart.com/?srsltid=AYCX9vLrSvdtmRCb9n-42rQxES4t_KRp3PoyuqaQy-0nlYzrIcmW_8nR>
— India’s largest business-to-business online marketplace — in March next year.

Surging imports

 | The US merchandise-trade deficit widened in September for a third straight 
month amid an ongoing surge in imports of capital goods. The shortfall in goods 
trade grew 2.1% from the prior month to $134.9 billion, the largest since March 
2025, Commerce Department data showed. The value of merchandise imports rose 
2.4%, while exports advanced 2.6%. Imports of capital goods — a category that 
includes the computers and accessories, semiconductors and telecommunications 
equipment fueling the AI buildout — jumped 7.5% to a new record.
