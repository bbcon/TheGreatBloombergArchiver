Pope Leo XIV is heading to Latin America at quite a moment.

After last-minute surgery for lung cancer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/pope-leo-keeps-south-america-trip-in-november-after-lung-surgery?cmpid=BBD110626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11051>
, the first American pontiff begins an 11-day tour de force today covering 
19,000 miles.

It will take him through Uruguay 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-19/inside-the-vatican-press-pool-traveling-with-pope-leo-xiv?cmpid=BBD110626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11051>
, Argentina and wind up in Peru, the country where he spent 20 years, took 
citizenship and honed the pastoral skills that eventually carried him to Rome.

It’s a homecoming. But it’s also a test of the spiritual and increasingly 
political voice Leo has been finding over his first 18 months in the Vatican.
Pope Leo prays at St. Peter’s Basilica in the Vatican City on Jan. 6.
Photographer: Alberto Pizzoli/AFP/Getty Images
The mild-mannered Midwesterner once dubbed the Quiet American has been an 
unlikely adversary 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/pope-says-technology-must-help-people-warns-of-dehumanization?cmpid=BBD110626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11051>
 for President Donald Trump.

Their clashes back in April over the Iran war showed flashes of steel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-08/trump-spope-leo-feud-pits-vance-rubio-against-american-catholic-voters?cmpid=BBD110626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11051>
 beneath Leo’s measured style — and put him on the right side of US voters who 
punished Trump’s Republicans this week for the conflict and its impact on 
surging prices.

Now the pope is heading into a part of the world the president unashamedly 
regards as Washington’s backyard.

Trump has been pushing to strengthen US influence in Latin America 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-fails-to-curb-china-s-economic-influence-in-latin-america?cmpid=BBD110626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11051>
, finding allies among a growing cast of right-wing leaders, from Peru’s Keiko 
Fujimori to Argentina’s Javier Milei.

But Brazil’s leftist president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, stopped the 
conservative sweep of the region in its tracks with his surprise defeat of
MAGA-friendly Flávio Bolsonaro 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/argentina-s-government-confirms-flavio-bolsonaro-entered-country?cmpid=BBD110626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11051>
.
The pope in Bernebeu stadium in Madrid on June 8.Photographer: Claudia 
Paparelli/Bloomberg
Into this new, charged political climate steps Leo.

Both Fujimori, a Catholic, and Milei, who was raised Catholic but has 
increasingly embraced Judaism, are enthusiastically rolling out the welcome mat 
for him.

Yet their tougher lines on Iran and migration sit uneasily with the pope’s 
anti-war message and call for migrants and refugees to be treated humanely.

Leo is expected to travel as pastor above all. But geopolitics will be riding 
alongside.

Another clash may be on the agenda. — Carla Samon Ros
