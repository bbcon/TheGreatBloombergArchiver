Renewed US and Iranian tit-for-tat strikes are ratcheting up tensions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/iran-says-us-attacked-qeshm-island-in-latest-exchange-of-strikes?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
 yet again in the Middle East.

The odds of a resumption of all-out fighting appeared to have abated after the 
Republicans lost control of both houses of Congress in the US midterms, in no 
small part due to the unpopularity of the nine-month-old war.

President Donald Trump has already declared victory and says he would prefer 
to rely on economic pressure to ensure the Islamic Republic never obtains 
nuclear weapons.
Iranians carry a giant national flag during a rally in front of the former US 
embassy in Tehran on Nov. 4.Photographer: Atta Kenare/AFP/Getty Images
While diplomatic efforts to bring about a lasting peace continue, they have 
taken a back seat 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/iran-threatens-to-strike-civilian-sites-if-us-does-the-same?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
 over recent weeks.

Kuwait was targeted by drones for the first time since early September, 
although it wasn’t immediately clear if the attack was launched from Iran or 
Iraq, where Iran-backed militias are based.

Those strikes and similar Iranian ones on Bahrain 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/iran-strikes-bahrain-base-repeats-path-to-us-deal-remains-open?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
 appeared to be in retaliation for an American aerial assault on Iran’s Qeshm 
Island — which the US says was a response to Tehran targeting its personnel and 
assets.

The back-and-forth continues against the backdrop of intensified fighting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/saudi-backed-forces-in-yemen-step-up-attacks-on-houthis?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
 between a Saudi Arabia-led coalition and Houthi rebels who are making advances 
toward Aden, the seat of Yemen’s internationally recognized government.
Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz.Photographer: Gallo Images/Orbital 
Horizon/Getty Images
While an escalation in the clashes between the US and Iran isn’t necessarily 
imminent 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/trump-says-us-looking-into-any-iran-attack-plan-for-washington?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
, the presence of three US aircraft carriers in the region and Tehran’s refusal 
to back down to US demands indicate it isn’t off the table. Oil marketsappear 
sanguine 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/smaller-tankers--hormuz-shuttle-trade-oil-flows-wti-brent-crude?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
 for now, with Brent crude trading well off its recent highs.

Trump’s options of striking Iran or just leaving the conflict in limbo both 
bode ill for a region that’s endured months of instability and hardship. Gulf 
governments also fear the US could just walk away.

Its people remain on tenterhooks, waiting to see how events unfold. — Fiona 
MacDonald 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APODdiKV0_I/fiona-macdonald?cmpid=BBD111826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11565>
