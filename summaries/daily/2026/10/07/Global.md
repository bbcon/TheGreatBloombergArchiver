**Energy and Geopolitics**
The Strait of Hormuz has become the axis of a multi-front energy disruption. Iranian attacks on tankers are accelerating even as Saudi Arabia prosecutes a counter-offensive in Yemen against Houthi forces that seized Bab el-Mandeb in September — adding a second critical chokepoint to the risk map. The Trump administration's simultaneous naval blockade of Iranian ports has all but eliminated Iranian oil exports, pressuring Tehran's economy, but Iran appears to be responding through proxy escalation rather than diplomatic concession. The G-7 has announced a coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels of emergency diesel and crude reserves; analysts characterize this largely as fulfillment of prior commitments rather than new supply. Diesel shortages are propagating into agriculture costs across Australia, Canada, and Europe, raising the prospect of a secondary food inflation impulse.

**AI Credit and Bubble Risk**
SpaceX's reported plan to raise $40 billion in debt — $10 billion in bank loans and $30 billion in investment-grade bonds — to purchase Nvidia chips represents a structural escalation in AI-driven credit creation. Bridgewater's Ray Dalio has characterized the AI boom as a "classic bubble," warning that rising interest rates could be the catalyst for a correction as paper wealth is eventually converted to cash. Global IPO proceeds are on pace for $230 billion year-to-date, 45% above last year, but the 2021 record of $373 billion appears out of reach. Rising issuance is already compressing forward multiples even as earnings grow, a dynamic BCA Research's Noah Weisberger describes as structurally negative for near-term equity returns.
