Donald Trump’s decision to waive new US sanctions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-17/trump-s-russia-sanctions-waiver-called-mistake-by-key-gop-allies?cmpid=BBD101926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10112>
 on Russia on Friday has quickly prompted bipartisan American criticism that 
has come alongsidean angry response 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/zelenskyy-says-trump-is-pushing-ukraine-to-capitulate-to-russia?cmpid=BBD101926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10112>
 from Ukraine and Europe, both of which feel increasingly abandoned because of 
his actions.

For anyone who has watched the US president and his use of the levers of 
economic power for any significant time, it shouldn’t have been a surprise 
because it reinforced what ought to be a rule: Politics always trumps 
geopolitics.

That matters when thinking about everything from tariffs to sanctions and 
export controls and how the current occupant of the White House deploys them. 
Especially in a world in which geopolitics – and geoeconomics – is what CEOs 
and investors are focused on when they’re not thinking about AI.

In a social-media post 
<https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/117463828588421761> Sunday, Trump 
portrayed his controversial deal to allow Russia to export diesel free of 
sanctions as part of a broader attempt to broker a peace in Ukraine. He accused 
its president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, of inflaming the conflict by attacking 
Russian refineries and more.

But he also hinted at the domestic politics driving his actions.

“Farmers, ranchers, and businesses of all kinds are suffering greatly from 
this,” he wrote. Which meant he was pointing to the domestic constituencies 
whose anger over surging diesel prices since the launch of the Iran war has put 
Senate seats in what were supposed to be Republican strongholds in play in 
midterm elections that are just 15 days away.

The clue is, of course, in the America First label Trump has always put 
forward for his approach to power.

US self-interest matters more than anything else, administration officials 
will say. To pursue anything other than that would just repeat past misguided 
policies, they argue.

Yet it goes beyond that. Domestic politics is what really matters.

If bolstering American power in the world was what really drove policy, plenty 
of people in Washington – as a growing cast of Republicans did over the weekend 
– would make the case for standing firm on Russian aggression.

“The current path, unfortunately, just benefits Putin,” Senator Ted Cruz, a 
Texas Republican ally of Trump, told Fox News on Saturday.

Trump’s primary goal as he races around the US to campaign for Republicans is 
finding solutions to his domestic political challenges. And that’s something 
that he has done plenty of times before.

Faced with repeated polls showing his levies are unpopular, Trump and his 
administration have created a growing number of doors in his tariff wall. Even 
as they have sought to rebuild it following the Supreme Court’s ruling that the 
vast majority of the duties he introduced in 2025 were illegal, forcing 
billions of dollars to be refunded to companies that paid them.

The latest example came with an announcement that followed the September visit 
of Chinese leader Xi Jinping to Washington. Afterwards, US officials unveiled a 
four-page list of 77 categories of products from China that they were proposing 
to remove duties on.Everything on the list 
<https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/US-Public-List.pdf> from 
fireworks to vacuum flasks with toys, magic tricks, linens, and microwave ovens 
in between were consumer goods.

The point being that American consumers – or voters – and their appetite for 
cheap Chinese products appeared to be a no-go zone in the economic competition 
between China and the US. At least in an election year.

Trump isn’t done with tariffs, of course. There’s undoubtedly more to come 
after the midterm elections.

The executive order 
<https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/implementing-the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-act-of-2026/>
 that he signed on Friday waiving the sanctions on Russia also called for 
officials to draft up a tariff target list of countries making new purchases of 
Russian oil and gas, as mandated by a new sanctions law that passed Congress in 
September. Which besides applying new sanctions also gave Trump the power to 
impose duties of up to 100% on goods from any country buying Russian petroleum 
exports. Though those tariffs too have been waived for now by Trump.

Trump has repeatedly declared his love for tariffs and other economic tools. 
He’s made clear with his war on Iran and other international interventions that 
he has an interest in geopolitics.

But as he indicated on Friday, nothing trumps his interest in the pure 
domestic politics that gave him the power he now has.
