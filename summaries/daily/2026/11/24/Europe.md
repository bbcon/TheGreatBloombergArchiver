• European car sales rose 3.4% YoY in October — in line with recent trend, representing a ninth consecutive month of gains — with EVs and plug-in hybrids comprising 43% of registrations, up from 31% a year earlier.
• Germany's business outlook improved for a seventh consecutive month, cementing expectations that the economy is emerging from a prolonged stagnation.
• Spain faces a snap election Sunday amid significant public protests over a housing crisis, even as the economy expands at more than twice the eurozone average.
• European natural gas prices surged as a colder weather outlook intensified concerns over winter supply adequacy, already strained by Iran war-related export disruptions.

**Economic Conditions**
The European picture is notably bifurcated. Germany's sequential improvement in business sentiment offers the clearest sign yet that Europe's largest economy may be turning a corner, though the improvement is from a depressed base. Spain's macro credentials — above-average growth, declining unemployment, narrowing fiscal deficit — contrast sharply with public sentiment: housing prices have risen roughly twice as fast as wages for young workers, and two-thirds of adults under 34 still live with their parents. The snap election on Sunday injects near-term political uncertainty. European gas prices surging into a cold snap highlights the region's continued vulnerability to energy supply disruptions tied to the Middle East conflict.

**Corporate and Policy Developments**
Sanofi agreed to acquire US biotech Insmed for $41 billion, the largest pharmaceutical acquisition in three years, with Insmed shares rising 14% and Sanofi gaining 6.2% in Paris. Intesa Sanpaolo opened a €36 billion bid for Monte dei Paschi, the world's oldest bank. Commerzbank is weighing a partial exit from US Treasury trading ahead of new US clearing requirements, a decision complicated by the pending UniCredit takeover. An ECB official expressed support for a "prudent" rate increase, reinforcing policy divergence with other major central banks as inflation pressures persist.
