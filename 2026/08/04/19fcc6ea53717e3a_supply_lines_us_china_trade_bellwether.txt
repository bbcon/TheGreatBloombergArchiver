A bellwether for US-China trade volumes is signaling sustained strength amid 
tariffs, anenergy shock 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/hormuz-traffic-at-a-trickle-as-ship-attacks-heighten-concerns?cmpid=BBD080426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7242>
 and uncertainties heading into a high-stakes summit next month between the 
leaders of the world’s biggest economies.

Matson, based in Honolulu, is the largest US container carrier even though it 
ranks just 30th in the world based on total capacity, according to Alphaliner
data <https://alphaliner.axsmarine.com/PublicTop100/>. While the company 
transports cargo under theJones Act 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/trump-s-temporary-jones-act-waiver-sparks-long-term-policy-fight?cmpid=BBD080426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7242>
 to and from Hawaii, Alaska and Guam, it also operates a premium expressservice 
<https://www.matson.com/china/index.html> between China and the US West Coast.

Transpacific shipping has been volatile during President Donald Trump’s second 
term through tariff escalations and ceasefires with Beijing. But despite 
figures that show China’s share of overall US imports is declining, Matson 
executives lifted theirfull-year outlook 
<https://investor.matson.com/news-releases/news-release-details/matson-inc-announces-second-quarter-2026-results-and-raises-full>
, saying they did robust business in the first half and the near-term future 
looks much the same.

“We’re optimistic that the US consumer remains resilient and will be 
supportive of freight demand in the transpacific for the remainder of the 
year,” CEO Matthew Cox said during an earnings call on Monday.

The China service, where container volumes advanced 15.2% last quarter from a 
year earlier, is expected to be “at or near capacity through the peak season,” 
he said, referring to the seasonal rush in the third quarter.

The following quarter, Cox said, should bring “a more traditional seasonality 
pattern compared to the elevated period of freight demand experienced in the 
transpacific market in the fourth quarter of 2025” after the US and China 
called a one-year pause in their trade war.

Stock Surge

Matson shares are up about 68% this year, compared with an 11% increase in the 
S&P 500 and a 23% gain in the Bloomberg Intelligence North America Freight 
Transportation and Logistics Peer Group.

Among the more notable shifts Matson cited is more cargo originating from 
Southeast Asian economies like Vietnam and Thailand, which now represents 20% 
to 25% of the China service volume, according to Cox. That diversification is 
“significantly higher than the level achieved in the beginning of 2025,” he 
said.

“Many of the customers that are using us in Vietnam and in Thailand are the 
same customers that trust us with cargo out of China” and “have multiple 
facilities in different countries,” Cox said.

Matson’s outlook, though, hinges partly on continued diplomatic calm in the 
US-China economic relationship. Some days that looks feasible, like on Monday 
whenBloomberg reported <bbg://news/stories/TJ7I4TRKV2TH> that China secured its 
largest amount of US soybeans so far this year.

Other times the ties seems strained, especially lately on issues of advanced 
chips and AI, as Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping prepare to meet in late 
September.

As Bloomberg reports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/china-is-getting-more-anxious-about-mythos-before-trump-meets-xi?cmpid=BBD080426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7242>
 today, officials in Beijing are concerned about the cyber capabilities of 
Mythos and other AI frontier models developed by US companies, seeing these as 
potential weapons in the battle for global economic hegemony.

‘Upset the Cart’

“The US and China’s governments, we think, are both interested in creating a 
stable trade environment, and that will persist through the end of the year and 
into next year,” Cox said. “Of course, something else may come up, but our 
expectation is very much the case that neither the US or Chinese government 
want to upset the cart at this point in time.”

Among the big questions is how hard Trump will push for additional taxes on 
Chinese imports given their impact on inflation. Still pending is the US 
administration’s Section 301 report on excess factory capacity, which puts 
Beijing’s export-heavy industrial policy in the crosshairs for more tariffs.

Trumponomics Podcast: The Tariff Trade-Off 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2026-07-29/trumponomics-the-tariff-trade-off-podcast?cmpid=BBD080426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7242>

Trump’s tariffs have domestic political consequences, too. Contradicting his 
administration’s repeated assertions, a National Bureau of Economic Research
paper 
<https://www.nber.org/papers/w35561?utm_campaign=ntwh&utm_medium=email&utm_source=ntwg8>
 circulated this week said the tariffs rolled out by the White House since the 
start of 2025 have raised prices for consumers in waves.

The first one is immediate, and it happens at the border, with 90% of the 
tariff hitting US importers, according to the report co-authored by New York 
Fed researchers. Another wave hits households with a lag, so it can take up to 
a year to filter through supply chains, it concluded.
