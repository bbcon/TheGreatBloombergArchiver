For months, Indonesia has been trying to show MSCI Inc. it is taking concerns 
about market governance in the country seriously. In the early hours of Friday 
morning Jakarta time, the index provider delivered a damning update.

MSCI says it is considering what is effectively a double downgrade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/indonesia-s-emerging-market-status-under-consultation-msci-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10189>
 for Indonesian equities, bypassing frontier status and relegating the 
Southeast Asian nation to standalone-market status because of the severity of 
its investability problems. The move would classify Indonesia alongside the 
likes of Argentina, Lebanon and Palestine. MSCI is expected to make a decision 
by mid-2027.

A downgrade to frontier status alone would likely trigger significant outflows 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/why-indonesia-risks-being-removed-from-msci-s-global-indexes?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10189>
 of investment from Indonesian equities. Goldman Sachs estimated that passive 
outflows alone could reach as much as $13 billion in an extreme scenario. A 
move to standalone status, which would remove Indonesia from MSCI’s main global 
indexes altogether, would be “unambiguously worse,” according to Citigroup 
analysts.

Both the timing and the severity of the statement caught investors — and 
government officials — off guard. The benchmark Jakarta Composite Index tumbled 
11.6%, the most since January 1998. A sharp decline at the open triggered a 
30-minute trading halt. Indonesia’s richest tycoons areset to lose 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/tycoons-lose-billions-as-indonesia-faces-ejection-from-indexes?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10189>
 more than $6 billion in a single day, according to the Bloomberg Billionaires 
Index.

Jakarta has scrambled to respond. The Indonesia Stock Exchange said it plans 
to seek clarification from MSCI on the rationale behind the reclassification 
plan, pointing to what it called “significant progress and reform measures.” 
But investors say there islittle chance of a reprieve 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/indonesia-outflows-to-worsen-as-msci-mulls-cut-analysts-say?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10189>
 for Indonesian shares until there is more clarity on the country’s status.
