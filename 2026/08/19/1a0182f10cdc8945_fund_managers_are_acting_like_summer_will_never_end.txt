Chips With Everything

As the end of this long, hot summer rolls into sight, who exactly is buying 
the market and why? Stocks took a reverse on Tuesday, with a renewed selloff 
for chipmakers, but it shouldn’t be overstated. The S&P 500 is still barely 
1.5% down from its all-time high, and up 12.4% for the year in the face of a 
protracted war in the Middle East, while bond yields are the highest in decades.

Several investor surveys help explain how this is possible. The latest Bank of 
America globalsurvey of fund managers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/bofa-poll-sees-few-bears-left-as-investors-pile-into-stocks?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 finds a potent rebound in optimism:

One point worth noting: The passage of tax cuts in both of Donald Trump’s 
terms led to highs in investor sentiment. They automatically raised the price 
it made sense to pay for stocks, by ensuring that companies could hold on to 
more of their profit; and down the line a new Democratic administration with a 
mandate for tax hikes — which currently begins to look possible — would be 
really bad news for stocks.

For now, the market is underpinned by fund managers’ optimism on the economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-08/to-understand-the-us-economy-just-board-an-airplane?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
. Most now believe there will be “no landing” and no slowdown in growth — great 
for stocks but bad for bonds as it implies rates will need to rise:

Further, executives’ optimism on earnings calls has brought the percentage of 
fund managers expecting double-digit earnings growth over the next year to its 
highest since 2021. This is a powerful brew for equities:

As for retail money, the success of the DRAM exchange-traded fund, which 
invests only in memory-chip makers, shows positivity from retail investors. 
Launched less than six months ago, the fund has dropped by more than 30% in the 
last two months — and yet its total assets have continued to rise in that time, 
as retail investors treat it as an opportunity to fill their boots:

Morningstar 
<https://www.morningstar.com/funds/bonds-tech-stocks-drive-historic-streak-us-fund-flows>
 found that total net inflows into long-term US funds (both stocks and bonds) 
topped $100 billion for the fourth month in a row in July. That’s 
unprecedented. When professional and retail money are this positive, the stock 
market will go up, come what may.

Oil

Fund managers seem optimistic in large part because they’re confident that the 
oil price won’t veer out of control. On average, they think crude will be $76 
per barrel at year’s end:

Oil at such a price 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-06/why-oil-s-not-at-200-after-the-biggest-supply-shock-in-history?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 would tax the economy, but needn’t get in the way of a “no landing” scenario. 
One point of concern is that the asset allocators were markedly more optimistic 
than those who invest directly in the oil markets. January 2027 Brent futures 
have risen back above $85, a high for the war period barring a few weeks in May:

Confusion hangs over the US strategy on Iran, if indeed there is one. But big 
money managers appear a tad more complacent about the risks than those closest 
to them — which in turn suggests more downside than upside for stocks.

Politics

BofA found no consensus on how markets might react to a Democratic midterm 
sweep 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-18/primary-results-democrats-greatest-divide-for-2028-is-education?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 (taking control of both the House and the Senate). Fund managers don’t think 
it will happen. Only 23% expected a sweep — slightly reduced from July after 
some fractious Democratic primaries:

Comparing this to the odds produced by the political nerds trading on 
prediction markets, it does look as though fund managers haven’t caught up with 
events. Both Kalshi and Polymarket make a Democratic sweep a 50-50 shot:

As the summer at last winds down and the election campaign comes into focus 
after Labor Day, there’s plainly a risk that investors will catch up to the 
threat of a very fractious two years of politics ahead.

Raring to Go

More than two months after SpaceX, presented as an AI play, made the largest 
IPO in history, Anthropic PBC, another supersize AI darling, isreadying for one 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/anthropic-plans-to-give-ceo-extra-voting-power-information-says?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 that might eclipse Elon Musk’s company. Bettors on Polymarket now put the 
chances that it will reach $2 trillion this year at about 70%:

Following July’s great reversal for tech stocks, there’s increasing confidence 
that Anthropic will cause the next big IPO splash, while rival OpenAI is now 
expected to wait until next year:

Since Nippon Telegraph & Telephone’s landmark offering in 1987, it has taken 
more than six years, on average, for a new IPO to claim the record. AI is 
accelerating that. The reported October listing for Anthropic, given its last 
private valuation of about$1 trillion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-28/anthropic-raises-at-965-billion-valuation-eclipsing-openai?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 and the scale of itsrevenue growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-surpasses-65-billion-ahead-of-ipo?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
, is a strong candidate to challengeSpaceX’s record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-11/elon-musk-s-spacex-set-to-make-history-with-record-breaking-ipo?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
:

With OpenAI also firming up its plans 
<https://openai.com/index/openai-submits-confidential-s-1/>, the stage is set 
for a new generation of mega caps. AsPoints of Return has detailed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-04-23/ipos-trampled-underfoot-by-a-stampede-of-unicorns?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
, this isn’t great news for the market as a whole. When insiders decide it’s 
time to cash in, that’s a negative signal; several past IPOs established market 
tops. They also create a huge headache for index providers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-06-08/market-selloff-altitude-sickness-brings-stocks-down-hard?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 and the passive funds that use them.

For Anthropic, an annualized revenue of more than $65 billion provides a 
powerful catalyst, but whether it can command a $2 trillion valuation is 
another question. PitchBook’s Harrison Rolfes points out that Anthropic is 
already roughly one-third of the way to the $190 billion to $200 billion 
revenue forecast for 2028on a run-rate basis 
<https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/revenue-run-rate/> 
(in other words, assuming that it could maintain its current rate of monthly 
revenue growth). That would value it at roughly 15 times revenue, while a 
hypothetical $2 trillion valuation would be about 31 times. For reference, the 
S&P 500 as a whole trades at 3.8 times revenues. Rolfes says:
This is extraordinary, but increasingly supportable if this growth persists 
and margins improve. Anthropic’s IPO may therefore become the first major 
public-market test of whether investors will value token consumption like 
recurring software revenue or more like a high-growth compute utility.
How safe are these assumptions? As Points of Return noted here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-08-10/aindicators-hint-at-doubts-in-credit-markets?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
, a plunge in the price ofAI tokens 
<https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-tokens-explained/> (the basic units of text 
processed by large language models) raises concerns that frontier models — like 
Anthropic’s Claude or OpenAI’s ChatGPT — are losing pricing power.

Anthropic’s steady revenue growth, however, offers a counterpoint. Customers 
are putting Claude to work across an expanding range of tasks, and their rising 
usage can offset falling prices by generating more tokens.

With IPOs worth potentially more than $3.6 trillion in the pipeline, this 
flurry of listings underscores how capital-intensive AI is. It requires 
long-duration funding for training, research, and development of the kind that 
public equities provide. That is pushing the unicorns to the public markets.

The July selloff shows that investor sentiment can shift quickly. Neostellar 
Capital’s Evan Schlossman argues that companies must make a judgment call on 
the prevailing risks:
You’re seeing right now that the risk of staying on the sidelines with AI and 
the way it’s shaping and accelerating is outweighing the risk of geopolitical 
instability and other broader things in the market.
As IPOs come to dominate the agenda in coming weeks, the key question is what 
the weight of newly issued shares will do to a market accustomed to years of a 
shrinking number of shares — a problem thatcould grow particularly severe next 
year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-06-02/spacex-to-boldly-raise-money-where-no-ipo-has-gone-before?cmpid=BBD081926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7866>
 as lock-up periods end and founders are allowed to sell more shares. There are 
also risks in concentration and in valuations that still rely on earnings that 
remain far in the future. But for now, it looks like Anthropic is going public 
soon, and there will be buyers.

—Richard Abbey

International Vigilantism

One key point about the bond selloff is that it’s not indiscriminate. The BofA 
survey finds fund managers are underweight bonds, but by no more than the 
historical norm — this isn’t some desperate dive for the exit:

At the national level, it’s the countries whose fiscal policy have been called 
into question that have suffered the biggest selloff. Other factors may be at 
work, but “bond vigilante” angst about deficits is definitely a big driver.

Exhibit A) is France, where the spread of 10-year OATS over German bunds 
ballooned after President Emmanuel Macron’s disastrous snap election two years 
left a legislature split three ways and incapable of doing anything to break 
the fiscal logjam. After signs of recovery, The gap is now its widest since 
Mario Draghi’s“whatever it takes” speech <https://www.bis.org/publ/work749.htm> 
lanced the sovereign debt crisis in 2012:

The higher spread shows the worries about French credit quality. Concerns 
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1_xwnb3cymc> about the country’s ability to 
service its debts are amplified by the high absolute level of yields at which 
the government must borrow:

With a potentially epochal presidential election less than a year away, it’s 
hard to see how the European Union’s second-largest economy can calm these 
fears any time soon — and there’s a risk that the moment of crisis could come 
before, rather than after, the election.

Then there is Japan, which has intervened to try to strengthen its currency as 
its bond market corrects after decades of deflationary slump with frightening 
pace. The difference with China, whose bonds traditionally trade at a much 
higher yield due to booming growth, is extraordinary. The two countries have 
traded places in a matter of months:

Normally, a change in yield differentials like this would cause a big 
appreciation of the yen. The fact that the opposite is happening, even to the 
extent that authorities are intervening, speaks volumes about the level of 
concern over Japan’s debt overhang. As in France, bond markets appear to be 
running out of patience with a problem long in the making.
