Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Asian oil team leader Serene Cheong looks at the White House’s latest attempts 
to economically hobble Iran.

It was touted as a financial D-Day for Iran, but there’s little in the details 
to suggest a turning point in the country’s conflict with the US.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had vowed to unveil measures against the 
Islamic Republic that would amount to “the greatest coordinatedeconomic 
isolation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/bessent-to-detail-us-plans-to-isolate-iran-s-economy-on-monday?cmpid=BBBXT082526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8152>
 in the history of the world.”

On Monday, he announced sanctions on dozens of small, lesser-known companies 
and individuals, but the Chinese buyers of billions of dollars’ worth of 
Iranian oil and the banks that make those purchases possible were left 
untouched.

For now at least, the strategy doesn’t look very different to what the US has 
already been doing, and exposes the limitations of what it’s willing to do in 
the future.

In continuing to target the logistics firms, agents and middlemen involved in 
the Iran-China oil trade, it’s effectively playing a game ofwhack-a-mole 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/dozens-of-chinese-firms-hit-by-bessent-s-d-day-iran-sanctions?cmpid=BBBXT082526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8152>
, given that these companies have shown they can reemerge in a different form 
in a matter of days.
US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent speaking to members of the media outside 
the White House in Washington last week.Photographer: Yuri 
Gripas/Abaca/Bloomberg
An obvious choice for maximum impact would have been to blacklist banks from 
China, or even the United Arab Emirates, that form the backbone of the trade. 
Taking aim at anothermajor Chinese refiner 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-24/us-sanctions-china-refinery-iran-shadow-fleet-ahead-of-talks?cmpid=BBBXT082526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8152>
 that buys Iranian oil — like it did with Hengli Petrochemical (Dalian) 
Refinery Co. Ltd. in April — would also have upped the ante.

These measures, however, would have risked antagonizing Beijing before a 
meeting of the US and Chinese leaders in less than a month.

In fact, whether the US needs to do anything more to strangle the 
Iran-to-China oil flows at this point is questionable. Its naval blockade of 
the Islamic Republic’s ports is provingvery effective 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/iranian-oil-supply-to-chinese-refiners-squeezed-by-us-blockade?cmpid=BBBXT082526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8152>
, with a growing fleet of fully laden tankers stuck in the Persian Gulf and 
floating stockpiles of Iranian crude in East Asia having all but run out.

This approach may already be forcing China’s independent refiners, Iran’s main 
customers, to look for alternative supplies. It does, however, come at the cost 
of keeping US warships deployed for extended periods, something that’s 
generated negative headlines back home.

Of course, it’s possible that this is just the beginning, and tougher 
financial measures may be unveiled in the days and weeks to come. But going by 
what’s been announced so far, the latest efforts seem more like business as 
usual than a pivotal moment akin to the Normandy landings.

—Serene Cheong, Bloomberg News

Chart of the day

Gold is near the highest since mid-May after a rally sparked by a surprise 
bond-market intervention that’s revived concerns over US fiscal policy and led 
to weakness in the dollar. That marks a return to the so-calleddebasement trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/gold-holds-near-three-month-high-as-traders-weigh-treasury-moves?cmpid=BBBXT082526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8152>
, which powered the precious metal’s blistering rally last year. The advance 
has also taken bullion above its 200-day moving average, which is often viewed 
as a bullish technical signal.
