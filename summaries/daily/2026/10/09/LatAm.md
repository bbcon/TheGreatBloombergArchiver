**Brazil: Electoral Risk and Climate Policy**
The October 25 runoff carries significant implications for Brazilian climate and land-use policy. Flávio Bolsonaro has questioned the scientific consensus on human-caused climate change, and his Liberal Party allies in Congress have pushed to expand legally permissible deforestation. A Bolsonaro victory would mark a sharp reversal from Lula's positioning of Brazil as a global forest-protection leader following COP30 in Belém. Both candidates support expanding Amazon oil production and paving the BR-319 highway through protected rainforest — areas of unexpected convergence — but differ fundamentally on fossil fuel phaseout timelines and international climate commitments. Land-use change accounts for roughly 40% of Brazil's gross greenhouse gas emissions, making the electoral outcome consequential for global carbon accounting.

**Chile: Weather and Commodity Risk**
The Santiago flooding follows prior storm disruptions to northern Chilean mining operations. Chile's role as the world's dominant copper producer means that sustained infrastructure or operational disruptions carry commodity market implications, though current damage assessments remain preliminary.
