• Brent crude fell below $87 a barrel, extending this week's decline to about 8%, and WTI slid 1.9% to $80.81, as Iran and Oman discussed an "interim framework" to resume shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
• Central banks are diverging sharply: Australia and the ECB struck hawkish tones on resilient growth and energy-driven inflation, a Bank of Japan hike is largely priced in for next month, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole remarks are awaited as a US policy signal.
• Wheat and corn prices are at multi-year highs as Ukraine and Russia, which together supply more than a quarter of global wheat exports, have curtailed shipments amid mutual attacks on ports and vessels, raising food-security warnings from Egypt to sub-Saharan Africa.
• MSC, the world's largest container line, is partially resuming Red Sea and Suez sailings, joining Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd in a tentative normalization of routes largely avoided since late 2023.

**Energy and Inflation**
The oil pullback looks fragile rather than durable: hopes for a Hormuz reopening have been raised and dashed before, and European gas — with winter contracts costing more than double year-ago levels — has overtaken oil as bond investors' top inflation concern. US refiners, running at their highest rates since before the pandemic, are deferring maintenance to capture record margins, leaving fuel markets little cushion against outages.

**Food Security**
The UN has warned that when food becomes unaffordable, vulnerable populations face a stark choice: "starve, move, or rebel," echoing dynamics behind the 2010-11 Arab Spring. Global food prices remain below the 2022 peak reached after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, but officials call the standoff over Black Sea grain exports more precarious given added strain from war-driven fertilizer costs and heat-damaged harvests.
