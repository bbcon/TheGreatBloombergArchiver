‘Illusion of Stability’

There have been plenty of warnings over the years issued by both official and 
unofficial bodies about the US and other nations following unsustainable fiscal 
paths. But rarely do advanced economies get chided for emulating — in a bad way 
— some of the things seen in crisis-hit emerging nations in the past.

That’s effectively what one of the world’s largest financial-industry 
associations did in a report out on Wednesday. Without naming Bessent and his 
recent move to expand buybacks of longer-dated debt, the Institute of 
International Finance made mention of “direct purchases” of debt in “secondary 
markets.” In other words, buybacks.

The IIF, which includes most of the world’s biggest banks and is led by a 
former senior US Treasury official — Tim Adams — also highlighted that 
rich-country debt managers are selling more short-term debt.

Bills, as securities due in up to 12 months are known, are cheaper right now 
to sell than longer-term debt, like 10-year or 30-year securities. But it means 
debt costs become much more volatile. And with inflation high, central banks 
are raising short-term interest rates — making bills costlier as well.

Bessent has referred to his maneuvers as a “Treasury twist,” in a reference to 
historical Federal Reserve initiatives to pull down longer-term borrowing 
costs. There’s little doubt why he would want to do that, with 30-year US 
mortgage rates now back upover 7% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/us-mortgage-rates-surge-above-7-to-a-more-than-two-year-high?cmpid=BBD092426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9248>
, the highest in more than two years.

Tinkering around 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/us-more-than-doubles-long-dated-debt-buyback-size-to-6-billion?cmpid=BBD092426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9248>
 with buybacks anddebt maturities 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/us-keeps-debt-sale-guidance-steady-deepening-reliance-on-bills?cmpid=BBD092426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9248>
 amounts to “searching for quick fixes to long-term problems,” the IIF said. 
“Such measures may provide temporary relief, but they cannot resolve the 
structural drivers of rising debt. The unsuccessful experience of many emerging 
markets in using financial engineering to contain borrowing costs has also made 
investors wary of its effectiveness — and its potential side effects.”

The US Treasury declined to respond to a request for comment.

A separate publication on Wednesday catalogued that the bulk of the increase 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/us-jobs-fed-speak-are-what-really-drove-yields-research-finds?cmpid=BBD092426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9248>
 in US 10-year yields over the past several years came in three-day windows 
around monthly US jobs reports and speeches by key Fed officials. That 
highlights the role of monetary policy expectations in setting borrowing costs.

Given the massive borrowing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-2026-budget-gap-hits-1-96-trillion-with-a-month-left-to-go?cmpid=BBD092426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9248>
 that governments are now engaging in, it’s no surprise, then, that the IIF 
also said “investors are increasingly concerned about central bank 
independence.” And such worries may be bound to escalate. That’s because 
governments are getting hit by “skyrocketing” interest costs.

The whole situation has been masked somewhat, because inflation has been 
pumping up GDP figures and leaving debt ratios looking somewhat stable, as the 
chart above shows. But it’s all just an “illusion of stability,” the IIF said. 
“A deceptively benign picture.”

Need-to-Know Research

Oil shocks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-9?cmpid=BBD092426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9248>
 used to push the US into recession and force the Fed to make tough choices on 
letting inflation run or slowing the economy. But a new paper finds the 
nation’s current-day status as a net energy exporter has created a new paradigm.

While gasoline prices can be painful and some consumers and firms end up worse 
off, overall the Brookings Institution paper found net benefits. “Consumption, 
equity valuations, and activity strengthen broadly across sectors and regions,” 
Northwestern’s Diego Känzig, Harvard’s James Stock and Northwestern’s Luca 
Zanotti wrote. Employment also sees net gains.
Source: Diego Känzig, James Stock and Luca Zanotti
That suggests a different playbook for Fed policymakers. “Now, if these shocks 
are actually expansionary, you have a much easier situation to deal with,” 
Känzig said in an interview. “That’s what we call in economics a divine 
coincidence, because by raising rates, you can counter the inflationary 
pressures and also avoid the economy from overheating.”
