The US Is Eyeing a Brazil Rare-Earths Mine to Close Gap With China
Washington eyes a rare earth mine in Brazil as it races to build a supply 
chain beyond China. ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌

US-China competition has intensified during US President Donald Trump’s second 
term, and one of the clearest battlegrounds is rare earths and critical 
minerals. At the center of that rivalry is South America’s largest economy, 
Brazil.

The pressure intensified after China imposed export controls 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/trump-xi-summit-to-take-stock-of-china-commitments-greer-says?cmpid=BBD090326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8309>
 on seven heavy rare earths and related products in April 2025. Shipments 
plunged, leaving some some American and European automakers scrambling for 
magnets, and in some cases forcing factories to cut production.

Brazil looks like an obvious alternative. It holds about a quarter of the 
world’s estimated rare-earth reserves but accounted for less than 1% of global 
mine production last year. China, by comparison, has nearly half of known 
reserves anddominates 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-fails-to-curb-china-s-economic-influence-in-latin-america?cmpid=BBD090326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8309>
 the more difficult parts of the supply chain, handling about 91% of the 
processing for magnetic rare earths.

As I recently reported 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-fails-to-curb-china-s-economic-influence-in-latin-america?cmpid=BBD090326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8309>
 with a Bloomberg colleague in Beijing, China spent decades building that 
position globally, including across Latin America. Washington is now trying to 
catch up.

The Pela Ema mine 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/brazil-rare-earth-mine-shows-hurdles-of-breaking-free-from-china?cmpid=BBD090326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8309>
 in central Brazil shows what that effort looks like — and why it won’t be 
easy. It is one of the few commercial-scale sources outside Asia of the 
magnetic rare earths needed for high-performance magnets. About $5 billion in 
US-backed investment and financing is flowing into the project, including 
Oklahoma-based USA Rare Earth’s planned acquisition of its owner Serra Verde 
Group.

Several mining experts have told me Pela Ema is among Brazil’s strongest 
rare-earth assets. But the project also illustrates how much of the supply 
chain remains unresolved.

Related: Trump Touts $3 Billion in Minerals Projects to Counter China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/trump-touts-3-billion-in-minerals-projects-to-counter-china?cmpid=BBD090326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8309>

During its ramp-up, the mine has recovered a smaller share of the valuable 
minerals contained in its ore than initially expected, while upgrades to its 
processing plant continue. And once the material leaves the mine, it still 
needs to be separated into individual rare-earth oxides, a step overwhelmingly 
dominated by China.

Therein lies the challenge: to develop a yet-unproven US solution at 
commercial scale so it’s ready to handle all of Pela Ema’s output.

Brazilian politics could make the equation harder still. President Luiz Inácio 
Lula da Silva is pushing a new critical-minerals framework aimed at ensuring 
more processing and manufacturing happens at home, while lawmakers are debating 
tighter scrutiny of foreign investment in strategic mineral assets.

And even as the Trump administration pushes for Brazil’s minerals, Lula has 
made clear that he intends to keep working with China, including oncritical 
mineral processing <https://x.com/LulaOficial/status/2081580214367244525> — 
showing just how long the road to loosening Beijing’s grip on the sector may be.

Perfect storm 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/dry-bulk-shipping-rates-surge-on-tight-capesize-vessel-supply?cmpid=BBD090326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8309>
 | A key measure of bulk-shipping rates has touched a two-year high and risks 
climbing further, according to analysts, as bad weather crimps the availability 
of Capesize vessels just as exporters ramp up delivery. The Baltic Dry Index 
rose 5.5% to 3,331 points on Wednesday in London, touching the highest since 
December 2023, driven largely by a rise in the constituent Capesize rate which 
surged over 8%. Bad weather has hit the Pacific at a time of strong demand in 
both that ocean and the Atlantic. “We see the current surge as something of a 
perfect storm, with vessel supply tightening and demand firing in both basins 
at the same time,” said broker Thurlestone Shipping.
