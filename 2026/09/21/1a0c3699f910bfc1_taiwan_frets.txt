The US administration’s reported withholding of a $14 billion weapons package 
for the self-governed democracy Beijing views as its own have added to the 
unease as Taipei steps up preparations for scenarios likean invasion or blockade
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/taiwan-coast-guard-plans-visit-to-philippines-as-china-pressure-grows?cmpid=BBD092126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9135>
.

Taiwanese faith in Washington is divided. A United Daily News survey published 
today found 47% of respondents lacked confidence that the US would continue 
providing Taiwan with military assistance and arms, compared with 44% who 
expressed confidence it would.

Taipei may be watching for any shift in US language amid suggestions that 
Beijing wants Washington to change its position from saying it “does not 
support” Taiwan independence to saying it “opposes” it. Such wording could 
diminish the longstanding US policy of strategic ambiguity toward Taiwan.

Still, Taiwan’s government downplays the chances of a major shift, at least 
publicly.

Former US deputy national security adviser Matt Pottinger struck a similar 
note, saying he didn’t expect any “grand bargain” or “unwise deal.”

Many in Taiwan will be hoping he’s right. — Yian Lee
