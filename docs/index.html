Tuesday, October 13, 2026

US · Asia · Europe · LatAm · Global

US

  • US September CPI data due Wednesday; consensus expects headline to tick up from 3.4% to 3.6% YoY and core from 2.4% to 2.5%, keeping pressure on monetary policy — actual print not yet released.
  • Bank earnings dominate Tuesday's session, with JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, Citigroup, and Goldman Sachs all reporting; bank stocks have been mired in a correction ahead of results.
  • AI-related equity weakness extended into US markets, with Nasdaq 100 futures down 0.6% and chip stocks heading for a sixth consecutive session of losses.
  • S&P 500 futures were near flat at 7,862, with markets absorbing rising geopolitical risk premiums in energy alongside AI valuation concerns.

Monetary Policy

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller noted that the surge in long-term Treasury yields is already performing some of the Fed's tightening work, helping to curb demand in rate-sensitive sectors. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.19%, with real 10-year yields having risen a full percentage point this year to 2.88% — a level that, under normal circumstances, would exert significant downward pressure on risk assets and gold. The Fed under Kevin Warsh has proven more hawkish than markets anticipated earlier in the year, and Wednesday's CPI print will be closely scrutinized for signals on whether further rate hikes remain on the table. Record fuel prices are expected to keep both headline and core readings elevated.

Markets and Corporate News

Chip stocks led the equity selloff, with an AI valuation debate sharpening after Broadcom CEO Hock Tan warned at a Singapore event that many investors "have no clue what they are investing in" and are setting expectations "almost like they are in a casino." Bank of America's fund manager survey found technology overweight positioning at its highest since August 2020, at 37% of respondents. Gold has shown notable resilience despite the headwinds of higher real yields and a stronger dollar, declining only approximately 3% year-to-date, supported by central bank buying and September ETF inflows that were the strongest since early 2022. ---

Asia

  • South Korea's KOSPI fell 5.4% to its lowest level since July, with Samsung and SK Hynix each declining more than 8% on AI valuation concerns — a move that extended a six-day losing streak for chip-sector ETFs.
  • Bank of Japan officials are signaling no back-to-back rate hike in October, preferring to assess data before a potential move as early as December; the BOJ raised rates to 1.25% last month.
  • China's CXMT claimed a memory chip technology breakthrough surpassing Samsung and SK Hynix — an unverified assertion that nonetheless jolted markets and illustrated the competitive pressure building in the sector.

Monetary Policy — Bank of Japan

BOJ policymakers appear inclined to hold rates steady at the October meeting, having lifted the benchmark to 1.25% in September. Officials see limited justification for consecutive hikes absent a material acceleration in upside inflation risks, according to people familiar with internal deliberations. The December meeting is being treated as the more likely window for the next move. This posture suggests the BOJ remains cautious about front-loading tightening in an environment where global growth risks are rising and domestic data require further validation.

Markets and Corporate Developments

The KOSPI's sharp decline reflected concentrated exposure to semiconductor names at a moment when global investors are reassessing AI infrastructure valuations. The RBI widened currency curbs to support the Indian rupee, which recorded the largest gain in Asia on the session. LVMH reported that mainland China luxury sales fell by double digits in the third quarter, reinforcing concerns about the durability of Chinese consumption — a read-through for broader regional consumer demand. AirTrunk separately delayed a planned Singapore REIT IPO until next year, citing institutional investor skepticism, an additional data point in the cooling of data center capital market activity. ---

Europe

  • The IMF's World Economic Outlook, released at its Bangkok annual meetings, raised the 2026 global growth forecast marginally to 3.1% while flagging surging sovereign bond yields as the top financial stability risk — directly relevant to France and the UK.
  • French high school students entered a third consecutive week of protests, coinciding with the start of parliamentary budget debate; political disruption is measurably weighing on business confidence.
  • Hungary's Prime Minister Peter Magyar faces a credible test of his euro-adoption roadmap, which requires cutting the fiscal deficit by more than half over three years to meet 2030 membership criteria.

Fiscal Stress and Sovereign Markets

European sovereign debt dynamics were among the most discussed themes at the Bloomberg New Economy Forum in New Delhi, where Blackstone President Jon Gray argued that fiscal strains in France and the UK represent a more pressing near-term risk than US debt dynamics, citing lower growth rates and wide deficits. The IMF echoed this framing, urging the UK chancellor to "stay the course" on fiscal consolidation while using cost-of-living support sparingly. A Bank of England official separately indicated that faster-than-expected UK economic growth strengthens the case for a further rate hike. European equities outperformed Asian peers on Tuesday, aided by declining oil prices and easing bond yields, though the macro backdrop remains fragile.

Corporate and Political Developments

LVMH shares slipped 1.5% in Paris trading after the luxury group confirmed double-digit sales declines in mainland China last quarter, threatening a broader luxury sector recovery thesis. The EU finalized a deal capping Chinese hybrid vehicle imports at 350,000 units annually from December, rising 6% per year — a measure welcomed by European carmakers but prompting Beijing to reserve WTO dispute rights. France's budget process is beginning under conditions of elevated political noise, with student unrest adding to uncertainty around the government's fiscal consolidation credibility. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes authorized Senator Flávio Bolsonaro to visit former President Jair Bolsonaro, who remains under house arrest less than two weeks before the presidential runoff election — a politically significant development ahead of a closely watched vote.

Political Economy

The authorization of the family visit injects a symbolic element into Brazil's presidential runoff campaign at a sensitive moment. The outcome of the election will have material implications for fiscal policy, central bank independence, and Brazil's broader macroeconomic trajectory. Separately, in Mexico, the president of the Business Coordinating Council cited persistent crime, judicial overhaul uncertainty, and tougher tax enforcement as factors weighing heavily on corporate investment sentiment — a combination that may constrain private-sector activity and complicate the Banco de México's task of supporting growth without reigniting inflation. No major macro data releases from the region were reported for this period. ---

Global

  • Brent crude traded near $104–$106 per barrel after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia's main airport over the weekend killed at least 16 people, including a US citizen; analysts warn a miscalculation that broadens the conflict could push prices above $120.
  • The IMF raised its 2026 global growth forecast modestly to 3.1% and projected an acceleration to 3.4% in 2027, but warned the world economy is approaching the limits of its resilience amid energy supply strain and rising financial stability risks from surging bond yields.
  • Trump's deal lifting US sanctions on Russian diesel exports opened a significant rift with European allies and Ukraine while providing little near-term relief at the pump; Putin secured tangible concessions without offering any reciprocal movement on the war.

Energy and the Middle East

The Houthi strikes on Riyadh represent the deadliest attack on Saudi Arabia in decades and have materially complicated the energy pricing environment. Brent futures for six months forward reached a record, signaling that markets are abandoning assumptions of near-term conflict resolution. The East-West pipeline at Yanbu — capable of handling 7 million barrels per day but effectively capped at 3.5–4 million — represents a specific vulnerability. Analysts at BCA Research argue the Houthis are acting rationally, calibrating their strikes to avoid triggering overwhelming US retaliation while serving their internal Yemeni objectives. A shutdown of Red Sea or bypass routes around Hormuz could readily push Brent above $120, compounding existing inflationary pressures globally.

AI Capital Flows and Financial Stability

The IMF's Global Financial Stability Report identified surging bond yields as the single largest near-term risk to the financial system, a concern amplified by the circular capital flow dynamics underpinning the AI investment boom. The gap between surplus-saving nations — China and Japan — and deficit economies, principally the US, has returned to its highest level since the lead-up to the 2008 financial crisis and is approaching a 150-year peak. Columbia Business School economist Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh estimates cumulative AI infrastructure investment could reach $9 trillion by 2032, surpassing the 19th-century railroad boom as the largest capital expenditure cycle in US history relative to GDP. Whether this investment generates sufficient productivity gains to justify current valuations remains the central unresolved question for global financial stability.
