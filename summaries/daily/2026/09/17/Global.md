**Oil and the Strait of Hormuz**
The partial restoration of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline is a meaningful near-term development for energy markets. The pipeline, which had been used to route oil exports around the severely disrupted Strait of Hormuz following the Iran war, was itself struck by drone attacks last week. Its partial resumption reduces the immediate supply risk premium, explaining the decline in Brent to $104. However, the broader structural disruption to Middle Eastern energy infrastructure remains unresolved. Houthis have captured additional territory in Yemen and are tightening control over Bab el-Mandeb, a second critical chokepoint for seaborne energy trade. The return of container ships to the Red Sea — welcomed by carriers but described by the Global Shippers Forum as a "reckless gamble" — reflects the difficult risk-reward calculation facing the shipping industry in the absence of guaranteed security assurances.

**Monetary Policy Divergence and EM Stress**
The Fed's hawkish pivot has meaningfully tightened global financial conditions. The dollar's broad rally following the FOMC decision directly pressures economies with large US dollar-denominated debt overhangs. The BOJ faces a particularly acute dilemma: hiking into a strengthening dollar risks appearing insufficient, while inaction allows yen weakness to persist. More broadly, the combination of still-elevated energy prices, an emerging food inflation shock from El Niño and the European summer drought, and a Fed committing to further tightening creates a challenging macro backdrop for emerging market assets heading into year-end.
