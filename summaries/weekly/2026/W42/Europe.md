**ECB Policy and Fiscal Stress**
The ECB's internal debate over further tightening sharpened materially this week. Wunsch's Wednesday statement that additional hikes are likely sits in genuine tension with Lagarde's simultaneous emphasis on the absence of second-round inflation effects: if wage growth remains contained, the case for tightening weakens even as energy keeps headline CPI elevated toward 4%. Separately, ECB policymaker Dolenc suggested market-driven financial tightening may partially substitute for further rate action, while frontrunner to succeed Lagarde, Klaas Knot, signaled a mildly hawkish lean. The IMF at Bangkok explicitly called for a "prudently hawkish bias" among central banks, adding external pressure on the ECB's deliberations.

**France and Sovereign Markets**
France's fiscal situation remains the dominant European tail risk. Bond market pressure has reached a point where yields at multi-decade highs are now actively constraining government policy choices. Finance Minister Lescure expressed qualified optimism that market discipline is generating cross-party momentum for fiscal consolidation — an argument that bond vigilantism is doing work that parliamentary arithmetic alone could not. Whether this dynamic translates into durable budget support across France's fragmented political landscape remains genuinely uncertain. Blackstone's Jon Gray, speaking at Bloomberg's New Economy Forum in New Delhi, characterized France and the UK as more pressing near-term fiscal risks than the US, citing lower growth rates and wide deficits.

**Trade and Corporate Developments**
The EU finalized a deal capping Chinese hybrid vehicle imports at 350,000 units annually from December, rising 6% annually — welcomed by European carmakers but prompting Beijing to reserve WTO dispute rights and characterize the concession as a one-time occurrence. EU leaders separately mandated the European Commission to develop a market-access exclusion mechanism by year-end, representing a meaningful escalation toward a systemic trade instrument directed primarily at China. Germany has notably shifted toward supporting safeguard measures, a realignment that could accelerate Brussels' overall posture. LVMH shares slipped 1.5% in Paris trading after confirming double-digit mainland China sales declines last quarter, threatening the luxury sector recovery thesis.
