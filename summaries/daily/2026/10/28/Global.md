**Energy and Iran**
Oil markets are pricing a binary outcome in the US-Iran conflict: either a negotiated de-escalation or an extended and intensifying confrontation. The WSJ report of US warnings to Gulf states regarding potential post-midterm strikes has amplified the near-term risk premium. A third scenario — a prolonged stalemate that becomes a structural feature of the market — appears underpriced. At current Brent levels above $114, energy costs are functioning as a persistent inflationary headwind across developed markets, complicating central bank signaling in the US, Europe, and Australia simultaneously.

**AI Inflation and Power Demand**
The AI infrastructure buildout is generating inflationary pressure through three channels: elevated memory hardware costs, pass-through to consumer electronics, and power price increases from data center electricity demand. Goldman Sachs estimates a peak 60 basis point boost to US core PCE. Separately, Sierra Club analysis projects US power sector emissions could rise 42% by 2030 if current data center construction trajectories are realized, effectively erasing 15 years of grid decarbonization progress. Trump's proposed rollback of methane emissions rules adds a further dimension to the regulatory retreat from climate commitments, with the EU also proposing to delay methane disclosure requirements for energy imports amid winter supply concerns linked to the Iran conflict.
