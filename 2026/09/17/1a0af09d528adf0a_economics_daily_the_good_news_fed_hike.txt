Warsh’s Hawkish Hike

For the first time in three years, the Federal Reserve is hiking again 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/fed-raises-rates-as-warsh-bucks-trump-to-contain-inflation?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
. While the dominant narrative in the 2022-23 cycle was a desperate move to 
contain the biggest inflation surge in decades, this time around the US central 
bank head has thrown in a feel-good message.

Asked in his press conference why the Fed pulled the trigger on a 
quarter-point rate increase on Wednesday when it stood pat in July, Chairman 
Kevin Warsh cited three things. Number one, he said, “the economy has 
strengthened.” He went on to say it “appears to be strengthening,” which added 
someforward momentum 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/trump-denied-rate-cut-as-federal-reserve-targets-war-stoked-inflation?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 to his initial observation.

“New hiring, private sector earnings, business capital investment — each of 
these markers has improved in recent months and is pointing in a good 
direction,” Warsh said. “Credit flows 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-26/artificial-intelligence-investment-now-competes-with-governments-for-capital?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 have been robust, particularly for businesses.”

Stephen Stanley at Santander US Capital Markets, said “perhaps the most 
striking development from Chairman Warsh’s press conference was the degree of
optimism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/us-retail-sales-rebound-more-than-expected-in-broad-advance?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 regarding the economy.”

How far tying a rate hike to economic strength 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/fed-raises-rates-as-warsh-bucks-trump-to-contain-inflation?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 may go in mollifying Donald Trump — who has long demanded the Fed slash rates 
— remains to be seen. The president’s initial response was a generic call for 
ratesto go to 1% <https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/117282643318690567>. 
(The Fed’s new target range is 3.75% to 4%.) In comments after the decision, 
Trump blamed the hike on a“hostile” board 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/trump-says-interest-rates-should-be-1-or-less-after-fed-hike?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 and said he’s “relying on Kevin.”
Kevin WarshPhotographer: Daniel Heuer/Bloomberg
Warsh cited two other dynamics for the rate rise. One was that “too many 
categories” in US inflation reports continue to post annualized increases above 
3% on a six-month and 12-month basis.This metric 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/fed-s-warsh-lays-out-data-dashboard-to-read-us-economy?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 will now clearly be a big focus for investors and economists.

Finally, he effectively pointed to the Iran war pushing up costs, without 
using the word “Iran” — potentially another move to lessen the risk of direct 
conflict with the president. Warsh instead referred to “geopolitics” and “
hotspots <> around the world.”

Having voted with all his colleagues to pull the trigger, Warsh secured his 
credibility 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/treasuries-hold-gains-as-fed-hikes-rates-signaling-more-ahead?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
, many Fed watchers said — an opinionarguably supported 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/global-bonds-recover-as-warsh-s-inflation-fight-calms-market?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
 by the bond market. And while he again forswore offering “forward guidance,” 
some viewed him as hinting he’s ready to do more.

“Today’s action starts to show we’re serious about this,” he said. “We removed 
a dose of accommodation.” Note the words “starts” and “dose.” Both could convey 
there’s more to come.

“This is certainly not ‘a one and done,’” rate hike, said Stephanie Roth, 
chief economist at Wolfe Research, on Bloomberg TV. She said Warsh’s hawkish 
signals suggest “considerable more accommodation to be removed.”

Need-to-Know Research

Global gender parity remains more than a lifetime away for women — even though 
the gap with men is now smaller than ever, according to the World Economic 
Forum.

As we explain here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/men-and-women-will-see-full-equality-in-only-120-years-wef-says?cmpid=BBD091726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=8991>
, the organization’s annual report on the matter found current progress means 
it will still take 120 years to reach full equality. That’s a marked 
improvement, and up from 170 years measured a decade ago, but still means that 
no-one alive today is likely to experience it.

The WEF’s report combines indexes of political empowerment, economic 
participation and opportunity, health and survival and educational attainment 
to assess gender disparity across the world. The overall gap across 145 
economies has narrowed to 69.4%, the best result recorded in two decades of 
such reports.
