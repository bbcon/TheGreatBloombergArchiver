• Atlanta Fed's GDPNow estimate jumped to 4.3% for the current quarter, up from Q2's 1.5%, with Apollo's Torsten Slok crediting 0.2 points to tariff refunds after the Supreme Court struck down most Trump-era import duties.
• Goldman Sachs said a September Fed hike is now "very unlikely," citing softer retail sales, weaker employment and slowing inflation; the dollar fell to its lowest since May.
• Investment-grade issuance has reached nearly $1.5 trillion this year, up 36% YoY, an AI-driven borrowing binge pushing Treasury yields to a 25-year high; the 10-year held flat at 4.69%.
• July FOMC minutes (a 9-3 vote to hold) are due Wednesday, alongside retail earnings from Home Depot, Target, Lowe's and Walmart.

**Growth and Fed Policy**
GDPNow now tracks 4.3% annualized growth this quarter, up from Q2's 1.5%, though partly aided by one-off tariff refunds rather than pure momentum. Goldman argues markets remain too hawkish: with retail sales, employment and inflation all softening, a September hike looks unlikely, and futures are pricing accordingly. The dollar's slide to a three-month low reflects that repricing.

**Credit Markets and Volatility**
Equity volatility remains subdued — the VIX closed below 15 weekly in August for the first time this year — even as bonds show strain. Hyperscalers' AI borrowing is competing with heavy Treasury issuance for demand, pushing corporate and government costs higher rather than crowding one another out. Anthropic's disclosed 14-fold jump in Q2 revenue to $11.5 billion ahead of its IPO extended a rally in AI-linked names.
