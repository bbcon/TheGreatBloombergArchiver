The Bank of Japan hiked and the yen tanked. Whether it was the split vote by 
board members, mixed signals by Governor Kazuo Ueda or just something else, the 
weakening partly undoes efforts by Japanese and US authorities to bolster the 
beleaguered currency.

The yen sank as much as 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/yen-drops-against-dollar-after-boj-raises-rates-as-expected?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9056>
 1.3% against the dollar. TheBOJ raised 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/boj-hikes-rates-at-fastest-pace-since-1990-as-inflation-persists?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9056>
 its policy rate by a quarter point to 1.25%, with two board members, Toichiro 
Asada and Ayano Sato, voted against the hike, showing there’s internal 
resistance to tighter policy.

The outsized reaction shows the complex job facing central bankers and the 
growing expectations traders are putting on them. The European Central Bank 
last week hiked rates for a second time. Markets are pricing at least three 
more hikes in the deposit rate from its current level of 2.5%. The US Federal 
Reserve on Wednesday lifted borrowing costs for the first time since 2023. 
Traders see at least one more increase this year and as many as two more in 
2027.

With high energy prices triggered by the Iran war fueling inflation, central 
banks are limited in what they can do to cap price gains. At the same time, 
elevatedbond yields 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/bond-rout-s-silver-lining-emerges-with-chance-to-grab-5-yields?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9056>
 are raising concern about the ability of governments to service their growing 
debts. While central bankers may not like it, they are increasinglybecoming 
vulnerable 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/ecb-s-zigman-warns-growth-is-at-risk-if-inflation-isn-t-tackled?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9056>
 to what markets demand.

Spirit of the age: The two founders of Au Vodka are taking home at least £75

shortly after leaving school, according to Bloomberg calculations.

to Kentucky-based Sazerac Co. this week, just over a decade after pushing their 
way into the world’s biggest spirits category with gold bottles and 
neon-colored vodka flavors. Born in Swansea, the childhood friends founded Au 
Vodka in 2015 when Morgan was 19 and Quinn was 21.
