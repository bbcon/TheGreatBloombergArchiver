Pedro Sánchez is an anomaly in European politics.

Not only is he the last Social Democrat still standing among leaders in a 
major country in the European Union. His defiantly liberal approach to 
immigration goes against the grain of his peers, as does his stubborn refusal 
to heed calls to increase defense spending.

But perhaps the biggest mystery 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/sanchez-targets-toughest-comeback-yet-with-early-spain-election?cmpid=BBD112026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11676>
 is how has he survived this long without having passed a single budget since 
late 2022.

His time might finally be up. Two railway disasters 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-31/sanchez-rethinks-reelection-plan-after-spain-rail-disaster-socialist-party?cmpid=BBD112026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11676>
 this year took place under his watch. Then there were a series of corruption 
scandals within his inner circle.
Alberto Núñez Feijóo, leader of the People’s Party, center, at a rally in 
Badalona on Nov. 12.Photographer: Angel Garcia/Bloomberg
And yet even his own fiercest political enemies have learned from experience 
to never write him off. Aheated debate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/spain-s-sanchez-struggles-in-debate-against-main-rival-feijoo?cmpid=BBD112026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11676>
 last night between Sánchez and opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo, the 
frontrunner to become prime minister, reflects the tone of this campaign: ugly 
and acrimonious.

One major figure was missing in that debate, but whose presence looms large 
over them both. That is Isabel Díaz Ayuso, the formidable president of the 
Madrid region, home to more than 7 million people in and around the capital.

It was impossible not to spot her voice in several of Feijóo’s attacks on his 
opponent. She is arguably themost influential figure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/isabel-diaz-ayuso-on-disaster-prime-minister-pedro-sanchez-and-spain-election?cmpid=BBD112026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11676>
 in Spanish politics even if her name isn’t on the ballot. That puts her in the 
company of other powerful conservative women reshaping the political landscape 
in Europe, from Giorgia Meloni in Italy to Marine Le Pen in France.

In an interview with Bloomberg, she pulls no punches, describing Sánchez as a 
“disaster” and his party as “a sect.” She, of course, has a cult following of 
her own.

Together, they are mirror images of a particularly aggressive strain of 
politics we can expect more not less of.— Daniel Basteiro 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXUW8P-C8ck/daniel-basteiro?cmpid=BBD112026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11676>
