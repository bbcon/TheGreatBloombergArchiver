Southeast Asia in Spotlight

Before President Donald Trump’s visit to China this week, and Thursday’s 
historic trilateral 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/trump-to-meet-with-xi-putin-nov-19-in-china-white-house-says?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
 with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, he stops in Manila, where US ties with 
Southeast Asia will be in the spotlight.

The region has seen its exports to America climb over the past decade, boosted 
by Trump’s first trade war with China. Chinese companies, amongst others, were 
incentivized to shift production to places including Vietnam and use them as 
new bases for supplying their US customers.

Southeast Asia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/world-leaders-at-manila-summit-seek-trade-to-offset-us-risks?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
 has become “a key destination for companies pursuing China+1 strategies,” Adam 
Farrar, senior Asia-Pacific geoeconomics analyst at Bloomberg Economics, wrote 
in a note last week.

Through September, US imports from members of the Association of Southeast 
Asian Nations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/philippines-marcos-to-join-brics-summit-in-india-push-closer-asean-ties?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
, or Asean, were up over 40% from the same period of 2025, pushing the American 
deficit with the bloc past $340 billion. By comparison, the bilateral US 
deficit with China was $131 billion, down 18% in that period.

While many Southeast Asian economies sought trade deals with Trump after his 
“Liberation Day” gambit in April last year, some final agreements remain 
outstanding. Singapore already had a free-trade deal, while Cambodia and 
Malaysia signed pacts last year. Thailand is hoping Trump will sign one during 
his visit Tuesday to Manila, where he’s set to attend a summit of Asean leaders.

Vietnam remains in a more complicated position, Farrar wrote in his note. 
Hanoi said in October that negotiations werelargely complete 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/vietnam-says-us-trade-deal-nearly-done-with-final-comments-sent?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
, but US officials have been more cautious. The American trade deficit with 
Vietnam alone has clocked more than $190 billion so far this year — the biggest 
of any nation.

The surge in imports from Vietnam has stoked concerns that it’s being used as 
a transshipment hub forChinese goods 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-15/futuristic-shenzhen-shows-trump-the-difficulty-of-stopping-xi-jinping-s-china?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
, a key concern for the Trump administration and one that has held upfinalizing 
a deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/us-pushes-vietnam-on-trade-barriers-ip-violations-in-deal-talks?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
. Keep watch for any comments from Trump and his lieutenants on the trip.

The host nation, the Philippines, also has yet to conclude a deal with the US, 
despite being acollective-defense 
<https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/l/treaty/collectivedefense/> level treaty ally. 
(On the defense front, also keep an eye out for any further word on Washington 
hopes for acooperation framework 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/hegseth-says-us-seeking-asean-defense-cooperation-framework?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
 with Asean members.)

Need-to-Know Research

A new Fed board paper offers a current assessment on the impact of AI in the 
US economy. The impact of large-scale capital spending has been clear on both 
GDP and on prices, though AI has yet to show up in scale in terms of effects on 
the job market and on productivity, it finds.

“AI adoption is growing rapidly both among consumers and firms, though most 
firms are currently not using AI very intensively or at all,” a quartet of Fed 
economists wrote in thepaper 
<https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026074pap.pdf>. “Effects on 
the labor market so far appear to have been small and somewhat mixed, but may 
be larger going forward. Similarly, productivity appears to have moved higher 
in recent years but that change mostlypredates the diffusion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-12/why-china-is-eyeing-a-curious-tax-on-its-government-bond-market?cmpid=BBD111626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11431>
 of generative AI.”

The macroeconomic effects of AI could swell if data center investment demand 
were to “outstrip the capacity to supply needed materials and labor for a 
prolonged period of time,” the economists said — pointing to the potential for 
even stronger inflationary effects. If AI starts to boost productivity more 
substantially, however, it could ultimately prove disinflationary, they also 
said. “At this point, there is enormous uncertainty” over how things may evolve.
