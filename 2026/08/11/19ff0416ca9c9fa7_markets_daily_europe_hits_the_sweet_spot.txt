Europe’s ‘sweet spot’

European markets are charging ahead this year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/resilient-europe-turns-into-a-winning-bet-for-money-managers?cmpid=081126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7566>
, defying doom-laden forecasts that the Iran war would tip the region into 
stagflation.

The Stoxx Europe 600 Index has rallied 12%, with benchmarks in Germany, Italy 
and France scaling record highs. German government bonds are outperforming US 
Treasuries, while the euro stands near a two-month high.

What’s behind the performance? The fastest earnings growth in four years, plus 
an economy that is picking up but remains cool enough not to trouble 
rate-setters at the ECB. In particular, some bond fund managers say Europe 
looks appealing against the US given a lack of clarity around Fed policy.

“The economy is in a sweet spot that doesn’t require the ECB to hike rates as 
inflation isn’t out of control, and at the same time growth is strong enough to 
boost stocks,” said Sophie Huynh at BNP Paribas Asset Management. “We’ve added 
some calls on European equities because there’s potential room for further 
upside.” —Sagarika Jaisinghani 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVngXf5Td30/sagarika-jaisinghani?cmpid=081126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7566>

Bessent’s limited firepower

Scott Bessent says the US will do whatever it takes to to support Japan in 
propping up the yen. The problem is, the US Treasury secretarymay not have what 
it takes at his disposal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/bessent-s-whatever-it-takes-yen-pledge-masks-limited-firepower?cmpid=081126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7566>
.

The Treasury’s main instrument for currency intervention — the Exchange 
Stabilization Fund — has holdings of less than $220 billion. As a gauge of 
comparison, Japan on its own spent an estimated $53 billion on yen operations 
July 30, the day before the coordinated move with the US.

It would take a lot of firepower to change the course of the yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/yen-underperforms-g-10-peers-as-intervention-boost-fades?cmpid=081126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7566>
 and overcome the fact that US interest rates are so much higher than in Japan.

“The US can influence the narrative by coordinating with Japan on 
intervention, but can’t rewrite the fundamentals,” said Nathan Thooft, a senior 
portfolio manager at Manulife Investment Management. “The pockets are deep, but 
not limitless.” —Daniel Flatley 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATR8CMu5BXo/daniel-flatley?cmpid=081126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7566>
 andAnya Andrianova
