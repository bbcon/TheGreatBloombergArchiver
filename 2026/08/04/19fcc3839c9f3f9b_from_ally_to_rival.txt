Anwar Ibrahim still has Malaysia’s top job, but a series of local election 
results suggests thatpower is seeping away 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/malaysia-s-anwar-on-defensive-as-ally-s-success-turns-to-rivalry?cmpid=BBD080426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7322>
 from the prime minister to his deputy in the ruling coalition, Ahmad Zahid 
Hamidi.

Zahid’s Barisan Nasional bloc and Anwar’s Pakatan Harapan form the glue that 
binds together a multi-party national government, yet ran against each other in 
back-to-back state votes this year.

Barisan Nasional, which ruled Malaysia for more than 60 years until 2018, 
emerged as the clear winner 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/anwar-s-bloc-loses-control-of-another-malaysian-state-in-setback?cmpid=BBD080426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7322>
, sweeping supermajorities in Johor and Negeri Sembilan state assemblies. 
Anwar’s Pakatan Harapan lost seats in both polls.

The paradox is that, on paper at least, Anwar should be doing well 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-07/anwar-to-cut-small-business-costs-after-poor-poll-bernama-says?cmpid=BBD080426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7322>
.

Malaysia’s recent economic growth has exceeded expectations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/ai-and-chips-are-turning-malaysia-into-asia-s-growth-standout?cmpid=BBD080426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7322>
, the currency has strengthened and inflation remains under control. Foreign 
investments are at a record high driven byAI-related data center 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/ai-and-chips-are-turning-malaysia-into-asia-s-growth-standout?cmpid=BBD080426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7322>
 and semiconductor demand.
Anwar Ibrahim.Photographer: Samsul Said/Bloomberg
But very little of that has translated into benefits for most Malaysians. 
Instead, many feel squeezed by rising costs and a lack of purchasing power, 
exacerbated by a monthly cap on fuel subsidies introduced by Anwar’s government.

Now even his own supporters have turned on him over what they see as promises 
broken.

An anti-corruption crusader, Anwar came to power pledging institutional 
reform, but is seen to havedone little on that front 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-25/anwar-s-broken-promises-spark-protests-demanding-his-ouster?cmpid=BBD080426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7322>
.

His opponents have united against him using identity politics as a wedge. 
Anwar’s Pakatan Harapan is a multiracial coalition whose biggest constituent 
member is the Democratic Action Party, dominated by ethnic Chinese Malaysians.

The two largest parties for Malay-Muslims, who make up two-thirds of the 
population, are now loosely collaborating, even though one of them — Zahid’s 
Barisan Nasional — remains in Anwar’s government. The recent state elections 
showed just how quickly Anwar could be swept aside in favor of a new 
Malay-Muslim alliance.

Anwar is hanging on for now, with no sign a national election is imminent.

But there’s no doubt the momentum is with Zahid — making an awkward 
partnership even more uncomfortable. — Ram Anand
