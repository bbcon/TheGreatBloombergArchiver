Questioning the AI boom

Given the amount of investor cash that has flooded into stock market bets on 
AI, bulls have little room for error: Those wagers have to pay off, and 
relatively quickly, or prices will head lower.

That’s why the talk about a slowdown in AI development 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/anthropic-ceo-says-it-s-time-to-slow-pace-of-improving-ai-models?cmpid=091426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8815>
 is such a threat to the market. It’s adding toexisting concerns over valuation 
and momentum 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/anthropic-s-ai-warning-may-weigh-on-chips-but-trade-seen-intact?cmpid=091426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8815>
, which is a polite way of saying plenty of investors think this is a bubble.

The usual AI bellwethers are taking a hit:

 * Softbank shares slumped 11% in Tokyo. Masayoshi Son’s company is one of 
OpenAI’s biggest backers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/softbank-group-shares-fall-after-openai-ipo-delay-ai-warning?cmpid=091426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8815>
, with its investment in the ChatGPT maker heading toward $65 billion.
 * Makers of computer memory sank, with South Korea’s SK Hynix and Samsung 
down 6.4% and 4.1%, respectively and Kioxia in Japan dropping 6.4%.
 * In Europe, chip-related stocks are among the biggest decliners, with ASML 
falling 4.1% and Soitec in France plunging 12%.
 * In US premarket trading, Nvidia is down 2.5%, while memory stocks Sandisk, 
Western Digital, Seagate and Micron are 4% to 5% lower.
These fears about the safety of AI are landing in a market that’s already 
skittish. Oil prices are jumping anew and investors are ramping up their bets 
on a Fed interest rate hike.

For now, the declines are relatively contained. But they just show how jumpy 
traders are.

Still on 5% watch

With the yield on the 10-year US Treasury note still hovering just below 5%, 
the question is: So what?

There’s nothing inherently significant about that level 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/a-5-treasury-yield-is-raising-new-risks-for-markets-economy?cmpid=091426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8815>
, which the benchmark yield hasn’t closed above since 2007.But round numbers 
are often key pivot points that can catalyze decisions by investors and 
policymakers. 

More broadly, Treasury yields are important because they serve as a baseline 
for other loans. In the stock market, they’re used to gauge the present value 
of profits expected in the years ahead. The more yields rise, the smaller those 
earnings look.

So elevated bond yields could start exerting a drag on stocks, which have been 
hovering near records because of the profits thrown off by the AI boom and the 
resilience in the economy.

“If you saw bond yields move to the 5% or 5.25% level I think that’s where you 
will see some indigestion from the equity market,” said Grace Peters at 
JPMorgan Chase Private Bank. “5% psychologically has an impact.” —Greg Ritchie
