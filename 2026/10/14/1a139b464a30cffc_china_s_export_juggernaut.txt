China’s export juggernaut just keeps going 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-30/china-export-shock-why-it-s-hard-for-economic-rivals-to-fight-back?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10078>
.

The country’s exports soared 36% last month 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-s-trade-surplus-nears-940-billion-as-export-growth-surges?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10078>
 from a year earlier, with its surplus in the first nine months of the year on 
track to exceed the $1.2 trillion record set in 2025. The numbers show how 
China’slopsided trade relations with the world 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-14/what-china-s-extraordinary-trade-surplus-means-video?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10078>
 have grown even more extreme since the tariff war started by President Donald 
Trump over a year ago, especially as global demand exploded for AI services and 
inputs like chips.

And it’s no longer about the insatiable global demand for cheap goods. After 
years of exporting disinflation to the rest of the world, higher input costs 
have led to an abrupt jump in Chinese export prices. Shipments of high-tech 
products soared almost 64% in September, while surging prices for those chips 
and commodities like oil and metals have inflated the trade figures.

As domestic demand stagnates, the booming exports are also generating more 
pushback from China’s largest trade partners. After months of standoff with the 
European Union, Brussels and Beijing have agreed to cap imports of hybrid cars 
from China, according to the bloc, though China describes the deal as ensuring “
export space and steady growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/china-says-hybrid-car-deal-with-europe-to-ensure-export-space?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10078>
” for its carmakers. Bloomberg News reported exclusively today that Germany will
press the EU to quickly place quotas on additional sectors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/germany-to-push-eu-to-impose-caps-on-additional-chinese-imports?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10078>
 of goods being imported from China, replicating the cap on hybrids.

The pressure is on, for everyone.
