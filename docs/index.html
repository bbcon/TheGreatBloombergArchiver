US

Corporate: Nvidia Post-Huang

Nvidia's third-quarter results provided the clearest evidence yet that AI infrastructure demand remains intact despite the leadership crisis triggered by Jensen Huang's departure. Revenue of $115.8 billion and forward guidance of approximately $130 billion for Q1 2027 both exceeded consensus, as did gross margins of ~71.5%. Interim CEO Colette Kress struck a notably more cautious tone than her predecessor, emphasizing "disciplined" and "rigorous" standards for future investments — a direct response to criticism over the circular financing arrangements that characterized the Huang era. Nvidia's board ruled out a Huang return at the earnings conference, though Congressional and Department of Justice inquiries into his alleged ties to Megaspeed International and potential export control violations continue. The stock remains down approximately 24% month-to-date.

Markets and Monetary Policy

The four-day technology selloff paused on Nvidia's results, with Nasdaq 100 futures rising 1.2%. Broader sentiment remains cautious: BofA's fund manager survey found the consensus has shifted toward a soft-landing scenario and away from US equity outperformance, with the weighted-average S&P 500 target for end-2027 at 7,926 — just 8% above current levels. The 10-year Treasury near 5.10% continues to reflect persistent inflation concerns, with Fidelity International arguing that even a 6% yield would not render Treasuries "massively cheap" given structural fiscal pressures and positive equity-bond correlations. ---

APEC and US-China Dynamics

Xi Jinping used the Shenzhen APEC summit to warn against "unilateralism and protectionism," framing China as a defender of open trade as new US semiconductor restrictions loom. Xi greeted President Trump with notable warmth ahead of a formal bilateral Thursday and an unprecedented trilateral summit with Vladimir Putin thereafter. The backdrop is fraught: the year-old US-China trade truce expires in January, and China's Ministry of Commerce has explicitly threatened countermeasures if Washington proceeds with remote-access chip restrictions. Nvidia's decision to cease shipping Hopper data center chips to China — despite having received US approval to sell a modified version — adds further texture to the technology decoupling dynamic.

Currency and Central Banks

Bank Indonesia's surprise hike illustrates the tension facing emerging-market central banks: domestic growth considerations argue for accommodation, but currency pressures — the rupiah is Asia's worst performer this year — are forcing tightening. UBS has issued its most bullish yuan forecast yet, projecting appreciation toward 6.25 per dollar by end-2027, consistent with PBOC signaling via the reference rate fix. ---

Europe

  • UK CPI accelerated to its highest level in a year, fueled by energy prices linked to the ongoing US-Iran conflict, adding complexity to Bank of England policy deliberations
  • Pablo Hernández de Cos, BIS chief, is now firmly on track to succeed Christine Lagarde as ECB president, with the German cabinet expected to formalize backing Wednesday
  • France's cost-of-living protests drew at least 146,000 people, with the 10-year OAT-Bund spread having reached 178 basis points in October — the widest since 2012 — as far-left candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon rises in presidential polls

ECB Leadership

A Franco-German deal has effectively settled the ECB's succession question. Hernández de Cos, regarded as the more dovish of the two leading candidates, will likely take the presidency — a result that appears to reflect a Macron diplomatic win despite Germany's traditional preference for hawkish nominees. Berlin secured the chief economist role for Princeton economist Markus Brunnermeier when Philip Lane departs in May. France is expected to claim the markets portfolio currently held by Isabel Schnabel, with former OECD Chief Economist Laurence Boone the leading candidate. The composition matters: should French sovereign spreads deteriorate further, Boone would likely be in operational control of any bond-market stabilization measures.

France: Political and Fiscal Stress

France's political situation is deteriorating along multiple dimensions simultaneously. Street protests over cost of living, a minority government facing a budget impasse, and rising borrowing costs are converging ahead of next year's presidential election. Mélenchon has risen to 17-18.5% in polls — currently the most probable opponent for Marine Le Pen in a second round — and his platform includes canceling the roughly 20% of French sovereign debt held by the ECB, a measure the ECB considers illegal. Investors are unlikely to treat this as background noise. ---

LatAm

  • Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum met with Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, seeking to repair a tariff dispute while carefully avoiding actions that might aggravate US-Mexico relations
  • US Congressional Democrats are preparing multiple probes into the Trump administration's $100 billion deal with a Venezuelan businessman to control more than a dozen of the country's oil fields
  • Mexico's data center potential remains constrained by an outdated electricity grid and regulatory bottlenecks, limiting its ability to capitalize on nearshoring demand

Mexico: Geopolitical Navigation

Sheinbaum's attendance at APEC — notably flying economy class in a gesture that resonated on Chinese social media — reflects the difficulty of Mexico's position: deepening economic ties with China risks friction with Washington at a moment when the US-Mexico trade relationship is already under stress. Mexico's geographic proximity to the US makes it a natural data center location, but analysts note that infrastructure deficits, particularly in power, are preventing the country from translating that locational advantage into investment.

Venezuela and Regional Political Economy

The Trump administration's sweeping arrangement to transfer operational control of Venezuelan oil assets to a controversial businessman is drawing significant Congressional scrutiny from Democrats, who are preparing formal investigations targeting Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. The deal's opacity and scale — $100 billion — raise governance questions that extend beyond Venezuela to broader US credibility in the hemisphere. The episode also highlights the complex intersection of sanctions policy and energy supply at a moment when Middle East supply disruption is a live concern. ---

Middle East: War in Limbo

The US-Iran conflict has entered an ambiguous phase that presents distinct risks to global energy markets and regional stability. Full-scale escalation appears less likely following Republican losses in the US midterms — the war's unpopularity was a significant factor — but the tit-for-tat exchange of strikes shows no sign of abating. Three US aircraft carriers remain in the region. Oil is increasingly transiting the Strait of Hormuz on smaller vessels as supertanker availability is squeezed, a structural adaptation that adds cost and complexity to global energy logistics. Gulf governments are reported to fear a scenario where the US simply disengages without a durable resolution.

AI and Capital: Global Dimensions

The Nvidia earnings cycle and the Anthropic IPO together crystallize the central tension in global capital allocation: AI infrastructure demand appears structurally robust — Apollo's Marc Rowan estimates $8 trillion of financing needed — but investor confidence in the trade is fragile following the Huang scandal, a 36% decline in the MSCI Global Semiconductors index since June, and fund managers' record rotation away from technology. The AI investment debate is also surfacing at COP31, where the sector's energy footprint is becoming a contested issue, with 90 entities endorsing Turkey's voluntary AI sustainability pledge but fossil fuel transition language remaining unlikely to appear in the final summit text.
