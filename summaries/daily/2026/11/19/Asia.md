• Bank Indonesia delivered a surprise 25bp rate hike to 6.0% — against consensus expectations for a fifth consecutive hold — as the rupiah fell to record lows.
• Bank of Japan Governor Ueda signaled that a weaker Q3 GDP print would not deter a December hike; swap markets price the probability at approximately 84%.
• Asian equities fell for a second consecutive session, with the Hang Seng declining 1.9% and Taiwan's Taiex dropping 4.4%, as chip stocks including TSMC, Samsung, and SK Hynix retreated.

**Central Bank Policy**
Bank Indonesia's surprise hike illustrates the bind facing emerging-market central banks as dollar strength driven by US tightening transmits pressure globally. The rupiah had already plunged to record lows, and despite consensus for a pause, Governor Destry Damayanti's board moved to defend both the currency and institutional credibility. Capital Economics anticipates two further 25bp hikes, though the more immediate question is whether monetary tightening can offset idiosyncratic political risks under President Prabowo Subianto, whose approval ratings have fallen to 45% from 81% in under a year. MSCI's pending review of Indonesia's equity market classification — a potential downgrade to standalone status due next June — adds a structural overhang.

**Japan and South Korea**
In Japan, Governor Ueda explicitly downplayed Q3's GDP contraction, maintaining that rising wages and moderating inflation keep the December hike scenario live. South Korea's financial regulator has for the first time formally asked major banks, brokerages, and insurers to disclose their AI-sector exposures, reflecting growing systemic concern about the debt financing the global buildout. South Korean annual exports surpassed $1 trillion for the first time, driven by record semiconductor shipments.
