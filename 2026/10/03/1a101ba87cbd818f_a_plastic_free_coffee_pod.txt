Beyond the pod

By Olivia Raimonde 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOpjWq8Dk/olivia-raimonde?cmpid=BBD100326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261003&utm_content=9659>

Coffee-pod makers have spent years trying to find a design that doesn’t leave 
a crumpled piece of plastic or aluminum with every brewed cup.

This week, Keurig Dr Pepper Inc., one of the world’s biggest makers of coffee 
pods, launched a new way of brewing coffee it says will slash waste.

Called AltaRounds, the one-cup portions are made from compressed coffee 
wrapped in a coating derived from seaweed that the company sources from France. 
The puck-shaped capsules come eight to a box, and most varieties will cost 
$1.37 each — comparable to one of the company’straditional K-Cup pods 
<https://www.amazon.com/Green-Mountain-K-Cup-Magic-count/dp/B0DKFXTL94/ref=sr_1_13?crid=3JN5B8299CLOM&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.9e0HV8B0wQ8gWPapPlmlD_9qfZktbScKTm6vNFARxg2CxXSWAOXy1MIiD2aL-hS3wqeXDoTGK-mbStZhOdqlqrMHZuFa7xrPydZDUyP43iowBMu_Fmj5exCuBRP30FKDfclvrP-1rQQUKhdRi4cghG1fH_wKDa1yfwxQUQdOVr-HhvIqnFrRjkLhgT7AqgPlJ8M2EVWDnZbKmcJWnMf3cvYbeBEYxEr0V9qWdMbxUBw5_zYnf2e1O2CdvBXsf93_iVx88VXitzj024qcJ0t4w1T3_ExiXkbl03s6WJailhw.pxDy6e1kPrVz_UtWazMMlOX9xzJBUWOXjCNTPelqtAo&dib_tag=se&keywords=green+mountain+smallest+count+keurig+coffee+pods&qid=1790867351&sprefix=green+mountain+smallest+count+keurig+coffee+pods%2Caps%2C163&sr=8-13>
.

“You can dispose of the AltaRounds any way you would normally dispose of your 
coffee grounds,” said Monique Oxender, the company’s chief sustainability 
officer. “I put it on my garden. I put it on my house plants.”
AltaRounds compressed coffee pucks. KeurigSource: Keurig
Some producers have tried recycling coffee pods, though conventional recyclers 
struggle to deal with small pieces of plastic. In 2024, the US Securities and 
Exchange Commissioncharged KDP 
<https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-122> with making inaccurate 
statements about the pods’ recyclability after two of the country’s largest 
recycling companies said they wouldn’t accept the packaging.

The market for single-use coffee packaging is growing. The global coffee pods 
market was valued at $40.5 billion in 2024 andprojected to expand 
<https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/coffee-pods-market-report> 
to $58.2 billion by 2030, according toGrand View Research 
<https://www.grandviewresearch.com/>, a market research and consulting firm.

Other coffee companies have created compostable packaging, but there are 
questions about whether these products can break down entirely and concerns 
about the lack of infrastructure to process them.

The AltaRounds don’t need to be shipped to an industrial composting facility, 
though that’s an option. KDP says they can be composted at home, the same way 
as ordinary coffee grounds, disintegrating within 12-16 weeks and fully 
biodegrading within 6 months.
Keurig Alta coffee maker with AltaRounds, compressed coffee pucks. Keurig
Source: Keurig
Using them will require buying a new coffee machine, and because it can also 
make espresso, it will cost more than those that take traditional pods. KDP 
hasn’t said whether it plans to eventually shed the widely used K-Cup, which 
will still work in the new Alta coffee maker.

“The consumer’s going to lead us on that,” said Oxender. “In order to 
transition a product, you’re going to have to get to scale.”

Less insulation, more defense

By Jess Shankleman 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASoVYP9ojQ0/jess-shankleman?cmpid=BBD100326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261003&utm_content=9659>
 andOlivia Rudgard 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWBnLoYN_tg/olivia-rudgard?cmpid=BBD100326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261003&utm_content=9659>

The UK is considering cuts to its flagship program to upgrade millions of 
homes, as it seeks more than £2 billion ($2.6 billion) in energy-department 
savings to help fund higher defense spending.

The £15 billion Warm Homes Plan is among the programs being examined for cuts, 
according to a person familiar with the discussions.

The plan to improve the energy efficiency of as many as 5 million homes by 
2030 was only announced in January, but all government departments must 
contribute 1% of their capital spending budgets to funddefense investments 
<https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-defence-investment-plan/the-defence-investment-plan-funding-explainer?utm_source=chatgpt.com>
 in response to the Iran war.

The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (Desnz) has been asked to find 
an additional £2 billion of savings by the end of the decade.

Desnz has one of the largest capital budgets, reflecting the huge sums 
committed to Britain’s energy transition. Miatta Fahnbulleh, its new secretary, 
faces the dilemma of having to trim one of the main policies designed to 
achieve her key priority of cutting household bills by reducing energy 
consumption.

The Warm Homes Plan promised to upgrade as many as 5 million homes by 2030, 
with the addition of better insulation, solar panels, batteries and heat pumps. 
Now, the areas most likely to see cuts are those helping higher-income 
households, while those supporting lower-income homes would likely be 
protected, one of the people familiar said.
Photovoltaic solar panels on homes in Lenham, UK.Bloomberg
Carbon capture and storage is also a target for savings, while spending on 
hydrogen is under scrutiny, according to another person familiar, who asked not 
to be identified discussing a confidential matter.

No final decisions have been made, and the eventual package could change as 
officials work through options ahead of the Oct. 28 budget.
