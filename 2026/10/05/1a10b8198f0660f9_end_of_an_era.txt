The power and passion of Brazilian politics was on show on the eve of the 
election as supporters of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva massed on São 
Paulo’s central Paulista Avenue.

Part campaign rally, part hero worship, it was a raucous event spanning 
pensioners, families with children, trade unionists, bare-chested muscle men — 
all sporting the red of the president’s Workers’ Party.

It now feels more like a farewell to a political icon after Flávio Bolsonaro 
unexpectedly emerged from yesterday’s first round asthe overwhelming favorite 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/bolsonaro-lula-set-to-face-off-in-brazil-runoff-datafolha-says?cmpid=BBD100526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9700>
 to win the presidency.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-04/bolsonaro-surges-to-lead-as-brazil-swings-to-the-right-video?cmpid=BBD100526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9700>
WATCH: A victory for Bolsonaro would accelerate President Donald Trump’s effort 
to reassert US primacy over the Americas.
Policies have taken a back seat to vibes in this election, with Lula the 
veteran leftist facing off against the eldest son and political successor of 
the far-right former president, Jair Bolsonaro.

Both candidates suffered high levels of voter rejection, but the numbers are 
now stacked against Lula, his dreams of a record fourth term fading.

With scandal overshadowing the campaign and many Brazilian households 
struggling, the president’s old ability to connect was found wanting.

Voters instead put their faith in Bolsonaro, a MAGA-friendly senator with 
baggage of his own but who at about half Lula’s age seems fresh by comparison.

Brazil now looks to be on the verge of following Latin America in shifting to 
the right 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/bolsonaro-allies-gain-senate-strength-in-clash-with-top-court?cmpid=BBD100526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9700>
.

Lula has held out against Donald Trump’s efforts to extend US control across 
the region. He campaigned on Brazil’s sovereignty and a refusal to be 
Washington’s “backyard.”

There’s an echo of that geopolitical dynamic along Paulista in the Museum of 
Art, a São Paulo landmark, with its exhibition on Latin American identity and 
the colonial experiences at the hands of Europe and the US.

How — and whether — such sentiments play with Brazil’s voters will become 
clear after the Oct. 25 runoff.

But on that and other arguments, time is running out for Lula to make his case.
