A new solar solution

By Nadja Popovich 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXGJc4xBk9o/nadja-popovich?cmpid=BBD081026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7539>
 andMark Chediak 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APj9IOx5Yhk/mark-chediak?cmpid=BBD081026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7539>

For years, Chinese-linked solar producers have stayed one step ahead of US 
trade enforcement.

The country-by-country trade investigations have proved frustrating for 
American manufacturers, long squeezed by China’s dominance of the global solar 
market. Now, far-reaching new trade measuresordered <> by President Donald 
Trump aim to put an end to the game of solar tariff “Whack-a-Mole,” US solar 
manufacturing proponents say.

The order imposes tariffs and price floors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-signed-order-on-tariffs-trade-actions-on-polysilicon?cmpid=BBD081026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7539>
 on imported polysilicon, the foundational material used to make solar panels 
and semiconductors, in an effort to bolster domestic production and reduce 
reliance on foreign supplies. Unlike most previous trade actions, which focused 
on finished solar products from specific countries, the new measures reach 
deeper into the global solar supply chain. Imported polysilicon derivatives, 
including wafers, cells and modules, will be subject to 15% tariffs beginning 
Dec. 4. The measure also sets minimum import prices for each product.

“Every time US solar cell and module producers seek trade relief, the Chinese 
companies shift their unfair trade practices to other countries,” said Tim 
Brightbill, a co-chair of the international trade practice at law firm Wiley 
Rein who has represented domestic solar manufacturers in several major trade 
cases. “We are hopeful that if this Section 232 action is done right, it could 
be an important step toward addressing this problem,” he added, referring to 
the national security solar tariff measure.

The US first imposed steep duties on Chinese solar panels and cells in 2012 
under the Obama administration, accusing the country of dumping unfairly 
subsidized solar products and distorting the market for American manufacturers. 
Chinese companies responded by increasingly shifting solar cell and panel 
production to countries not subject to the tariffs, but factories abroad often 
remained reliant on Chinese inputs and investment.

Since then, the US has investigated a series of countries for helping China 
sidestep tariffs and for dumping solar products of their own. American trade 
officials first targeted Taiwan in 2014, and a decade later hit Vietnam, 
Thailand, Malaysia and Cambodia with duties ofup to 3,521% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-21/us-imposes-new-duties-on-solar-imports-from-southeast-asia?cmpid=BBD081026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7539>
. Production then shifted to Indonesia, Laos and India, which prompted the US 
to impose preliminary duties on imports from those nations earlier this year. 
Successive import surges have opened more countries to scrutiny, including 
Ethiopia and South Korea. Analysts at Wood Mackenzie pointed to the Philippines 
as a potential next target for investigators. Its exports to the US climbed 
rapidly in 2026, at the same time the country wasbecoming more reliant on 
Chinese solar cells <>.
An employee inspects a silicon wafer at the Hanwha QCells solar panel 
manufacturing plant in White, Georgia, on June 8.Photographer: Matt 
Odom/Bloomberg
Trump said in his order that the new polysilicon tariffs are designed to 
replace global safeguard duties on solar cells and panels he put in place in 
2018. Those tariffs, which expired in February, were undercut by exemptions 
that covered large volumes of imports.

China has been “the key driver” behind the successive waves of solar trade 
disputes, said Lauren Piper, a senior analyst who tracks US solar tariffs at 
the Rhodium Group, a nonpartisan research firm. She pointed to the country’s 
dominance at nearly every stage of the global solar supply chain.

In 2024, China produced 86% of the world’s solar panels <> and controlled an 
even larger share of the upstream products needed for panel production, 
including 92% of photovoltaic cells, 97% of wafers and 93% of polysilicon, 
according to data from the China Photovoltaic Industry Association.

As US solar panel imports have declined in recent years, investment in 
domestic manufacturing has increased, with industry analysts largely 
attributing that growth to a combination of tariffs and tax incentives enacted 
under the Biden administration, some of which have since been rolled back by 
Trump and Republicans in Congress.

China’s typhoon threat

By Mary Hui 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXDwzJNHmiM/mary-hui?cmpid=BBD081026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7539>

A typhoon that struck the Chinese coast on Sunday is steadily weakening after 
making landfall, but is set to bring widespread heavy rain through this week 
that could threaten crops.
Officials help evacuate local residents in Hangzhou, Zhejiang province on Aug. 
9.Source: AFP/Getty Images
Typhoon Dolphin has weakened to a tropical storm after making two landfalls 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/china-issues-red-alert-airports-cancel-flights-as-typhoon-nears?cmpid=BBD081026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260810&utm_content=7539>
 in eastern China on Sunday evening,according to 
<https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/QS5X4B6YpG55MZKVyQVENA> the China Meteorological 
Administration. Powerful winds and intense rains had triggered widespread 
transport disruptions, including the closure of Shanghai’s two main airports.

The weather system is continuing to move northwards, posing a “potentially 
significant inland rainfall event” that could damage farmlands, saidJames Caron 
<https://www.linkedin.com/in/james-caron-3956689/>, director of meteorological 
operations for North America and Asia at Atmospheric G2. Heavy rain “at this 
point in the growing season” can be damaging rather than beneficial, he said.

Torrential rains totaling up to 400 millimeters (15.7 inches), thunderstorms 
and strong convective weather are set to menace large swathes of land around 
the eastern Yangtze on Monday and Tuesday, including Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui, 
Hubei and Henan provinces,according to 
<https://www.nmc.cn/publish/agro/ten-week/index.html> the CMA.
