**Energy Markets and the Strait of Hormuz**
The Strait of Hormuz has become the axis of a multi-front energy disruption. Iranian tanker attacks in the first week of October — ten incidents recorded by Thursday — have accelerated even as Saudi Arabia prosecutes a counter-offensive against Houthi forces that seized Bab el-Mandeb in September, adding a second critical chokepoint to the risk map. The market has shown notable adaptability: supertankers are being drawn into the Gulf rather than repelled, with shipowners earning up to $40 million per round trip, and Middle Eastern producers are slashing prices to compete for buyers willing to absorb shipping risk. The G-7's coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels of emergency reserves was broadly characterized by analysts as fulfillment of prior commitments rather than new supply.
