Relentless rise in yields

You might think fixed-income markets are due a bounce, given how much negative 
news is priced in — government bonds globally just turned intheir worst quarter 
since 2024 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/global-bonds-face-worst-quarter-since-2024-on-inflation-fears?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
. But no, yields are marching higher again today.

 * The US 10-year yield climbed to the highest since 2002 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/treasury-10-year-yield-hits-highest-since-2002-on-rate-outlook-mup7ryq2?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
.
 * Long-term borrowing costs hit 6% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/uk-long-term-bond-yield-reaches-6-for-first-time-since-1998?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
 in the UK for the first time in almost three decades.
 * A key measure of French bond risk is at the highest since 2012.
There’s a long list of reasons why bonds are selling off, with $100 oil near 
the top of the list — we run down10 of them here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/ten-reasons-investors-are-driving-government-bond-yields-higher?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
.

But the biggest factor is the economy. Investors typically turn to bonds when 
the growth outlook is grim, but right now, it’s holding up just fine, as shown 
by yesterday’sUS consumer spending data 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/us-consumer-spending-rises-by-most-in-a-year-core-pce-up-0-2?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
. (That’s a big reason that the stock market is holding up so well despite the 
rise in interest rates.)

A strong economy also means there’s unlikely to be much of a drop in yields 
unless the Fed really cranks up rates to cool down inflation.

“There’s clearly some nervousness in the market but let’s not forget that in 
the case of the US, yields are up for a positive reason,” said David Kruk at 
investment manager La Financière de l’Échiquier in Paris. —Phil Serafino 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ABoP3GGeTtg/phil-serafino?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>

Political risk in France

In a year when stock markets around the world have powered higher, France 
stands out. The country’s equities areEurope’s most unloved 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/budget-fight-looms-with-paris-market-looking-frail-taking-stock?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
.

French stocks still haven’t recovered from Emmanuel Macron’s surprise decision 
to call general elections in June 2024, or the political crisis that ensued. 
With Parliament fragmented, successive governments have struggled to get 
France’s budget deficit under control.

Bond investors have taken fright, pushing up yields 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/france-s-10-year-bond-risk-premium-rises-to-120-basis-points?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
 and demanding a bigger premium over German bunds. The government istrying 
again today 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/france-to-lay-out-budget-in-latest-test-of-investor-nerves?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
 on the budget, unveiling a 2027 plan with €54 billion of spending cuts and tax 
hikes.

All the drama has weighed on consumer and business confidence, and thus the 
economy. French stocks now sell at a discount to the broader European market 
rather than the premium they’ve historically commanded.

“A lot of investors dismiss it, but political risk is a thing,” said Kevin 
Thozet of Paris-based fund manager Carmignac. “It clearly weighs on 
investments, consumption.” —Michael Msika 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AP7hp2DanV8/michael-msika?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
 andJulien Ponthus 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWBnmBKci6k/julien-ponthus?cmpid=100126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9543>
