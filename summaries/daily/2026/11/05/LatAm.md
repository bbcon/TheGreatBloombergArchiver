**Brazil: Political Risk as Template**
Brazil's experience from its own 2022 election cycle continues to serve as an instructive case study in how political uncertainty interacts with monetary policy. The post-Lula-victory selloff forced Banco do Brasil to abandon a rate reduction, illustrating the vulnerability of EM central banks to sudden shifts in risk sentiment. The contrast with the relatively sanguine US market reaction to the Democratic sweep — where yields fell and equities rose — highlights how domestic institutional credibility and fiscal trajectory shape the market's interpretation of political outcomes.

**Chile and Regional Monetary Policy**
Chile's rate expectations represent the most significant near-term policy development in the region. Investors are pulling forward their timeline for the first rate increase since 2022, suggesting that inflation dynamics have proven more persistent than the central bank's prior guidance implied. The shift in market pricing is occurring rapidly, indicating a meaningful reassessment of the disinflation trajectory. Elsewhere, Mexico's Banxico appears set to hold, consistent with its cautious posture amid still-elevated core inflation and peso dynamics tied to US policy uncertainty.
