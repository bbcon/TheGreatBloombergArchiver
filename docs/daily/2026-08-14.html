Daily Brief

Friday, August 14, 2026

US

  • US sold $25 billion of 30-year bonds at 5.216%, the highest yield in a quarter century, following the highest 10-year auction cost since 2007.
  • The saving rate fell to 2.7% in June, less than half its level two years ago and near the record low in data back to 1947.
  • July core inflation was subdued, reinforcing bets the Fed holds off on further tightening next month; July retail sales, due Friday, are the next test of spending.
  • OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate topped $40 billion, roughly double its 2025 pace, as it and Anthropic race toward IPOs.
  • Washington imposed tariffs up to 100% on imported drones, escalating a push to cut reliance on foreign suppliers ahead of a planned Xi-Trump meeting.

Deficit Financing and the Consumer

Elevated yields reflect investors demanding greater compensation for a growing fiscal deficit, a headache for Bessent ahead of November's midterms. Gavekal's Tan Kai Xian warned that once the saving rate hits zero, consumers must borrow or sell assets to sustain spending; with delinquencies low and no sign of re-leveraging, his base case is asset sales, pressuring consumption if valuations soften.

Markets and Corporates

Equities paused after closing at a record. Reddit jumped 12% ahead of its August 18 S&P 500 inclusion, while Applied Materials fell 5.3% on a tepid outlook reaction. BofA said Republican midterm gains would extend the rally, and Wells Fargo's Mike Mayo flagged banks as key AI beneficiaries. Thrive Capital's fund grew sevenfold to $3.7 billion on early OpenAI bets.

Asia

  • Malaysia's Q2 GDP was revised up to 6.0% year-on-year, beating the 5.8% consensus, on stronger AI-linked exports and steady domestic demand.
  • China's July CPI rose at its slowest pace in six months, while PPI eased for the first time since the Iran war began in late February.
  • Taiwan is projected to post its first year of double-digit GDP growth since 2010.
  • Indonesia's Prabowo pledged to narrow the budget deficit to 2.4% of GDP in 2027 from 2.85% this year, alongside an intensified anti-corruption drive targeting bureaucrats and state firms.
  • Record rain in Chiba stranded roughly 7,000 travelers at Tokyo's Narita Airport, while carry traders rebuild yen shorts around each round of BOJ intervention.

Growth and Policy

Malaysia's upside surprise and Taiwan's projected double-digit expansion contrast with China, where cooling CPI and easing factory-gate deflation point to a still-fragile recovery; monthly data are expected to show the tech boom failing to offset weak investment and consumption. The Philippine central bank signaled a less aggressive pace of rate hikes as growth slows. Indonesian bonds have steadied after an earlier selloff tied to fiscal concerns, though Prabowo's fresh anti-graft push against state enterprises adds a new source of uncertainty for investors.

Markets and Corporates

Hang Seng fell 1.1%. Unitree's Shanghai IPO was subscribed 5,526 times by retail investors, underscoring enthusiasm for China's humanoid-robotics and AI capital-raising push. China accused New Zealand of acting as a "pawn for anti-China forces" after Wellington alleged Chinese espionage.

Europe

  • UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham faces a cost-of-living dilemma as he weighs rebalancing business taxes toward larger companies ahead of his first budget in October.
  • Swiss economic momentum accelerated to its fastest pace since 2021, even as an unusually hot UK summer risks becoming a drag on growth.
  • Drought and heat are threatening Europe's fall planting season, with UK farmers delaying wheat sowing and the Danube's record-low levels straining Black Sea grain transport.
  • ECB President Christine Lagarde's trip to the WEF in Geneva next week is likely to revive speculation about an early departure, after she said in July she would stay through year-end.
  • Stoxx Europe 600 rose 0.1% to 659.74; HelloFresh fell 6.3% in Frankfurt after a Barclays downgrade, while Maersk gained 5.4% in Copenhagen on a Nordea upgrade.

Growth and Weather Risk

Switzerland's acceleration stands out against a UK economy where persistent heat is emerging as a growth headwind. Parched soil across Europe is delaying autumn sowing decisions, and low water levels on the Danube are straining a critical Black Sea grain corridor — risks compounding an already elevated global food-price backdrop.

UK Politics and Corporates

Nigel Farage retained his seat in a UK by-election, though the win is unlikely to quiet scrutiny of his finances. Burnham and Chancellor John Healey are expected to focus October's budget on cutting business rates for smaller firms. An explosion and fire hit a KNDS ammunition plant near Rome.

LatAm

  • Colombia faces a budget deficit of as much as 8% of GDP this year as earthquake reconstruction costs mount, leaving little fiscal room to respond.
  • Bogotá rejected an offer of earthquake search-and-rescue assistance from Mexico while accepting aid from the US, Israel, El Salvador and Ecuador, raising questions about the politicization of disaster relief.
  • Antofagasta cut its copper production guidance after extreme rain and snow disrupted its flagship Chilean mine, highlighting the operational risk severe weather poses in the world's top copper producer.

Colombia's Fiscal Strain

The earthquake has compounded an already stretched budget position, and the government's selective acceptance of foreign rescue teams has drawn domestic scrutiny even as reconstruction costs — still unquantified — threaten to widen the deficit further.

Chile's Copper Disruption

Antofagasta's output cut adds to a pattern of weather-related supply disruptions this year, a reminder that extreme rainfall and snow, not just labor or ore-grade issues, are becoming a recurring constraint on Chilean copper supply.

Global

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US will unveil "unprecedented" economic-isolation measures against Iran next week, part of a "one-two punch" that includes a continued blockade of Iranian ports.
  • Oil tankers are going dark for longer to move crude through the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, while satellite imagery shows major oil spills near Iran's Qeshm Island and the UAE's Fujairah hub.
  • Berkeley Earth raised the odds that 2026 becomes the hottest year on record to 69%, up from 12% in July, as an unusually powerful El Niño builds; 2027 is expected to set a new record regardless.
  • The US Climate Prediction Center puts the odds of the current El Niño reaching a strength not seen in 76 years at 69% for October-December.
  • Russia and Ukraine escalated attacks on each other's grain and energy infrastructure, including a drone strike that sparked a fire at Russia's Baltic port of Ust-Luga.

Middle East Oil and Sanctions

Houthi forces again targeted Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery, even as a wave of Middle Eastern crude reached American shores, offering some relief to a squeezed market. A sanctioned tanker carrying Russian oil ran aground and is leaking near Oman's Hallaniyat Islands, compounding the environmental toll of five months of war.

Climate and Food Security

El Niño's unusual strength is compounding drought stress on European crops and disrupting Black Sea grain routes already strained by military attacks, adding to a global food-price backdrop at a three-year high.
