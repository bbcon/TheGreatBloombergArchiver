Unwanted Mail

China’s exports have been on a tear, growing at double-digit speed for the 
past six months to hit a record $447 billion. But for at least one type of 
shipment, the numbers are headed in the opposite direction.

Exports of small cheap parcels (think t-shirts or tat from Temu) have been 
falling almost every month this year, and the latest data show a steep decline 
across the board.

In September, parcel shipments to the European Union, Asean, Australia, 
Canada, Japan, South Korea, the UK and Saudi Arabia all slid by more than 10%, 
according to figures released this week by China’s customs department.

The reasons might vary by country, but there’s an underlying theme. Whether 
they cite safety risks or the need to protect local retailers, governments have 
taken action against the trade.

The EU brought in a new tax 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-23/europe-sees-6-billion-reasons-for-new-levy-on-online-shoppers?cmpid=BBD102326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10355>
 on the parcels from July 1. The €3 duty on items that cost less than €150 is 
aimed at slowing the flood of low-priced merchandise from outside the EU, often 
sold by sites like Shein and Temu. Thailand alsoended 
<https://www.bdo.th/en-gb/insights/thailand-tax-and-legal-alert-thailand-implements-new-tax-rules-for-low-value-imported-goods-(eff>
 a duty exemption for low-value imports at the start of this year.

Shipments to the US have also dropped after Washington closed a loophole in 
mid-2025 that allowed small packages to come in duty-free.

But American shoppers still bought more than $1 billion-worth of such products 
from China in September, according to the Chinese data. Even with a higher 
tariff, the appeal of things like fast and cheap fashion from Chinese 
e-commerce apps hasn’t dissipated.
Packages awaiting delivery in New York
Chinese sellers have at least one growth market to make up for some of what 
they’ve lost in the parcel trade. Exports to Russia also hit $1.1 billion last 
month, up more than fivefold from the same month a year earlier.

The likely explanation in that case: Ukrainian strikes this year have targeted 
the fulfillment centers for Wildberries and Ozon, the local competitors of 
Shein and Temu. That’s probably helped create a hole in the market that Chinese 
firms are now filling.

Need-to-Know Research

How do the world’s most powerful central bankers spend their time? Mostly in 
staff meetings, it turns out.

In a new National Bureau of Economic Research working paper 
<https://www.nber.org/papers/w35913?utm_campaign=ntwh&utm_medium=email&utm_source=ntwg13>
, Thomas Drechsel of Johns Hopkins University and Dali Tsakadze of University 
of Maryland analyzed 20,000 daily calendar entries of the Ben Bernanke, Janet 
Yellen and Jerome Powel, as well as their predecessors, seeking insights into 
how the Federal Reserve operates and is led.

All chairs spent about 30% of their time in staff meetings “highlighting the 
crucial role of economists in the Fed’s decision process.” The authors also 
found that media engagements became more frequent during Bernanke’s tenure. 
(They had no early analysis of theKevin Warsh-era 
<https://www.federalreserve.gov/foia/chairman-warsh-calendar.htm>).

Powell held “significantly more” meetings with lawmakers, including gathering 
twice as often with Senators than his predecessors and having more frequent 
contact with Treasury, they found. Ben Bernanke’s calendars provided much less 
detail, while records from earlier chairs such as Paul Volcker were mostly 
photos of handwritten diaries “which makes them costly to analyze.”
