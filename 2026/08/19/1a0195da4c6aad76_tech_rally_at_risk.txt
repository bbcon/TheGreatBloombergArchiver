Surging long-term borrowing costs are turning into the latest risk for the 
rally in AI-related stocks.

A gauge of Asian tech shares tumbled 3.7% on Wednesday, the most in three 
weeks. Losses were particularly heavy in Korea, where the Kospi plunged 5.8% 
and China — with the ChiNext sinking 6.3%. Japan’s Topix fell 3.1%.

Tech firms are facing the prospect of needing to offer higher interest rates 
when issuing debt to fund growing capital expenditure. Alphabet paid just under 
7% to borrow longer-dated funds in its debut Australian dollar bond deal, its
highest ever 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/alphabet-set-to-sell-long-aussie-bond-at-record-near-7-cost?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
 for a note.

A roughly $1 trillion wave of capital spending tied to AI could keep 
fixed-income markets volatile as hyperscalers race to fund infrastructure, 
according to the head of fixed income for Asia at Robeco. AI hyperscalers are “
almost price insensitive 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/funding-1-trillion-ai-capex-may-expand-bond-supply-robeco-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
” to funding costs given the scale of their buildout and that’s helping 
increase supply of longer-dated bonds on top of government debt issuances in 
the US, Japan and elsewhere, Thu Ha Chow told Bloomberg News.

Such a flood of issuance is weighing on 30-year government bonds around the 
world at a time when investors aregrowing worried 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-18/the-bond-market-is-sending-a-warning-with-rising-long-term-debt-costs?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
 about inflation and fiscal deficits. Yields on 30-year US Treasuries rose to 
the highest since 2007 this week, while French borrowing costs climbed to 2008 
levels and their German peers to 2011 levels.

In the wake of the selloff, SK Hynix unveiled plans to buy back 40 trillion 
won ($29 billion) of stock and return more of its profits to shareholders in an 
effortto calm worries 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/sk-hynix-announces-28-6-billion-share-buy-back-on-ai-boom?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
 about the durability of AI spending.

The memory chipmaker said it will buy back as many as 24 million treasury 
shares between Aug. 20 and Nov. 19 and cancel them. It also boosted its 
shareholder return pledge to more than 50% of free cash flow, up from a 
previous target of within 50%. Its US shares rose as much as 7.1% in pre-market 
trading.

The United Arab Emirates said it was cutting all economic ties with Tehran 
after accusing Iran of firing ballistic missiles at its territory, ramping up 
regional tensions.

The Persian Gulf nation, traditionally one of Iran’s main commercial partners, 
said itwould halt trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/uae-cuts-economic-ties-with-iran-after-missiles-target-territory?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
 and financial transactions with Iran until further notice in light of 
“escalations that undermine regional and international peace and security,” the 
UAE said in a statement.

A missile threat alert sounded in Dubai on Tuesday evening, urging residents 
to seek shelter. The Defense Ministry later said two projectiles that targeted 
maritime traffic fell into the sea, with only one reaching UAE waters.

China recovered a rocket booster on land for the first time, a major step 
toward catching up with US companies such as Elon Musk’s SpaceX.

A Zhuque-3 rocket successfully landed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/china-recovers-rocket-on-land-in-push-to-catch-up-with-spacex?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
 on its legs in the northwestern province of Gansu after helping send a 
satellite into orbit on Wednesday, according to the official Xinhua News 
Agency. The rocket took off from Jiuquan, one of China’s main space launch 
centers.

The breakthrough marks a significant milestone for China’s space program and 
the craft’s developer, LandSpace Technology, as reusable rockets can 
substantially lower the cost of launching satellites into space.

India has replaced Indonesia as Asia’s least-preferred stock market in a 
survey of fund managers by Bank of America.

The lack of a clear AI exposure remains the key concern 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/india-stocks-top-indonesia-as-asia-s-least-favored-in-bofa-poll?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7881>
 for Indian equities, with weak growth emerging as the next most important 
risk, according to the survey, which showed 32% of the respondents were net 
underweight on the nation. Lack of reforms and high valuations also emerged as 
reasons for the bearish outlook on Asia’s fourth-largest equity market.

In contrast, sentiment improved for Indonesia, with 27% of the fund managers 
saying they were net underweight on the market, compared with 32% in July.
