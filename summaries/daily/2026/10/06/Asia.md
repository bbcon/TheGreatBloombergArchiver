• Bank of Japan Governor Ueda gave no signal of a back-to-back rate hike in October, reiterating standing policy views and leaving markets positioned against near-term tightening.
• DeepSeek closed in on a $12 billion funding round backed by Tencent and CATL; Moonshot AI reached a $50 billion private valuation ahead of a planned Q1 2027 Hong Kong IPO.
• India's central bank is expected to deliver its first rate hike in nearly four years at Wednesday's meeting, a significant policy inflection for the world's most populous economy.
• Jio Platforms is seeking a roughly $114 billion IPO valuation, below earlier expectations, in what would still rank as India's largest-ever listing.

**Monetary Policy**
Governor Ueda's comments at a public appearance Tuesday offered little to disturb prevailing market consensus that the BOJ will hold rates at its October meeting. With inflation dynamics still uncertain and the yen having weakened materially, the central bank appears content to proceed gradually. The contrast with India is sharp: the Reserve Bank of India is expected Wednesday to raise rates for the first time since 2022, a decision that reflects the inflationary pass-through from record diesel prices and a weak monsoon — Maharashtra, India's wealthiest state, has cut water supplies by at least 10% after the worst monsoon in over a decade.

**China AI Capital Markets**
China's AI funding cycle is accelerating ahead of anticipated public listings. DeepSeek's $12 billion round — anchored by Tencent and CATL — and Moonshot AI's $50 billion valuation reflect sustained investor appetite for exposure to China's AI champions at the private stage. Both companies are targeting early 2027 listings, suggesting a concentrated IPO pipeline in Hong Kong that will test market absorption capacity. Meanwhile, Germany and France are jointly pushing the EU to adopt new tools capable of severing Chinese firms' access to the bloc's $8 trillion goods-and-services market, adding a trade-policy overhang to Chinese technology's international ambitions.

**Corporate and Capital Flows**
In Japan, a bidding contest for TDK's magnetic-heads business between Toshiba and Seagate — valued at potentially several billion dollars — highlights the strategic importance of HDD supply chain control in an AI infrastructure buildout that is driving data storage demand. TDK is the sole independent maker of HDD magnetic-recording heads, making this acquisition consequential for both suitors and their downstream customers, including Western Digital.
