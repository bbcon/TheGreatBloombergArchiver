Will Japan need to step in again to rescue its currency? That’s the question 
traders are asking 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/yen-surrenders-nearly-half-its-gains-from-us-japan-intervention?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7480>
 after the yen gave up nearly half its gains since the dramatic intervention by 
Japan and the US.

The currency traded around 158.40 versus the dollar on Friday afternoon, well 
off the strong point of 155.23 reached on Monday. It had been near a 
four-decade nadir around 164 per dollar last week before the first joint
yen-buying operation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-07/jpy-usd-yen-s-pullback-shows-limits-of-us-japan-intervention?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7480>
 from Japan and the US since 1998.

US and Japanese officials have warned investors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/katayama-to-say-japan-us-acted-together-on-yen-reuters-reports?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7480>
 they’re determined to keep defending the yen if needed. Yet they face powerful 
forces that have been driving the currency down, including the rate 
differential with the US, Japan’s high debt load and geopolitical uncertainty. 
On top of that, the yen has been afavored carry trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/hedge-funds-turn-most-negative-on-the-japanese-yen-since-2007?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7480>
 funding currency, which adds todepreciation pressures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/japan-confirms-intervention-on-three-days-in-spring-to-buoy-yen?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7480>
.

Despite the firepower used to support the yen — the BOJ spent an estimated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/boj-data-point-to-34-billion-japanese-fx-intervention-on-friday?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7480>
 $87 billion on two days last week — the factors weighing on the currency 
haven’t changed. Whether the Bank of Japan can back up its intervention with 
higher rates remains to be seen. Overnight index swaps imply about a 60% chance 
of a rate hike by September.

Intervention “could buy time to have a more credible policy mix or to craft a 
better message to investors,” said Idanna Appio, portfolio manager at First 
Eagle Investments. “But I don’t think on its own, it can be successful.”

All this suggests the fight to shore up the currency isn’t over.

—Richard Frost
