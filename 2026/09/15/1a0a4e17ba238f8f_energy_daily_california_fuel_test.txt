Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, US 
oil reporter Will Kubzansky explores the impact of the global fuel crunch on 
drivers in the Golden State.

Considering how central the automobile is to life in California — think of its 
interstate highways, LA’s urban sprawl, a thriving car culture — it’s ironic 
that the state has the highest fuel prices in the US.

The squeeze on global supplies is now pushing those costs up further.

While US average gasoline prices are above $4 gallon 
<https://gasprices.aaa.com/>, Californian motorists are paying more than $6. 
Diesel is in excess of $8 a gallon, again significantly above whatevery other 
state is shelling out 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-diesel-prices-rise-past-6-a-gallon-for-first-time-ever?cmpid=BBBXT091526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8884>
.

At one station in San Diego, the trucking fuel was listed at $9.99 a gallon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/california-gas-station-with-9-99-diesel-exemplifies-fuel-crisis?cmpid=BBBXT091526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8884>
 on Friday, the highest possible price on most electronic marquees.
A sign displays diesel fuel at $9.99 a gallon at a Shell gas station in San 
Diego last week.Photographer: Bloomberg
That premium is a long-running feature of economic conditions in the state, 
which is cut off from America’s extensive fuel pipeline network. In recent 
years, much of California’s gasoline demand has been met by imports from South 
Korean and Indian refineries, which have now been curtailed by the impact of 
the Iran war.

For months, energy analysts have warned of global demand destruction 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-25/the-hormuz-billion-barrel-oil-shock-is-about-to-crash-demand?cmpid=BBBXT091526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8884>
 owing to high prices, a consequence not only of the disruption to the Strait 
of Hormuz but Ukraine’s attacks on Russian refining capacity. On Friday, the 
International Energy Agency further slashed itsoil consumption forecast 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/iea-warns-oil-demand-may-have-to-fall-further-as-iran-war-drags?cmpid=BBBXT091526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8884>
.

So far, most of the drop-off in demand has occurred outside the US 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/india-s-oil-demand-growth-set-for-pandemic-low-on-war-crunch?cmpid=BBBXT091526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8884>
. Despite its higher-than-average exposure to energy inflation, California’s 
gasoline and diesel purchases haven’t collapsed.

But the state has seen a more gradual decline in fuel consumption in recent 
years due to improved efficiency andadoption of electric cars 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-20/driving-an-electric-car-saves-you-the-most-money-in-these-9-us-states?cmpid=BBBXT091526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8884>
.

California’s gasoline prices are now around 15 cents off the wartime high set 
in May. The cost of diesel — the lifeblood of commercial trucking and 
agriculture (California is the largest farming state by revenue) and much else 
— is setting fresh records every day.

Unless prices stabilize or retreat soon, more motorists in the Golden State 
could be compelled to make a permanent change in behavior.

—Will Kubzansky, Bloomberg News
