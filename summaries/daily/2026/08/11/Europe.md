• The Stoxx Europe 600 has rallied 12% this year, with Germany, Italy and France at record highs, defying earlier forecasts the Iran war would tip the region into stagflation.
• UK retail sales value rose an annual 1.3%, half the pace of a year earlier, with non-food sales falling for the first time since April as the heat wave curbed spending, the British Retail Consortium said.
• Drought has been declared across almost three-quarters of England, with reservoir storage roughly 12% below average, as Europe endures its fifth heat wave of the year.
• Swiss Re warned European wildfires have caused as much as €19.1 billion in damage this summer, with 30C-plus days up 64% versus the 1950s.

**Growth, Inflation and the ECB**
Europe's rally reflects the fastest earnings growth in four years alongside an economy strong enough to support stocks but not hot enough to force the ECB's hand, per BNP Paribas's Sophie Huynh, who called it a "sweet spot." German bunds outperform Treasuries and the euro trades near a two-month high on Fed uncertainty. UK consumers, by contrast, pulled back.

**Heat, Drought and Economic Costs**
Swiss Re's warning points to a broadening economic toll from extreme heat: only about 20% of European households have air conditioning versus 76% in North America, leaving a continent warming nearly twice as fast as the global average poorly equipped. Oxford Economics flagged low Rhine water levels as a threat to German chemical, metals and construction firms, implying higher inflation as gas increasingly sets power prices while hydropower and nuclear output are curtailed.
