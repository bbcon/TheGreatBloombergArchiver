 in financial assets.

Today’s demonstrations are bringing out teachers and some political parties as 
well, joining with high school students across the country calling for more 
educators, smaller class sizes and building renovations as well as a reform of 
the system that allocates university places.

All of that takes money that’s hard to come by as France tries to rein in its 
yawning budget gap 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/how-macron-s-decade-in-power-left-france-europe-s-weakest-link?cmpid=BBD100626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9759>
.

While protests aren’t uncommon in France, with end-of-day violence to boot, 
this episode is proving particularly uncomfortable for the government.

In addition to concerns that injuries to teenagers could swing popular opinion 
sharply against President Emmanuel Macron’s administration, the student 
movement also appears to be inspiring others to take to the streets.

Workers at refineries operated by TotalEnergies have called a strike for Oct. 
8, ports employees are threatening a 24-hour work stoppage on Oct. 30 and five 
French unions called on people to join nationwide strikes on Nov. 5 in support 
of higher pay, public services and more jobs.
Student protests in Pau, southern France, yesterday.Photographer: Quentin 
Top/AFP/Getty Images
As a bonus, investors will need to spend some time assessing the National 
Rally’s so-called counter-budget and outline for where it’s looking toslash 
spending 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/le-pen-aims-to-bring-french-deficit-below-3-in-2032-at-latest?cmpid=BBD100626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9759>
 if leader Marine Le Pen were to win the 2027 presidential election.

It’s not clear where the National Rally can realistically find the savings to 
assuage bondholders’ worries about the country’s future budget path, or address 
students’ demands.

When Cuba suffered its seventh national blackout this year, the guests at La 
Campana in Havana kept partying unaware that the lights had even gone out, 
thanks to the event center’s solar panels and backup batteries. The chronic 
power woes have made life a misery for the island’s 9.4 million people, but the 
pain is being mitigated by one of thefastest solar adoption 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/cuba-energy-crisis-solar-exports-from-us-china-help-counter-blackouts?cmpid=BBD100626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9759>
