Whispers of contagion

Traders are on the alert for signs of contagion in Europe’s bond market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/whispers-of-contagion-risk-are-returning-to-europe-s-bond-market?cmpid=100526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9693>
 after a selloff triggered memories of the region’s debt crisis 15 years ago.

The bonds of euro-area members had largely retreated in lockstep for much of 
the year, but on Thursday, something snapped: Bonds from France — already under 
pressure — tumbled further, along with Italy, Belgium and Greece.

A lot has changed since Europe’s last crisis — for example, France is now the 
weakest link 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/how-macron-s-decade-in-power-left-france-europe-s-weakest-link?cmpid=100526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9693>
. But investors are still worried about the threat of contagion.

That’s when the financial stress in one sovereign bond market infects others, 
generating chaotic price moves of the kind that force central banks to step in 
during severe scenarios. The concern is nowspilling over to currencies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/euro-falls-to-17-month-low-on-region-s-fiscal-political-risks?cmpid=100526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9693>
.

“We are starting to see first signs of contagion,” said Jeff Mueller of Morgan 
Stanley Investment Management. “If the erratic price action observed on Oct. 1 
continues for some time, this may draw some attention from policymakers.” —
Alice Gledhill
