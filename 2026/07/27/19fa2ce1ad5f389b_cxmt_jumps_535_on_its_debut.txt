CXMT surged as much as 535% in its Shanghai trading debut on Monday, 
propelling the memory chipmaker to become China’s largest onshore-listed 
company as investors piled intoone of the country’s biggest AI champions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/china-memory-champion-cxmt-set-to-debut-after-9-8-billion-ipo?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7010>
.

The blockbuster debut 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-07-27/chinese-chip-giant-cxmt-debuts-after-9-8-billion-ipo-video?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7010>
 follows an up to 66.6 billion yuan fundraising in the second-largest IPO in 
China’s history, underscoring investors’ voracious appetite for a company 
viewed as central to Beijing’s ambitions to build a self-sufficient 
semiconductor industry.

Formerly known as ChangXin Memory Technologies, CXMT has emerged as China’s 
leading memory chipmaker 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-07-27/china-chipmaker-cxmt-jumps-in-shanghai-debut-video?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7010>
 and the country’s best hope of challenging foreign suppliers in dynamic 
random-access memory, or DRAM — a critical component in everything from 
smartphones to AI servers. The company is the world’s fourth-largest DRAM 
producer, making it a rare pure-play way for investors to bet on China’s role 
in the global AI infrastructure buildout.

To be sure, semiconductor shares have been volatile in recent weeks as some 
investors grew concerned that the mega-listing would signal a peak in the 
AI-driven rally. But for now, thewind seems to be in its sails 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/cxmt-can-rally-more-than-1-200-on-market-share-win-nomura-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7010>
.
