AI Prop

China’s domestic economy remains firmly stuck in the slow lane as the ongoing 
property slump saps consumer spending and a drip feed of stimulus does little 
to revive investment.

Even still, the nation’s economic growth target for this year can’t be written 
off just yet as the artificial intelligence boom fuels surging exports. That 
was on display in data Tuesday showing August industrial output was arare 
bright spot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/china-s-consumption-and-investment-wilt-as-factories-power-ahead?cmpid=BBD091526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8905>
.

Against that backdrop, Anthropic and OpenAI’s calls to tap the brakes on AI — 
discussed inMonday’s newsletter 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-14/bessent-touts-american-ai-superiority-but-has-beefs-with-its-champs?cmpid=BBD091526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8905>
 — are not just a threat to China’s tech ambitions. They’re also a threat to 
it’s macro-economic outlook.

Any industry-wide slowdown “would severely dampen” China’s AI-driven exports, 
Bloomberg Economics’s China economistsChang Shu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATwU2T7qXnU/chang-shu?cmpid=BBD091526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8905>

Need-to-Know Research

The global economy in the next 25 years will see the ranks of middle- and 
upper-income consumers jump by some 1.2 billion people, with India leading the 
charge, according to HSBC economists Bethan Ellis and James Pomeroy.

Other takeaways from the duo in a note this month include that by 2050 Asia 
will have a majority of the world’s upper-income ranks. That criterion is 
defined as $110 of income per day, using purchasing power parity exchange 
rates, which account for different price levels of products across countries.

Consumer spending is seen rising roughly 2.4% a year, after inflation, with 
recreation as the biggest driver. Meantime, while working-age populations are 
set to fall in Europe, North America and Oceania (which includes Australia and 
New Zealand), “the movement of consumers up the income distribution means their 
upper-income population is nonetheless set to rise 75%,” HSBC said.
