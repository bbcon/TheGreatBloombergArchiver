Getting ready for Anthropic

Investors have spend the past few weeks worrying about AI. The prospect of 
tough regulation and moratoriums on new data centers with Democrats in charge 
of Congress, plus concerns over circular financing deals and billions in new 
debt have put a big dent in stock prices as of late.

And now, here comes the Anthropic IPO 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/anthropic-targets-64-4-billion-ipo-nearing-spacex-s-record?cmpid=111126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11279>
. The Claude chatbot maker and its co-founders are looking to raise as much as 
$90.4 billion in the IPO and a private placement, at a market value of $1.4 
trillion. The valuation could be hiked further, according to the FT.

The growing frenzy reflects the transformative power that many believe AI will 
have on a wide range of professional activity. The IPO is likely to be the 
biggest listing of all time, when the private placement is factored in.

“Anthropic really represents the first AI pure-play business in the public 
markets,” said Matt Witheiler of Wellington Management. “You have AI enablers 
but you don’t have a native AI company until now.”

Key selling points:

 * Surging sales: Third quarter revenue was nearly $17 billion as of Sept. 30, 
versus $1.4 billion in the same period a year ago.
 * New models: Anthropic unveiled new versions of its powerful Mythos and 
Fable models on Monday, both of which are faster than prior versions. 
Some investors remain skeptical 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/anthropic-ipo-lockup-risks-triggering-ai-correction-selby-says?cmpid=111126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11279>
. Jack Selby, who runs the venture capital firm Copper Sky and is a managing 
partner in Peter Thiel’s family office, warned that the deal may become the 
catalyst for a major reversal in the AI trade.

Selby is a longtime critic. More than a year ago, he called 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-16/ai-euphoria-could-be-biggest-bubble-yet-tech-veteran-selby-says?cmpid=111126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11279>
 the euphoria surrounding the industry the biggest bubble yet in private 
technology investing.

“An underperforming Anthropic IPO has to be a very prominent player in what 
will be a pretty massive correction in the market with respect to AI,” Selby 
said in an interview on Bloomberg Television.

Selby challenged the idea that demand for AI is infinite, noting that DeepSeek 
and other Chinese models are roughly six months behind. These models are much 
cheaper to run than those produced by frontier labs like Anthropic.

“If they’re six months or nine months behind what Anthropic does, what does 
Anthropic do that is so proprietary that it is better or has to be a national 
security interest versus these Chinese models?” he said. —Bailey Lipschultz

Sticky inflation

Traders who joined the rush into bonds just got smacked 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/traders-who-rushed-to-safety-of-bonds-get-rattled-in-reversal?cmpid=111126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11279>
.

Fresh evidence of sticky inflation and hawkish comments from Fed officials 
combined on Tuesday to increase the odds of rate hikes. That sent Treasuries 
into a tailspin. Two-year US yields soared by the most in 19 months and briefly 
pushed 10-year yields back over 5%.

The price data was bad enough that investors see a December rate hike as a 
done deal. Fed Governor Christopher Waller, speaking in London before the CPI 
report, warned that officials may need to raise interest rates by a half 
percentage point next month if there’s no progress on inflation.

It’s a reminder to investors to keep a close eye on the Fed. —Edward
