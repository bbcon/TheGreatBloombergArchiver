The dollar and the data

One spillover effect from the Fed’s attempt to tame inflation and the 
resulting surge in bond yields:The dollar is having a great run 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/dollar-powers-to-best-month-since-june-on-fed-s-inflation-fight?cmpid=093026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260930&utm_content=9477>
.

The Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index’s 1.9% gain this month is its biggest since 
June. The war in Iran has kept energy prices elevated and pushed Treasury 
yields up, with the 30-year reaching the highest level since 2002.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh ignited a fresh move higher in yields when he vowed in 
August to bring inflation down. Those yields are starting to look appealing to 
some investors, pulling money into US assets.

Traders are now watching the September jobs report set for Friday, which will 
test aggressive monetary tightening bets.Today, the US releases the personal 
consumption expenditures price index, the Fed’s preferred measure of inflation.

“US data surprises are the directional signal for the dollar in the near 
term,” said Jayati Bharadwaj at TD Securities.—Anya Andrianova 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARyaBUHvMI8/anya-andrianova?cmpid=093026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260930&utm_content=9477>
