Climate financing imperative

By Eric Roston 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQdgGkZhizQ/eric-roston?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>

With war roiling energy markets and the decades-old US-led geopolitical 
alignment in tatters, leaders gathered for theBloomberg Green summit 
<https://events.bloomberglive.com/event/bloomberg-green-new-york-2026/agenda> 
during Climate Week NYC to discuss how to dispatch more money to accelerate the 
transition to a clean-energy economy.

“The climate targets will not be won in negotiation rooms, they will be won in 
factories, on farms, on construction sites,” Rifat Hisarcıklıoğlu, chair of the 
COP31 Business Forum, said at Tuesday’s COP31 Global Business Forum – New York 
Dialogue. The event was hosted by Bloomberg LP, the parent company of Bloomberg 
News, at the company’s headquarters, as part of the summit.

New York’s climate week occurs a month after catastrophic flooding in Nepal 
killed at least 1,300 people — and in the run-up to this year’s UN climate 
negotiations, in Antalya, Turkey, in November.

Speakers at the Bloomberg Green summit discussed the most pressing issues 
facing governments and businesses as extreme weather events put climate change 
into focus. Here are our journalists’ top takeaways:

Electrification goal is tough, but may be within reach

Murat Kurum, Turkey’s environment minister and president of the upcoming UN 
climate talks, said that the tragedy in Nepal should focus minds onhow to 
supercharge 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/turkey-s-environment-minister-lays-out-agenda-for-cop31-summit?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>
 the deployment of climate and clean-energy solutions.

“We believe we will be safer if we increase military spending, but the 
greatest threat is at our doorstep, which is climate change,” Kurum said. 
Australia will lead negotiations during the Antalya talks, which Kurum said 
will focus on six areas, from municipal waste reduction to green 
industrialization.
Murat KurumPhotographer: Michael Nagle/Bloomberg
The top-line goal of the Antalya talks — and consequently much of Climate Week 
NYC — is to implore countries to make electricity meet 35% of their final 
energy consumption by 2035. The International Energy Agencypublished 
<https://www.iea.org/news/electrification-takes-centre-stage-as-hormuz-shock-spurs-countries-to-seek-to-enhance-energy-security>
 an analysis Tuesday showing that the combination of available technologies and 
energy prices could put the world “within striking distance” of that goal. The 
report was undertaken at the request of Turkey and Australia.

Institutional and financial barriers are diverse and imposing 

Small- and medium-sized businesses make up 90% of most economies, said John 
W.H. Denton, secretary general of the International Chamber of Commerce. If 
these firms can’t find ways to finance their own paths forward, “we will not 
deliver the transition,” he said.

The highways of international finance need to accommodate more traffic. Much 
of the new energy investment is from advanced economies to peers, with 
higher-risk emerging countries unable to attract finance. That includes a 
lengthy list of infrastructure projects that haven’t risen sufficiently in 
attention yet, like power transmission, said Makhtar Diop, managing director of 
the International Finance Corporation.
Ajay Banga.Photographer: Michael Nagle/Bloomberg
The leading economies are currently engaged in a buildout of artificial 
intelligence data center infrastructure that the rest of the world is unlikely 
to share, said Ajay Banga, World Bank Group president.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/microsoft-google-confront-data-center-fears-as-backlash-grows?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>

The electricity and computing requirements needed for AI are outrunning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/world-bank-s-banga-says-data-centers-aren-t-for-emerging-markets?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>
 some developed economies, “let alone these developing countries,” Banga said. 
“It’s not coming, trust me,” he said.

China’s climate leadership role is uncertain 

Strain on the global diplomatic climate process itself is also pushing 
attention to the private sector’s capacity to deploy finance and build 
infrastructure. President Donald Trump officially pulled the US out of the 
Paris Agreement, for the second time, earlier this year. That’s created a 
leadership vacuum that China may begin to fill. The clean-energy giantis 
considering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/china-said-to-weigh-potential-bid-to-host-2028-un-climate-summit?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>
 making a bid to host the UN climate talks in 2028.

China has deployed more renewable energy since 2019 than the rest of the world 
put together. Its rise as the world’s clean-energy goliath puts itin a new spot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/kerry-says-us-is-ceding-in-clean-tech-race-with-china?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>
, according to former US Secretary of State John Kerry.
John Kerry.Photographer: Michael Nagle/Bloomberg
“China’s kind of feeling their way, learning how to maneuver on that 
international stage as a leader of these things,” Kerry said. “We don’t know 
yet whether they really will be able to do it.

In the US, domestic debates about the role of business have wound back half a 
century. White House rollbacks of laws and regulations that have saved lives 
since the early 1970s are freeing up companies to avoid participation in the 
clean energy economy.
Christine Todd WhitmanPhotographer: Michael Nagle/Bloomberg
The US Environmental Protection Agency, for example, recently stopped 
assessing the economic value of health benefits from pollution rules. Former 
EPA Administrator Christine Todd Whitmancondemned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/former-epa-chief-whitman-condemns-agency-s-recent-climate-rollbacks?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>
 the move.

 “When you have the administrator saying, ‘Well, actually, human health isn’t 
going to factor too much anymore, and when we’re doing regulations it’s going 
to be the impact on the industry [that is a bigger factor]’ — that’s just 
wrong, because that’s the job of the Department of Commerce,” she said. “Leave 
it to them to worry about that bottom line.”

Phasing out fossil fuels

By Laura Millan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASzdyi2b6bE/laura-millan?cmpid=BBD092426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9276>

Germany has provided a roadmap setting out its transition away from fossil 
fuels by 2045, as Europe’s largest economy seeks to reduce its vulnerability to 
global energy-market shocks.

The government of Chancellor Friedrich Merz unveiled the details  
<https://www.bundesumweltministerium.de/fileadmin/Daten_BMU/Download_PDF/Klimaschutz/taff_roadmap_en_bf.pdf>
on Wednesday to coincide with the annual United Nations General Assembly and 
climate week in New York. The framework, which largely consolidates existing 
policies, includes strategies to tap into low-carbon technologies to show how 
Germany can achieve its 2045 goal.

“This will enable Germany to significantly reduce imports of fossil fuels and 
lower its dependence on supply chains vulnerable to geopolitical crisis,” 
Carsten Schneider, Germany’s environment minister, said at a UN event onclimate 
action <https://webtv.un.org/en/asset/k1z/k1zg4qsbxi> in New York.

Germany is now the third major European economy, after France and the 
Netherlands, to map out its transition away from fossil fuels. The fallout of 
the Iran war on oil and gas prices is piling pressure on countries to move 
toward alternative energy sources, a narrative that was underscored by world 
leaders gathering at the UN’s headquarters this week.

Speaking at his last UNGA before he steps down at the end of the year, UN 
Secretary General António Guterres said that he believes a “future beyond 
fossil fuels is in sight, the question is how.”
António GuterresPhotographer: John Lamparski/Bloomberg
The remarks by Guterres included a “call on every government to adopt a 
national plan to transition away from fossil fuels aligned with 1.5C, with 
clear timelines and protection for affected workers and communities.”
