**Drought and Industrial Impact**

Europe's inland waterway crisis represents a compounding supply shock for an industrial base already contending with elevated energy costs and trade disruptions. Bloomberg Economics estimates that one month of severely restricted Rhine navigation reduces German industrial production by up to 1% and adds inflationary pressure through higher freight costs. The Rhine corridor connects Rotterdam — Europe's busiest seaport — with the Rhine-Ruhr steel belt, Frankfurt's financial hub, and the Ludwigshafen chemical complex. BASF and other large users have invested in low-draft barges and inventory buffers since prior droughts, which may moderate the near-term impact, though a prolonged dry spell could still cause significant damage.

**Romania's Overlapping Crises**

Romania's situation is the most acute: historic drought, double-digit inflation, a stagnant economy, and a months-long government formation impasse are converging simultaneously. The country's nuclear plant, which normally supplies 20% of national electricity, required unprecedented military intervention this week to maintain cooling water flows. The political vacuum strengthens the far right, which already leads in polls. As a NATO member hosting US logistical assets tied to the Iran conflict, Romania's instability carries implications beyond its borders.
