Thursday, October 15, 2026

US

  • September CPI printed 3.7% YoY, above the 3.5% consensus, marking the fastest US inflation in five months and reigniting rate hike fears.
  • Fed funds futures briefly priced a 50/50 chance of an October hike before subsiding; a December move is now the base case for most traders.
  • The Nasdaq 100 fell 2.0% Wednesday — its worst session since July — with losses deepening in overnight futures; the Russell 2000 entered correction territory.
  • Bank of America shares shed $22 billion in market capitalization after reporting a $1.2 billion fine for money laundering and sanctions violations.

Inflation Dynamics

September CPI rose 3.7% YoY, exceeding the 3.5% consensus and marking the fastest pace in five months. The beat was broad-based: services inflation remains the dominant contributor, but notably the Cleveland Fed's trimmed mean, Atlanta Fed's sticky-price CPI, and Powell-era "supercore" (services ex-shelter) all moved higher, with supercore back above 3%. College tuition also re-accelerated after years of below-inflation increases. Wage growth for the lowest-paid quartile is now trailing headline inflation, adding a political dimension to an already uncomfortable data print. The Fed's Beige Book, released Wednesday afternoon, echoed these pressures, with regional contacts citing intensifying input cost increases.

Monetary Policy

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is scheduled to speak Friday at the IMF meetings in Bangkok — effectively the final pre-blackout opportunity to signal the October 27–28 FOMC outcome. Markets are closely watching, as Warsh surprised with hawkish rhetoric at Jackson Hole. Most traders still expect the Fed to hold in October and consider a hike in December, but the inflation surprise has narrowed that conviction. The bond market itself may be doing some of the Fed's work: after spiking to 5.33%, the 10-year Treasury yield retreated sharply to 5.08%, suggesting buyers emerged at elevated yields and reducing the urgency for immediate Fed action.

Markets

CoreWeave's 2032 bonds fell to 78 cents on the dollar — from par four months ago — as investors questioned whether AI infrastructure borrowing costs can be covered by returns on invested capital. Shares fell as much as 17%. ---

Asia

  • TSMC reported a better-than-expected revenue outlook for Q3, sustaining AI-driven growth, but failed to allay broader concerns about sector sustainability.

  • The IMF raised its 2026 Asia-Pacific growth forecast to 4.8% but warned of deceleration to 4.4% in 2027, identifying AI investment as both the region's current cushion and its prospective vulnerability.

Technology Selloff

The AI-driven equity correction that began on Wall Street hit Asia with particular force, given the region's concentration in semiconductor supply chains. Korea's Kospi bore the brunt, tumbling 8.6% as the Nasdaq's 2% decline combined with distress signals from CoreWeave's debt to prompt a broad reassessment of AI-infrastructure investment returns. Japan's Nikkei 225 slid approximately 5%. The episode illustrates the asymmetric exposure of Northeast Asian markets to a US-centric AI trade: they absorbed capital inflows during the boom and are now serving as the primary shock absorber in the correction.

China Export Surge

Chinese exports rose 36% YoY in September, putting the country on pace to surpass last year's record $1.2 trillion trade surplus. The divergence between surging export momentum and deteriorating domestic confidence — the CSI 300 is down nearly 20% since June — remains pronounced. Beijing's goods trade surplus with the EU widened to $34.8 billion in September, up over 20% YoY, intensifying pressure on Brussels to extend import restrictions beyond the recently concluded hybrid-vehicle agreement. ByteDance is separately pursuing MediaTek for bespoke data-center chips, having already secured an arrangement with Qualcomm, as US export controls force Chinese AI developers to diversify their compute supply chains. ---

Europe

  • Germany more than doubled its 2026 growth forecast, offering tentative signs of economic stabilization, though the improvement appears insufficient to meaningfully alter Chancellor Friedrich Merz's political standing.
  • ECB officials signaled a potentially less active policy path: Primoz Dolenc noted that market tightening may reduce the need for further ECB action, while Klaas Knot, a frontrunner to succeed Lagarde, indicated a mildly hawkish stance may be warranted.
  • EU leaders convened a two-day Brussels summit with China trade relations and energy competitiveness as the primary agenda items.

Germany and ECB Policy

Germany's government more than doubled its 2026 growth forecast, and individual corporate indicators — such as construction firm Habau Deutschland — point to cautious optimism. However, with Merz's coalition fractious, approval ratings low, and elections approaching, the question is whether the economic upturn arrives early enough to shift the political narrative. On monetary policy, ECB commentary remained split: Dolenc suggested that market-driven financial tightening could substitute for further rate action, while Knot's hawkish lean and IMF Managing Director Georgieva's call for a "prudently hawkish bias" among central banks suggest the policy debate remains genuinely open.

EU-China Trade

The Brussels summit placed EU-China trade tensions at the center of European policy deliberations. Following the recent agreement capping Chinese hybrid vehicle exports, the EU is evaluating whether to extend similar restrictions to additional sectors. Beijing characterized the hybrid concession as a one-time occurrence and issued warnings via state media against further measures. Germany and France have been vocal on both sides of the debate, reflecting the difficulty of forging a unified EU position when member states' trade exposures to China vary considerably. ---

  • Flávio Bolsonaro's first-round lead has raised expectations of Petrobras asset sales, including potential divestiture of refineries, aging oil fields, and possibly the company's controlling stake in Braskem.
  • Chile's President Kast is set to meet Bolivia's President Paz in La Paz, a rare diplomatic engagement reflecting South America's shifting political alignment.

Brazil Election and Energy Policy

The October 25 presidential runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro carries meaningful implications for Brazil's energy sector. A Bolsonaro administration would likely revive the asset-divestiture agenda pursued under his father, with Petrobras's Bahia cluster, the Urucu field, and its Braskem stake among probable candidates for sale. Braskem is already in creditor negotiations over deep indebtedness, and the question of whether Petrobras injects capital or exits the position entirely may be resolved by the election outcome. Independent producers PRIO and PetroReconcavo are viewed as natural buyers of divested oil assets.

Amazon Infrastructure and Climate

The ongoing paving of BR-319, a 550-mile highway through the Amazon connecting Amazonas state to southern Brazil, illustrates the tension at the center of Lula's reelection campaign: balancing development in one of Brazil's poorest regions against deforestation commitments that have been a signature achievement of his administration. Native forest cover in the Brazilian Amazon has shrunk by 208,000 square miles over 40 years, virtually all along major road corridors. Bolsonaro has signaled support for additional Amazon highway construction, while Lula's backing of BR-319 despite environmental objections reflects the political constraints of governing a deeply unequal country. ---

Strait of Hormuz and Energy Markets

The sinking of a vessel in the Strait of Hormuz following a strike by unidentified projectiles marks a further escalation in the disruption to one of the world's most important energy transit corridors. The BIS released research Thursday warning that chokepoint disruptions transmit more broadly through production networks than ordinary supply shocks, and specifically cited the Hormuz situation: oil inventories have been drawn down substantially during the disruption, leaving the market increasingly vulnerable to sharper price increases should transit remain constrained. Brent's retreat on the day reflects the dominant influence of the broader risk-off tone rather than a reassessment of supply fundamentals.

Global Monetary Policy Divergence

The IMF meetings in Bangkok crystallized the divergence in global monetary conditions. The Fund's Georgieva called for a "prudently hawkish bias" from central banks and praised Warsh's willingness to act on price stability. Yet the ECB faces a different calculus, with market tightening potentially substituting for further rate increases, while Australia's rising unemployment has already prompted traders to scale back hike expectations. The BIS research adds analytical weight to the view that supply-side inflation driven by geopolitical chokepoints complicates standard policy prescriptions, requiring central banks to assess the persistence and substitutability of specific disruptions before responding.
