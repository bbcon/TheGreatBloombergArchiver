Bear market echos

Gut-wrenching. Stomach-churning. Head-spinning. However you want to describe 
the recent stock market moves,volatility has been extreme 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/stomach-churning-swings-rock-wall-street-as-ai-doubts-creep-in?cmpid=112626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11904>
.

Wednesday was a case study: Just 15 minutes in, the Nasdaq 100 Index was down 
nearly 5%. The euphoria unleashed Monday by Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia had 
vanished without a trace. Yet losses gradually faded over the afternoon, 
leaving the Nasdaq down just 0.6% at the close.

Jonathan Corpina, who has been on the trading floor of the Big Board for three 
decades, said the turbulence has faint echoes of the 2008 financial crisis.

“The feeling every day was what’s next?,” said Corpina of Meridian Equity 
Partners. “All of this now feels similar to that — not the magnitude of the 
selloff and what it did to the economy, but the constant worries.”

There’s no doubt about what’s stoking the swings.

“The market is no longer debating whether AI will change the world. It’s 
debating who will make money, how much they will make, and whether the 
extraordinary amount of capital being deployed will earn an adequate return. 
Those are very different questions,” said Nadine Terman of Solstein Capital.

Markets are quiet today as Americans scarf down turkey and argue with their 
relatives. And that makes it a good time to consider just how much has changed 
in the AI debate, and how quickly.

Just over three weeks ago, US voters delivered a shock rebuke to their 
AI-loving president and pushed back against the construction of data centers. 
Two weeks ago, Jensen Huang was being ousted from his post at Nvidia.

Given that, perhaps volatility is to be expected. —Charles Riley

A house divided

Speaking of arguments, this one lands just in time for Thanksgiving:

Two equity analysts at a single Wall Street research firm have taken the 
unusual step ofposting conflicting views 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/anthropic-s-stock-sparks-rare-divide-inside-one-wall-street-firm?cmpid=112626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11904>
 on the outlook for Anthropic shares.

On one side, Andrew Freedman, a communications and software analyst at Hedgeye 
Risk Management, has put out reports since the company’s IPO and said in an 
interview that the stock is trading at levels that should be attractive for 
long-term investors. Meanwhile, his colleague Felix Wang, a global tech 
analyst, sent a note to clients recommending they short the stock.

After Wang published his view, Freedman distanced himself from it, saying he 
was “not involved in the research or process that led to that decision.”

The opposing views within one firm illustrate the broader rifts in the market 
about how to value AI — and one of the key companies behind the technology. —
Henry Ren 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/anthropic-s-stock-sparks-rare-divide-inside-one-wall-street-firm?cmpid=112626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11904>
