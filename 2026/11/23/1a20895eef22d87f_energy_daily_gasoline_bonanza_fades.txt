Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Will Kubzansky looks at US refiners’ remarkable year.

There’s never been a better time than 2026 to be in the business of making 
gasoline, diesel and jet fuel. But after soaring to record profitability on the 
Iran war, US oil refiners are finally starting to come down to earth.

That’s not because hostilities in the Middle East are easing — Iranian attacks 
on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz have actually intensified in recent weeks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/iran-intensifies-hormuz-tanker-attacks-just-as-oil-prices-slide?cmpid=BBBXT112326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11728>
. The severe disruption to oil product shipments from the Persian Gulf caused 
by the conflict is in itsninth month 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/iran-war-has-permanently-redrawn-global-energy-markets-iea-says?cmpid=BBBXT112326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11728>
. Meanwhile, curbs on fuel cargoes fromRussia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-31/russia-extends-ban-on-most-diesel-exports-through-january?cmpid=BBBXT112326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11728>
 and China are ongoing.

What has turned the tide for US refiners is their own supply response. It’s a 
reminder of the old adage in the commodities business — the cure for high 
prices is high prices.

The sector has let rip this year, operating close to maximum capacity. US 
gasoline supplies are now above normal levels for this time of year, albeit at 
a point in the calendar when stockpiles are typically at their lowest and 
demand is also seasonally weaker.

That’s helped push gasoline prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-11-19/gas-prices-are-falling-as-a-side-effect-of-the-diesel-shortage?cmpid=BBBXT112326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11728>
 into big declines in recent weeks. Accounting for real-world crude prices and 
renewable fuel credits, the so-called crack spread — or profit margin — for 
making gasoline is now barely positive, and is even negative in some parts of 
the country.

Refiners are no doubt thankful, then, that US diesel margins are still 
elevated at over $80 a barrel as of Friday, even if that measure is at its 
lowest level since mid-July.

There’s scope for crack spreads to widen again. One catalyst could be Russia, 
which mayfall short 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/russian-diesel-exports-drop-as-ukraine-renews-refinery-attacks?cmpid=BBBXT112326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11728>
 of its ambitious diesel export plan as Ukrainian drone strikes on its 
refineries continue apace.

Even with the recent gasoline price weakness, refiners have still had a 
stellar run, outpacing other industry segments in the S&P 500 such as tech and 
healthcare. Marathon Petroleum Corp. and Valero Energy Corp., two of the 
biggest US operators, have seen their stock prices double this year.

It’s a remarkable turnaround for a sector that was previously unloved by 
investors.

—Will Kubzansky, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Corporations announced 27 clean energy power purchase agreements totaling 4.2 
gigawatts in October, led by Google’s 3.6 gigawatt nuclear deals, according to 
BloombergNEF. Year-to-date volumes reached 40.8 gigawatts, just ahead of the 
record 39.8 gigawatts at the same point in 2025. Hyperscalers and data centers 
continue to drive dealmaking, especially in the US.
