• Brazil's MSCI ETF fell approximately 18% on Monday following Lula's presidential election victory, the largest single-day decline since Brazil's 1999 currency devaluation.
• The Brazilian real suffered its steepest intraday selloff since abandoning its currency peg in 1999, before partially recovering.
• Flávio Bolsonaro abandoned plans to contest the election result; his Liberal Party formally conceded to Lula.
• Venezuela's opposition leader María Corina Machado returned from exile, complicating Washington's working relationship with acting President Delcy Rodríguez.

**Brazil: Selloff and Opportunity**
The market reaction to Lula's victory was disproportionate to the policy risk it represents. His party lost ground in the legislature, with Bolsonaro's Liberals holding over a third of Senate seats, constraining Lula's fiscal latitude. Historical patterns in Latin American markets suggest post-election selloffs following perceived left-wing victories have consistently created buying opportunities, with markets repricing once policy reality proves less damaging than feared. Local-currency bonds yielding approximately 12.8% and the real have attracted interest from contrarian investors. The episode nonetheless reflects the continued dominance of foreign passive flows in driving LatAm equity volatility.

**Venezuela: Transition Complexity**
Machado's return introduces a meaningful complication to the US-Venezuela arrangement that had been quietly functioning under acting President Rodríguez, including a sweeping oil sector deal. Machado's stated opposition to the businessman central to that accord signals potential friction. Washington has now outlined its clearest election timetable for Venezuela, but the incentive structure for disrupting a commercially productive bilateral relationship remains limited ahead of US midterms.
