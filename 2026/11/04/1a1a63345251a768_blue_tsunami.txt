bringing you the latest in global politics. If you haven’t yet, sign uphere

.

A blue tsunami has come crashing down on Donald Trump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/inflation-backlash-trump-fatigue-powers-huge-wins-for-democrats?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
. And with the stinging rebuke come expectations, abroad as well as at home, 
that a hitherto unbridled presidentcan be restrained 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-04/midterm-elections-2026-trump-must-now-contend-with-democrats-in-control?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
.

Whether he will is another question, as is which version of Trump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/2026-midterm-elections-what-democrats-wins-in-congress-mean-for-trump?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 the world will now see after the US midterm elections: a slightly conciliatory 
president showing willingness to work with Democrats, or a defiant “Mr. Veto.”

A weakened and humiliated Trump could react in a number of ways on the global 
stage that, true to his nature, remain highly unpredictable.
Democrat supporters in Austin, Texas, yesterday.Photographer: Jordan 
Vonderhaar/Bloomberg
The most immediate test internationally will be his next move in Iran.

The choices remain escalation — which Israeli leader Benjamin Netanyahu 
strongly advocates — orfinding an off-ramp 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/trump-plays-down-prospect-of-strikes-on-iran-after-midterm-rout?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 that offers relief on energy prices.

One clear lesson of the election was that voters came out in droves to express 
their anger with out-of-control living costs, compounded by an unpopular war on 
Iran and Trump’s love of tariffs.

In blaming the president who made the midterms a referendum on himself, they 
also turned it into a bad night for Israel.

Michigan elected America’s first Muslim senator 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/democrat-el-sayed-wins-michigan-senate-race-ddhq-projects?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
, whose sharp criticism of Netanyahu’s policies reflects a growing generational 
divide in the US electorate, where younger voters holding more pro-Palestinian 
views are willing to defy a political establishment wary of Israel criticism.

That could change.
Democrats at an election night event in Detroit, Michigan.Photographer: Sarah 
Rice/Bloomberg
Trump announced yesterday that the US would cut $4 billion in annual military 
aid to Israel this year, a much shorter timeline than previously agreed.

He may be feeling his way to ending his trade wars, too, starting with 
overtures to Canada 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/canada-s-carney-says-us-has-lowered-its-demands-in-trade-talks?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
.

US voters also threw a wrench in Trump’s go-for-broke support for artificial 
intelligence, which he views as a race against China.

There was a strong backlash against data centers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/ai-data-center-boom-is-set-for-upheaval-after-us-election-rout?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 and the unchecked power of Big Tech, notably in Elon Musk’s adoptive state of 
Texas. In Ohio, the Republican pushing for the world’s largest AI center lost. 
Beijing will take note.

For the rest of the world digesting the results, there are some further 
takeaways.

Europe is heading into a string of big elections with voters preoccupied by 
many of the same issues afflicting US households. Far-right populists, 
especially those that embraced MAGA, may have to check their expectations of 
power.
Republican supporters at an election night event in Dallas, Texas.
Photographer: Mark Felix/Bloomberg
Forget immigration: Incumbents and challengers alike now know that a 
laser-like focus on affordability can win elections.

While many world leaders, particularly those on the receiving end of Trump’s 
jabs, may be privately exultant, public gloating would be counter-productive. 
That goes especially forcontaining Russia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/russia-plots-ways-to-exploit-a-weakened-trump-after-midterms?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 in its war on Ukraine, where continued engagement from Trump remains critical.

Ultimately, much depends on how Democrats play their renewed relevance as they 
eye 2028. While it’s clear Americans are rejecting Trump, it’s not yet certain 
how far they’re shifting to the left.

Going down the path of retribution risks misjudging the public mood, as much 
to their own detriment as that of America’s international allies. — Flavia 
Krause-Jackson 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ACWlz_feWqg/flavia-krausejackson?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>

Global Must Reads

Saudi Arabia’s fiscal deficit more than doubled last quarter as oil revenues 
tumbled due to disruptions from the Iran war and lower fees paid by state 
petroleum giant Aramco. The $22.4 billion third-quarter budget gap dovetails 
with an economic contraction of 3.8% over the same period, underscoring the
financial strain 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/saudi-budget-deficit-more-than-doubles-as-war-strains-finances?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 the kingdom is under.

Indonesia’s military is mobilizing tens of thousands of troops to bolster 
security in Jakarta as authorities investigate the alleged wiretapping of 
President Prabowo Subianto, conduct that a senior official saysmay amount to 
treason 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/indonesia-arrests-ex-police-chief-for-allegedly-wiretapping-prabowo-others?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 or an attempt to overthrow the government. The upheaval coincides with a 
souring public mood over rising prices and the state of the economy.

A political standoff in Poland is set to raise fresh doubts about the future 
of the central bank’s governor, with lawmakers expected to vote this month on 
whether toput him on trial 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/poland-constitutional-crisis-has-central-bank-chief-adam-glapinski?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 for alleged political meddling before 2023 elections. Adam Glapinski has 
denied any wrongdoing and disputes the parliament’s right to suspend him, 
citing a top court decision that’s been contested by Prime Minister Donald 
Tusk’s coalition.
Tusk yesterday.Photographer: Damian Lemanski/Bloomberg
More than a week after a student-led bloc unexpectedly defeated Serbia’s 
longtime leader Aleksandar Vučić at the polls, it still hasn’t taken up its 
seats in parliament or named a prime minister. But the old regime is already
being dismantled 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/serbia-s-leaderless-force-starts-dismantling-vucic-s-regime?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
, with officials at the central bank and the state prosecutor’s office calling 
for investigations into potential crimes committed during Vučić’s tenure, and 
top brass from the state broadcaster quitting or apologizing for biased 
coverage.

South African President Cyril Ramaphosa says the outcome of the US midterms 
signals a shift in the geopolitical landscape that couldopen the way for a reset
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/south-africa-s-ramaphosa-sees-new-era-after-us-election-results?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 in Pretoria’s strained ties with Washington. He spoke as political parties 
that have presided over crumbling infrastructure and a collapse in electricity 
and water provisionface a reckoning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/south-africa-municipal-elections-cyril-ramaphosa-african-national-congress-democratic-alliance-mkp?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 in today’s municipal elections.
Ramaphosa and Trump in Washington in May 2025.Photographer: Jim Lo 
Scalzo/EPA/Bloomberg
President Santiago Peña asked Paraguay’s central bank chief to resign 
yesterday, hours after the monetary authority intervened in Ueno Bank over a
solvency crisis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/paraguay-central-bank-intervenes-in-ueno-bank-on-solvency-fears?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 that’s rippling through the South American nation’s political ranks.

Spanish Transport Minister Óscar Puente resigned over his role in a high-speed 
train crash 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/spanish-transport-minister-quits-in-political-blow-for-sanchez?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
 that killed 69 people, a major blow for Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez ahead of 
a snap election on Nov. 29.

Russia’s return to the ice hockey World Championships, decided by a sports 
tribunal, hasincensed Ukraine’s allies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/europe-races-to-oust-russia-from-ice-hockey-world-championships?cmpid=BBD110426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10992>
, some of whom said they won’t take part in games involving the country.
