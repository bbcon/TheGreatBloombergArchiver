• Eurozone Q3 GDP grew twice as fast as consensus forecast in the final week, though France contracted 0.1% QoQ against a +0.1% expectation while simultaneously reporting CPI at 3.8% YoY — its highest since late 2023.

**French Fiscal and Political Stress**
France crystallized as the eurozone's most acute near-term risk throughout October. The French-German sovereign spread widened from approximately 58 basis points in March to 148 basis points by the third week — a pace of deterioration reflecting political dysfunction, fiscal slippage, and social unrest compounding simultaneously. Violent cost-of-living protests in the third week included approximately 71,500 students demonstrating, with refinery strikes at TotalEnergies adding economic disruption. The government's 2027 budget — encompassing €54 billion in consolidation measures targeting pensions, public-sector wages, and sick leave — faces a minority parliament that has removed two predecessors. The arithmetic is perilous: Prime Minister Lecornu must secure passage of politically sensitive cuts ahead of April's presidential election, with polling showing Marine Le Pen's implied probability of winning approaching but remaining below 50%. Moody's decision against a downgrade in the final week provided tactical relief but left the structural credibility problem unresolved. Nearly €215 billion of French corporate bonds now trade tighter than sovereign debt — an 18-fold increase since January — a market signal with few historical precedents in a major developed economy.

**ECB Policy and Leadership**
The ECB's October hold was fully anticipated, but Lagarde's simultaneous announcement of an early February departure removes an important continuity anchor. ECB Governing Council member Wunsch stated explicitly mid-month that the ECB "will probably have to hike again" — the clearest hawkish signal since the two increases delivered earlier in 2026 — while Lagarde simultaneously cautioned that energy-driven inflation has not yet produced second-round wage effects. This tension captures the ECB's genuine dilemma: tightening into sovereign stress and slowing growth risks amplifying the French dynamic, yet holding while headline CPI resurges to 3.8% erodes credibility. Policymaker Dolenc suggested market-driven financial tightening may partially substitute for rate action — a formulation that implies the OAT-Bund spread is doing some of the ECB's work. Succession frontrunners Klaas Knot and Pablo Hernandez de Cos will be considered at the EU leaders' November 26–27 summit; whoever takes the role may inherit crisis-management responsibilities almost immediately.

**Energy Risk and UK**
European gas storage entered October at 73% of capacity versus a seasonal norm near 90% — the lowest recorded level for this time of year. Covering the shortfall via additional LNG imports would cost an estimated €3 billion at prices more than 100% above pre-conflict levels; alternatively, a roughly 7% year-on-year demand reduction would be required to protect inventories. ENTSOG warns an unusually cold season could reduce storage to 11% of capacity by end-March. October's mild weather provided a temporary reprieve but did not resolve the structural shortfall. The UK presented a contrasting picture: Chancellor Healey's late-October budget avoided adverse gilt market reaction, and above-consensus PMI and employment data in the final week suggested British economic momentum is tracking ahead of the continent — though elevated Bank of England rate expectations and rising energy costs leave limited fiscal headroom.
