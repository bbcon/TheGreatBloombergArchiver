**Eurozone Political and Fiscal Risk**
The euro's 6% decline since late August is increasingly attributable to French political dynamics rather than ECB policy alone. With Marine Le Pen leading polls ahead of next year's presidential election and the government struggling to pass a budget, speculative short positions on the euro have reached their highest level in over six years. Analysts note a feedback loop in which deteriorating French fiscal credibility constrains the ECB's room to tighten, compounding currency weakness. The ECB's own climate liaison, Kocher, separately warned that rising temperatures are adding to inflationary pressures confronting the central bank.

**Climate Negotiations**
At COP31 in Antalya, Thursday's revised draft text — nine pages, half the length of Monday's circulated version — removed prior calls to "phase out fossil fuels" and watered down methane reduction language. The retreat came hours after Trump, Xi, and Putin jointly pledged to expand refined petroleum product supply. EU Climate Chief Hoekstra acknowledged the geopolitical reality while warning against a new energy "trap."
