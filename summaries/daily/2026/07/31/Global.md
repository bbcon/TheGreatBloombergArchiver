**Oil Markets and the Iran Conflict**
The roughly 20% monthly gain in Brent crude is the dominant global macro story of July, with direct implications for inflation, central bank policy, and growth across both importing and exporting economies. The US-Iran conflict continues to disrupt Middle Eastern exports, US commercial crude inventories have fallen to their lowest since September 2018, and the national average gasoline price has risen above $4 per gallon. Washington has explicitly ruled out another major Strategic Petroleum Reserve withdrawal beyond the 172 million barrels announced in March — a constraint made more binding by the SPR sitting at its lowest level since the early 1980s. US refiners are running at record utilization rates and posting exceptional profits; Valero reported its most profitable quarter on record. The conflict's resolution timeline remains highly uncertain: Bloomberg New Energy Finance's base case for European gas storage projections assumes Strait of Hormuz reopening in mid-September, a scenario that cannot be taken as given.

**Geopolitics and AI**
The Hamas disarmament agreement announced by President Trump represents a potentially significant diplomatic development in the Middle East, though officials cautioned that implementation details remain unresolved over the coming weeks. Separately, US-China tensions over artificial intelligence intensified: Beijing warned Washington against sanctioning Chinese AI companies ahead of the September summit, while the US moved to tighten robotics export controls. The cycle — Chinese technological advance followed by American policy response — shows no sign of abating, with consequences for global semiconductor supply chains, AI valuations, and the trajectory of bilateral relations.
