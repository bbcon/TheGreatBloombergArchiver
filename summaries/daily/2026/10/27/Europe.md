• France's fiscal deficit exceeds 5% of GDP despite government revenues above 50% of GDP, leaving Paris with limited room to either raise taxes or cut spending ahead of the presidential election.
• UK Chancellor John Healey is set to raise the national living wage approximately 4% in Wednesday's budget, with no closure of the age-band pay gap.
• HSBC's earnings miss dragged down European bank stocks broadly.

**France: Fiscal and Political Pressure**
French consumer confidence dropping to its lowest level since 2013 reflects the cumulative weight of energy costs, political uncertainty, and post-riot social disruption. The fiscal position is structurally constrained: with revenues already above 50% of GDP — well above the G7 average of under 36% — the government has limited space to raise taxes without economic damage, while entitlement reform faces intense political resistance. The ECB's bank lending survey showed euro-zone credit conditions tightening and loan demand weakening, reinforcing the case for ECB caution.

**UK: Budget and Policy**
Wednesday's budget represents Chancellor Healey's first significant fiscal statement under Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who has separately signaled interest in revisiting the UK's relationship with the EU — stopping short of a formal rejoining proposal for this Parliament. The 4% living wage increase is modest relative to broader wage dynamics. With gilt yields elevated and borrowing running above forecasts, Bloomberg editors argue Healey should prioritize health and disability benefit restraint alongside revenue measures.
