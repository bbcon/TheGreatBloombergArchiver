When US President Donald Trump returned to the White House last year, his 
economic team arrived amid a flurry of speculation that a “Mar-a-Lago Accord 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-25/will-trump-devalue-the-us-dollar-with-a-mar-a-lago-accord?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
” might be coming in international economic diplomacy. Scott Bessent had talked 
about “some kind of a grandglobal economic reordering 
<https://www.c-span.org/clip/public-affairs-event/user-clip-scott-bessent-at-free-mkt-conf/5141624>
.”

After becoming Treasury secretary, Bessent described a vision of coordinating 
with partners inNorth America 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-28/mexico-open-to-new-tariffs-on-china-to-avoid-trump-duties?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 to create a tariff wall against China, and doing a deal with longstanding 
allies to “approach China as a group 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-09/bessent-sees-a-deal-with-allies-then-group-approach-on-china?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
.” That’s in the context of describing Beijing officials as being “the worst 
offenders in the international trading system,” with a supply-side, 
export-reliant growth model. And he warned Europe against any pivot toward 
China.

In recent weeks, the opportunity for just such a coordinated, China-centric 
strategy has suddenly emerged. Germany, for decades one of the strongest voices 
in Europe favoring open trade and investment ties with China, has begun to 
shift tack. With some German industries now facing a debilitatingdownward spiral
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-23/germany-s-troubled-chemical-makers-risk-disorderly-decline?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
, Chancellor Friedrich Merz has been calling for international coordination to 
address a cheap yuan. (The International Monetary Fund has reckoned the 
currency isundervalued 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-18/imf-warns-china-s-economic-policies-are-causing-damage-to-others?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 by as much as 21%.)

In June, Merz said he was considering something along the lines of the 1985 
Plaza Accord 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-19/germany-s-merz-seeks-currency-talks-to-fix-undervalued-yuan?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
, referring to a pact between the US, Germany, France, Japan and the UK to 
recalibrate key exchange rates that were viewed as out of whack. On July 13, 
Merz said “we need apolitical currency dialogue 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/merz-urges-currency-dialogue-with-china-as-trade-worries-rise?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 with China.” Two days later, he said Europe’s finance ministerswill take up 
the issue 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/merz-says-germany-isn-t-blocking-unicredit-deal-for-commerzbank?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
.

While Trump called out China’s yuan policy long before Merz — even tagging the 
country for a time as a currencymanipulator 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-08-05/u-s-treasury-department-labels-china-a-currency-manipulator?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 — he appears to favor individual economic deals, not group ones. So rather 
than seizing the chance to engage with Europe in a broad China initiative, 
Bessent and his teammates have instead pursuedtheir own track 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-shares-concern-over-restrictive-us-measures-with-bessent?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 with Beijing.

“The US agenda has shrunk radically, while Europe’s agenda has expanded to 
include the classic topics of macroeconomic coordination,” says Brad Setser, a 
senior fellow with the Council on Foreign Relations. “The opportunity clearly 
would have existed for coordinated action against China had the US played its 
cards differently.”

It’s not just Germany that’s shown signs of increased determination to address 
what some fear will be a job-destroying shockwave of Chinese goods. French 
President Emanuel Macron has for months been warning that the European Union 
may beforced to act 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-07/macron-warns-eu-may-hit-china-with-tariffs-over-trade-surplus?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 against what he describes as an unsustainable trade deficit with China.

In February, a public think tank that reports to France’s prime minister 
released what observers called asurprisingly candid assessment 
<https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/2026/2026-02-09%20-%20Rouleau%20compresseur%20chinois/HCSP-2026-RAPPORT-CHINE-9fevrier11h.pdf>
 of the dangers to European industries posed by China.

“To avoid a spiral of ‘destructive destruction’ in which entire sectors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-09/german-machinery-slump-drags-on-in-blow-to-merz-s-turnaround-bid?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 of European industry would collapse within a few years, the EU must rethink 
its playbook,” Clement Beaune, head of the Haut-commissaire à la Stratégie et 
au Plan, wrote in the report. (Note how he flipped the classic “creative 
destruction” concept from 20th century Austrian economist Joseph Schumpeter.)

Beaune’s suggested playbook features a 30% tariff on China or a 20% to 30% 
depreciation of the euro against the yuan.

Europe’s shift on China trade comes amid concern in Brussels about Beijing’s 
ties with Moscow, as the Kremlin continues topursue its war with Ukraine 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-26/beijing-warns-eu-after-china-firms-included-in-russia-sanctions?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
.

Looking east, Japan has been anxious for coordination 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/us-backing-of-japan-yen-actions-marks-new-normal-in-coordination?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 with the US on its own currency. And it’s been waging a one-on-onegeoeconomic 
battle 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-29/china-blacklists-more-japan-entities-as-takaichi-feud-deepens?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 with China since last year, when Beijing slapped Tokyo with export controls on 
key industrial inputs. Logic suggests Japan would welcome a big Group of Seven 
push on China. Any deal resulting in a stronger Chinese yuan could potentially 
offer relief for Asian currencies more broadly, includingSouth Korea’s won 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/korea-cites-close-ties-with-us-japan-after-overnight-fx-surge?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
.

“At the moment, it seems like everyone that has issues with their trade 
relationship with China are all going at Chinaon their own 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/merz-is-inching-germany-toward-backing-eu-push-against-china?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 rather than coordinating together,” says Jacob Gunter, head of the Economy and 
Industry Program at Berlin’s Mercator Institute for China Studies.

A history buff, Gunter likens the current situation to the early Napoleonic 
wars. That’s when the Austrians, Prussians, Russians and British failed to 
tightly coordinate against a hegemonic threat.

“The Austrians show up on day one and fight Napoleon and get defeated, and 
then on day three the Russians show up and they fight Napoleon and get 
defeated, and so on,” Gunter recounts. The same as now, “there’s no real 
coordination,” he says.

Alicia Garcia-Herrero, chief Asia Pacific and Middle East economist at 
Natixis, puts it more bluntly: “Trump has dumped the Europeans on China.” The 
US president “is not interested in the exchange rate anymore, but rather onthe 
AI race 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/bessent-says-us-will-scrutinize-chinese-ai-models-for-ip-theft?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
,” she says. China’s leverage with regard torare earth elements 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-14/how-xi-is-using-rare-earths-against-trump-in-the-us-china-trade-war?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 and magnets is also too big “for Trump to pick that fight,” she adds.
Alicia Garcia-Herrero
In its latest assessment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/us-treasury-calls-out-china-for-lack-of-transparency-on-currency?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 of China’s currency policy, the US Treasury — in the view of Goldman Sachs 
Group Inc. analysts — relaxed its language regarding China. Even as Europeans 
call out major yuan misalignment, the US on July 23 dropped aJanuary description
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-29/us-calls-on-china-to-strengthen-substantially-undervalued-yuan?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 of the currency being “substantially undervalued.”

A Trump administration official contested the Goldman assessment, saying the 
Treasury report didn’t include toned-down language and that the yuan had 
appreciated since the previous release. The official went on to note that there 
is no joint G-7 pressure effort on the yuan — something two US senatorshad 
called for 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-18/bessent-urged-by-us-senators-to-push-china-on-undervalued-yuan?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 prior to the report.

Setser of the Council on Foreign Relations, also a former US Treasury 
official, says things may change. By the time of the next report, Europeans 
will havehit an October deadline 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-02/eu-flinches-in-china-trade-reset-fearing-painful-retaliation?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260801&utm_content=7042>
 for making progress on trade. And Trump will be beyond his latest summit with 
Chinese President Xi Jinping. Further ahead, Trump’s successor could consider a 
joint approach.

“There certainly would be an opportunity again to try to make a more coherent 
North Atlantic policy, or for the G-7 to join forces,” Setser says. “If current 
trends continue and China’s surpluses only get bigger over the intervening two 
years, that will be even more necessary.”
