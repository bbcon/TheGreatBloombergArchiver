• September CPI printed 3.7% YoY, above the 3.5% consensus — the fastest pace in five months — with supercore returning above 3% and Cleveland Fed trimmed-mean measures also moving higher.
• The 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.36% intraday in the second week — a 24-year high breaching the 2007 peak — before ending the month near 4.93% as long-duration bonds posted their first monthly outperformance since February.
• The FOMC held rates steady in late October with two hawkish dissents; the AI earnings season delivered a sharp bifurcation between Microsoft's Azure beat and Alphabet's revenue miss, sending the Nasdaq 100 down approximately 3.3% for the month even as the equal-weighted S&P 500 gained 5.7%.

**Inflation and the Consumer**
The month's dominant domestic data story was the breadth and persistence of inflationary pressure. August core PCE held at 3.0% YoY in line with consensus, but real consumer spending rose 0.6% MoM — the strongest monthly gain in over a year — funded not by income growth but by savings drawdown, with the personal saving rate falling to 4.1%, a near four-year low. The personal saving rate's decline reflects a wealth effect from Q2's record $12.8 trillion rise in household net worth, a dynamic that may prove fragile given elevated rates. September CPI's 3.7% print — with the beat broad-based across supercore, sticky-price, and trimmed-mean measures — confirmed inflation's stickiness, with SocGen modeling suggesting diesel prices above $6.50 per gallon and refineries running at 97% capacity are sustaining cost-push pressure well into 2027. Against this, Conference Board consumer confidence fell to its lowest since 2014 in September, and PepsiCo cut its earnings outlook mid-month — signals that aggregate spending strength masks meaningful distributional stress, particularly among lower-income households bearing disproportionate energy cost burdens.

**Monetary Policy**
October's Fed communication was unusually dissonant. The month opened with Chair Warsh's hawkish posture and Treasury Secretary Bessent signaling continued tightening, driving fed funds futures to price a terminal rate near 4.86%. Fed Deputy Chair Jefferson's mid-month remarks — that the Fed "may need more time" before deciding on another hike — triggered a 21-basis-point repricing in the October 2027 contract and a 12-basis-point intraday rally in the 10-year yield. The late-October FOMC hold, contested by two hawkish dissenters including Governor Cook, left December as the base case for any additional move. FOMC minutes from the September hike — the first in three years — showed all 19 officials in agreement, with several characterizing policy as not yet restrictive. Warsh separately announced the Fed would reduce its annual decision calendar from eight to six meetings effective 2027, a structural change with implications for market communication cadence.

**Equity, Credit, and Rates**
The 10-year yield's breach of 5.36% inverted the equity risk premium for the first time in roughly 25 years, removing a structural anchor for valuations. Investment-grade credit spreads remained notably contained — a disconnect with underlying rate stress worth monitoring. The AI complex provided index support through mid-month before a sharp reassessment: Goldman Sachs prime brokerage data showed global fundamental long/short managers down 1.4% on the worst single session, ending a 53-session streak without a 1% S&P drawdown. CoreWeave's unsecured bonds traded near 78 cents on the dollar at their worst, implying a greater-than-60% five-year default probability and crystallizing how 5%-plus risk-free rates interact with leveraged AI infrastructure models — though Barclays noted those bonds represent under $2 billion of a $35 billion debt stack, suggesting the stress remains idiosyncratic for now. The merchandise trade deficit widened to a record $134.9 billion in September, driven by AI-related capital goods imports, underscoring how the AI investment cycle is simultaneously supporting growth and widening external imbalances.
