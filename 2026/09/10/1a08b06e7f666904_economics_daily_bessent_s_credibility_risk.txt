Daring Traders

Scott Bessent’s career in financial markets got a rocket boost at age 30, when 
he had a role in George Soros’s legendary bet against Britain’s central bank. 
It was a powerful example of how investors can beat policymakers when 
fundamentals are on their side.

Now 64 and US Treasury secretary, Bessent reckons he has the power to vanquish 
bettors who dare to take him on. While he’s repeatedly said that he’s not 
aiming to change the “equilibrium” price of assets such as US Treasuries, his 
bravado suggests he believes his signals for investors canshape outcomes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
.

Whether traders are bidding up the price of oil, pushing down the Japanese yen 
or, most importantly, driving up US Treasury yields, they’re makinga big mistake
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/traders-brush-aside-scott-bessent-s-bet-against-me-tough-talk?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
, he insists, because he’s on the other side of the trade, armed with valuable 
information on government policy plans they don’t possess.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-09/us-treasury-triples-debt-buyback-video?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
In the jargon of game theory, it’s called asymmetric information, as the 
former hedge-fund trader is fond of pointing out. He’s uttered it publicly 
multiple times in the past few weeks alone, including Tuesday, when he dared 
currency traders to “bet against me 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-dares-traders-to-bet-against-yen-i-am-the-house-now?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
 if you want.”

Since Bessent began jawboning the oil and bond markets, both of which are 
crucial to the welfare of American households ahead of key mid-term elections, 
they have gone against him.

Brent crude oil has jumped over $100 a barrel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-9?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
, pushing gasoline higher at the pump. And on Wednesday, after Bessent rolled 
out his first amped-up bond buyback in a bid to push down yields, theyshot up 
instead 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/us-more-than-doubles-long-dated-debt-buyback-size-to-6-billion?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
, extending a two-week selloff that’s sent benchmark 10-year Treasury rates to 
a fresh three-year high of 4.85%.

How Bessent’s wagers pan out in coming months will go far in determining 
whether the onetime trader goes down as a masterful manipulator or a 
policymaker whose hubris was exposed by fundamentals that didn’t go his way. 
For now at least, Mark Spindel, founder of Potomac River Capital, says “the 
market has called his bluff.”

Market participants point to fundamentals being needed to sustain a move lower 
in Treasury yields. Those range from a slowdown ingrowth and inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/us-consumer-sentiment-drops-year-ahead-inflation-views-improve?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
, lower energy prices, less uncertainty around Federal Reserve monetary policy, 
fiscal consolidation or a slowdown in corporate debt issuance, Wells Fargo 
macro strategists Angelo Manolatos and Francis Brown wrote in a note.

The Treasury declined to respond to a request for comment. White House 
spokesman Kush Desai said the Treasury chief “has consistently leveraged — and 
augmented — his gravitas and the power of the American economy to deliver for 
both President Trump and the American people.”

Need-to-Know Research

As this newsletter highlighted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-14/hong-kong-onetime-mecca-for-capitalism-adopts-five-year-planning?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
 in July, the once-freewheeling mecca of Hong Kong is poised to join the 
mainland fold and receive its first Five-Year Plan, a longstanding tool of 
Communist governments. Economists are now sharpening their expectations for 
what’s coming when it’s unveiledon Sept. 16 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/hong-kong-to-unveil-its-first-five-year-plan-with-policy-address?cmpid=BBD091026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8728>
.

If it follows mainland practice, it “may include quantifiable economic and 
social targets,” in a first for Hong Kong, Raymond Yeung, chief economist for 
greater China at ANZ Bank, wrote in a note this week. As Hong Kong adopts a new 
framework, “economic policy will be more proactive than previously a minimalist 
approach, with increased public spending and countercyclical actions.”

One consequence will be a magnitude of public borrowing unseen in the old 
days. With key performance indicators to meet, “increased demand for fiscal 
support seems unavoidable,” Yeung wrote. After multiple years of fiscal 
deficits since the pandemic, partly due to poor land sales, already “debt 
financing has become a new normal in Hong Kong’s public finance,” he said.
