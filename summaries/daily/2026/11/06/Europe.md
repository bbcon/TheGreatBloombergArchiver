• Bank of England raised rates to 4% on Thursday; Governor Bailey stated a further hike is "probably more likely than not" if elevated energy costs return.
• Stoxx Europe 600 fell 1.8% Friday but is outperforming global peers significantly — on course for its largest quarterly outperformance versus global equities since early last year.
• Nominations for ECB President to succeed Christine Lagarde closed Friday at 5 p.m. Brussels time; Spain proposed Pablo Hernández de Cos, the Netherlands proposed Klaas Knot.

**Bank of England Policy**
The Bank of England's decision to raise rates to 4% came despite emerging optimism earlier in the week that potential de-escalation of the Iran conflict could ease energy price pressures. Bailey acknowledged that a jump in inflation is "quite baked in at this point" even under a rapid de-escalation scenario, reflecting the degree to which energy cost pass-through has already been embedded in the price level. The forward guidance — that another hike is more likely than not if energy costs re-accelerate — leaves the BoE in a tightening posture that diverges from fading Fed rate-hike expectations.

**ECB Succession and European Markets**
The ECB leadership contest entered a formal phase Friday with the nomination deadline. The two leading candidates — de Cos and Knot — represent meaningfully different policy temperaments, with Knot associated with a more hawkish tradition. The outcome will carry substantial implications for the ECB's pace of normalization heading into 2027. European equities have benefited from relative insulation from the AI selloff, and Barclays' Emmanuel Cau noted that "indiscriminate selling looks overdone" and that Europe may benefit further if Middle East energy tail risks continue to recede following Qatar's diplomatic outreach to Washington.
