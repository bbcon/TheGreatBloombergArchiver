Building an industry

By Frances Schwartzkopff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOY6BUObWPw/frances-schwartzkopff?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>

The fund managing the wealth of the Danish billionaires behind Lego A/S 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/LEGO:DC?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>
 intends to significantly ramp up investments in plastics technologies, as 
Europe prepares to enforce much stricter recycling rules.

The climate unit of Kirkbi A/S 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/289651Z:DC?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>
 plans to double the size of its $1.6 billion portfolio over the coming five 
years. Plastics recycling will represent “an area where we plan to invest 
heavily,” Anupam Bhargava, chief executive officer of Kirkbi Climate, said in 
an interview.

The European Union 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/345300Z:BB?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>
 has set a 2030 deadline for all packaging to be recyclable. The requirement — 
known as the EU’s Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) — is part of 
a largerset  
<https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ:L_202500040&pk_campaign=todays_OJ&pk_source=EUR-Lex&pk_medium=X&pk_content=Environment&pk_keyword=Regulation>
of measures designed to cut carbon emissions and waste in the EU.
A worker loads plastic bags into a shredding machine to be recycled and turned 
into pellets in Kasama, Japan.Photographer: Carl Court/Getty Images
The new rules “will create added confidence for investors like us that the 
market will eventually mature,” Bhargava said. “We can dive in knowing that the 
market economics don’t work today, but if there’s no chance for the market 
economics to ever work, then that’s very difficult.”

Recycling as a business area has long been held back, due to a combination of 
low investment and stubbornly competitive petrochemical prices that have made 
virgin plastic cheaper than recycling.

Today, “there’s no solution where you can invest in a technology company and 
that will produce amazing results,” Bhargava said. That’s because companies 
involved in recycling work “in a value chain that is currently highly 
fragmented and not very efficient.”

With the packaging regulation deadlines looming, however, “the industry needs 
to get ready now,” Bhargava said.

Kirkbi is the holding company of the Lego group whose colorful plastic 
building blocks are sold around the world. The company managed189 billion kroner
 <https://www.kirkbi.com/> ($29 billion) in assets as of year-end. Kirkbi 
Climate, which invests in the energy transition and land use in addition to 
plastics recycling, has 10 billion kroner under management. Future investments 
will be roughly split between energy and recycling, with a focus on a handful 
of EU countries and plastics, Bhargava said.

Capturing more

By Akshat Rathi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOobfNzkQ/akshat-rathi?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>
 andAlastair Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQvVwWuhxcQ/alastair-marsh?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>

The world’s largest direct air capture plant 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-21/carbon-removal-startup-climeworks-is-cutting-22-of-staff?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>
 is sucking more carbon dioxide from the atmosphere at a lower cost, an 
improvement for a sector struggling to scale economically as global warming 
surpasses dangerous levels.

Swiss carbon-removal company Climeworks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1649973D:SW?cmpid=BBD091026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8744>
 said its Mammoth facility in Iceland removed 675 tons of carbon dioxide in the 
first half of this year, almost six times as much as a year earlier. The cost 
of running the plant has fallen by more than half, the company said in a
statement 
<https://climeworks.com/blog/mammoth-direct-air-capture-performance-update>, 
without disclosing the actual expense

The better performance from Mammoth, which began operations in 2024, and 
others like it may be crucial to combating global warming, but only if the 
technology can grow big enough to make a meaningful dent.

Several billion tons of carbon removal may be required 
<https://www.stateofcdr.org/asset/SoCDR-2nd-Edition-Chapter-8.pdf> annually by 
mid-century to keep temperatures below 2C above pre-industrial levels, 
researchers led by Matthew Gidden of the International Institute for Applied 
Systems Analysis wrote in a 2024 report. Carbon emissions from fossil fuels 
reached38.1 billion tons 
<https://globalcarbonbudget.org/fossil-fuel-co2-emissions-hit-record-high-in-2025/>
 last year, according to the Global Carbon Project.
