A South Korean shipping line is entering the fledgling market to offer 
container services between Asia and Europe through the Arctic.

In a first by a South Korean operator, PanStar Group is launching a pilot 
program to connect Busan with Rotterdam, Hamburg and Poland’s Gdansk. Crossing 
the northern sea route takes about three weeks, roughly half the one-way trip 
from Asia to northern Europe through the Suez Canal.

Related: South Korea’s Exports Extend Momentum as Chip Boom Persists 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/south-korea-s-exports-extend-momentum-as-chip-boom-drives-growth?cmpid=BBD081126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7519>

On its website <https://www.panstar.co.kr/en/business/arctic>, PanStar said 
its offering is part of a “new era in maritime logistics” that will reduce 
carbon emissions and improve supply chain stability. The first voyage is 
expected to depart Asia later this month.

The Korean company is joining a Chinese container service 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2025-09-09/trade-war-latest-shipping-through-the-arctic?cmpid=BBD081126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7519>
 that has transited the Arctic in recent years during months when ice flows 
pose the least threat to shipping — from late-summer through November.
A map on PanStar’s website shows an Arctic route connecting Korea’s shipping 
hub with northern European portsSource: PanStar
Wars in Ukraine and Iran, along with rising trade tensions, are rewiring 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-15/iran-s-strait-of-hormuz-closure-turns-desert-roads-into-vital-trade-routes?cmpid=BBD081126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7519>
 the global trading system and forcing companies to build redundancies into 
their supply chains. Waterways where goods once flowed unimpeded have become 
expensive and delay-plagued chokepoints.

River Restrictions

Climate extremes are also wreaking havoc on maritime trade and forcing 
diversions. Heat waves and droughts in the northern hemisphere have reduced the 
depth of inland waterways from North America’s St. Lawrence Seaway to Europe’s 
Rhine and Danube rivers — leading to tonnage restrictions and surcharges.

Read More: China Eyes Arctic Shortcut as Top Container Lines Stay Away 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-03/china-eyes-arctic-shortcut-as-top-container-lines-stay-away?cmpid=BBD081126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7519>

Even with the ice caps melting, the Arctic alternative is unlikely to pose 
major near-term competition to the traditional routes through the Suez and 
Panama canals.

According to a recent report 
<https://www.coface.com/news-economy-and-insights/will-arctic-shipping-routes-really-reshape-the-map-of-global-trade>
 by Coface, a trade credit insurance and risk-management firm, the share of 
trade between East Asia and Europe or North America that could use the Arctic 
route within five years is estimated at 3.5%.

It’s “highly unlikely” that the world’s major container lines will follow, 
according to Peter Sand, chief analyst with Xeneta, an Oslo-based digital 
freight platform. But “it may serve specific exporters and markets during the 
traditional peak season in the third quarter,” he said.

Russian Icebreakers

It’s a geopolitically tricky, too, because passage involves Russia’s 
icebreakers and navigation permits.

“This is one of the reasons that Donald Trump has wanted Greenland is because 
ultimately if you’re coming across the Arctic you’re going to pass Greenland,” 
Craig Fuller, the CEO of FreightWaves, toldBloomberg Television 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-10/freightwaves-ceo-says-freight-recession-is-over-video?cmpid=BBD081126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7519>
 on Monday.

“This is really a continuation of something that shouldn’t be a surprise to 
anybody” because it’s been “the dream” of the Chinese and Russians to gain more 
access to the North Atlantic and Arctic Ocean to avoid Mideast bottlenecks and 
establish another trade lane, Fuller said.
