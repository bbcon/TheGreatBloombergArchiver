France has a long history of political violence.

It has given the world the guillotine, the Terror and the Paris Commune, not 
to mention dozens of food riots and hundreds of burned cars and tires. In the 
fullness of time, theunrest of the past few days 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/french-violence-raises-alert-as-protests-flare-across-europe?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
 will barely register in the history books.

But for a nation careening into a possible bond-market crisis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/france-s-bonds-drop-as-rioting-heaps-pressure-on-government?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
 with no oneclearly at the wheel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/how-macron-s-decade-in-power-left-france-europe-s-weakest-link?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
, the scenes of burning town halls, looting, police swinging truncheons and 
monuments defaced by graffiti have proven a shock — a national “shame” in the 
words of President Emmanuel Macron, who addressed the country on television 
yesterday.

With the embers still smoldering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/short-of-time-and-influence-macron-fights-to-remain-relevant?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
, Macron left town for a swing through the Gulf nations. That leaves his 
ministers and putative successors to trade recriminations.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-19/macron-calls-for-order-after-violent-protests-in-france-video?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>

The traditional first-choice solution to unrest — spending to keep the peace — 
is no longer an option.

Deepening concerns over public finances have sent France’s borrowing costs 
soaring and Macron’s government is intent on austerity. A deeply divided 
National Assembly last week began discussing a budget proposal for next year 
that features about €43 billion ($48 billion) in deficit-cutting measures.

With a presidential election campaign underway, the leading candidates are 
doing little to calm the simmering anger over the cost of living and other 
grievancesechoed throughout Europe 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/france-seeks-to-contain-unrest-as-protest-violence-hits-europe?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
.

Far-right leader Marine Le Pen says the country was “held hostage between 
violence and powerlessness.” She blamed the party of her far-left rival 
Jean-Luc Mélenchon for trivializing violence: “France cannot, must not, and 
will never get used to such scenes.”

For his part, Mélenchon pointed the finger at police provocation for some of 
the disorder and disassociated himself from vandals and looters.

With a nationwide strike called for Nov. 5, the past few days may have been a 
shame, yet there’s no evidence the violence has been awakeup call 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-10-19/france-riots-liberte-egalite-misery-why-france-and-paris-are-burning?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
. — James Hertling

Chart of the Day

Much of the world is struggling with a boom in discarded plastic, and Turkey — 
which hosts the COP31 climate summit next month — is the top recipient of 
plastic waste from the UK and European Union. It imports hundreds of thousands 
of tons a year, which can poison marine life, contaminate farmland and enter 
the human food chain. And the country’swaste intake is expected to grow 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-19/turkey-is-europe-s-plastic-dumpsite-giving-it-up-will-be-hard?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>

And Finally

Brazil registered the lowest Amazon deforestation rate on record over the past 
year, allowing President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva to claimsuccess on a key 
issue 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/brazil-sees-record-low-amazon-deforestation-rate-in-win-for-lula?cmpid=BBD101926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10265>
 that’s been at the center of his platform just a week ahead of a runoff 
election. The decline marks a sharp reversal from the years under former 
President Jair Bolsonaro — the father of Lula’s opponent, Flávio Bolsonaro — 
when deforestation rose to a 15-year high.
