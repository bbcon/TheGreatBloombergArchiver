“We’ve got this.” Those were confident words from Nvidia’s interim CEO Colette 
Kress as she addressed analysts in a conference call after the company reported 
earnings — its first since the departure of longtime leader Jensen Huang. There 
was a clear message: Huang isn’t coming back, and the company will befine 
without him 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/nvidia-delivers-solid-outlook-in-first-post-huang-earnings?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11556>
.

Nvidia’s shares rose postmarket as the chip giant lifted its sales forecast 
for next year, boosting broader markets.US stock futures climbed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11556>
 though Asian equities fluctuated as traders weighed the durability of the 
artificial intelligence trade. South Korea’s memory chipmakers Samsung 
Electronics and SK Hynix rallied.

Still, caution abounds. Nvidia is becoming more selective about financing the 
AI boom, with Kress outlininggreater scrutiny 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/the-bank-of-nvidia-is-becoming-more-disciplined-and-selective?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11556>
 of future deals. And the company is no longer shippingHopper data center chips 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/interim-nvidia-ceo-says-it-s-no-longer-sending-ai-chips-to-china?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11556>
 to China, despite winning the US government’s blessing to sell a version of 
that model to the Asian nation last year.

Kress is due to face Nvidia’s staff at an all-hands meeting. And Huang agreed 
to brief Congress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/nvidia-s-huang-to-brief-congress-on-affair-after-subpoena-threat?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11556>
 next month on his affair with a suspected Chinese spy, responding to US 
lawmakers’ demands for answers about the scandal that rocked one of the world’s 
most valuable companies. Given how volatile the AI trade has been recently, 
time will tell if the optimism today lasts.

Malaysia Says Growth Stronger Than Expected But AI Risks Loom

Malaysia’s economy expanded faster than initially estimated in the third 
quarter, supported by AI demand and resilient domestic spending, as officials 
said they’re trying to gauge the impact of new US rules on the technology. “The 
Malaysian economy is well-positioned to build on its momentum in 2027,” Bank 
Negara Malaysia Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said, predicting growth of more 
than 5% this year. “Downside risks include re-escalation of conflict in the 
Middle East and lower-than-expected commodity production.”
