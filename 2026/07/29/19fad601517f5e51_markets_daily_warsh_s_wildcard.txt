Warsh’s wildcard

Ask anyone on Wall Street whether the Fed is going to raise rates today, and 
the answer you’re likely to get is:“We really have no idea.” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/citadel-securities-fed-rate-hike-call-adds-to-market-angst?cmpid=072926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7118>

Welcome to the new tight-lipped era under Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who has 
made clear that he has no interest in signaling decisions in advance.

As a result, markets are scrambled in their thinking on how things will play 
out. There’s beenrecord demand this week 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/traders-make-record-rush-to-fed-futures-as-doubts-shadow-market?cmpid=072926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7118>
 to hedge against a hike, although Treasuries have been rising in recent days.

Yields on two-year notes ticked up 2 basis points to about 4.31% on Wednesday 
morning after falling the past three days.

Here’s what others are saying:

 * Citadel Securities: A hike this week “would emphatically end the forward 
guidance era” while underscoring the Fed’s independence, says Frank Flight, 
head of macro strategy.
 * Santander: “We’re actually going into the meeting with legitimate 
uncertainty. While I have them holding off in July, I think there’s certainly a 
non-trivial chance that they go,” said Stephen Stanley, chief US economist.
 * Monetary Policy Analytics: A hike would bolster Warsh’s credibility, said 
economist Derek Tang. Leaving rates unchanged risks cementing an “all hat and 
no cattle” reputation, he said.
 * PGIM: “He has really set the stage for a hike. Putting off the decision now 
would raise the probability to do a 50 in September,” said Robert Tipp, chief 
investment strategist. —Ye Xie
