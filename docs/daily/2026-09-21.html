US

Monetary Policy and the AI Credit Cycle

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's recent hawkish commentary has crystallized expectations of a tightening cycle, with the Fed responding to an energy-driven inflation shock rather than an organic demand boom. This matters acutely for the AI infrastructure trade: JPMorgan estimates more than $4.1 trillion of $5.5 trillion in projected AI capex will be debt-financed. Barclays' duration-times-spread analysis shows six hyperscalers — Amazon, Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, SpaceX, and Microsoft — now account for 8.4% of aggregate spread risk in the US investment-grade corporate bond market, exceeding the combined footprint of the six largest banks. Credit spreads have not yet signaled stress, but Gavekal Research notes the Wicksellian spread between corporate real returns on invested capital and real financing costs is approaching its 20-year median threshold — the level at which the probability of a generalized investment slowdown rises sharply.

Asia

Bank of Japan and the Yen

The BOJ voted 7-2 to raise rates — its highest target since 1995, and the fastest tightening pace since 1990 — but Governor Kazuo Ueda explicitly discouraged expectations of back-to-back hikes, stating such sequencing is "typically reserved for situations where inflation is extremely high." Japanese CPI currently sits below 2%. The dovish framing unwound much of the yen's earlier September gains, and with CFTC data showing speculative positioning now net long yen at levels approaching the extremes seen when USD/JPY fell to 140 in 2025, the currency appears vulnerable to further flushing of those positions. Longer-term yen appreciation remains the base case — real wage growth is at multi-decade highs — but the near-term path appears uncertain.

Taiwan and Geopolitical Risk

Taiwan is watching the Trump-Xi summit with unease. A United Daily News survey found 47% of respondents lack confidence in continued US military support, against 44% who retain it. Reports that Washington withheld a $14 billion weapons package have compounded anxiety. Taipei's government publicly downplays the risk of a grand bargain, a view echoed by former US Deputy National Security Adviser Matt Pottinger. ---

Germany's Political Fragmentation

The CDU's collapse in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania — where the AfD won with 38.2% — and the Left party's victory in Berlin, where it placed first ahead of the ruling CDU on a platform of housing expropriation, represent a significant deterioration in Chancellor Merz's political standing. Merz has vowed to remain in office, but his governing coalition's cohesion is now in question. The political turbulence arrives as Germany's economy faces renewed headwinds: the auto sector is in visible distress, with VW's profit warning and mass protests reflecting the structural pressure from Chinese EV competition. Bloomberg Economics notes the economy may lose momentum before recovering.

Credit and Sovereign Risk

Poland's Moody's downgrade complicates Prime Minister Tusk's fiscal plans at a politically sensitive moment. In France, the 2027 budget is being calibrated to meet EU fiscal guidance. Greece received an upgrade. Separately, a Fidelity bond fund manager managing $14 billion warned that tech-sector debt is growing toward systemic scale, keeping tech exposure in income portfolios below 2%. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's presidential election campaign is generating political friction over ethanol policy, after the government increased the ethanol content in gasoline — disrupting a decades-long political consensus around the country's low-carbon fuels program.

Brazil Political Economy

Brazil's ethanol program, long a source of bipartisan support and a competitive advantage in low-carbon fuels, is becoming a flashpoint in a tightening presidential race. The government's decision to raise the ethanol blend ratio in gasoline has fractured the traditional coalition behind the policy, with fuel-cost concerns feeding into broader inflation anxiety among voters. The development intersects with the global energy shock: higher oil prices driven by the US-Iran conflict are amplifying fuel-cost sensitivity across the region, and African central banks — facing similar dynamics — are signaling rates will stay elevated for longer to shield against energy pass-through. No further significant macro data or central bank developments were reported for the broader Latin American region in this period. ---

Global

  • Brent crude fell 2.4% to $101.39 on signs of diplomatic progress toward a US-Iran settlement and reports that Hormuz cargoes continue to move, though geopolitical risk remains unresolved.
  • Black Sea shipping disruption is emerging as a secondary inflation vector: wheat prices have jumped to three-year highs as Russian and Ukrainian port attacks since July have severed grain exports that represent more than a quarter of global wheat trade.
  • The Trump-Xi summit (September 23–25) is the week's pivotal geopolitical event; pre-summit talks yielded agreement on a bilateral AI dialogue and operationalization of a Board of Trade covering non-sensitive goods.

Energy and Hormuz

US retail diesel at $6.50 per gallon — up more than 87 cents in September alone — illustrates how the Iran war's energy shock is transmitting into the broader economy. Oil tanker freight costs from Houston to Asia have reached approximately $26 per barrel, rendering some long-distance crude trades uneconomical. Qatar's energy minister publicly rebuked Treasury Secretary Bessent's assertion that Hormuz would become "worthless" within two years, calling it "completely wrong." Trafigura has spun off its supertanker fleet and plans an Oslo listing, citing an industry-wide boom.

Black Sea as "The Other Hormuz"

Analysts at CSIS draw an explicit parallel between Hormuz's share of seaborne oil trade and the Black Sea's share of global wheat trade. Bond markets are not yet fully pricing food supply risk, according to investors, creating a potential asymmetry. A strengthening El Niño is compounding agricultural stress across Asia-Pacific, with Verisk Maplecroft identifying India, the Philippines, and Indonesia as high civil-unrest risk markets should food price pressures intensify.
