Carry trades win big

Scott Bessent’s attempts to drive down US bond yields are giving investors 
another reason to plowmore money into carry trades. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/-it-s-a-carry-world-em-trade-notches-longest-run-since-2008?cmpid=082426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8109>

It’s a popular but often risky strategy in which investors borrow cheaply in 
currencies like the US dollar, Japanese yen or euro, and put the money to work 
in higher-yielding currencies like the Turkish lira, where interest payments on 
bonds or money-market funds can be as much as 40% or higher.

The thinking is that the more the Treasury tries to lower yields, the more the 
dollar will weaken.The emerging-market carry trade has returned about 22% since 
the end of 2024.

“It’s a carry world,” says Cathy Hepworth of PGIM. “There’s a ton of money 
looking for yield.”
