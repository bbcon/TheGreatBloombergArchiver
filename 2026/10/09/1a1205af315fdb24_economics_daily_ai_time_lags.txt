AI Time-Lags

If the world’s artificial intelligence ran on a single giant machine, it would 
be one designed in the US and manufactured in Asia.

That’s an extreme simplification of course. But it captures a distinction in 
howthe boom 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/wto-lifts-goods-trade-outlook-as-ai-demand-outweighs-war-strains?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
 is playing out either side of the Pacific.

America’s tech giants are at the forefront of the race, ploughing in hundreds 
of billions of dollars. Their spending is key to US growth, via data-center 
construction and investment in software andequipment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/us-core-capital-goods-orders-increase-by-more-than-forecast?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
. And the boost is likely larger than official statistics capture, according to 
Bloomberg Economics.

Still, and noting that caveat, there isn’t much sign of an overall economic 
acceleration in the US. In the past year or so, as the AI juggernaut gathered 
pace, GDP forecasts in Bloombergsurveys <> haven’t budged much.

Where they’ve really jumped is in Asian economies that cater to AI’s physical 
buildout and are riding anexport wave 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/south-korea-s-export-growth-extends-rally-as-chip-boom-rolls-on?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
.

To be sure, that’s partly because it’s harder to move the needle for the US — 
a much bigger economy. But it also reflects the logic of the US position in AI. 
It’s the pioneer, betting on gains to be reaped when the technology is fully up 
and running and boosting productivity all over. And it’s happy to borrow from 
the world (runningtrade deficits in AI-related stuff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/us-trade-gap-widens-to-largest-since-early-last-year-on-imports?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
, a drag on GDP) to build this machine.

That may take time. Productivity data 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/us-labor-productivity-picks-up-as-firms-seek-to-curb-costs?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
 are hard to parse, and clearly some companies have gotten more efficient. But 
Torsten Slok at Apollo Management, after taking a look at the key gauges, 
concluded this week that the AI productivity payoff is still “a forecast rather 
than an observation.”

By contrast, the lift is easy to observe in places like South Korea and Taiwan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/taiwan-s-growth-tops-forecasts-even-as-ai-buildout-worries-mount?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
, which make the chips and other kit the AI machine needs. Both were already 
export powerhouses long before ChatGPT existed. But they’ve soared to a new 
level now, with year-on-year increases running around 80% and 60% respectively.

It’s a more immediate payoff. But it comes with a caveat flagged by ASEAN 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/asia-economies-among-most-at-risk-from-any-ai-correction-researchers-warn?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
 this week and borne out in the Bloomberg Economics scenario charted below.

The reason: While everyone’s in trouble if the boom turns to bust 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-20/ai-s-wobbly-house-of-cards-puts-markets-and-us-economy-at-risk?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
, Asia’s may be deeper. As manufacturer of the AI machine, it stands to suffer 
via a trade channel as well as a financial one.

Need-to-Know Research

While 10-year US yields have punched through 5% and French bond premiums have 
soared in recent weeks, Portuguese rates, as of Thursday, remained under 4%. 
Part of that reflects the tough work Portugal’s governments did in the years 
after the European debt crisis, Oxford Economics says.

“Portugal has rebalanced its economy, put its public finances on a much 
stronger footing and avoided major economic scarring,” Oxford’s Ben May wrote 
in a note this week. A2011 bailout 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2011-05-16/eu-finance-ministers-approve-78-billion-euro-bailout-package-for-portugal?cmpid=BBD100926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9872>
 by the IMF and European Union forced painful fiscal and economic reforms. 
After that, the Finance Ministry’s significant control over spending as part of 
the reform package “kept spending plans on track and even made underspending 
relative to target the norm.”

“This spending restraint starkly contrasts with the policies pursued” in the 
likes of the US and elsewhere, May wrote. “Importantly, there’s ongoing 
cross-party support for running a tight fiscal ship, reflecting the 
electorate’s desire to avoid another painful crisis.” For the US, UK and 
France, “as was the case with Portugal, external pressures will probably be the 
catalyst for any significant belt-tightening,” he said.
