Manus was supposed to have been swallowed by Meta. Then Beijing intervened. 
China forced the unwinding of the $2 billion acquisition, leaving the 
Chinese-founded AI startup to start over as an independent company. A few 
months later, that reset is looking rather lucrative: Manus is raising about 
$500 million at a$4 billion valuation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/manus-eyes-4-billion-value-in-first-round-since-meta-breakup?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
, sources said, twice what Meta had agreed to pay. For a company that became a 
test case for howmessy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-27/china-blocks-meta-s-2-billion-acquisition-of-ai-startup-manus?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
 the US-China fight over technology can get, it’s quite the comeback.

The AI boom is creating some unusual borrowing needs, too. GMI Cloud wants 
$300 million 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/nvidia-partner-gmi-cloud-seeks-loan-to-buy-chips-for-thai-site?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
 not for a data center, but for what goes inside it, sources said. The Nvidia 
partner is talking to banks and private-credit funds about a loan to buy chips 
for a facility in Thailand, with Tencent set to use the computing power. Until 
recently, lenders piled into financing the giant buildings needed for AI. Now 
the chips themselves, expensive enough to require hundreds of millions of 
dollars in debt, are becoming an asset class for lenders.

And while money pours into getting hold of more computing power, Huawei is 
racing to make China less reliant on the American technology behind it. The 
company isbringing forward 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/huawei-set-to-unveil-china-s-best-answer-to-nvidia-ai-chip-reign?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
 the debut of its next-generation Ascend 960DT chip to early 2027, several 
months ahead of schedule. Huawei still can’t match Nvidia’s best chips 
one-for-one, so it’s trying something else:connecting huge numbers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-23/huawei-plans-three-year-campaign-to-overtake-nvidia-in-ai-chips?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
 of its own together and making the whole system more powerful. Welcome to the 
next phase of the AI boom: fight over who owns it, borrow billions to build it, 
then wire it all together and hope you can keep up.

, and still find itself falling behind. The Fed’shawkish hike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/the-fed-s-unanimous-25-basis-point-hike-puts-trump-and-warsh-on-collision-course?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
 sent the yen tumbling back toward 156 per dollar, just as Japan’s central bank 
prepares for its own expected increase Friday. The problem is that a BOJ hike 
is already almost entirelypriced in 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/boj-board-hawk-calls-for-nimble-hikes-to-prevent-price-risks?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>

again, pushing the yen toward 160 and putting intervention back on the table. 
Simply hiking may no longer be enough.

Meanwhile bond investors, after eight bruising days, finally got a little 
relief 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/global-bonds-recover-as-warsh-s-inflation-fight-calms-market?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
. From a rate hike, of all things. The Fed’squarter-point increase 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/fed-raises-rates-as-warsh-bucks-trump-to-contain-inflation?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
, and Kevin Warsh’s insistence that the inflation fight isn’t over, reassured 
markets that policymakers won’t let rising prices run unchecked, pulling 
10-year Treasury yields back below 5% and snapping their longest climb in 
months. But this hardly looks like the all-clear: global government yields had 
just hit a 19-year high as war-driven oil prices revived inflation fears. And 
that BOJ decision we mentioned above is the next big test. For bonds, the fire 
alarm may have stopped ringing; the building is still pretty warm.

India’s biggest stock exchange is finally asking investors to put a price on 
itself. The National Stock Exchange hasopened the books 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/nse-subscription-for-india-ipo-starts-with-valuation-in-focus?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9009>
 on a $2.4 billion IPO, potentially India’s second-largest, and the big 
question is whether it’s worth the premium. At the top of the range, NSE would 
trade at about 43 times earnings, far above many of the world’s biggest 
exchanges, though it also boasts a remarkable 62% profit margin. The wrinkle: 
some 60% of operating revenue comes from options trading, just as regulators 
are trying to curb speculation. After waiting a decade to list, NSE is about to 
find out just how much that wait was worth.
