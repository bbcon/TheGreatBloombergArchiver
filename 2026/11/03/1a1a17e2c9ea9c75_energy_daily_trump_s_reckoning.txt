Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Washington reporter Ari Natter looks at the potential fallout for the energy 
sector if US Democrats win control of one or both chambers of Congress.

With polls showing Democrats likely to recapture the House of Representatives 
— and possibly the Senate — in today’sUS midterm elections 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/trump-faces-referendum-as-voters-head-to-polls-in-midterm-elections-2026?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
, the party has been deliberating what it would do with its newfound leverage.

Winning a majority in one or more chambers would give Democrats control of 
congressional committees 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-01/2026-midterm-elections-what-a-democratic-win-would-mean-for-trump?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
 that have the power to investigate the Trump administration, issue subpoenas 
and bring government officials before Congress to answer questions.

Potential targets abound, and energy and natural resources are attracting a 
fair amount of attention.

“I’m bracing for an incredible workload,” Representative Jared Huffman 
<https://huffman.house.gov/>, a California Democrat poised to lead the House’s 
natural resources committee, said in an interview. “It’s going to feel like 
whiplash, because for the last two years there’s been zero oversight and 
investigation from this Congress.”

House lawmakers say they plan to probe the administration’s moves to buy out 
offshore wind leases 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-refunds-4-billion-to-cancel-us-offshore-wind-projects?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
, exempt oil and gas drillers from environmental laws and take equity stakes in
critical-minerals companies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-23/usa-rare-earth-how-the-magnet-maker-landed-1-6-billion-in-us-funding?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
.
The Trump administration has agreed to billions of dollars in settlements this 
year to cancel planned offshore wind projects.Photographer: John Moore/Getty 
Images North America
Hearings and document requests would also likely focus on the Energy 
Department’s process for slashing billions of dollars in grants to blue states, 
as well as President Donald Trump’s efforts togut agencies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-28/at-least-880-workers-were-fired-at-us-weather-agency?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
 such as the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and the National 
Science Foundation.

“We have some oversight to do to try and right the ship,” Zoe Lofgren 
<https://lofgren.house.gov/about>, a California Democrat and the party’s top 
representative on the House science panel, said in an interview.

In the Senate, key figures including Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren have 
indicated they plan to look into Trump’sdiesel supply agreement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/trump-says-putin-agreed-to-release-diesel-into-global-market-mv1bqka7?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
 with Russia,suspicious wartime oil trading 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-10/warren-seeks-federal-probe-of-suspicious-oil-trades-amid-war?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
 and a$1.6 billion deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-07/democrats-probe-cantor-fitzgerald-ties-in-usa-rare-earth-deal?cmpid=BBBXT110326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10891>
 involving critical-minerals company USA Rare Earth Inc.

It’s too soon to say what those efforts might reveal. But at the very least, 
such investigations threaten to complicate Trump’s energy agenda, tying up 
government agencies with multiple requests for documents and for officials to 
testify before Congress.

Ultimately, Democrats will hope to erect guardrails against a president who’s 
governed the past two years with exceptionally little restraint from Capitol 
Hill.

—Ari Natter, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Copper prices remained elevated in October as disruptions at mines in Chile 
kept the market tight. Chilean output has been hit by storms, accidents and 
labor disputes, while tighter scrap supply in China added to price pressure.
BloombergNEF <https://about.bnef.com/> sees supply capacity falling short of 
demand from 2028, with the gap widening as mine production peaks around the 
start of the 2030s.
