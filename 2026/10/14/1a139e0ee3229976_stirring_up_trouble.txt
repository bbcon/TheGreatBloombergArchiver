An infamously complex conflict in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo has 
grown even knottier.

Joseph Kabila — the former leader sanctioned by the US 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-30/us-sanctions-former-congo-president-joseph-kabila-for-rebel-ties?cmpid=BBD101426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10076>
 and sentenced to death in absentia last year for treason — announced on Monday 
thathe’s joining up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/congo-s-ex-president-kabila-joins-rebellion-deepening-crisis?cmpid=BBD101426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10076>
 with a Rwanda-backed rebel group to unseat President Felix Tshisekedi.

Kabila, who ruled Congo for almost two decades, labels his successor a tyrant 
who’s trying to stay in power beyond his allotted mandate, which is set to end 
in 2028. He promised his new movement, the Front for National Refoundation, 
would stop Tshisekedi by force if necessary.

The announcement shook a country already used to uncertainty. It also further 
underminesa fragile peace deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-18/why-trump-s-congo-rwanda-peace-deal-is-so-fragile?cmpid=BBD101426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10076>
 signed between Rwanda and Congo in Washington in December, lauded by President 
Donald Trump as one of the wars he’d ended.
Joseph Kabila.Photographer: Jospin Mwisha/AFP/Getty Images
Washington has worked hard to resolve the conflict through diplomatic efforts, 
promises of shared economic prosperity and, more recently, a series of 
increasingly onerous sanctions against Rwandan government officials and its 
army.

Instead, tiny Rwanda has dug in.

Rwandan President Paul Kagame has long seen eastern Congo as a security 
nightmare 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-27/rwanda-s-kagame-accuses-congo-of-violating-peace-accords?cmpid=BBD101426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10076>
 and a source of wealth for his nation. It appears he’s resolved that if he 
can’t get the Congolese to fix it, he will try himself.

Kagame has allies in Congo’s small population of Tutsis and other speakers of 
the Rwandan language who have at times faced discrimination. And nowthe M23 
movement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/rwanda-backed-rebels-build-30-000-strong-force-un-experts-say?cmpid=BBD101426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10076>
 has a more powerful Congolese face.

Tensions are high about what comes next. Kabila’s family is from Katanga, 
Congo’s copper-rich southeastern region. His father led a rebellion in the 
1990s that began by seizing the mines and suffocating the country’s economy, 
quickly toppling dictator Mobutu Sese Seko.

Without an army of his own, Kabila would need Rwanda’s help, and it’s not 
clear the nation has the stomach to force a coup.

The US says there is no military solution to the conflict in eastern Congo. A 
bad situation is in danger of becoming worse.— Michael J Kavanagh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APvuwFLxR9Y/michael-j-kavanagh?cmpid=BBD101426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10076>
