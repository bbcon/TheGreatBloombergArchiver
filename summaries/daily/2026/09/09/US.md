**Monetary Policy and Bond Markets**
Treasury Secretary Bessent's dual-front market management strategy sharpened in focus on Wednesday. His expanded bond buyback program, framed as an effort to relieve what he characterized as a "fever" in the bond market, was set to disclose its size in a first round of operations. The 10-year yield held near 4.81%, close to multi-decade highs. Critics, including Stanley Druckenmiller — who mentored Bessent in his hedge fund career — have argued publicly that "governments defending prices against fundamentals always lose," a warning that underscores genuine uncertainty about whether the intervention will prove durable or merely delay an adjustment.

**Trade Policy**
Washington moved quickly after Ottawa's retaliatory tariffs, imposing new restrictions on Canadian dairy and alcohol imports effective within three weeks, alongside procurement exclusions for Canadian firms. Bloomberg Economics estimated a worst-case scenario — full USMCA collapse with reciprocal retaliation — could deliver a 2% permanent hit to Canadian GDP. The escalation coincides with Ottawa and the EU accelerating talks on a broader bilateral trade and security arrangement, a dynamic that may progressively complicate Washington's negotiating position.

**Equities**
Earnings momentum remains a counterweight to macro headwinds. Analyst estimate revisions have been net positive for 21 consecutive weeks — the longest upgrade streak in five years — providing bulls with a fundamental anchor even as oil prices and bond yields weigh on sentiment.
