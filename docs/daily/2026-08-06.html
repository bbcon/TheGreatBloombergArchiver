US

Monetary Policy and Inflation

The Fed remains the dominant domestic story. Chairman Kevin Warsh's mixed messaging at last week's press conference — combined with Treasury Secretary Bessent's sign-off on yen intervention — has prompted investors to revisit the "Sell America" trade, weakening the dollar toward its lowest levels since early June. ISM data for the services sector showed continued elevated price pressures, while consumer inflation expectations tracked by the New York Fed reached their highest since 2022 for the three-year horizon. Long-run five-year/five-year breakevens remain anchored near 2%, creating a notable divergence between financial market confidence and household sentiment. Warsh's five task forces reviewing Fed frameworks — including panels on data and inflation targeting — add further uncertainty; any shift away from core PCE as the headline target would risk credibility before the 2% goal is achieved, analysts at Oxford Economics warned.

AppLovin fell 15% on a revenue miss. WPP surged 30% in London after stronger-than-expected turnaround results. The S&P 500 remained near record highs, with weakness confined to specific tech sub-sectors. ---

Technology and Markets

Volatility in Asian technology stocks has reached its most extreme level since the Global Financial Crisis, with the MSCI Asia-Pacific tech index swinging 3.2% lower on Thursday after a 4.2% rally the prior session. The driver remains AI-related memory chips: Sandisk's guidance shortfall served as the proximate catalyst, underscoring how dependent the rally had become on an unbroken demand trajectory. South Korea sits at the epicenter of this dynamic. Chip workers at SK Hynix and Samsung have emerged as a new social elite — bonus packages of up to $400,000 are reshaping dating markets and university admissions, where semiconductor programs have become harder to enter than Seoul National University's natural sciences faculty. Yet the Kospi's 31% drawdown from peak signals that investor confidence in the durability of the AI hardware cycle is deteriorating.

Europe

  • Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel's candidacy to lead the ECB is reported to be advancing.
  • Europe's dry rivers face little relief from near-term rainfall, prolonging shipping and power restrictions.

Germany and the ECB

June factory orders data was the standout European macro release, with the 3.1% monthly gain — led by mechanical engineering and data-processing equipment — exceeding analyst forecasts and adding to evidence that Germany's long-awaited industrial recovery may be gaining traction. The result is meaningful given Germany's role as the eurozone's manufacturing engine, though it represents orders rather than output and global demand uncertainty remains. Nagel's reported progress toward the ECB leadership adds a domestic political dimension; a Bundesbank president at the helm would likely reinforce the institution's hawkish credentials at a moment when European inflation dynamics are still unsettled.

Politics and Migration

Germany's political landscape remains fractured. Polls ahead of two September state elections in eastern Germany show the AfD positioned for strong gains, while Berlin itself saw the anti-capitalist Left party polling first for the same election cycle on an affordability-focused platform. Separately, the mass crossing at Spain's Ceuta enclave — whose origins remain disputed — triggered demands from Berlin and Rome that Madrid restore border control, highlighting migration as a persistent fault line across the EU's member governments. ---

Brazil and Mexico

Brazil's rate cut marks a continuation of its easing cycle, though the pace and market reaction were not fully detailed in available coverage. In Mexico, easing inflation — the slowest since late 2020 — has improved consumer sentiment and lifted Sheinbaum's approval, providing political cover ahead of potential central bank decisions. Mexican and Czech central bank officials were flagged as likely to hold rates steady. The US suspension of activities in Michoacán injects supply-chain uncertainty into the avocado trade, one of the bilateral relationship's more politically visible agricultural flows, arriving as US-Canada and US-Mexico USMCA negotiations remain unresolved ahead of the August 19 tariff deadline.

Argentina

Milei's investment liberalization agenda suffered a legislative setback as the foreign land bill was diluted, reflecting the limits of executive momentum against nationalist sentiment. The China swap renewal is more consequential near-term: it stabilizes Argentina's reserve position at a critical juncture without requiring fresh IMF engagement, though it deepens Beijing's financial footprint in the country. ---

Global

  • Gold surged 4.3% to above $4,600/oz — its strongest day since February — as dollar weakness overtook real-rate dynamics as the primary driver; State Street's Aakash Doshi cited the dollar sliding to its weakest since early June.
  • WTI crude traded near $75.30/bbl and Brent near $79.47/bbl, well below levels that would sustain acute monetary tightening pressure.

Hormuz and Energy

The Iran-Oman agreement on a proposed transit route represents the most concrete diplomatic progress since the US-Iran ceasefire in mid-June, and markets responded accordingly — crude oil retreating, gold finding support from a weaker dollar rather than geopolitical risk premium. The UAE has served as a critical buffer, moving more oil through Hormuz than any other producer over the past two months. Saudi Aramco's unexpected price cut for Asian customers may reflect both diplomatic signaling and a softer demand read. Houthi threats to Red Sea shipping remain an active complication for alternative eastbound routes, limiting the degree to which the Hormuz situation can be considered resolved.

Monetary Policy Divergence and the Dollar

The combination of Warsh's ambiguous Fed communication and Bessent's yen intervention support has reignited "Sell America" dynamics in bond and FX markets — Treasury yields near multi-decade highs, the dollar softening — even as US equities hold near records. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly warned that elevated inflation may require more aggressive action. India is emerging as one of Asia's monetary policy holdouts. The structural divergence between anchored long-run inflation expectations in financial markets and elevated consumer near-term forecasts remains an unresolved tension with global implications for rate paths.
