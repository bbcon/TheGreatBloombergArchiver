**ECB Policy and Leadership**
The ECB's hold Thursday was fully anticipated, but Lagarde's simultaneous announcement of an early February departure removes an important anchor of continuity. Her presidency leaves a nuanced legacy: she successfully returned eurozone inflation to the 2% target without triggering recession and created the Transmission Protection Instrument in 2022, but the ECB was widely criticized for moving too slowly to tighten after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Her stated rationale — a desire to speak freely on non-monetary matters, including potentially French domestic politics ahead of April's presidential election — itself generated internal friction. EU leaders will convene November 26–27 to begin a successor selection process; Klaas Knot and Pablo Hernandez de Cos are most frequently cited. Whoever takes the role may inherit a crisis-management brief almost immediately.

**France: Stagflationary Pressure**
France's Q3 GDP contraction of 0.1%, combined with CPI at a near three-year high of 3.8%, presents a structurally difficult policy combination. The fiscal deficit exceeds 5% of GDP despite government revenues above 50% of GDP — well above the G7 average — leaving Paris with limited room to either raise taxes or cut spending ahead of the April presidential election. French sovereign spreads have widened materially, and Credit Agricole's CEO issued an unusual France-specific risk warning. Moody's opted against a downgrade Monday, providing brief relief after French yields reached 24-year highs the prior week, but the underlying fiscal credibility concern has not been addressed. ECB officials have explicitly acknowledged that sovereign bond volatility will tighten financial conditions, partially substituting for rate action.

**UK Fiscal and Corporate Developments**
Chancellor Healey's Wednesday budget achieved its primary objective of avoiding an adverse gilt market reaction, avoiding broad tax increases while introducing a lower capital gains rate for entrepreneurs. Analysts noted it leaves limited fiscal headroom given rising energy costs and the prospect of further Bank of England rate hikes — Bloomberg Economics' BOESPEAK model reached its most hawkish reading since early 2023. European bank earnings were broadly constructive: UBS attracted $55.1 billion in net new wealth assets Wednesday, though CEO Ermotti escalated rhetoric around Swiss capital requirements with an explicit relocation warning. GSK fell 5.4% Wednesday on slumping vaccine sales; Adyen dropped 13% Thursday after discontinuing medium-term guidance.
