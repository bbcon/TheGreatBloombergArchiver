• China's biggest stimulus package since 2024 underwhelmed markets, with analysts broadly concluding it would support the lower end of the 4.5%–5% GDP growth target without resolving structural imbalances.
• China's manufacturing PMI expanded for the first time since June, offering a modestly positive end to Q3.
• Overseas investors sold $2.1 billion of Indian equities and $1.1 billion of sovereign bonds in September as rising oil prices and global yields weighed on sentiment.
• The Reserve Bank of Australia hiked its policy rate to 4.6%, the highest since 2011, intensifying pressure on Australia's housing market.

**China Stimulus: Targeted Rather Than Transformative**
Beijing unveiled mortgage subsidies for lower-priced homes and expanded central bank relending quotas by 200 billion yuan ($30 billion) to support technology and infrastructure lending. The package represents the largest policy intervention in two years but falls well short of the broad demand stimulus that would address China's structural imbalances. Goldman Sachs described the measures as "more significant as a policy signal than as a near-term growth impulse," while Morgan Stanley characterized them as a "targeted cushion." The manufacturing PMI's return to expansion in September provides modest reassurance, but the property sector's downward trajectory and weak domestic consumption appear unlikely to reverse materially. Equity markets sank to more than one-year lows this month, reflecting investor skepticism.

**India and Australia Capital Flows**
Foreign outflows from Indian assets accelerated in September, with the rupee among Asia's worst performers this quarter and the 10-year government bond yield touching a two-year high. The combination of elevated oil prices and rising global yields is eroding the carry attractiveness that drove record inflows in June. In Australia, the RBA's 25bp hike to 4.6% — combined with the removal of tax incentives for property investors — is visibly repricing the housing market, with high-end Sydney values down 10.7% over the past year.
