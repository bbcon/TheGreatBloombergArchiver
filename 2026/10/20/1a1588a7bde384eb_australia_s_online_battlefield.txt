Australia is serious about its crusade against online harm. The government is 
set to sueFacebook, TikTok and other major digital platforms for alleged 
non-compliance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/australia-to-sue-facebook-tiktok-for-alleged-breaches-of-ban?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10309>
 with a nationwide social media ban for under-16s, Communications Minister 
Anika Wells said in an interview.

The country’s online safety regulator, eSafety Commissioner, said in March it 
wasinvestigating Facebook and Instagram 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-30/meta-tiktok-flagged-for-potential-australia-kids-ban-breaches?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10309>
 — both owned by Meta — TikTok, Snapchat and YouTube for potentially failing to 
adhere to the law.

The legislation requires the companies to take reasonable steps to stop 
underage users from holding social media accounts, part of a bid to protect 
them from harmful online content. But an eSafety report in July found most 
underage users wereable to keep their accounts or create new ones 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/australia-s-social-media-ban-sees-only-small-dip-in-teen-users?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10309>
, largely due to a lack of effective age checks on the platforms.
