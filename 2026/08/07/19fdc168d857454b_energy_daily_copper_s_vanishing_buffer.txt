Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Europe Metals and Mining Team Leader Mark Burton takes a look at what’s behind 
the copper crunch that’s pushing prices of the metal higher.

The copper market is contending with a fresh bout of supply tightness in the 
London Metal Exchange’s warehousing network, and it’s looking severe enough 
that it might lift prices tonew highs <bbg://news/stories/TJCWITT96OSI>.

Futures on the LME this week pushed past $14,000 a ton, a ceiling that’s only 
been breached on a handful of days this year. Some traders see prices soon 
surging past the record $14,500-plus level that was touched during a bout of
speculative buying 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-29/copper-rallies-to-record-on-lme-as-base-metals-extend-bull-run?cmpid=BBBXT080726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7494>
 in China at the end of January.

The timing of the move may seem counterintuitive for a metal that’s long been 
viewed as a bellwether for the global economy. The war in Iran has piled 
pressure on manufacturers who are facing a surge in prices for fuel, energy, 
and other industrial inputs, and it may prompt central bankers to raise 
interest rates that would add to the squeeze on their bottom line.

Rather than being reflective of any brightening in that demand outlook, the 
rally has instead been fueled by a steady outflow of metal linked to trade 
dislocations caused by the gravitational pull of theworld’s two largest 
economies <bbg://news/stories/TJ8TGCT96OSG>.
The copper market is tightening fast, with a surge in US-bound shipments and 
rising orders in China fueling a rally that could take global benchmark prices 
to new highs.Photographer: Dhiraj Singh/Bloomberg
In the US, copper imports are surging as traders capture arbitrage profits 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/us-copper-inflows-surge-as-market-awaits-trump-tariff-call?cmpid=BBBXT080726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7494>
 linked to the possibility that the Trump administration will impose tariffs. 
And in China, orders for imported copper are rising fast as buyers face 
constraints on both the supply of mined ore and scrap copper.

Collectively, the US and China are creating a two-way pull on the LME’s 
dwindling inventories, with readily available stocks now standing at just 
92,900 tons.

That would be enough to last manufacturers in the 27-million-ton-a-year copper 
market a little over a day. And while there are still much bigger stockpiles in 
the global industrial supply chain — including vast inventories in the US — the 
volumes stored with the LME have an outsized impact on the direction of prices.

Previous drops below the 100,000-ton threshold helped to trigger spikes in 
spot prices on multiple occasions in recent years.

The reasons for the latest bout of tightness may appear prosaic to investors 
who are usually more focused on the megatrends — industrialization, 
urbanization and decarbonization — that have reliably driven copper demand 
higher, even in the face of manufacturers’ stiff resistance to rising prices.

But with copper miners facing structural geological constraints 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-07/codelco-confronts-debt-output-concerns-as-copper-demand-soars-on-ai?cmpid=BBBXT080726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7494>
 in delivering enough new production to meet those needs, even more isolated 
bursts in spot demand can quickly put the market off-balance.

The LME’s warehousing network is designed as a last-ditch source of supply to 
absorb such stresses, and when inventories start to run dry, they quickly 
become the tail that wags the dog.

—Mark Burton, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Power disruptions are improving the economics of microgrids as a way to make 
access to electricity more resilient. Wildfires in California increasingly 
cause outages by damaging grid infrastructure or triggering precautionary 
shutoffs. Average annual wildfire acres in California have risen almost 
fivefold since 1990. Concerns about reliability have helped spur decentralized 
systems that can operate independently of the main grid. Installations have 
risen after major blazes.
