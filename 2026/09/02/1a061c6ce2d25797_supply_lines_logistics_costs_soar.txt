Logistics Costs in the US Hit Higher Cruising Altitude
Transport, inventory and warehousing costs show few signs of easing in the US

Logistics costs in the US have reached a higher cruising altitude since the 
war in Iran began six months ago, adding to worries about “supply-driven 
inflation,” according to a new survey.

The latest Logistics Managers’ Index released 
<https://www.the-lmi.com/august-2026-logistics-managers-index.html> this week 
showed aggregate logistics costs — what businesses shell out for inventories, 
warehousing and transportation — is hovering around 240, near where it’s been 
since the US-Israel invasion of Iran in February.

The fighting, which flared again 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/us-launches-new-round-of-iran-strikes-as-strait-tensions-grow?cmpid=BBD090226_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8308>
 overnight, kicked off an energy shock that’s raised the cost ofdiesel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/diesel-margins-surge-to-highest-on-record-as-supply-tightens?cmpid=BBD090226_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8308>
, chemicals and fertilizers.

The gauge is designed on a scale of 0 to 300, with 150 set as the breakeven 
level. It remained under 200 for much of 2024 but increased last year as 
President Donald Trump put tariffs on American imports. It jumped to about 230 
in March this year and has remained close to that level since.

In August, the LMI’s broad read on logistics costs stayed elevated despite 
underlying signals normally associated with easing cost pressures:

 * Warehouse capacity rising at its fastest pace this year
 * A slower rate of contraction in transport capacity
 * And a deceleration in inventory building.
“Essentially, this month’s report paints a picture of logistics costs that 
seem to be rapidly increasing no matter what the underlying situation is,” the 
report said. “These high costs are not only putting significant pressure on 
supply chains, but on consumers as well.”

Inflation Fight

The report notes recent San Francisco Fed research 
<https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/data-and-indicators/supply-and-demand-driven-pce-inflation/>
 breaking down inflation by its supply- and demand-side forces.

The LMI report also noted that inflation has outpaced income growth and that 
the labor market shed jobs in July, though the August employment reportdue out 
Friday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-29/us-jobs-report-seen-backing-warsh-view-of-labor-market?cmpid=BBD090226_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8308>
 is expected to show a modest rebound.

Read More: Carney Sees Trade Talks Resuming If US Stops ‘Throwing Shade’ 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/carney-says-trade-talks-can-resume-if-us-stops-throwing-shade?cmpid=BBD090226_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8308>

“The escalation of the trade war between the US and Canada is unlikely to help 
matters,” the LMI report said.

With about $68 billion worth of goods crossing the border in June, it said 
“the proposed 50% tariff on our second-largest trade partner would have 
significant cost impacts on several industries, with construction, auto 
manufacturing, and energy costs being hit particularly hard.”
