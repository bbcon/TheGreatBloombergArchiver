As France’s presidential campaign heats up, Emmanuel Macron is taking the 
rentrée — the back-to-school season — seriously, determined to show that he 
won’t spend his final months in office sitting on the sidelines.

His continental tour, taking in Sweden, the Netherlands and a Franco-German 
summit, is designed to burnish his legacy as a champion of European sovereignty.

Then there’s his visit to London, where yesterday he marked the loan of the 
Bayeux Tapestry to the British Museum, a project freighted with symbolism about 
the centuries-old ties and rivalries between France and the UK.
The Bayeux Tapestry before its voyage to the UK.Photographer: Loic 
Venance/AFP/Getty Images
Macron and Andy Burnham, who met for the first time 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/burnham-to-address-uk-parliament-as-mps-return-from-summer-break?cmpid=BBD090326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8447>
 since Burnham took over as prime minister, will today reviewdefense and energy 
cooperation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/sweden-officially-signs-contract-for-new-frigates-from-france?cmpid=BBD090326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8447>
.

Burnham is expected to continue Keir Starmer’s post-Brexit rapprochement with 
Europe as the continent seeks greater independence from Donald Trump’s US.

It’s one of several common challenges for the UK and France.

Both capitals share uncomfortable fiscal pressures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/french-lawmaker-urges-fast-track-budget-to-avoid-costly-impasse?cmpid=BBD090326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8447>
 that have put them at the center ofglobal bond-market jitters 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/surge-in-uk-borrowing-costs-halves-government-s-fiscal-headroom?cmpid=BBD090326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8447>
.

Dealing with irregular migrants crossing the Channel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/uk-and-france-plan-to-counter-people-smugglers-mega-dinghies?cmpid=BBD090326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8447>
 has long been a joint bone of contention.

That gives the Bayeux Tapestry added symbolism: Since the Norman Conquest of 
1066 that it depicts, the Channel has been both barrier and bridge between the 
English and their continental counterparts; a moat separating rivals, a trade 
and cultural link.

Burnham has suggested that a progressive resurgence of the sort he represents 
could turn back the tide of anti-immigration populism.

The political counterpoint will be on show tomorrow, when Jordan Bardella, 
leader of Marine Le Pen’s far-right National Rally party, is due to speak at
Reform UK’s party conference 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/farage-plots-fightback-at-biggest-ever-reform-uk-conference?cmpid=BBD090326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8447>
 in Birmingham.

In London last night, Macron compared the effort to bring the fragile Bayeux 
Tapestry across the Channel to an odyssey.

With his 10-year stint at France’s helm nearly over, a final judgement on the 
impact of Macron’s own political journey is still pending. — Ania Nussbaum

Roughly 5,000 US sailors disembarked from the USS Abraham Lincoln aircraft 
carrier in the notorious city of Pattaya in Thailand after a grueling 286 days 
at sea. That stretch included a modern-day record stretch between port calls 
after the ship was redeployed for combat operations in the Middle East, leading 
to long periods of monotony so trying that some sailors apparently threatened 
to jump overboard. The pressure on the carrier points tobroader strains on the 
US military
