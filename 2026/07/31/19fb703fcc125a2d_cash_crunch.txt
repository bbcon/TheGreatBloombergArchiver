Deep Dive: London’s Cabbies vs. Driverless Cars
The Goulding family in London.Photographer: Holly-Marie Cato for Bloomberg
David Goulding and his four sons drive London’s iconic taxis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/waymo-s-london-launch-tests-the-future-of-iconic-black-cabs?cmpid=073126_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7211>
 for a living, heirs to a century-old service that has survived wars, the 
onslaught of Uber, and Covid. But they now face perhaps their greatest 
disruption yet: competition from vehicles with no driver at all.

 * Vehicles from Waymo, Wayve and Baidu are currently being tested with 
“validation” drivers behind the wheel, and could, pending government approval, 
offer driverless rides to Londoners by the end of the year.
 * The world’s most thoroughly trained cabbies spend up to four years 
completing “the Knowledge” — one of the world’s toughest professional 
qualifications.
 * It’s a trial fewer drivers have taken on in recent years, as customers 
switch away from London’s black cabs to cheaper private-hire services such as 
Uber.
 * The head of the taxi drivers’ association believes autonomous cars won’t 
dent the black cab trade but will struggle to work effectively in London and 
are likely to “cause more congestion in Europe’s most congested city.”
