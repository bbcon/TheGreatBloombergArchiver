**Monetary Policy and Growth Divergence**
The BOJ's decision to hold at 1.25% was entirely in line with consensus, but Governor Ueda's tone at the subsequent press conference leaned hawkish, explicitly noting that a December hike remains on the table. The yen strengthened in response. The juxtaposition within the region is striking: Taiwan's economy is expanding at a pace rarely seen in developed markets, fueled almost entirely by AI semiconductor demand, while Japan navigates a careful normalization after three decades of deflation. These trajectories imply continued divergence in regional capital flows and currency dynamics.

**China: Politics and Corporate Results**
The Chinese Communist Party's fifth plenum concluded with record-low attendance among senior officials — a reflection of Xi Jinping's anti-corruption purge that has shortened average provincial party chief tenure to 26.5 months from 43 months. Zhang Shengmin, the Central Military Commission's anti-graft chief, was elevated to the Politburo — the first such promotion in over three decades. Separately, strong bank earnings suggest the worst of China's financial sector profitability squeeze may be passing, though the sustainability of margin recovery warrants monitoring given ongoing property sector headwinds.
