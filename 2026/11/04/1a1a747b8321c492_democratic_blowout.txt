A blue wave hits Washington

By Zahra Hirji 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVypnhGtqak/zahra-hirji?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>

For two years, the Trump administration and Republicans in Congress have 
exerted almost absolute control of US environmental policy, reorienting 
government supportaway from climate action 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2025-trump-policies-climate-science/?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
, clean energy and stricter pollution controls to further embrace fossil fuels 
and deregulation.

That’s about to change in a big way. After securing decisive majorities in 
both the House and Senate in ahistoric blue wave 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/live-blog/2026-11-03/us-midterm-elections-live-updates?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
 Tuesday, Democrats will have new leverage. Former officials and policy experts 
expect them to use it to step up scrutiny of the Trump administration’s record 
on energy and the environment. The shakeup could also complicate, or sink, a 
bipartisan deal on permitting reform.
Attendees react as a news broadcast displays election results during an 
election night event with Ohio Democrats in Columbus, Ohio, on Nov. 3.
Photographer: Jessica Phelps/Bloomberg via Bloomberg News
With both chambers flipped, Democrats next session will set the agenda for 
hearings and will wield investigative powers, able to issue subpoenas for 
officials and industry to testify or produce documents for review. Their 
victory allows for a level of oversight that’s simply not possible for the 
minority party.

Appropriations could be a focus, given that the Trump White House has 
repeatedly frozen and withheld funds appropriated by Congress, such as for 
National Science Foundation and Environmental Protection Agency grants.

“I would expect Congress to reassert its oversight of the budget and start 
looking at what they’re going to do to enforce that,” said Josh Freed, senior 
vice president for the climate and energy program at the liberal think tank 
Third Way.

Democrats are planning investigations into Interior Secretary Doug Burgum and 
his team, including over the bungled Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool upgrades 
and officials’ dealings with oil and gas companies, Politico has reported. EPA 
chief Lee Zeldin may also face scrutiny.

Another possible target of investigators: businesses that made deals with or 
got regulatory reprieves from the administration.

“Democrats with gavels and subpoena authority in one or both chambers are 
going to put significant countervailing pressure on industry,” said Zealan 
Hoover, who was a senior EPA advisor during the Biden administration. He 
anticipates congressional reviews of companies that cut deals to trade offshore 
wind leases for fossil fuel infrastructure and got out of Clean Air Act 
requirements.

The midterms also have the potential to impact policy before the next session 
of Congress — through the fate of the controversial permitting reform bill, the 
Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, or BAAJA. A handful of 
Republicans and Democrats in the Senate announced in late September that they’d
secured a deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/senators-reach-deal-on-bill-to-speed-permits-for-energy-projects?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
 to speed up the approvals of pipelines, power lines and other infrastructure 
projects. But since then, a number of Democrats, environmentalists and other 
stakeholders haveraised concerns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/environmental-groups-criticize-senate-permitting-deal-as-bad-bargain?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
 with the proposal.

Going into the elections, senators were planning to introduce an amendment to 
the bill in the coming days, followed by a procedural vote and final vote 
within weeks; the House likely wouldn’t vote until after Thanksgiving.

But it’s an open question whether there’s sufficient Democratic support to 
pass the deal now. There’s a possibility that the party decides to punt 
discussions down the road to when they also control the White House, some 
policy experts have warned, noting that Trump has already threatened to veto 
any legislation coming out of a Democrat-controlled Congress. If they drop the 
issue now, there’s a strong chance that an alternative doesn’t pass until 
there’s a Democratic president.

Bracing for change on data centers

By Lynn Doan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQdgBfXOpUg/lynn-doan?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
,Brody Ford 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVFMLHVLs6o/brody-ford?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
, andBrian Eckhouse 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASeC4C-3atc/brian-eckhouse?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>

Data center developers seeking to build dozens of sprawling artificial 
intelligence campuses across Texas were already dealing with a suspension of 
approvals under Republican Texas Governor Greg Abbott.

Under Gina Hinojosa, elected 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/democrat-hinojosa-wins-texas-governor-race-cnn-projects?cmpid=BBD110426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10996>
 on Tuesday as the first Democratic governor of the Lone Star State in more 
than three decades, they’re facing their worst-case scenario.

As part of her campaign, Hinojosa repeatedly called for an immediate 
moratorium on data center development. She’s described Abbott’s vows to rein in 
data center operators as “nothing more than an election year stunt” and has 
accused developers of “raising our utility rates and depleting our water.” 
She’s vowed to halt approvals until “a legislative session that puts Texans 
first so that we don’t have any more communities in Texas who are powerless 
against these global corporations.”
Gina Hinojosa during a rally outside a polling location at the Austin 
Recreation Center in Austin, Texas, US, on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026.Photographer: 
Jordan Vonderhaar/Bloomberg
Combining Hinojosa’s victory with those of other Democrats who won 
gubernatorial races from Georgia to Ohio and Pennsylvania could leave half of 
proposed AI data center additions across the US vulnerable to stricter local 
scrutiny and pushback. All of them made more aggressive data center regulation 
a central part of their campaigns, and their wins underscore the intensity of 
the growing voter backlash against the sites powering AI systems worldwide.

The results were part of a broader Democratic rout that wrested party control 
of the US House and Senate away from Republicans, propelled by voters’ 
frustration with the cost of living and the war in Iran. Democrats are poised 
to put closer scrutiny on Silicon Valley interests that had largely been spared 
during President Donald Trump’s first two years in office.

From the largest US firms like Meta Platforms Inc. and Amazon.com Inc. to data 
center specialists such as CoreWeave Inc., the industry is pouring trillions of 
dollars into the computing hubs essential to AI. That’s in turn fueling 
unprecedented demand for the gear that goes into those data centers, from 
Nvidia Corp. accelerators to memory and cooling systems.

The threat now is that policy-makers turn up the heat on those increasingly 
unpopular projects, slowing the infrastructure buildout and hampering the pace 
of AI rollouts that major companies are counting on to boost their bottom lines.
