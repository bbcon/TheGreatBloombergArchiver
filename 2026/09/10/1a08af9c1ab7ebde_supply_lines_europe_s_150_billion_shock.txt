European Union companies are being outmatched by Chinese competitors across 
the globe, a growingthreat to jobs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/vw-board-gives-ceo-blume-unanimous-backing-to-overhaul-carmaker?cmpid=BBD091026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8702>
 that’s stepping up pressure on officials in Brussels to do something.

Bloomberg Economics just published an analysis that quantifies the EU’s loss 
of market share to Chinese manufacturers: 0.7% of its gross domestic product in 
2025, or about $150 billion. That’s up from 0.5% a year earlier. (Click here <> 
for the full report on the Bloomberg Terminal.)

“That’s a substantial bite out of already feeble growth, and there’s likely 
more to come,” BE’s Nicole Gorton-Caratelli and Maeva Cousin write in the 
report. “That impact looks set to grow. China’s exports have continued to rise 
in 2026, especially in key European export goods like automotives and some 
chemicals.”

Full Story: Europe Is Trying to Gauge How Much Pain It Can Take From China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/europe-is-trying-to-gauge-how-much-pain-it-can-take-from-china?cmpid=BBD091026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8702>

China shock 2.0 is an increasingly urgent threat for EU leaders aiming to 
respond with new tariffs tools, export controls and investment screens at a 
summit in Brussels next month.

Perhaps the bigger concern is Brussels will struggle to come up with a unified 
response because of fears that the economic costs of Beijing’s retaliation 
might be painful, too.

One EU diplomat said they essentially have to choose between the current, 
lower-intensity shock that threatens to wipe out some industries or to embrace 
more short-term pain in a bid to preserve those jobs for the future.

Chinese officials are confident in their leverage heading into any potential 
showdown, emboldened by Beijing’s ability last year toweaponize its dominance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/china-is-exporting-20-fewer-magnets-to-us-despite-trade-truce?cmpid=BBD091026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8702>
 in critical minerals against the west.

“The basic logic of a trade war isn’t about who wins or loses — it’s about the 
ability to inflict pain,” said Tu Xinquan, a former adviser to China’s Commerce 
Ministry. “Ultimately, it tests which side has a greater capacity to withstand 
that pain.”
