**Brazil: Election and Markets**
The October 4 first-round ballot is the region's most consequential near-term political event. The election has been complicated by disinformation — including a fabricated claim about Brazil's patron saint that reached the Supreme Court — and by the court's own unprecedented institutional strains. Market implications are significant: Capital Economics estimates Brazilian equities outperformed peers by roughly 20% in the 100 working days following Bolsonaro senior's 2018 victory and underperformed by 15% after Lula's 2022 win. A Flávio Bolsonaro victory would also raise the probability of US tariff relief on Brazilian goods — currently at 25% — while a Lula win could weigh on the real despite its year-to-date resilience. August labor market data provided Lula a favorable pre-election data point, with job creation beating expectations. If neither candidate secures an outright majority Sunday, a runoff follows October 25.

**Commodity Dynamics and Regional Policy**
Chile's copper output deterioration — the worst monthly performance in over 15 years — is providing fundamental support to global copper prices at a moment when AI infrastructure investment is generating structural demand. The supply shortfall is unlikely to reverse quickly given the capital intensity and lead times involved in expanding mine capacity. Brazil's beef exporters face an abrupt disruption as Chinese tariffs triggered by quota exhaustion redirect supply toward alternative markets, with implications for global protein price dynamics. Colombia's central bank held rates as expected, navigating between still-elevated inflation and slowing growth, with El Niño-related hydroelectric and agricultural risks complicating the path to easing — a dynamic T. Rowe Price is monitoring through sovereign credit exposure analysis. Mexico expressed growing confidence in reaching a bilateral US trade agreement covering steel, aluminum, and light vehicles, a development that would meaningfully de-escalate bilateral tensions and support manufacturing investment sentiment, though the outcome remains subject to US domestic political dynamics ahead of November midterms.
