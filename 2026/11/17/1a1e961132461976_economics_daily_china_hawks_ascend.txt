Hawks Ascend

Just days after the downfall of the artificial intelligence industry’s most 
vocal advocate for selling advanced chips to China — Nvidia founder Jensen 
Huang — the US Commerce Department has unveiled a new set ofdraft export 
controls 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-limit-remote-access-to-advanced-ai-chips?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
. The company itself has responded byforming a new committee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/nvidia-forms-export-control-board-committee-after-chip-curbs?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
 for its board to oversee the issue.

If authorities follow through, they will amount to one of the most stringent 
set of restrictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/us-chip-curbs-force-xi-to-weigh-response-before-trump-summit?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
 in years, and will pose a test for a US-China trade truce that’s stood for a 
little over a year now. Coming on the eve of Trump’s third tête-à-tête with 
Chinese President Xi Jinping of 2026, they also demonstrate that, for now, 
China hawks hold the high ground in Washington.

Slated to be on the Air Force One journey to Asia with the president: both 
Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who 
have emerged as opposite poles in the China debate.

After news coverage of Nvidia’s Huang put a spotlight on the chipmaker’s 
large-scale sales tocustomers in southeast Asia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/asia-s-data-center-hotbed-hit-by-us-chip-curbs-as-trump-visits?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
, concerns deepened that much of that business was ultimately tied to Chinese 
companies that are restricted from directly buying Nvidia’s most advanced 
products.

Bessent went public 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/bessent-says-treasury-favored-megaspeed-on-trade-blacklist?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
 on Friday in saying that the Treasury, State Department and US Trade 
Representative’s office had all favored putting Megaspeed, a big buyer of 
Nvidia chips in Singapore, on the list of entities subject to specific US 
licensing requirements. Essentially blaming Lutnick for Megaspeed’s chip 
purchases, he said, “There was a breach of protocols and we’re going to make 
sure that the breach doesn’t occur again.”

When Commerce released its statement Monday, it specified that the interim 
rule was “written by and at the direction of 
<https://media.bis.gov/sites/default/files/documents/BIS%20Issues%20Rule%20Addressing%20Remote%20Access%20to%20American%20Super%20Intelligence%20Technology.pdf>
” the Treasury and National Security Council.

That marks a “striking” institutional shift, Michael Deng 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXV_5RkpeTk/michael-deng?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
, a geo-economics analyst with Bloomberg Economics, wrote in a note Monday. 
“The sequence suggests Commerce wasunwilling to go as far 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/nvidia-crisis-spurs-scrutiny-of-agency-that-oversees-chip-curbs?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
” as it did.

A senior US official told reporters the measures shouldn’t trigger retaliation 
from Beijing, given that China has publicly denied relying on chip smuggling or 
remote access to top American chips. “Washington is presenting this package as 
enforcement against routes China says it doesn’t use,” as Deng put it. The 
US-China meetings this week on the sidelines of the Asia Pacific Cooperation 
forum in Shenzhen, China may reveal whether indeed thetrade truce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-extends-pause-on-24-us-tariffs-to-january-keeps-10-rate?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
 holds.

“If Beijing instead treats the rule as a new restriction, the remote-access 
crackdown could make any APEC understanding harder to sustain and reopen the 
technology fight,” Deng wrote.

Need-to-Know Research

The reverberations of last week’s bigger-than-expected increase in US consumer 
prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/us-core-cpi-rises-more-than-forecast-0-3-supporting-rate-hike?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
, along with hawkish messaging from Fed policymakers, continue to sound, with 
Deutsche Bank now anticipating that 10-year Treasury yields will remain above 
5% for much of the next two years.

As recently as September, just in the wake of the Fed’s first rate hike in the 
current cycle, Deutsche Bank had expected that 10-year yields to retreat 
somewhat in coming months, to 4.75% by June. Now, the bank’s strategists 
including Matthew Raskin are penciling in 5% for June, 5.05% for the end of 
next year and 5.15% for December 2028.

That suggests no relief for US mortgage rates 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/us-mortgage-rates-decline-for-fourth-straight-week-to-7-11?cmpid=BBD111726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11499>
. The key role 10-year yields play in setting home loans was one reason why 
Treasury chief Bessent chose them as his key metric. Last week, Deutsche Bank’s 
US economics team led by Matthew Luzzetti added a rate hike to its projections, 
now seeing three quarter-point moves by March. By the end of 2028, the team 
sees the Fed’s neutral rate settling around neutral — 3.75% to 4%, or where it 
is right now.
