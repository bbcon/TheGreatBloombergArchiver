Starbucks is putting the sale sign up in one of its biggest and more 
successful markets.

The US coffee chain has tapped advisers to prepare for a potential sale of its 
Japan business for several billion dollars 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/starbucks-is-said-to-engage-banks-for-sale-of-japan-business?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11840>
, Bloomberg reported exclusively today. It’s working with banks including 
Citigroup and Morgan Stanley and has started sounding out interest from some 
strategic and financial investors.

Starbucks has about 2,000 coffeehouses in Japan, making it one of the 
company’s biggest markets. It has a long history in Japan, where it outperforms 
local chains such as Doutor and Tully’s. The storied brand established a joint 
venture with Sazaby League in 1995 and held an IPO for the local unit in 2001. 
In 2014, Sazaby sold its shares in the venture back to Starbucks, which 
delisted the unit from the Japanese stock market a year later.

The plans, and the recent sale of control of its China stores 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-03/starbucks-sells-60-stake-in-china-unit-to-boyu-for-4-billion?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11840>
, signal that Starbucks ispivoting to asset-light joint ventures to accelerate 
growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/starbucks-said-to-weigh-japan-unit-options-including-stake-sale?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11840>
 outside of the US, our Bloomberg Intelligence analysts have said previously. 
But while the coffee culture is booming globally, there’s also alot more 
competition for Starbucks these days 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-04/starbucks-sold-china-control-to-bidder-pledging-stability?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11840>

China Dismisses US Tariff Prospect as ‘Political Maneuvering’

China called the prospect of new US tariffs on its exports “political 
maneuvering” and said the two nations should work together to maintain their 
trade truce. The comments came in response to a report by Bloomberg News that 
the Trump administration was planning to announce new tariffs on goods from 
China, as well as the European Union and more than a dozen other economies.
