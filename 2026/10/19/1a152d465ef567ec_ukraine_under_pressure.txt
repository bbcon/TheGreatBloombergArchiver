A couple of hundred miles along the Mediterranean coast from Antalya, where 
Turkey is hosting next month’s COP31 climate summit, is Adanalioglu Beach. 
Along its sands,pieces of shredded plastic 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-19/turkey-is-europe-s-plastic-dumpsite-giving-it-up-will-be-hard?cmpid=101926_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10248>
 in bright, artificial colors regularly wash ashore.

 * Experts blame recycling facilities in nearby Adana, which process waste 
from the UK and Europe as well as domestic plastic, for allowing the particles 
to leak into the environment.
 * They litter the banks of irrigation canals feeding the Seyhan River, which 
empties into the Mediterranean Sea about a dozen miles from the beach, with sea 
currents able to carry that pollution along the coast.
 * Turkey is the top recipient of plastic waste from the UK and the EU, 
importing hundreds of thousands of tons a year.
 * Ahead of COP31, Turkish officials and environmental groups are pressing for 
tougher action on plastic pollution and recycling. Last year,global 
negotiations to curb plastic waste failed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-15/global-plastic-talks-fail-as-hardline-positions-block-deal?cmpid=101926_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10248>
 as some countries wanted to focus on collection and recycling. Now, Turkey is 
seeking a commitment to halve the growth in global waste by 2035.
