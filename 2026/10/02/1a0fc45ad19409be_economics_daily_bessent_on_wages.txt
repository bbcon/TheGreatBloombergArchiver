Bessent on Wages

Slicing and dicing economic indicators to focus on the most attractive figures 
has probably been a practice for public office holders since the invention of 
measured data. What’s particularly interesting in recent years is hearing a 
similar message from officeholders from different parties.

Both Janet Yellen and her successor, when faced with high US inflation rates, 
have dug out figures that show many Americans are actually enjoying improved 
purchasing power nonetheless. Yellen’sdata-point 
<https://home.treasury.gov/news/featured-stories/an-update-to-the-purchasing-power-of-american-households>
 back in 2024 was that the median-earning worker could buy the same basket of 
goods and services as in 2019, but with nearly$1,400 left over 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-25/yellen-says-biden-would-seek-extension-of-some-trump-tax-cuts?cmpid=BBD100226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261002&utm_content=9588>
 to save or spend.

For Scott Bessent, his favored sub-indicator lately has been wage gains for 
the bottom quartile of workers, who have the most to gain from the end of taxes 
on tipped income and overtime — elements of President Donald Trump’s 2025 tax 
package.

In early August, Bessent touted 5.5% wage growth 
<https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/cnbc-transcript-us-treasury-secretary-scott-bessent-speaks-with-cnbcs-squawk-box-today.html>
 for the lowest quartile of workers, saying he was “sick of hearing about the 
K-shaped economy” — the term for diverging economic conditions for wealthy and 
lower-income groups. He didn’t specify which particular gauge he was using.

But just weeks later, he cited a 4.7% year-to-date increase for the bottom 
quartile. If using the same indicator as he did in August, that suggested even 
by his own metric things had deteriorated. The US Treasury didn’t respond to a 
request for comment.

Bank of America internal data 
<https://institute.bankofamerica.com/content/dam/economic-insights/monthly-employment-report-august-2026.pdf>
 appear to match Bessent’s numbers, and its research unit indeed last month 
said that the long-standing K-shaped pattern in wages had “reversed.” Lower tax 
withholding thanks to Trump’s legislation is part of the story, BofA 
researchers found. Another is “the rising rate of job switching amongst hourly 
and lower-income workers,” who appear to be getting improved wages when they 
make a move, BofA said.

The question for Bessent, Trump and the Republicans ahead of the Nov. 3 
congressional elections is whether they get the political reward for these 
patterns. Voters were apparently unimpressed with Yellen’s median-income figure 
back in 2024, booting Democrats out of the White House and removing their 
control of the Senate.

Republican lawmakers, meantime, just left Washington to kick off their 
campaigning,without taking action 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/republicans-drop-midterm-affordability-push-as-voter-angst-grows?cmpid=BBD100226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261002&utm_content=9588>
 on affordability legislation.

Need-to-Know Research

The US baby-boom generation holds about half of US household wealth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-household-net-worth-jumped-last-quarter-on-stock-market-rally?cmpid=BBD100226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261002&utm_content=9588>
, and as its members pass, that will entail around $140 trillion in household 
wealth changing hands over the coming two decades. according to estimates from 
Moody’s Ratings. Part of this changeover will involve the property market.

“As older homeowners sell or transfer properties, housing supply should rise 
gradually,” Moody’s analysts including Madhavi Bokil wrote in a report this 
week. This dynamic will be countered in part by the continuing constriction of 
supply from homeowners with ultra-low-rate, early-2020s mortgages being 
unwilling to sell. But the counterbalancing effects are waning, Moody’s 
suggests. “Prices are already falling in around a quarter of markets.”

Inherited homes made up 7.4% of property transfers in 2025, the team wrote. 
Some beneficiaries may be “less price-sensitive than traditional 
owner-occupiers, particularly when homes have large embedded gains, little debt 
or stepped-up tax bases,” they said. Some areas may see more “disruptive 
transactions” than others — “even if national supply remains manageable.”
