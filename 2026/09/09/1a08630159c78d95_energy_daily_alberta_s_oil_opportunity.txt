Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Calgary-based reporter Robert Tuttle looks at the latest Canadian oil 
acquisition as the industry bulks up to meet burgeoning Asian demand.

Alberta is synonymous with oil sands, the vast deposits of sticky, bituminous 
material that lie just beneath the ground. But another oil region in the 
province is fast attracting attention for its own heavy crude.

Yesterday, Tamarack Valley Energy Ltd. announced a C$3.2 billion ($2.3 
billion) takeover 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/tamarack-valley-to-buy-headwater-exploration-in-all-stock-deal-mtsgmgke?cmpid=BBBXT090926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8645>
 of Headwater Exploration Inc., bringing together the two largest players in 
Alberta’s Clearwater play. The merged company is forecast to pump roughly 
80,000 barrels a day.

While that’s a fraction of the 3.5 million barrels a day produced from the oil 
sands, it highlights the fast pace of growth in the Clearwater formation. 
Output there has jumped almost 90% since 2017 andhundreds of drilling licenses 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/canada-unleashes-wave-of-oil-drilling-permits-in-next-big-play?cmpid=BBBXT090926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8645>
 have been issued this year alone, according to the local regulator.

That growth may have ramifications beyond shareholder returns as the Canadian 
government moves to diversify exports to Asian oil buyers, withUS trade 
relations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/trump-plans-to-cut-canadian-products-out-of-government-contracts?cmpid=BBBXT090926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8645>
 souring.

Producers will need to ramp up output at a pace not seen in more than a decade 
in Western Canada to fill more than 2 million barrels a day of newpipeline 
capacity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-03/carney-taps-government-owned-trans-mountain-to-build-oil-pipeline-to-serve-asia?cmpid=BBBXT090926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8645>
 planned for the coming years.
Pipelines in Hardisty, Alberta. The Canadian government is working at speed to 
boost the volumes it can ship from the west coast to Asia.Photographer: 
Bloomberg
The Clearwater play first rose to prominence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2017-10-04/here-s-the-hot-canadian-oil-play-you-probably-never-heard-about?cmpid=BBBXT090926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8645>
 a decade ago as a slew of companies began drilling in the Marten Hills and 
Nipisi region, north of the provincial capital of Edmonton.

But it’s only more recently that the area has begun to have a real impact on 
Alberta’s energy industry.

The crude produced there is similar to the dense, high-sulfur output from the 
oil sands. Crucially, it can be pumped more quickly and cheaply using 
conventional gear, allowing companies to boost volumes fast when prices spike.

Clearwater’s crude can be extracted for about $37 a barrel, with decline rates 
(the pace at which output drops off over time) roughly half that of the Permian 
Basin, the largest oil patch in the US, according to Bloomberg Intelligence 
analyst Kevin Liu.

“The low decline, free-cash-flow yield story is finally more visible to 
investors,” Liu said. “A more consolidated Clearwater was always inevitable in 
the grand scheme of things.”

—Robert Tuttle, Bloomberg News
