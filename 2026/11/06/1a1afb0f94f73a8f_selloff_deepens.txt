Deep Dive: Fishermen Struggling to Stay Afloat
Entrance to Billingsgate Fish Market in London, UK, on Friday October 29, 2026.
Photographer: Jose Sarmento Matos/Bloomberg
Beneath the glass towers of London’s Canary Wharf before dawn, workers wheel 
cod and lobster across the floor in ice-laden crates. By 7 a.m. at 
Billingsgate, the UK’s biggest fish market, fishermen in welliesrub shoulders 
with financial traders 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-06/uk-fishing-costs-surge-with-12-000-diesel-fuel-bills?cmpid=110626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11088>
 in suits at the on-site cafe, tucking into a local favorite: a scallop and 
bacon roll.

 * Keeping the mollusk affordable is becoming a growing concern as diesel 
prices continue to surge, with food producers among the heaviest impacted.
 * At sea, the squeeze is even more unforgiving. Marine fuel has more than 
doubled in the past six months, accounting for as much as 40% of the cost of a 
fishing trip now, according to the National Federation of Fishermen’s 
Organisations.
 * The pressure is keeping vessels ashore across Europe. The Western Fish 
Producers’ Organisation has halved the levy it charges members to help ease the 
fuel burden — the first time the cooperative has taken such a step.
