 from extreme weather, wars and protectionism.

That was among the messages Thursday from Maersk CEO Vincent Clerc, who 
explained on Bloomberg TV why the world’s No. 2 container carrier has abrighter 
outlook <bbg://news/stories/TJP6AHKK3NY8> despite waves of disruptions spurring 
higher energy costs and sustained uncertainty.

Read More: ‘Very Strong’ Demand Fuels Global Trade Amid Supply Shocks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/-very-very-strong-demand-fuels-global-trade-amid-supply-shocks?cmpid=BBD081326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7517>

“The underlying current is very, very strong, resilient demand for container 
transport on the back of electrification — whether it is generation, storage, 
new products such as electric vehicles, data centers, cooling,” Clerc said. 
“Anything that has to do with that is growing; the fabric of what we’re moving 
is changing and the volumes are growing very fast.”

One of the most pressing problems for global shipping are delays at Chinese 
ports, particularly afterrecent storms 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/pacific-typhoon-season-forecast-to-be-among-most-active-ever?cmpid=BBD081326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7517>
. Clerc noted that vessels are waiting 12 days outside Shanghai “because the 
terminal is so congested by the level of activity that there is. This has not 
happened before.”

‘Severe’ Congestion

According to new research <> from Bloomberg Intelligence’s Lindsay Chen, 
“severe Asian port congestion is set to persist through next week as 
disruptions from Typhoon Dolphin in East China spill over into Southeast Asian 
hubs, further squeezing tight global vessel capacity.”

Maersk’s rival Hapag-Lloyd reported improved results today 
<https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/en/company/ir/financial-news/financial-news.iry-2026.irid-506095911.html>
 too, citing “strong exports out of Asia and improved US demand, which helped 
offset the significant cost headwinds” faced last quarter. Combined, the two 
European carriers account for about one-fifth of global container volumes.

Port and logistics giant DP World, based in the United Arab Emirates, also 
sounded positive 
<https://dpw-p-001.sitecorecontenthub.cloud/api/public/content/6565d5adeabd401d8b33f9fbdf86ba63?v=00893a7d>
 on the future, saying the strength of its global network helped offset 
shipping disruptions at its flagship facility — the Jebel Ali port and free 
zone in Dubai.
