One-way bet

Bond traders are pricing in a Fed interest-rate increase today with a level of 
conviction that has proven right for decades 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/history-shows-fed-will-deliver-rate-hike-markets-have-locked-in?cmpid=091626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8954>
.

Interest-rate swaps tied to Fed meeting dates show traders see about a 94% 
chance that Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh and his colleagues will lift the benchmark 
policy rate by a quarter point from the current 3.5%-3.75% range.

Whenever expectations of a hike have been that high, the Fed has invariably 
delivered one, according to Bloomberg-compiled data going back to 2008.

“The market is not prepared for a hold or dovish hike,” said Caesar Maasry at 
investment manager Lunate.

The stakes are high after US bond yields surged to the highest since 2007, 
with surging oil prices fueling inflation concerns.

What’s the risk if the Fed surprises markets with a hold? Traders say that 
would drive long-term yields even higher and fuel anxiety that the Fed doesn’t 
have a grip on inflation. Meanwhile, short-term yields, which closely track Fed 
policy, would be depressed.

Still, some traders are guarding against a surprise. Demand has surged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/bond-market-s-extreme-short-counts-on-fed-to-deliver-rate-hike?cmpid=091626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8954>
 for options that would pay off if the Fed unexpectedly holds. —Ye Xie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOaIfVMEEws/ye-xie?cmpid=091626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8954>

Truss hangover

The UK’s strategy to prop up its long-maturity bonds by selling less in the 
wake of the Liz Truss-era crash isfailing to pay off 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/uk-long-bonds-are-struggling-even-as-the-government-sells-less?cmpid=091626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8954>
.

Instead, borrowing costs have climbed further since that meltdown in 2022, 
taking 30-year yields to the highest since 1998. They’re now only a whisker 
away from 6%, despite the UK shifting sales away from long maturities.

“Unfortunately some of it is muscle memory,” said Fred Repton at Neuberger, 
when asked about the hangover from the Truss era. “It’s starting to look a 
little bit precarious frankly, the level of yields and the cost of refinancing 
that is now continuing to increase.” —Georgia Hall 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXPC76oMnbY/georgia-hall?cmpid=091626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8954>
