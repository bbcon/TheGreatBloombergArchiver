Tough week for tech

CoreWeave is at the center 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/coreweave-stock-wipeout-exposes-divide-between-ai-fear-and-greed?cmpid=101626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10194>
 of this week’s tech selloff.

The stock has become ground zero for investors who see rising bond yields as a 
potential rally killer for the AI trade. Shares in CoreWeave plunged 20% in the 
first three sessions of the week as its debt tumbled to levels that typically 
indicate distress. By Thursday morning, investors were pricing a more than 60% 
chance of a default within five years.

Coreweave is working on the infrastructure buildout needed to develop AI 
technology. The safety of its debt is a key issue for investors because its 
spending on building data centers is being funded by issuing bonds. With 
Treasury yields climbing and inflation remainingstubbornly high 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/us-consumer-prices-climb-by-most-since-april-on-fuel-costs?cmpid=101626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10194>
, the cost of that financing is rising fast.

“The macro backdrop has created a sell-first, ask-questions-later market,” 
said Thomas DiFazio of Roundhill Financial. “In the short run, it is hard to 
say with a straight face that the all-clear is here.”

Still, some of the panic had eased by yesterday afternoon. Jensen Huang, CEO 
of Nvidia, which is a CoreWeave shareholder and supplier, made clear where he 
stood. “CoreWeave’s strength is improving every day,” Huang told Bloomberg. 
“I’m optimistic. Their cost of capital is actually decreasing.”

Investors responded. CoreWeave shares rallied almost 6%, ending the day with 
their best performance in over a month. The stock is up 74% since its IPO in 
March 2025 but down 62% from the peak in June 2025.

Irresistible rally

Taiwan’s professional stock pickers are barred from owning individual stocks 
in their personal accounts. With a huge rally underway,they’re quitting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/taiwan-fund-managers-quit-jobs-to-trade-their-own-money-in-stock-boom?cmpid=101626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10194>
.

Fewer than half of the 20 largest local equity mutual funds have retained the 
same portfolio manager for more than three years, according to fund-data 
website MoneyDJ. The temptation is clear: Taiwan’s benchmark stock index soared 
62% this year to become the world’s best-performing major equity gauge, adding 
about $1.8 trillion in market capitalization.

As seasoned professionals leave traditional money managers, they’re often 
ending up at less-regulated trading firms that allow more freedom for employees 
to trade their personal accounts. That’s coinciding with the allure of faster 
career progression where junior staffers spend less time early on doing more 
mundane work and, crucially, a chance to earn large bonuses.

“The trend is impossible to ignore,” said Huang Wen-ching of Taishin 
Securities Investment Advisory. “Private investment firms are springing up 
everywhere, and the pools of capital they command are getting bigger and 
bigger.” —Betty Hou
