If Europe was looking to Swedish exceptionalism to provide answers on the 
direction of travel of the far right, it will walk away sorely disappointed.

It’s a mess 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/swedish-voters-left-in-limbo-for-days-in-closest-ever-election?cmpid=BBD091426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8881>
, just like everywhere else.

At a time when global politics, economies and — if the titans of AI are to be 
believed — even the future of humanity are under assault, Sweden may seem a 
haven of sorts, wealthy and predictable.

But yesterday’s election was meant to settle the big question of whether the 
center-left, here in the form of the once-ubiquitous Social Democrats, could 
offer like-minded parties in Europe and perhaps even Democrats in the US a new 
model tovanquish the populist threat 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-10/sweden-election-agenda-set-by-far-right-leader-jimmie-akesson?cmpid=BBD091426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8881>
.

That, or whether liberal democracies need to hold their collective nose 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/drug-dealing-kid-gangs-hold-key-to-swedish-election-in-this-hood?cmpid=BBD091426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8881>
 and accept that a party with neo-Nazi roots has a right to govern if voters 
wish it.

The answer is an unsatisfying “who knows?” in a race too close to call, and 
one that will be settled by a fistful of votes.

Bizarrely, Peru, where vote counting dragged on for weeks, offers one clue.

There the outcome was settled by the diaspora 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/peru-s-diaspora-emerges-as-tie-breaker-in-razor-edge-presidential-race?cmpid=BBD091426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8881>
. And it might be Swedish expats, and their yet-uncounted votes cast in ballot 
boxes in Ikea, that tip the scales.

Politics in the home country of the Nobel Prize, Volvo and H&M went from being 
dominated by one party that created an enviable model for progressive taxation 
and generous welfare to being turned inside out by an insurgent force, the 
Sweden Democrats. It rode anti-immigration sentiment to become the 
second-biggest party.

Both were humbled in the most tightly contested election in more than a 
century.

The Social Democrats had their poorest showing in more than 100 years. The 
Sweden Democrats dropped to third place as they, too, hit a wall.

Voter frustration, political fragmentation and complicated coalition talks are

, Spain and Italy looming in 2027.— Flavia Krause-Jackson

Chart of the Day

The number of Britons aged 65 and over in work topped 1.7 million earlier this 
year as longer life expectancy and better health delay retirement, while 
cost-of-living pressures have also convinced people from the baby-boomer 
generation tostay on the payroll a little longer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/britain-s-boomers-cling-to-jobs-as-young-people-get-locked-out?cmpid=BBD091426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8881>
. Employment rates for the group — many of whom are beyond state pension age — 
are at a record high. But that has ramifications for those at the other end of 
the scale: In the early 2000s, Britain had as many as 10 young workers for 
every person over-65 with a job; today there are just two.
