China falls in love with EV camping

By Julia Zhong 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXeDYtoIWg0/julia-zhong?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>
 andK Oanh Ha 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQMnNNY2hgQ/k-oanh-ha?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>

Junya Zhen and Jie Cui quit their teaching jobs in China’s northern Hebei 
province in 2023 to travel across the country. The couple have spent months at 
a time sleeping in their hybrid Wey Lanshan SUV from Chinese car manufacturer 
Great Wall Motor, sharing their road-trip adventures on social media along the 
way.
Jie Cui and his partner Junya Zhen relax in their Chinese-made hybrid car 
during a June trip in Xinjiang.Source: Zhen and Cui
During the day they are on the move — streaming while they drive, preparing 
lunch and napping on back seats that fold flat to create a comfortable sleeping 
space. When the sun goes down, they livestream breathtaking views and promote 
car-camping gear. Their audience is lapping it up: The couple have more than 
570,000 followers on Xiaohongshu and Douyin, China’s versions of Instagram and 
TikTok, respectively.

Zhen and Cui are part of a growing trend. Government efforts to boost domestic 
tourism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-24/china-holiday-travel-booms-in-sign-of-consumer-sentiment-rebound?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>
 as well as consumers in search of cheaper holidays are enticing more Chinese 
travelers to take road-trip vacations. Many are doing so in deluxe electric or 
hybrid SUVs that can plug into the nation’s expansive charging network, letting 
them bypass traditional lodging and transforming “car camping” into a 
multibillion-yuan lifestyle economy.

China loves electric cars. The country is the world’s largest electric-vehicle 
market, and in 2025 BYDsurpassed Tesla 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-01/byd-sells-4-6-million-vehicles-in-2025-meets-revised-sales-goal?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>
 to become the world’s biggest EV maker. The nation’s charging infrastructure — 
the most extensive on the globe — has helped fuel a boom in EV travel. By the 
first quarter of 2026, China’s EV charging network had expanded 50% year on 
year to more than 21 million chargers; nearly all highway rest areas are 
equipped with them, according to government data.

Beginning in 2022, auto manufacturers rolled out camping and napping options 
aimed at outdoor enthusiasts. These range from the ability to power lights 
while parked to dedicated “Camp Mode” settings that fold seats completely flat 
and regulate overnight cabin temperatures. EV maker Xpeng’s “Sleep Mode” even 
includes looping white noise tracks of a crackling bonfire, crashing waves and 
a heavy storm.

These high-tech cabins have also turned cars into an extension of the living 
room and bedroom in a society where privacy is hard to come by, as 
multigenerational households are common. “A lot of people are going to their 
cars to have a one-hour nap,” says Daniel Wu, head of global brand and 
marketing for XPeng. “After work they’ll stay in the car for about an hour 
before heading home, doing things they enjoy — for example, playing games — 
simply because it’s such a comfortable space.”

Unlike traditional camper vans with fixed living spaces, new vehicles use 
their massive electric batteries to power camping functions such as overnight 
temperature control while also running appliances, from Korean barbecue grills 
to mini-fridges and 4K projectors. One startup, Rox Motor Tech, even offers an 
SUV with a rear-mounted kitchen complete with an induction cooker and water 
dispenser.

Free batteries, lower bills

By Summer Maxwell 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYSnifPVQIs/summer-maxwell?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>

At Café Mars in Brooklyn, an all-electric kitchen means better air quality for 
the chefs whipping up short-rib ravioli and tofu cannolis, fewer carbon 
emissions — and a $4,000 electric bill.

“Our electricity bill is insane. And that’s a tough thing,” said Paul D’Avino, 
the Italian restaurant’s owner and co-chef.
Paul D’Avino, owner of Café Mars.Photographer: Emma Rose Milligan/Bloomberg
So this summer, the 45-seat eatery is using six free plug-in batteries 
provided by David Energy, a startup power supplier.

The batteries allow the energy provider to buy and store energy when demand is 
low at night, and then discharge it during the day to avoid peak pricing, 
saving businesses like Café Mars 3% to 5% on their monthly bill.

“We are funding the battery up front and then we are extracting the value out 
of that battery in the power market,” said James McGinniss, David Energy’s 
chief executive officer. “And that value generated is enough to give our 
customers a discount, pay back the capital expenditures on the battery and 
leave some margin leftover for David Energy.”

As electricity prices soar and battery costs fall, a growing number of power 
providers andsmart home 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/sunrun-surges-over-30-on-deal-with-tesla-to-supply-power-for-ai?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>
 companies including Tesla Inc., Sunrun Inc. and Base Power Inc. are setting up 
these kinds of networks to tap into customers’ EV or standalone batteries when 
supplies are tight, profiting from swinging electricity prices. Experts say the 
practice canbolster 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2022-11-16/is-this-experimental-green-suburb-the-future-of-single-family-housing?cmpid=BBD072526_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260725&utm_content=6965>
 the grid without adding new transmission capacity.

“This all becomes enabled because batteries have gotten much cheaper through 
time,” said Allison Weis, global head of storage at Wood Mackenzie.
