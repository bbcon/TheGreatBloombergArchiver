**Corporate Earnings and AI Credit Stress**
The dominant US market narrative centers on CoreWeave, whose unsecured bond yields remain under pressure despite a slight Monday reprieve. Barclays' Ajay Rajadhyaksha frames the situation as contained: unsecured bonds represent less than $2 billion of a roughly $35 billion total debt stack and are structurally subordinated, suggesting the stress reflects idiosyncratic leverage rather than a systemic indictment of AI infrastructure economics. The more consequential variable, analysts argue, is the trajectory of GPU rental rates relative to financing costs — a spread business that holds only as long as compute demand exceeds supply. On the earnings front, 3M gained 4.4% in premarket after an expanded Amazon deal, while Steel Dynamics fell 6.1% on a third-quarter earnings miss, and Crown Holdings dropped 5% on a weak forecast.

**Fiscal and Monetary Policy**
The Social Security reserve exhaustion date — projected for 2032 — is entering the political horizon as midterm campaigns close. The incoming Senate class will govern through that window, raising the prospect of an explicit deficit-financed solution that could test bond market tolerance at a moment when the government debt burden is already elevated. Separately, Deutsche Bank economists endorsed the idea of reducing Fed policy meetings from eight to six annually, aligning the calendar more closely with key data releases — a proposal Fed Chair Warsh has previously floated.
