• Big Tech earnings dominated the week, with Alphabet down ~10% after-hours Thursday on a revenue miss while Microsoft gained 3.5% as Azure grew 48% — crystallizing the AI monetization debate.
• The FOMC held rates steady Wednesday as expected, though two members dissented in favor of an immediate hike; Chairman Warsh's press conference avoided explicit forward guidance.
• September durable goods orders beat estimates Tuesday, rising by the most in five months; the merchandise trade deficit widened to a record $134.9 billion in September, driven by AI-related capital goods imports.
• Amazon reported its slowest e-commerce growth in three years Friday with capex above consensus; Intel's revenue guidance missed materially, sending shares down ~25% in premarket trading.
• The Nasdaq 100 fell approximately 3.3% in October even as the equal-weighted S&P 500 gained 5.7%, illustrating how concentrated the damage remains among mega-caps.

**Monetary Policy**
The FOMC held the federal funds rate unchanged Wednesday, though the decision was genuinely contested — two members, including Governor Lisa Cook, dissented in favor of an immediate hike. Chairman Warsh's press conference offered no explicit forward guidance and avoided follow-up questions, leaving markets to interpret a tone that appeared reluctant to engineer a slowdown. The committee upgraded its economic characterization to "strong" growth from "solid," a marginally hawkish signal. Fitch data show 46% of US CPI components running above 3% year-on-year in September — double the 2018–2019 average — sustaining the case for a higher-for-longer posture. Markets continued to price a December hike, with the terminal rate expectation holding near 4.6%. Warsh separately announced the Fed would reduce its annual decision calendar from eight to six meetings, effective 2027.

**AI Capital Markets and Earnings**
Monday's financing activity was notable in scale: Goldman Sachs co-led Meta's roughly $98 billion equity offering, structured a $40 billion SpaceX chip financing facility, and was named lead bookrunner on Anthropic's IPO filing, which revealed $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue against $1.2 billion in the comparable prior-year period. Microsoft's results Wednesday provided partial reassurance — Azure revenue of $33.2 billion beat estimates and capex of $45.2 billion came in below the $45.9 billion consensus, suggesting capital discipline. Alphabet's second consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow Wednesday sent shares down roughly 10% after hours. By Friday, Amazon's deceleration and Intel's severe guidance miss confirmed the pattern: AI capital expenditure is accelerating precisely as legacy revenue growth slows. Early-stage AI credit is showing acute stress, with data center bond yields rising from sub-5% to near 7% and CDS spreads on Broadcom, SpaceX, and Oracle spiking to levels more typical of speculative-grade issuers.

**Market Internals and Rates**
Approximately 40% of possible stock pairs within the S&P 500 are negatively correlated — an unusually high figure reflecting zero-sum AI trade dynamics and elevated energy costs. The 10-year Treasury yield peaked near 5.01% mid-week before retracing to 4.93% Friday, with long-duration Treasuries delivering roughly 2.2% for October — the first month of long-bond outperformance since February. Whether this represents a structural turning point or a tactical pause remains uncertain. The US fiscal deficit is tracking above 6% of GDP for a fourth consecutive year, with interest payments exceeding $1 trillion annually, and the current political configuration offers limited near-term prospects for consolidation.
