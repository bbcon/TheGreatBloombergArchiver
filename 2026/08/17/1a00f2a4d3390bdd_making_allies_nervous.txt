US allies in Asia have watched warily as American military hardware has been 
redirected from the region to the Middle East for the war against Iran.

But unlike NATO members, they’ve so far avoided any major blowback from 
President Donald Trump for not sending the US support in the conflict.

His surprise decision today to downsize major US-South Korea military drills 
just as they were starting suggests there may yet be aprice to pay 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/trump-says-he-s-curtailing-military-drills-with-south-korea?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>
.

In a rambling social-media post, Trump hailed his “very good relationship” 
with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, describing his regime as “unthreatening 
and respectful.”

He reprised complaints about the cost of keeping a US military presence in 
South Korea, and highlighted President Lee Jae Myung’s rejection of his request 
for help on Iran.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-06/why-north-korea-s-economy-is-suddenly-booming?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>

It’s unclear what happens now. South Korea’s Defense Ministry says the joint 
exercises involving 18,000 troops over 10 days will go ahead as planned. Lee’s 
office says it will “coordinate closely” with the US on defense, and on “ways 
to make a practical and military contribution” around the Strait of Hormuz.

US and South Korea military leaders portray the drills as critical to preparing
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-north-korea-windfall/?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>
 for any North Koreanaggression 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/north-korea-says-new-us-nuclear-strategy-will-trigger-fresh-arms-race?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>
. That’s become more significant as Pyongyang’s capabilities have grown in 
areas likemissile development 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/north-korea-fires-ballistic-missile-ahead-of-us-military-drills?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>
 and drone deployment thanks to its alliance with Russia.

The US has scheduled major military exercises for later this year with other 
regional powers including Japan, Australia and Indonesia. Tokyo is also in 
sensitive talks with the US over the amount it pays to host American military 
bases.

Asian nations remain some of the most reliant on the US to deter China and 
North Korea 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/north-korea-deploys-new-destroyer-vows-to-push-for-nuclear-navy?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>

facing disruptions, Mexico is pitching a land route in the south as an
alternative for global trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-15/as-global-waterways-face-squeeze-mexico-pitches-ciit-as-alternative-corridor?cmpid=BBD081726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7795>

land between a port in the Gulf of Mexico and another on the Pacific coast. 
Now, the Interoceanic Corridor of the Isthmus of Tehuantepec is awaiting cargo, 
and Mexico’s bet is that the 308-kilometer rail link between the two oceans 
could become a key route for freight containers.
