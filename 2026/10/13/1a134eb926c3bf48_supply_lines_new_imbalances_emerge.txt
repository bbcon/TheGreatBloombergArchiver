The gap between surplus saving nations like China and Japan and deficit 
economies like the US is back at the highest level since the leadup to the 
2007-08 global crisis and is nearing a 150-year peak.

At the core of that imbalance are familiar forces. China’s ultracompetitive 
exports are creating record trade surpluses, while America’s gaping fiscal 
deficit and swelling $40 trillion debt pile are unsettling the world’s most 
important financial weather vane: thebond market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/us-treasury-yields-hit-5-as-wall-street-faces-new-rate-regime?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
.

According to Bloomberg’s latest Big Take 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-12/how-the-ai-boom-is-fueling-global-financial-risks?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
, new and potentially riskier layers are forming: an artificial intelligence 
boom fueling demand for high-end chips, and new pools of savings in places like 
South Korea and Taiwan which are flowing to fund American tech companies and 
their $9 trillion AI buildout.

In an October paper <https://www.nber.org/papers/w35865>, economist Stijn Van 
Nieuwerburgh of the Columbia Business School calculated that by 2032 the 
cumulative investment in AI-related infrastructure would reach nearly $9 
trillion, which would see it significantly outstrip the 19th-century railroad 
boom as the largest capital expenditure spree in US history as a share of GDP.

According to the Big Take, a circular flow of capital is helping boost yields 
on US Treasuries and sovereign bonds, raising the cost of servicing debts. 
Globally, it spreads the risks of an AI bust beyond Silicon Valley to investors 
in Asia and beyond. And it’s all happening at a time whensuperpower rivalries 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/sunak-says-trump-is-a-symptom-of-breakdown-in-global-order?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
 andstrained alliances 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-28/us-china-trade-truce-and-trump-and-xi?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
 make the prospects of cooperation during a crisis more difficult to handicap.

AI’s Giant Money Loop Has Echoes of 2008
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2026-10-12/big-take-ai-s-giant-money-loop-has-echoes-of-2008-podcast?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
Bloomberg Economics, in an analysis published <> on the Terminal this week, 
said the rise of cross-border capital flows has been a defining feature of the 
global economy for more than a half-century.

“In theory, these flows deliver substantial benefits, channeling savings to 
where they can be used most productively, financing development in 
capital-scarce economies and expanding opportunities for investors in 
capital-rich ones,” the report states. “Things haven’t always worked out that 
way.”

Cue the multilateral institution responsible for policing financial and 
economic imbalances which is kicking off itsannual meetings 
<https://www.imf.org/en/meetings/2026/annual/schedule> in Bangkok this week. On 
Tuesday the International Monetary Fund joined a chorus of public- and
private-sector

 observers in predictingrobust years  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/wto-lifts-goods-trade-outlook-as-ai-demand-outweighs-war-strains?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
for global trade in 2026 and 2027.

The IMF said the volume of trade in goods and services worldwide will grow 
4.6% this year and 4.7% in 2027 — both more than a percentage point above the 
projected expansions in global GDP. But it also warned that the world economy 
is nearing the limits of its resilience as theenergy squeeze

India’s Stability Offer

At the Bloomberg New Economy Forum in New Delhi 
<https://www.bloombergneweconomy.com/2026-new-delhi-forum-program/> on Tuesday, 
Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi positioned India as a reliable partner 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/modi-says-india-offers-trust-as-globalization-becomes-coercion?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>
 in an increasingly uncertain world, warning that the supply chains, trade and 
technologies that once connected countries are increasingly being weaponized 
for coercive reasons.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-19/great-lakes-bind-us-canada-trade-imperiled-by-trump-tariffs?cmpid=BBD101326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=9929>

are compounded by the impact of artificial intelligence.

“Things that connected the world at one time are today being weaponized,” Modi 
said. “In a world filled with uncertainty, India offers the assurance of 
reliability and stability.”
