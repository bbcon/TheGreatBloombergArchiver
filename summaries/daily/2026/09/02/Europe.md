**ECB and Fiscal Stress**
Nagel's guidance removes residual ambiguity around the September ECB meeting: a 25-basis-point hike appears nearly certain. What remains genuinely uncertain is the path thereafter, as Nagel himself was notably cautious on the post-September trajectory. The ECB faces a familiar bind — energy-driven inflation from the Hormuz disruptions argues for continued tightening, while slowing growth momentum in Germany and France argues for pause. Germany's power grid suffered two separate incidents on Tuesday, knocking 4.2 gigawatts of coal capacity offline in what local authorities are treating as deliberate criminal acts — an additional supply-side shock for European industry already contending with elevated energy costs.

**UK Policy and Politics**
Prime Minister Burnham's signaling on water nationalization — describing the industry as "a leaking monument" to privatization's failures — is the most politically significant domestic UK development. The fiscal arithmetic is tightening: the global bond selloff has materially compressed the government's headroom, and the water nationalization option would require financing at precisely the moment gilt yields are rising. Germany separately announced the closure of Russia's Bonn consulate and Berlin cultural center following evidence linking Moscow to the Leipzig airport drone attack — a measured but symbolically significant escalation in European diplomatic pressure on Russia.
