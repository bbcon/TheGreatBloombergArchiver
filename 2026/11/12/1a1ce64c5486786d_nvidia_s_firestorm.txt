Deep Dive: Ray-Ban’s Family Feud
Francesco Milleri , CEO of EssilorLuxotticaPhotographer: Augusto Casasoli/Foto 
A3/Cont
Italy’s Del Vecchio family made billions from designer eyeglasses, while 
deftly riding a wave of deals sweeping the country’s banking industry. Now the 
executives running the family’s €45 billion ($50 billion) holding company, 
Delfin Sarl, are positioning it to benefit from its biggest financial 
investment — a nearly €7 billion holding in the nation’s largest insurer, 
Assicurazioni Generali. That’s iffeuding family members 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/ray-ban-family-feud-threatens-ambitions-for-generali-investment?cmpid=111226_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11320>
 and a Czech billionaire don’t knock them off track.

 * Against the backdrop of dealmaking, Chairman Francesco Milleri is also 
contending with a destructive family dynamic, with legal disputes and some Del 
Vecchio heirs pressing for an exit from the financial investments to 
concentrate on EssilorLuxottica, the anchor of the family’s fortune.
 * So far, the family friction hasn’t derailed Delfin’s investment strategy. 
But one of the heirs, Leonardo Maria Del Vecchio, has emerged as a scathing 
critic and called on the Delfin chairman to focus on his role as CEO of the 
family’s original holding, the Ray-Ban manufacturer.
 * Delfin has already been playing a pivotal role in Italy’s bank 
consolidation. It helped bring about the €16 billion takeover of Mediobanca by 
Banca Monte dei Paschi last year, and it’s now supporting the proposed €35 
billion acquisition of Monte Paschi by Intesa Sanpaolo.
