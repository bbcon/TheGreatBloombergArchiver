• UK labor market data surprised to the upside: joblessness fell and employers added workers, an unexpected boost ahead of Chancellor John Healey's budget next week.

• France implemented targeted curfews in several cities after weekend protests over cost-of-living pressures deteriorated into violence and looting affecting hundreds of shops.

**French Sovereign Stress**
The French OAT spread at 148 basis points is the widest since 2012, compared with just 58 basis points in March — a rapid deterioration driven by political dysfunction, fiscal slippage, and social unrest. Moody's is expected to review France's credit rating by week-end; its current rating sits two notches above those of S&P and Fitch, and a downgrade — while having limited technical impact — risks amplifying negative market psychology at a vulnerable moment. Far-left candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon, who has floated debt repudiation, remains a tail risk, though prediction market odds have not moved materially year-to-date. His prospects may have been further dented by violence attributed to the far left.

**Germany and UK**
Chancellor Friedrich Merz hosted Germany's first investor conference in Berlin, seeking private capital to supplement expansive fiscal spending on infrastructure and defense. Early indicators suggest the stimulus is beginning to take effect, though investor confidence fell for the first time since April — a reminder that recovery remains uneven. In the UK, stronger-than-expected employment data provides the Chancellor modest room ahead of next week's budget, though the underlying inflation environment continues to constrain policy flexibility.
