Abelardo de la Espriella won Colombia’s presidency by promising a 
zero-tolerance approach to crime 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/trump-ally-wins-colombian-election-heralding-pro-business-swing?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
. That pledge will be tested from day one.

It’s an early reminder that in Colombia as across Latin America, restoring 
order iseasier to promise than to deliver 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/colombia-president-de-la-espriella-takes-office-as-latin-america-shifts-right?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
.

Even before taking office, De la Espriella is confronting a host of challenges.

Among them are fraud allegations from outgoing President Gustavo Petro, 
protests called by defeated leftist rival Iván Cepeda andan alleged bomb plot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/colombia-seizes-explosives-ahead-of-de-la-espriella-inauguration?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
 linked to dissident FARC fighters near the western city of Cali, where foreign 
dignitaries are gathering for today’s inauguration.

Colombia is the latest country to join Latin America’s rightward shift.

De la Espriella campaigned on an agenda inspired by Javier Milei’s fiscal 
austerity in Argentina, Nayib Bukele’s security strategy for El Salvador and 
his own close ties to US President Donald Trump.

But he also inherits one of the region’s most difficult governing environments 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/narco-violence-fiscal-risk-await-trump-s-new-colombian-ally?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
, with a widening budget deficit, stubborn inflation, a deteriorating security 
situation and a congress beyond his coalition’s control.
De la Espriella speaks at an Urban Security Summit in Barranquilla on Tuesday.
Photographer: Carlos Parra Rios/Bloomberg
Experiences elsewhere in the region suggest he’s far from alone.

Across South America, conservative leaders have ridden frustration over crime, 
inflation and weak growth into office.

Yet the experiences of Milei, Peru’s Keiko Fujimori 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/peru-s-inflation-tops-forecasts-as-food-transport-prices-climb?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
 and José Antonio Kast in Chile show that winning elections is one thing; 
governing deeply polarized nations while cutting spending, restoring security 
and reviving growth is another.

Many also assumed close ties to Trump would be an advantage. Apart from the 
extraordinary financial backing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-09/bessent-says-20-billion-us-argentina-currency-swap-finalized?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
 Washington extended to Milei, that assumption hasn’t held up.

The White House has shown little appetite for underwriting the region’s 
economic or political challenges, leaving its ideological allies largely on 
their own.

That leaves De la Espriella — and Latin America’s new conservative governments 
— with fewer allies, little margin for error and scant time to bring about the 
change they promised.— Patricia Laya 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AP-48H_80ck/patricia-laya?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>

China’s latest barrage of countermeasures against the US drew on an expanding 
legal arsenal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/xi-flexes-expanding-legal-arsenal-to-warn-trump-ahead-of-summit?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>

The Democratic Republic of Congo is preparing a sweeping overhaul

sharply and about 17,000 contacts being monitored — a fraction of the number

Czech billionaire Michal Strnad’s purchase of a stake in Italian tire giant 
Pirelli last week was heralded as a key moment for the company — and also 
cemented his home country’s position as Eastern Europe’sleader in foreign 
investment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/czech-billionaires-play-outsized-role-in-european-m-a-market?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
. Strnad, who made his money from the arms industry, is part of a new 
generation of superrich who are giving the Czech Republic an outsized role in 
European mergers and acquisitions, helping drive a shift in the local economy 
as they outgrow their home market.

North Korea’s Kim Jong Un has more cash at his disposal than at any point 
during his 15 years in power. His family-run dictatorship raked in as much as
$22 billion in foreign revenue 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-north-korea-windfall/?cmpid=BBD080726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7483>
 from 2022 to 2025, according to a Bloomberg Economics analysis and external 
research. That historic haul is nearly quadruple the amount amassed in the 
previous four-year period, which came immediately after the US convinced China 
and Russia to sign off on United Nations sanctions aimed at depriving Kim of 
hard currency to advance his nuclear program.
