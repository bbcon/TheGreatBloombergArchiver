President Donald Trump’s “move fast and break things” approach to trade policy 
is testing the government’s ability to ensure compliance with the tariffs he 
has imposed.

One of Trump’s closest allies in Congress, Republican Senator Bernie Moreno, 
says that the Department of Homeland Security should do more to enforce trade 
laws and penalize tariff evasion.

US trade enforcement authorities have for decades focused limited resources on 
cases with the biggest payout potential. Such cases are complex and often take 
years to build, meaning illicit goods often continue flowing into the US market 
at below fair-market prices, undermining US commerce.

How the US Is Moving Beyond Tariffs to Address China Trade Issues 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-11/us-moves-past-tariffs-to-focus-on-supply-chain-revamps-in-china-trade-policy?cmpid=BBD081226_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7518>

During Trump’s second term, the stakes are even higher. Tens of billions of 
dollars in tariffs have hit compliant importers especially hard. Meanwhile, bad 
actors skirt duties and avoid detection, often misclassifying goods or routing 
them through third countries in order to avoid paying higher tariffs — known as 
illegal transshipping.

‘Critical Industry’

In a letter to DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, Moreno called out an incident 
at an Ohio auto parts factory as an example of how transshipment “has already 
threatened this critical industry in my home state.”

In January 2024, Homeland Security Investigations searched a Moraine plant of 
Harco Manufacturing, owned by Chinese auto parts manufacturer Qingdao Sunsong, 
after the parent company wasprobed 
<https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/media/letters/letter-us-auto-part-firms-warning-against-importing-illegal-chinese-products-evading>
 by the House China Select Committee for alleged transshipment.

The status of federal enforcement related to the 2024 raid is unclear, but as 
recently as June, lawmakers on the House China Select Committeedemanded an 
update 
<https://democrats-selectcommitteeontheccp.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-selectcommitteeontheccp.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2026.06.03-letter-to-doj-on-cjs-report-on-autos.pdf>
 from then-Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche on Sunsong and other Chinese 
auto part manufacturers operating in the US.

“I wholeheartedly support the Administration’s tariff agenda,” Moreno wrote 
Wednesday in the letter, which was shared with Bloomberg. “However, tariffs 
only work if they are collected and enforced without exception.”

Revenue Jump

The administration has made some headway, at least on the revenue collection 
side. Since last summer, the so-called Trade Fraud Task Force hascollected 
<https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/trade-fraud-task-force-surpasses-1-billion-recoveries-and-charged-losses-less-one-year>
 some $1 billion in civil and criminal penalties related to customs violations, 
according to the Justice Department and DHS.

Customs and Border Protection is increasingly turning to artificial 
intelligence to catch tariff dodgers and spot illicit goods before cargo even 
reaches the country. The agency has assessed more than $2.1 billion in 
commercial trade penalties in this fiscal year. Anexecutive order 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/trump-signs-order-directing-customs-crackdown-amid-trade-gaps?cmpid=BBD081226_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7518>
 in June directed CBP to further identify ways the agency can improve 
enforcement.

In the letter, Moreno urged the agency to “continue leveraging innovative 
methods to better uphold US trade laws,” adding DHS should look into additional 
legislative authorities needed to go after violators of US trade laws.

 | A European Union study found that proposed curbs on farm imports treated 
with pesticides risk pushing up consumer prices, adding to trade concerns 
raised by agricultural powerhouses such as the US and Brazil. In a preliminary 
study, the European Commission said that, in the scenarios that were assessed, 
“imports decline, EU production rises and consumer prices increase,” though the 
“magnitude depends strongly on exporters’ adaptation assumptions.” The study 
adds to concerns raised by 19 countries at a World Trade Organization meeting 
in June, including the US, Canada, Brazil, Australia and Argentina. Separately, 
a Kyiv-based think tank called on the EU to aim its sanctions atRussian imports 
<bbg://news/stories/TJN7PGVTTCZK> of key minerals that feed Moscow’s ability to 
produce weapons for President Vladimir Putin’s war in Ukraine.
