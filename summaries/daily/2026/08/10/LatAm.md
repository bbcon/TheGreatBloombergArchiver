**Brazil and Regional Commodity Dynamics**
Brazil's intensified deforestation enforcement has produced a notable cross-border displacement effect: as Amazon clearing has declined domestically, it has surged in Bolivia, where President Rodrigo Paz's government is actively encouraging commodity crop expansion as a growth strategy. The dynamic illustrates how regional enforcement asymmetries can redirect rather than reduce agricultural pressure on ecosystems. More broadly, Latin American commodity exporters remain exposed to the dual pressures of Iran-war-related energy price volatility and the prospect of higher-for-longer US interest rates, which continue to weigh on capital flows into the region and sustain currency depreciation pressure across several markets.

**Trade Exposure**
Canada's position — while not strictly Latin American — represents the most acute near-term trade risk in the Americas. Canadian negotiators are attempting to secure a trade agreement before threatened 50% US tariffs on a wide range of goods take effect on August 19. The outcome carries spillover implications for regional export dynamics, particularly as US trade enforcement posture under the current administration has demonstrated a willingness to deploy tariffs as negotiating leverage across multiple fronts simultaneously.
