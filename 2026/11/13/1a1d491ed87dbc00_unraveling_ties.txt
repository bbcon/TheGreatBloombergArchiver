Indonesia’s former president, Joko Widodo, this week became the latest target 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jokowi-era-fades-in-indonesia-with-son-forced-out-as-prabowo-s-vice-president?cmpid=BBD111326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11392>
 of his successor Prabowo Subianto’s efforts to consolidate power.

Widodo, better known as Jokowi, elevated Prabowo’s profile in the final years 
of his presidency, positioning the former general to finally attain the top 
spot after two failed bids.

They were an odd couple, for sure.

Jokowi was low-key technocratic and left a legacy of improved infrastructure. 
Prabowo, nearly 10 years older and shaped by a military career spanning some of 
the country’s most violent moments, is a commanding and centralizing presence.

He’s not so much building a global-facing nation, but rather taking control of 
its resources and the riches he thinks others have siphoned off.
Prabowo during the Armed Forces Day parade in East Jakarta on Oct. 5.
Photographer: Bay Ismoyo/AFP/Getty Images
So it’s not much of a surprise that the informal power-sharing arrangement 
they established for the Prabowo presidency — capped by Jokowi’s eldest son 
stepping in as vice president — didn’t last. Still, its unraveling has been 
dramatic.

The final act began last week, with the arrest of the Jokowi-appointed former 
national police chief.

Listyo Sigit Prabowo rose through the ranks under Jokowi, who built a strong 
base of support among the police force. He and others were detained on 
suspicion ofillegally wiretapping 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/indonesia-s-ex-police-chief-alleged-to-be-end-user-of-wiretaps?cmpid=BBD111326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11392>
 the president.

That put Jokowi’s camp and the police on notice that Prabowo and his allies, 
in politics and the military, were taking the upper hand.

Then this week the vice president and Jokowi’s son, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, 
resigned amid questions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jokowi-son-gibran-rakabuming-raka-resigns-indonesia-vice-president-prabowo?cmpid=BBD111326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11392>
 over his high school educational certification. The matter, which was heading 
toward potential action by parliament, could have been shut down by the 
president or his camp. Instead, Prabowo is nowpoised to nominate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/indonesia-s-prabowo-said-to-accept-resignation-of-vice-president?cmpid=BBD111326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11392>
 a potential successor.
Prabowo (left) with Gibran at a press conference in Jakarta yesterday after 
his resignation.Photographer: Fatih Mubarok/AFP/Getty Images
The president has a rough road ahead. His popularity is sinking, the economy 
is under pressure and the stock market is at risk of a double downgrade by MSCI 
next year.

Whatever happens next, Prabowo is making sure he’ll be the only one 
responsible.— Ben Otto
