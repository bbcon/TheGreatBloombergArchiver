**Post-Election Economic Implications**
The midterm results introduced a meaningful near-term downside risk to US AI-driven capital expenditure, the primary driver of GDP acceleration in recent quarters. Goldman Sachs estimates more than 45% of planned data center capacity sits in states where newly elected Democratic governors campaigned on construction restrictions or moratoriums. Texas alone — 25% of total planned capacity — elected its first Democratic governor since 1990. Economists broadly expect any moratoriums to be temporary, designed to allow regulatory frameworks to form rather than halt the AI trajectory outright. Deutsche Bank's Brett Ryan noted the pullback could constructively eliminate "projects that were already looking shaky," potentially extending the investment cycle rather than truncating it. The more acute risk is a negative wealth effect: the S&P 500's decline, if sustained, could erode consumer confidence and weaken household spending, which remains a primary growth engine.

**Labor Market and Fed**
October payrolls, due today, are expected to show 65,000 jobs added — a figure that would represent a marked deceleration from prior months. Market expectations for further Federal Reserve rate hikes have faded materially in the wake of the equity selloff. The Fed's John Williams signaled a data-gathering posture, and fewer scheduled meetings are under consideration, returning the institution to a more deliberate pace.

**Equity and Credit Markets**
The Thursday session was indiscriminate: Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, and memory names all fell sharply before rebounding 4–9% in Friday premarket trading. ING moved to sell a $500 million data center loan portfolio, an early signal that credit markets are repricing AI infrastructure risk. Airbnb (+12%) and Cloudflare (+14%) were notable exceptions, reporting strong earnings. Hedge funds, already under pressure in October, faced forced deleveraging as crowded AI positions were unwound rapidly, amplifying the move.
