‘It’s going to get funky’

Volatility could spike in global markets over the coming months due to 
interest-rate hikes, mounting concerns over fiscal deficits and the Mideast war.

With US investors returning Tuesday from the Labor Day holiday, here’s a 
rundown of what’s on the agenda this fall:

 * Central banks: Economists predict a quarter-point rate increase Thursday 
from the ECB, with hikes possible next week from the Fed and BOJ. The monetary 
policy outlook through year-end is up in the air, though, and shifting 
expectations could roil markets.
 * Fiscal dilemma: The UK’s worsening outlook is a growing risk for sterling, 
withgilt yields surging 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/uk-set-to-pay-most-since-1998-for-borrowing-after-gilt-selloff?cmpid=090826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8631>
 just weeks before a late-October budget. France faces similar concerns before 
next year’s presidential election.
 * Elections: The result of the US midterms is likely to shape fiscal policy. 
Democratic control of the Senate would potentially expose Donald Trump to 
investigations and make it harder for him to confirm nominees.
 * Corporate borrowing is already running at record levels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/us-high-grade-bond-sales-set-august-record-in-year-full-of-them?cmpid=090826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8631>
 thanks to all the financing needed for the AI buildout. This month will 
probably see another torrent of offerings — it’salready started 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/global-bond-rush-kicks-off-in-asia-with-banks-leading-the-charge?cmpid=090826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8631>
 — and that will add to the upward pressure on bond yields.
 * Geopolitics: Hostilities between the US and Iran have pushed oil prices up 
again, fueling fresh inflation fears. Trade tensions are also rising as Trump 
continues to push his protectionist agenda.
“It’s going to get funky,” said Charlie Jamieson at Jamieson Coote Bonds in 
Melbourne. “Running through it all is Trump and oil, so volatility is probably 
the only certainty.” —Matthew Burgess

Disconnect between inflation, rates

Today’s commodities rally highlights a big source of tension in markets: 
Central banks have pledged to keep inflation in check, yet investors still 
predict a relatively limited tightening in monetary policy.

Deutsche Bank strategist Henry Allen says that’s not sustainable 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/deutsche-bank-s-allen-says-traders-are-wrong-on-rates-or-prices?cmpid=090826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8631>
, given what’s happening with prices.

“Either inflation needs to fall, or the pressure on rates will continue,” 
Allen wrote in a note Monday. His take is that markets are underestimating the 
scale of hikes that will be needed.

Swaps markets imply just two Fed increases by the end of July, even though 
Chairman Kevin Warsh last month warned inflation isn’t meaningfully slowing. 
The US CPI report on Friday will be the next big signpost for investors.

Credit and equity markets have proved resilient, despite the elevated 
inflation and a bond selloff.Taken together, it suggests at least one part of 
the market is due a correction, Allen said.

“If the pressure on rates is maintained, that in turn will force an adjustment 
in risk assets,” he wrote. “Something needs to give here, with several asset 
classes vulnerable to the impact of a more aggressive tightening cycle from 
central banks.” —Alice Gledhill 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUFfTWYF4c4/alice-gledhill?cmpid=090826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8631>
