Deep Dive: An Arcane Shipping System
Vessels transit the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of the port city of Bandar 
Abbas in southern Iran on Sept. 5.Photographer: Atta Kenare/AFP/Getty Images
The messages from the world’s biggest supertanker owner often begin with 
pleasantries: “Dear Ladies and Gentlemen.” Within minutes, the WhatsApps are 
the talk of the oil-tanker industry, a multibillion-dollar corner of the energy 
market that’s been plunged into chaos by the Iran war this year, and where word 
of mouth is the main way information spreads.

 * The details vary, but the messages to shipbrokers from Sinokor Group, the 
South Korean company controlled by tycoon Ga-Hyun Chung, mostly boil down to 
the same thing — one of its vessels has just been hired at a new sky-high rate 
to ferry oil out of the Persian Gulf.
 * Some messages detail a new contract, listing the ship, counterparty and 
eyewatering price 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/sinokor-tanker-tycoon-s-whatsapp-messages-influence-oil-transport-prices?cmpid=102726_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10612>
. Others just refer loosely to negotiations underway. The format fluctuates 
from terse strings of numbers and names to full sentences and florid signoffs: 
“Very respectfully. Thank you very much and best regards,” concludes one 
message sent to brokers in August, and seen by Bloomberg. Usually, in the days 
that follow, the global benchmark price set by London’s Baltic Exchange takes 
another leap higher.
 * What would simply be fodder for gossip in most industries, brokers say that 
Sinokor’s WhatsApp messages have for months played an outsized role in setting 
the cost of transporting oil around the world. Rates for very-large crude 
carriers — the biggest kind of oil tanker — have soared more than 1,600% this 
year amid disruptions caused by the war, creating an unprecedented boom for the 
industry, but throwing a spotlight on the arcane system used to price the 
world’s shipping contracts, and the centuries-old institution that manages it.
 * The world of oil tanker rates is coming under fresh scrutiny this week, as 
hearings begin in a London lawsuit brought against the Baltic Exchange by 
trader Mercuria Energy, which argues that the benchmark rate is no longer fit 
for purpose because of the disruptions caused by the Iran war.
