Garage trading

Most people lose money 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/most-prediction-market-traders-are-losing-money-while-bots-rack-up-gains?cmpid=101426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10075>
 on prediction markets, often getting beat by sophisticated trading firms on 
other side of their wagers. Not this guy.

Brian Golden, a 44-year-old former theater director, has emerged as the most 
successful trader 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-13/kalshi-s-top-inflation-trader-beats-wall-street-banks-from-his-garage?cmpid=101426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10075>
 in Kalshi markets that let users wager on economic indicators.

Golden has made over $2 million in profits across contracts tied to economic 
questions. The bulk of the success reflects the expertise he has developed in 
the movement of consumer prices, a relatively niche area for prediction markets 
that happens to be one of the most closely watched economic statistics.
Brian Golden at his home on Sept. 7Photographer: Erica Hernández/Bloomberg
By his own admission, Golden is “not a math guy.” Instead, he’s done deep 
research into the intricacies of government data collection, allowing him to 
build a spreadsheet model mimicking how the US calculates inflation.

His forecasts have outperformed not just other Kalshi traders but most of Wall 
Street: over the last 18 reports, none of the 25 bank economists tracked by 
Bloomberg had a smaller gap between their predictions and the actual prints. 
Not bad for someone who trades from his garage.

For the reading coming this morning, Golden is expecting a 3.66% year-on-year 
increase, just above the 3.6% consensus of economists polled by Bloomberg.

The report has big implications for interest rates, and of course, Golden. On 
inflation day, he bolts awake before dawn in Los Angeles and opens bls.gov, 
frantically refreshing for the number he’s spent the last month trying to guess.

“It’s so sick,” Golden said of his obsession. “I just want to know the number.”

Bond traders dethroned

A generation of Wall Street bond traders are confronting, for the first time 
in their careers, the possibility ofgetting beaten for the year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/bond-traders-eclipsed-by-equities-in-rare-wall-street-reversal?cmpid=101426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10075>
 by their peers in equities.

Equities divisions at five big US banks are on track to deliver more than $66 
billion of revenue in the first nine months of the year, roughly $3 billion 
higher than what’s expected from units handling bonds, currencies and 
commodities.

Those figures stand to be firmed up today, when Bank of America and Morgan 
Stanley report earnings. Another quarter of the same would not only shift the 
pecking order and bonuses, but challenge the conventional wisdom that computers 
are set to take over stocks — eroding fees and revenue.

“It was not too long ago that people were concerned about electronification 
and equities trading,” Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon told analysts. “But as 
we sit here today, the opportunity set for us is greater than what anyone could 
have contemplated.” —Sridhar Natarajan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQsv6zME0W4/sridhar-natarajan?cmpid=101426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10075>
