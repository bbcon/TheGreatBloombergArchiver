AIndicators on the Way Forward

In every market boom, an indispensable dominant player is eclipsed by the very 
transformation it wrought. That happened to names likeNetscape and AOL 
<https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/aol-buys-netscape-for-4-2-billion/>
 as the internet took off; now, the market reaction toJensen Huang’s resignation
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jensen-huang-face-of-the-ai-boom-ousted-by-nvidia-board-as-ceo?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 in scandalous circumstances suggests a fear that Nvidia Corp., of all 
companies, might suffer that inglorious distinction for AI.

In five weeks since its Oct. 6 peak — a selloff that began before the latest 
revelations about Huang — the chipmaker has lost more than $1.8 trillion in 
market value, all with surprisingly little impact on the broader market:

That’s more than the entire stock market of Brazil or Italy, and about triple 
Mexico’s. The focus now turns to its earnings due Tuesday, where it cannot 
disappoint. Then it needs to find a successor to Huang in whom the market can 
believe. Thedivergence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/nvidia-sees-historic-split-from-s-p-as-stock-sinks-market-soars?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 in the past week undermined Nvidia’s status as the AI poster child.

Nvidia earnings should continue to be a useful bellwether for the AI buildout, 
particularly when viewed alongside that of the hyperscalers, as we argued in 
the firsttwo installments 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-08-10/aindicators-hint-at-doubts-in-credit-markets?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 of“AIndicators” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-09-21/aindicators-fed-hiking-cycle-is-a-red-flag-moment?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
— Points of Return’s exercise in applying a few quantitave measures to look for 
early signs of problems. As we brace for apost-Huang AI future 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/bessent-says-nvidia-needs-dramatic-shakeup-after-huang-ouster?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
, this is a great time to return to them.

Hyperscalers’ Earnings Season

Since the end of the third quarter, hyperscalers have lagged the broader 
market as their lackluster earnings and mixed outlooks heightened concerns 
about how soon their massive capital spending could pay off. Thepositive market 
reaction to Oracle Corp.’s forward guidance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/oracle-projects-30-billion-free-cash-flow-in-fiscal-2029?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 — which pointed to a return to positive free cash flow in the second half of 
fiscal 2028, followed by a step-down in capital spending — showed how much this 
now matters to investors:

While investors liked a possible pullback in capex, AI-related stocks sold off 
on fears of tighter regulation after the Democrats’ sweep of the midterms, 
which showed widespread“not in my backyard” (NIMBY) opposition 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-21/why-data-center-pushback-is-a-unique-form-of-nimbyism-video?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 to data centers. Construction proposals were put on the ballot in 41 places, 
and all but one failed. Investors want to see hyperscalers voluntarily reduce 
investment when it is generating returns, not because regulatory pushback has 
forced their hand. This uncertainty has exacerbated theselloff in chipmakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/chips-selloff-worsens-as-fears-about-ai-trade-grip-stock-market?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
’ shares, even as many rebounded last week on the theory that Nvidia’s troubles 
would benefit them competitively:

Political Risks

For some states, data centers could mean thousands of construction jobs, 
creating a difficult trade-off for lawmakers. Strategas’ Tom Tzitzouris 
suggests the facilities could move to Canada if pushback persists and demand 
for computing capacity keep growing:
Not necessarily my base case, but don’t be surprised if President Trump all of 
a sudden finds an incentive to start making a deal with Canada to start 
reducing those tariffs, and some of those data centers go across the border… 
Access to cheap power and the ability to still be within the US national 
security umbrella.
But such offshoring remains a long shot. The fractious US-Canada relationship 
is an obvious stumbling block. Alpine Macro’s Noah Ramos says the US would 
rather find ways to keep data centers on its own soil:
They will build gigawatts abroad, and they’re already doing that. The areas 
that are going to do that are colder regions, as it helps minimize cooling 
requirements. And then also places that have a high energy footprint, like 
Canada, and even some of the Nordic nations in Europe. But the US is still 
going to want to have the overwhelming majority of the hyperscalers built out 
on domestic soil just from a national security and data sovereignty perspective.
Ramos does not expect a material impact on the more than 30 GW of capacity 
additions projected for 2027, since moratoriums or pauses generally target new 
data-center projects rather than those that already have permits or are under 
construction. But many haven’t gotten that far. In this chart from Alpine, 
“announced” projects are at the planning or land-acquisition stage and face 
greater risk of being halted due to the backlash:

Credit and Financing

A deeper concern is whether these massive projects can pay for themselves. In 
previous investment booms, some of the earliestcredible signs of stress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-30/tech-firms-debt-protection-costs-are-surging-on-profit-concerns?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 emerged in credit markets. It’s vital to look there again this time.

Even as the Huang scandal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/inside-jensen-huang-s-final-hours-as-the-ceo-of-nvidia?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 unfolded, the cost of insuring the debt of several US technology heavyweights 
against default retreated from record highs in recent days. That’s a welcome 
relief. But default risk has more than doubled for the main players in a matter 
of months, withOracle 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/oracle-is-offering-10-billion-of-high-grade-dollar-bonds?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 andSpaceX 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/spacex-40-billion-off-balance-sheet-debt-snarled-by-ai-turmoil?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 at particularly high levels. That shows the serious questions about whether 
the massive AI borrowing binge will ever pay off, and ensures that future 
borrowing will be more expensive:

As “AIndicators” previously noted 
<https://blinks.bloomberg.com/news/stories/TLP4G1KGCTFL?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
, the scale of capex is already reshaping the corporate bond market. Barclays 
strategist Dominique Toublan finds that six major technology groups account for 
9.7% of the duration-times-spread (DTS) risk in the US investment-grade 
corporate bond market, based on data through Nov. 11. DTS combines a bond 
portfolio’s credit spread with its spread duration to measure overall exposure 
to spread risk. By comparison, the six largest banks — historically far more 
influential in corporate credit — account for just 7% of the Bloomberg US 
Corporate Bond Index’s DTS:

This concentrated risk is likely to worsen, particularly as October’s macro 
data prompted a rebound in Treasury yields last week after a brief respite. The 
trend remains firmly upward, and this can be expected to push borrowing costs 
higher across credit markets:

The surge in debt issuance increases competition for long-term capital. It 
looks ever more as though Big Tech is “crowding out” the government and forcing 
the Treasury to offer higher rates — the opposite of what typically occurs.

With the Fed now thought certain to hike rates 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/fed-s-williams-says-inflation-far-too-high-still-work-to-do?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 next month, the AI buildout will grow increasingly costly for hyperscalers. 
Higher financing costs need not derail projects already underway, but they 
raise the hurdle for subsequent rounds of investment when the market is banking 
on continued waves of expansion.

Demand and Pricing Power

Anthropic’s IPO 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-15/anthropic-s-ipo-makes-dario-amodei-the-new-face-of-the-ai-boom?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 this week is the crucial gauge of investor appetite. The first frontier AI lab 
to go public, it will be the benchmark for rivals like OpenAI, which plans to 
debut next year. Anthropic’s S-1 filing showed robust revenue growth; it must 
now prove that it can maintain its pricing power, as its compute costs are 
already locked in. So far, the auguries are good.OpenRouter 
<https://openrouter.ai/> data show a healthy demand for tokens — the basic 
units of text processed by large language models — which means people are still 
spending more on using AI. Every prompt and response consumes tokens, making 
this a useful metric of demand for AI services:

However, this increased consumption is coinciding with a broad weakening in 
the Silicon Data LLM Token Expenditure-Weighted Price Index, which tracks 
changes in token prices. The divergence suggests that rising usage is 
sustaining overall spending but that the price businesses will pay for each 
token is declining:

At what point will this deterioration start to show up in AI companies’ 
pricing power? That is hard to tell. Noah explains that improving technology 
has allowed labs to reduce their unit prices, measured in cost per token. That, 
so far, has unlocked greater token consumption and higher aggregate revenues. 
But the technological progress needs to continue.

In the case of Anthropic, whose IPO has been oversubscribed six times 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/anthropic-s-ipo-is-said-to-be-about-six-times-oversubscribed?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
, investors may be buying into the possibility that it can get customers to 
increase their per-token spending with new products similar to the recently 
announced office tools. A clampdown on Chinese AI models using unauthorized 
means to replicate their model’s capabilities would also help. Noah argues:
We are seeing that the US lead is broadening... they’re pulling away from the 
Chinese alternatives. If that is the case, that’s a very bullish indicator for 
the US labs because that means that the IQ scores on their models are going to 
accelerate again ahead of the Chinese and there’s going to be more of a 
leadership change there.
China’s open-weight labs are unlikely to concede ground without a fight. And 
the Huang scandal in itself reveals just how fraught this contest has become.

For now, Anthropic and the many companies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/openai-anthropic-valuations-fuel-vc-fears-of-startup-correction?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
 trying to follow its lead need that technological edge to endure. It enables 
them to compete both on price and on quality. Without it, the growing political 
and financial disquiet suggest that the investment case could easily unravel 
very quickly.

Survival Tips

Here’s a tip: Don’t be too far from home the next time US air traffic calls a 
totalground halt 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-14/faa-lifts-ground-stop-allowing-flights-to-resume-after-outage?cmpid=BBD111626_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11322>
. This is being written from Santiago, in Chile, which I am getting to know 
better after my Saturday flight was canceled. The logjam means an extra 30 
hours here and then a 12-hour layover in Mexico City. All being well.

There are worse places to be stranded than Santiago; its reputation as one of 
the duller Latin American capitals is unfair. It gave us PabloNeruda 
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0or7hSz-7gc&t=5s>, whose personally designed 
house <https://fundacionneruda.org/en/la-chascona-museum-house/> is crammed 
with objects he brought home from a wandering life that took him fromEaster 
Island 
<https://www.vhstigers.org/ourpages/auto/2007/12/15/1197770595866/Neruda%20Poems%20for%20Poetry%20Project%202015.pdf>
 toRangoon 
<https://www.irrawaddy.com/culture/nerudas-burmese-days.html#google_vignette> 
to all places in between. He died, deeply distressed, barely a week after the 
1973 military coup that broughtAugusto Pinochet 
<https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/amr220042008en.pdf> to 
power. TheMuseum of Memory and Human Rights 
<https://www.jstor.org/content/oa_chapter_monograph/j.ctt1v2xskk.9?seq=1> 
documents that regime’sdepravity 
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03064227608532531> in a way I 
will never forget. My fellow visitors all seemed similarly struck into silence 
as we left and returned to the modern, liberal Santiago reality where such 
things would seem impossible.

Go to both these museums if you ever have the chance. My profound thanks to 
Richard Abbey for getting out this newsletter in trying circumstances. And have 
a great week everyone.
