• July payrolls contracted 23,000, sharply missing the +80,000 consensus — the third consecutive summer of employment weakness — though initial jobless claims remain low, pointing to reduced hiring rather than elevated firing.
• July CPI landed exactly in line with consensus: core +0.2% MoM and +2.5% YoY, the slowest pace since March 2021, reducing near-term Fed hike pressure without eliminating it entirely.
• The Treasury sold 30-year bonds Thursday at 5.216%, the highest yield in a quarter-century, with the 10-year auction earlier in the week pricing at 4.683%, the highest since 2007.
• S&P 500 Q2 earnings growth accelerated to 34% YoY from 25% in Q1, the broadest profit expansion in years, though AI-sector credit stress is widening beneath the surface.
• The personal saving rate fell to 2.7% in June, less than half its level two years ago and approaching historic lows.

**Labor Market**
July's payrolls miss was driven primarily by a 60,000 slide in local government employment, partly reflecting the exhaustion of post-pandemic federal teaching assistance. The decline in the unemployment rate alongside the payrolls contraction reflects workers exiting the labor force rather than finding jobs: prime-age participation (ages 25–54) slipped to 83.4%, on track for its first annual decline since 2020. Critically, initial jobless claims remain low, suggesting minimal firing is accompanying the lack of hiring. JPMorgan's Michael Feroli acknowledged the seasonal pattern may self-correct in autumn, while Santander's Stephen Stanley argued the Fed, having cut rates in each of the prior two Septembers on similar weakness, will look through the miss this time given core inflation remains above target.

**Inflation and Monetary Policy**
Wednesday's CPI print broke the post-pandemic pattern of systematic surprises, landing exactly in line with forecasts for the first time in memory. Alternative core measures — trimmed mean, median, sticky-price, and the Fed's preferred supercore — all declined modestly but remain above 2.5%, leaving Chair Kevin Warsh with little justification to cut and limited urgency to hike. Markets assign minimal probability to more than one additional hike this year. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack maintained a hawkish tone, noting "some number" of increases may still be needed, underscoring that the committee remains divided. Friday's retail sales data represent the next significant read on consumer momentum.

**Deficit Financing and the Consumer**
Thursday's 30-year auction at 5.216% reflects investors demanding greater compensation for a fiscal backdrop of persistent deficits, above-target inflation, and solid growth. The personal saving rate's decline to 2.7% — near its all-time low — raises a structural question about the durability of consumer spending. Gavekal's Tan Kai Xian warned that once the saving rate approaches zero, households must borrow or liquidate assets to sustain expenditure; with delinquencies low and leverage stable, the more likely adjustment mechanism is asset sales, which would pressure consumption if equity valuations soften.

**AI Capex, Credit, and Equity Markets**
The S&P 500's record highs mask growing stress in AI-sector credit. Barclays data show the top six technology groups — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, and SpaceX — account for 8.4% of US investment-grade duration-times-spread risk, exceeding the 6 largest banks' 7.3% share. Alphabet reported negative free cash flow for the first time since its IPO; Meta's Q2 free cash flow fell 91% year-over-year. JPMorgan estimates $4.1 trillion of $5.5 trillion in projected AI capital expenditure will be debt-financed, making the buildout acutely sensitive to the Fed's path. Nvidia secured a $500 billion financing commitment from a coalition including BlackRock and Goldman Sachs; CoreWeave's backlog reached $104 billion. Reddit gained 12% ahead of its August 18 S&P 500 inclusion, while Applied Materials fell 5.3% on a cautious outlook.
