Weekly Brief

Nov 02 – Nov 08, 2026

US

  • October ISM Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.5, beating consensus and reaching its highest level since late 2021, while October ADP private payrolls also beat estimates — constructive data offset by severe equity market turbulence.
  • Democrats swept both chambers in Tuesday's midterms, flipping at least seven Senate seats and securing 232 House seats, a result that significantly exceeded pre-election expectations and introduced meaningful near-term uncertainty around AI capital expenditure.
  • The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% on Thursday — its worst session since 2020 — wiping $2.3 trillion in market value as AI regulation fears overwhelmed post-election relief; futures recovered 1.3% by Friday morning.
  • Gold rose above $4,000 per ounce on Tuesday, gaining 3.1%, as markets began pricing fiscal restraint under political gridlock and reduced oil-driven inflation risk.
  • October nonfarm payrolls, expected at 65,000, were due Friday as the critical labor market read of the week.

Manufacturing and Labor Market

October's ISM Manufacturing index rose one point to 55.5, posting its best production reading since late 2021 with new orders remaining firmly expansionary. ADP private payrolls similarly beat consensus, suggesting the labor market retains momentum into year-end. These prints offered a constructive macro anchor during a week dominated by political and market turbulence, though October nonfarm payrolls — consensus at 65,000, more than double September's pace — represented the definitive weekly data point and remained pending at time of writing.

Monetary Policy and Fiscal Risk

The Fed emerged as an institutional beneficiary of the election outcome. Senate Democratic control neutralizes Trump's ability to populate the Fed board with loyalists beyond the single scheduled vacancy, and the attempted removal of Governor Lisa Cook loses practical force under the new Senate arithmetic. Chair Kevin Warsh, confirmed by the narrowest margin in Fed history, will need to cultivate the incoming majority. Market expectations for further rate hikes faded materially following the equity selloff, with Governor Williams signaling a data-gathering posture. The more consequential medium-term risk is fiscal: with the debt ceiling roughly $800 billion below the $41.1 trillion limit and the X-date projected in early-to-mid 2027, a divided government faces both a December 11 funding deadline and a borrowing limit breach — an outcome Goldman Sachs analysts assess as likely to produce an inflationary "win-win" spending deal boosting both defense and non-defense outlays.

AI Correction and Equity Markets

The midterm results introduced immediate downside risk to AI-driven capital expenditure, the primary driver of recent GDP acceleration. Goldman Sachs estimated more than 45% of planned US data center capacity sits in states where newly elected Democratic governors campaigned on construction restrictions. Texas alone — representing approximately 25% of total planned capacity — elected its first Democratic governor since 1990. The Thursday selloff was indiscriminate: Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, and memory names all fell sharply before recovering 4–9% in Friday premarket trading. ING moved to sell a $500 million data center loan portfolio, an early signal of credit market repricing. Exceptions included Airbnb, which gained 12%, and Cloudflare, up 14%, on strong earnings. The equal-weighted S&P 500 diverged meaningfully from the Nasdaq throughout the week, confirming the correction remains sector-specific rather than macro-driven. ---

Asia

  • South Korea's October exports rose 76.1% year-over-year on a working-day-adjusted basis, reaching $115.3 billion and beating consensus, sustaining AI-driven trade momentum even as technology equities corrected sharply.
  • South Korea's Kospi fell as much as 8.8% on Friday, triggering a circuit breaker; the MSCI Asia Pacific Index tumbled 4% and Japan's Nikkei declined 6%, approaching bear market territory.
  • Indonesia's Q3 GDP accelerated to 5.5%, beating consensus, supported by government stimulus and household consumption.
  • Toyota surged more than 16% on Friday — its largest single-day move since 1987 — after raising its annual profit forecast on strong hybrid demand and announcing an expanded buyback.
  • China's CSI 300 has fallen 13% year-to-date, with the Star 50 losing 24% in October alone and erasing more than $3 trillion in value, even as state-backed funds intervened repeatedly.

China: Data Disappointment and Policy Response

China's official manufacturing PMI for October expanded at a slower-than-expected pace — the first deceleration since July — arriving against the backdrop of the CSI 300's worst monthly performance in over a decade. Authorities responded by deploying the same equity-support tools used in the 2024 stabilization episode: a PBOC swap facility operation, relending programs, and direction of "national team" funds into domestic equities. The CSI 300 recovered 0.8% on Monday and the Star 50 posted its best session since July mid-week, before both indices retreated again. The structural tension remains unresolved — durable stabilization requires demand-side fiscal stimulus and evidence that AI earnings justify current valuations, neither of which materialized. China's Fifth Plenum resolution, emphasizing centralized political control and a "top-down approach" for cadres, raised further long-run concerns: local government competition has historically been the engine of Chinese industrial dynamism, and subordinating that dynamic to political loyalty risks reducing adaptive capacity precisely when the economy most needs it.

Regional Trade and Technology Exposure

Taiwan reported its fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit economic growth, with September exports surging approximately 61% year-on-year to a record — the longest such run in nearly 39 years. Yet Taiwan equities saw $31.8 billion in foreign outflows last month, the largest on record, as investors reassessed AI trade positioning; the benchmark index fell 4.5% on Friday, entering a bear market. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each lost more than 6% on fears that a Democrat-controlled Congress would tighten AI and data-center regulation. The divergence between robust underlying trade data and equity repricing illustrates a market distinguishing between hardware demand — which remains structurally intact — and the valuation premium assigned to AI platform beneficiaries. India was the notable regional exception: the NSE Nifty 50 rose Friday and was on track for its best weekly gain in more than five years, as investors rotated into markets with minimal AI exposure.

Banking Sector and Political Risk

Asia-Pacific bank earnings deteriorated across the board. National Australia Bank reported rising credit impairment charges and announced cuts of up to 2,500 roles; Singapore's DBS suffered a decline in markets trading income; UOB flagged narrowing interest margins; and Westpac reported impairment charges more than doubling year-on-year. The common thread is higher funding costs compressing net interest income ahead of any meaningful rate relief. The RBA raised its benchmark rate to an 18-year high in a split 6-3 vote Tuesday, though Governor Bullock's subsequent guidance was interpreted as dovish, prompting the Australian dollar to weaken. Indonesia's political situation added a separate risk dimension: an investigation into the alleged wiretapping of President Prabowo Subianto prompted the military to deploy tens of thousands of troops to Jakarta, with senior officials characterizing the alleged conduct as potential treason. ---

Europe

  • The Bank of England raised rates unanimously to 4% on Thursday — the first hike in more than three years — with CPI running near 5% and Governor Bailey guiding that a further increase is "probably more likely than not."
  • Euro-area inflation quickened to double the ECB's 2% target, reinforcing the case for continued tightening and complicating the leadership transition, with ECB president nominations closing Friday.
  • Sweden hiked interest rates for the first time since 2023; Switzerland reported inflation jumping to a near-three-year high, adding pressure on the SNB.
  • Spain's Transport Minister resigned following a high-speed train crash that killed 69 people, delivering a significant political blow to Prime Minister Sánchez ahead of a snap election on November 29.
  • The Polish zloty fell to a three-year low after Prime Minister Tusk's government deployed riot police to the constitutional court and suspended the Chief Justice.

Bank of England Policy

The BoE's unanimous Thursday decision marked a material inflection after an extended pause. Bailey acknowledged that an inflation jump is "quite baked in at this point" even under a rapid de-escalation of the Iran conflict, reflecting the degree to which energy cost pass-through has already been embedded in the price level. The forward guidance — that another hike is more likely than not if energy costs re-accelerate — leaves the BoE in a tightening posture that now diverges from fading Fed rate-hike expectations. Research circulating ahead of the decision suggested UK rates are proving less effective at curbing inflation than anticipated, strengthening the committee's resolve. Sterling traded at $1.34 heading into the decision, suggesting markets had largely priced the move.

ECB Leadership and Eurozone Inflation

The ECB faces its most significant leadership overhaul in more than a decade. Christine Lagarde is departing nearly nine months early — citing a desire to speak freely ahead of France's presidential election — and four of six Executive Board seats will turn over within a year. Nomination submissions closed Friday, with the Netherlands proposing Klaas Knot and Spain proposing Pablo Hernández de Cos, candidates representing meaningfully different policy temperaments. The concentration of appointments raises coordination risks at a moment when euro-area inflation remains well above target. France compounds the picture domestically: major unions have called a nationwide mobilization, the Yellow Vest movement and student protesters have joined organized labor, and parliamentary budget negotiations remain deadlocked six months before elections.

Political Instability and Markets

Two distinct political stress events unfolded simultaneously. In Germany, weekend street violence across Frankfurt, Cologne, Hamburg, and Berlin added social instability to an economy already under industrial pressure. In Poland, Tusk's constitutionally unprecedented deployment of riot police to the constitutional court triggered an institutional crisis roughly a year before parliamentary elections, pushing the zloty to a three-year low. Despite these headwinds, the Stoxx Europe 600 was on course for its largest quarterly outperformance versus global equities since early last year, benefiting from relative insulation from the AI selloff. Meta raised €9 billion in a euro bond debut that drew record demand, suggesting credit appetite for high-quality issuers remains robust. ---

LatAm

  • Mexico's Q3 GDP beat consensus, accelerating on the strength of investment and exports, reinforcing the economy's nearshoring-driven resilience.
  • Brazil's real came under pressure after reports emerged that President Lula was considering meeting economist André Lara Resende, whose proposal to cut the Selic rate to match nominal GDP growth rattled confidence in central bank independence.
  • Paraguay's President Santiago Peña submitted his resignation following the collapse of a systemically connected commercial bank with government ties, though he vowed to contest a Senate removal vote to preserve potential immunity from criminal prosecution.
  • Chile rate expectations shifted materially, with investors rapidly pulling forward bets on the country's first rate hike since 2022 amid persistent inflation.

Brazil: Fiscal and Monetary Tensions

Investors articulated a coherent concern: Brazil's gross debt is on track to exceed 100% of GDP by the end of Lula's term per IMF projections, and the high Selic rate — while painful for growth — is what attracts the financing that keeps inflation contained and default risk manageable. Resende's argument that high rates cause, rather than respond to, fiscal deterioration found no buyers in markets. The comparison to Turkey's 2021 experience — when President Erdoğan pressured the central bank into an inflationary spiral — was widely invoked. Government denials offered some relief, but the episode underscored investor anxiety about fourth-term economic management. The central bank was widely expected to hold, with roughly 80% market probability assigned to a pause, reinforcing credibility at a moment when it was under threat. Lula's midterm election victory over MAGA-aligned Flávio Bolsonaro reinforced a bifurcated regional political map, with Brazil maintaining an independent posture distinct from Washington-aligned governments in Argentina and Peru.

Regional Political Economy

Paraguay's banking crisis represents the most acute near-term institutional stress in the region. The collapse of a government-connected commercial institution, and the resulting political crisis, highlights the fragility of governance structures in smaller economies with concentrated financial systems. The legal distinction between resignation and removal carries direct consequences for Peña's criminal exposure, making the Senate process a high-stakes proceeding. Separately, a developing super El Niño cycle is translating into measurable credit risk: BBVA initiated formal loan book reviews covering Peru, Argentina, and Colombia, with debt rescheduling identified as a potential tool for the most vulnerable borrowers. The phenomenon compounds existing inflationary pressure from energy costs and carries food price pass-through implications across the region. Emerging market external debt reached a record $9.4 trillion in 2025 per World Bank data, with lower-income nations paying average rates above 8% on new borrowing — a structural constraint on fiscal space that limits policy optionality as these shocks arrive simultaneously. ---

Global

  • Brent crude fell from near $120 per barrel at the start of the week to approximately $91–98 by Friday — a decline of roughly $20–25 — as Democratic midterm victories reduced perceived probability of further US-Iran escalation, with Monday's intraday decline of 11.4% the largest single-session drop in six months.
  • Gold rose above $4,000 per ounce on Tuesday, gaining 3.1%, as markets began pricing fiscal restraint under political gridlock; the previously tight positive correlation between oil prices and bond yields broke down, with the two moving inversely as growth concerns superseded inflation fears.
  • COP31 opens in Antalya, Turkey on November 9, with the US absent for the second consecutive year and development assistance flows down 23% from 2024 to 2025.
  • The Binance cryptocurrency exchange suffered a $6.3 billion hack attributed to North Korean state actors on Monday; Bitcoin held near $77,000, with markets treating the event as operational rather than systemic.

Oil Markets and the Iran Conflict

The week's dominant market dynamic was the sharp repricing of Iran-war risk premium embedded in energy markets. Monday's 11.4% single-day decline in Brent — triggered by Iranian President Pezeshkian invoking Supreme Leader Khamenei's authorization to pursue peace talks — was partially reversed Tuesday as Reuters reported the two sides remain far apart. The more sustained move came Wednesday and Thursday following the Democratic sweep, with Vice President Vance explicitly citing the conflict as costing Republicans the Senate "for at least six years." Trump publicly played down further strikes and characterized high energy prices as an electoral liability, introducing a genuine shift in the political calculus around escalation. Qatar's emir spoke with Trump by phone on de-escalating tensions, and oil pared earlier gains on the news. The outcome remains genuinely uncertain: Iran retains leverage through Strait of Hormuz disruptions and Houthi operations against Saudi Arabia
