In a sign of how quickly the inflationary picture has worsened, attention is 
shifting from whether the Federal Reserve will raise borrowing costs at its 
Wednesday meeting to how many hikes will follow.

Wall Street is pricing in 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/bond-market-s-extreme-short-counts-on-fed-to-deliver-rate-hike?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 a more than 90% chance that the central bank will deliver its first rate 
increase since 2023 — a level of conviction that hasproven right 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/history-shows-fed-will-deliver-rate-hike-markets-have-locked-in?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 for decades — and priced in another hike by the end of the year.

Pressure will be on Chairman Kevin Warsh to provide guidance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-09-16/fed-hike-who-s-afraid-of-big-bad-bond-yields?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 on the Fed’s future path during the post-meeting press conference. His failure 
to offer a clear explanation for the decision to hold rates at July’s meeting 
sparked a jump in long-term bond yields and a wave of criticism from traders 
and economists.

Warsh faces a difficult choice between appeasing bond investors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/fed-seen-hiking-rates-in-defiance-of-trump-decision-day-guide?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 looking for more aggressive tightening and keeping President Donald Trump 
happy. As recently as Sunday, Trump repeatedhis argument 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/trump-pushes-fed-to-lower-rates-despite-pressure-to-raise-them?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 that the US should have the lowest borrowing costs in the world. That’s even 
as a Treasury selloff drove yields to the highest since 2007.

The Bank of England is also looking down the barrel of rising inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/uk-inflation-rises-to-five-month-high-ahead-of-boe-rate-decision?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 before its meeting Thursday. UK drivers now face the most expensive diesel and 
petrol costs since 2022, helping drive inflation to a five-month high in 
August. While the bank is expected to keep rates unchanged, traders are pricing 
in four hikes over the next 12 months. Yields on 30-year bonds arethe highest 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/uk-long-bonds-are-struggling-even-as-the-government-sells-less?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 since 1998.

It wasn’t that long ago central banks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-18/the-bond-market-is-sending-a-warning-with-rising-long-term-debt-costs?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8958>
 were on an easing path. Now the only question is, how high will they go.
