• South Korea's August exports surged approximately 69% year-on-year, a decisive beat driven by AI-related semiconductor demand — the most significant hard data print in the region this session.
• China's private Caixin/RatingDog manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 in August from 50.9 in July, above consensus and marking nine consecutive months of expansion, the longest such run in five years.
• Japan's 10-year government bond yield touched 3.0% for the first time since 1996, with US Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly pressing the Bank of Japan to continue rate hikes amid renewed yen weakness.

**Chinese Manufacturing and Growth Momentum**

China's export-oriented manufacturers showed resilience in August, with new orders rising for the 15th consecutive month. However, the official PMI remained in contraction for a second month, and Goldman Sachs estimates GDP was expanding at roughly 4% annualised at the start of Q3 — down from 4.3% the prior quarter and below the government's 4.5–5.0% target. Separately, Beijing announced a ban on pre-sales of uncompleted homes without escrow protections, a structural reform of the property market that may reduce new project starts materially from 2027 onward, further pressuring local government land revenues.

**Japan and South Korea**

Japan's bond market continues its recalibration as the Bank of Japan allows yields to be set by market forces. The 3% threshold on 10-year JGBs is psychologically significant and, combined with external pressure from Bessent, keeps the pace of BOJ normalisation in focus. South Korea's export boom — 69% year-on-year — reflects surging global demand for memory chips and tech hardware. South Korean President Lee Jae-myung's cabinet reshuffled as approval ratings fell to a record-low 42%, with housing affordability cited as the primary source of public dissatisfaction.
