Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
energy reporter Dan Murtaugh explores one of the remote Chinese cities with the 
power, land and local support needed to underpin the nation’s massive AI shift.

Ulanqab is an unlikely battleground in the US-China tech rivalry.

About 350 kilometers (217 miles) northwest of Beijing, the city of under 2 
million was hitherto best known as a weekend getaway from the capital’s sticky 
summer heat, a place to ride horseback through a Martian landscape of dormant 
volcanoes.

Today, it’s a tech boom town 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/china-races-to-build-ai-data-centers-far-away-from-its-biggest-cities?cmpid=BBBXT090826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8634>
, with industrial parks on its eastern edge abuzz with the construction of data 
centers.

By around 2028, Ulanqab is set to become one of a handful of cities in China’s 
remote north and northwest that will overtake regions around megacities like 
Shanghai and Beijing in terms of computing equipment in active use, according to
research <bbg://news/stories/TL0QOFR24U8K> from BloombergNEF.

It forms part of a government strategy known as dongshu xisuan, which 
translates roughly as “Data in the East, Computing in the West.” The idea is 
that data centers near eastern metropolises will manage computing requests that 
don’t allow for a lag — like autonomous driving.

Work that can be carried out at a greater distance from the end-user, like 
cloud storage or model training, willmove further 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-china-data-centers-nvidia-chips/?cmpid=BBBXT090826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8634>
 into China’s less populated west.

According to BNEF, China is on track to more than double its capacity of live 
computing equipment in the next five years. Places like Ulanqab are being 
promoted precisely because they have abundant renewable energy, ample land and 
local governments eager for investment.

That gives China an advantage over parts of Europe and the US, where the 
data-center boom isincreasingly colliding 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/paxton-and-abbott-break-with-trump-sour-on-data-centers?cmpid=BBBXT090826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8634>
 with local concerns about power, water and land.

Of course, China’s strategy also creates a risk of too many cities pushing too 
many data-center projects, resulting in the kind ofovercapacity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-05/china-s-crowded-solar-industry-pivots-to-new-areas-of-growth?cmpid=BBBXT090826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8634>
 that’s already plaguing other industries. Beijing is trying to get ahead of 
that by forcing large projects to get national approval before construction can 
begin, according to BNEF.

But in China’s grasslands — where streets are named after tech giants like 
Apple Inc. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AAPL:US?cmpid=BBBXT090826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8634>
 andHuawei Technologies Co. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/40978Z:CH?cmpid=BBBXT090826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8634>
 — the AI boom isn’t meeting resistance. It’s being welcomed with open arms.

—Dan Murtaugh, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

BNEF sees gas storage sites across northwest Europe, Italy and Austria 67% 
full by Nov. 1, 14 percentage points below last year. In the base case, which 
assumes the Strait of Hormuz reopens in early November, inventories are 
expected to fall to 21% by the end of the winter.
