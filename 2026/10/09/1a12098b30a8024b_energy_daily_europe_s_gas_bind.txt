Europe’s Tough Choices to Avert a Gas Supply Crunch This Winter

Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Priscila Azevedo Rocha takes stock of Europe’s gas supply outlook as 
prices remain more than double what they were before the Iran war.

Europe has known for months 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/europe-faces-winter-gas-reckoning-as-global-fight-for-lng-brews?cmpid=BBBXT100926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9874>
 that it will enter winter with far less gas in storage than usual. Now that 
the heating season is underway, the question is: How will the region avoid a 
severe supply crunch if it gets really cold?

The choices are unenviable. Countries could pay up to secure more liquefied 
natural gas on the global market, but that’s a costly undertaking with the Iran 
war continuing toerode supplies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/world-s-top-lng-buyer-doesn-t-see-qatar-supply-coming-back-soon?cmpid=BBBXT100926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9874>
.

Alternatively, they could allow elevated gas prices to force consumption down. 
Yet the 2022 energy crisis already took out a large chunk of demand from 
industry, some of which has never returned, leaving the region with less slack 
this time around.

The numbers show the size of the challenge.

Europe’s underground storage sites 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/why-europe-s-gas-prices-and-storage-issues-are-causing-growing-concerns?cmpid=BBBXT100926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9874>
 are just over 73% full, compared with a seasonal norm approaching 90%. It 
already faces increased competition with Asia for limited supplies of LNG, and 
the options will dwindle further once a full European Unionban on Russian flows 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-03/eu-finalizes-deal-to-phase-out-russian-gas-imports-by-2027?cmpid=BBBXT100926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9874>
 kicks in early next year.

Covering the storage shortfall with additional imports would cost an estimated 
€3 billion ($3.4 billion), according to the Institute for Energy Economics and 
Financial Analysis. That’s 12% more than the same volume would have cost last 
year, reflecting the impact of the conflict in the Middle East.

If the EU opts to reduce consumption, the decline would need to be about 7% 
year-on-year to shield its supply buffer, the IEEFA said.

The tight market underscores Europe’s vulnerability as it’s grown more reliant 
on inventories in recent years, with stored gascovering 
<https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/nrg_cb_gasm__custom_22449251/default/table>
 about 30% of winter demand last season. That dependence has become a 
“financial liability,” the think tank said.

As always, the weather will be key. October has been mild so far and is 
forecast to remain that way for most of the month, buying Europe time. Yet some 
analysts see signs of disruptive cold spells later in the season.

Unusually cold weather could leave Europe’s stockpiles at just 11% of capacity 
by the end of March, according to gas-transmission group ENTSOG. That would 
give traders an even tougher job to refill storage next summer.

Europe isn’t going to run out of gas, but it’s ill-prepared 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-03/europe-faces-a-costly-winter-as-energy-prices-surge?cmpid=BBBXT100926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9874>
 for any spikes in demand. That means it may need to keep prices high to 
attract enough global cargoes, or consumers of the fuel will simply have to use 
much less.

—Priscila Azevedo Rocha, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Global oil refining capacity is set to rise by about 1.5 million barrels a day 
this year and next, reversing a drop in 2025, according toBloombergNEF 
<https://about.bnef.com/>. China and India account for more than 70% of 
additions over the two-year period across eight projects. Yet the ramp-up will 
take time, with much of the incremental throughput expected to emerge only in 
2027, BNEF said.
