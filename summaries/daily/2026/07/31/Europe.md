**Inflation and Monetary Policy**
Euro-area inflation picked up in July, a print that reinforces the ECB's case for additional rate tightening. The data arrives at a moment when the Bank of England is navigating a separate challenge: the institution's messaging has grown increasingly pointed in attributing UK inflationary and labor market pressures to US trade policy. With both central banks leaning toward further restraint, the policy environment for European growth remains tight.

**Wildfires and Economic Consequences**
The scale of this summer's European wildfires has moved from a climate story to an economic one. The Gironde fire in France and the merged Madrid-area blazes in Spain forced mass evacuations and destroyed homes and businesses at a scale European firefighting capacity was not designed to handle. Insurers and reinsurers contacted by Bloomberg are not treating this as a transient shock: they anticipate structurally higher premiums, larger catastrophe losses, and a need to shift risk to capital markets via catastrophe bonds. Climate researchers note that Europe is warming faster than any other continent, and that conditions this summer were made at least 20 times more likely by climate change in Spain. The economic cost — spanning property damage, business interruption, and infrastructure risk — is likely to compound as a second heat spell builds across the continent.
