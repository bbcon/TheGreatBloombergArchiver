With the clock ticking on President Donald Trump’s 30-day window before 
imposing tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/us-sets-50-tariff-on-some-canadian-goods-over-retaliation-claim?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>
 on Canadian goods, officials from Ottawa are hoping discussions in Washington 
this week will head off the simmering trade dispute.

Dominic LeBlanc, Canada’s minister responsible for Canada-US trade, and chief 
negotiator Janice Charette’s outreach comes more than week after the Trump 
administration threatened to collect a 50% tax starting Aug. 19 on about $20 
billion of Canadian goods imports. That’s about 5% of the total from the US’s 
northern neighbor annually.

Related: Netflix Tax To Be Cut in Canada Though Streamers Still Face Levy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/netflix-tax-will-soon-be-scrapped-canada-hints-in-court-filing?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>

Trump is invoking a Depression-era statute that’s never been activated — 
Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, which grants the president authority to 
impose duties on goods from countries deemed to discriminate against US 
commerce.

On those grounds, Trump is targeting three areas — dairy, autos and a 
provincial boycott of American wine and spirits.

“Canada, unlike other partners and allies, continues to retaliate against the 
United States for its efforts to rebalance trade and protect US industry in 
national-security sensitive sectors,” US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer 
said in astatement 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ambassador-greer-issues-statement-president-trump-imposing-section-338-tariffs-canada>
 on July 20.

Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada is weighing all options, suggesting 
retaliation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/carney-says-everything-s-on-the-table-if-us-imposes-new-50-tariffs?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>
 is possible if the US goes ahead with tariffs.

Read More: Canada Opens New Bridge to America Without Inviting Americans 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/canada-opens-gordie-howe-bridge-without-us-after-trump-s-50-tariff-threat?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>

Trump’s threat against his country’s No. 2 trading partner also drew pushback 
from some US-based labor unions whose membership extends across the border and 
who see the White House’s strategy as counterproductive.

China Threat

Instead, the focus of the US’s toughest trade barriers ought to be China, 
according to the international heads of the United Steelworkers and the 
International Association of Machinist and Aerospace Workers.

“Canada has been a trusted and valued partner for decades,” Roxanne Brown of 
the steelworkers union and the IAM’s Brian Bryant wrote in aletter 
<https://usw.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/7-2026-Joint-USW-IAM-Tariff-Letter-002.pdf>
 to Greer on Tuesday. “Our priority must be to align our trade policies to 
address the challenges posed by unfair and predatory trade practices of 
countries like China, not to drive a further wedge between our nations.”

The union chiefs added that unlike the US’s relationship with Mexico, 
“US-Canada trade is more balanced with wage, labor, environmental and other key 
issues that are aligned and compatible.”

Lately, Trump has sounded as unhappy as ever with the North American 
free-trade deal he signed in his first term, though he’s used such tactics 
before as leverage. Asked by Fox News on Tuesday if the US is looking to update 
the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement, Trump reiterated that “I’d rather be 
independent.”

‘Canada Needs Us’

“I don’t care. I mean, I don’t really want to,” he said. “Here’s the thing: 
Mexico and Canada need us. We don’t need them. The deal is important for them. 
It’s not important for us.”

In the Fox interview <https://www.foxnews.com/video/6402289547112>, Trump also 
said “we have the hottest car business” in history because of tariffs and shift 
in production back to the US — a claim that followedhis visit Monday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/trump-touts-tariffs-in-midterm-pitch-to-gm-workers-in-michigan?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>
 to a General Motors plant in Michigan where he said the automaker was having 
its best year ever.

Read More: CN Rail Boosts Outlook as Customers Navigate Tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/cn-rail-boosts-full-year-outlook-as-customers-navigate-tariffs?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>

Flavio Volpe, president of Canada’s Automotive Parts Manufacturers’ 
Association, disputed that assertion.

“They are swimming against a current like everyone in North America since the 
administration’s imposition of Section 232 tariffs in between their production 
and their customers,” Volpewrote on X 
<https://x.com/FlavioVolpe1/status/2082200662993977684> on Tuesday, referring 
to Trump’s existing levies on cars, steel and aluminum. “No one at GM or any 
automaker is benefitting, in any of the three countries.”

Chrystia Freeland, a former Canadian deputy prime minister, minister of 
foreign affairs and finance minister, was part of the negotiations when Nafta 
was renegotiated and rebranded as USMCA in 2017 and 2018. She said the 
president’s strategy isn’t about restoring fairness.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-07-26/trump-s-new-tariffs-are-testing-canada-s-patience-video?cmpid=BBD072926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7000>
“It’s about whether Canada is prepared to accept a profound change in its 
relationship with the United States,” she wrote in a New York Timesessay <> on 
Tuesday. “It’s about whether Canada is willing to go from being an ally and a 
partner, in a relationship governed by treaties and rules, to being a vassal, 
subject to Washington fiat.”

She’s calling for Canadians to “fight back. The only thing a bully understands 
is strength.”
