The headquarters of Deutsche Bank AG in Frankfurt, Germany.Photographer: Alex

Deutsche Bank’s traders are back. Seven years after an existential crisis 
forced the German lender to dismantle parts of itsglobal investment bank 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/how-deutsche-bank-plans-to-rebuild-its-trading-business-in-the-us?cmpid=111626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11443>
, its fixed-income unit is heading for record revenue of almost €10 billion 
this year—nearly double its 2019 haul.

 * Back from the brink. The rebirth is at least in part the work of Ram Nayak, 
who has managed to wrestle back market share from the dark days, with the 
business now ranking among the top fixed-income houses in Europe and Asia. This 
year’s revenue will place the German firm just behind Bank of America and ahead 
of Morgan Stanley, according to estimates compiled by Bloomberg.
 * America next. Emboldened by the turnaround, Deutsche Bank is rebuilding its 
US trading operation in areas including energy, emerging-market credit and 
mortgages. Nayak says a top-five position in the US is “very achievable.”
 * High stakes. Fixed income generated 84% of the investment bank’s revenue in 
the first nine months, leaving little room for missteps. The US expansion is 
also getting expensive, with compensation costs expected to approach €3 billion 
this year as Deutsche Bank hires more trading talent.
