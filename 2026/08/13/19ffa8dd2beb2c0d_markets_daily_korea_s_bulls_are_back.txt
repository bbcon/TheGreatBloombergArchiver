Bulls are back in Korea

To track the wild swings in investor attitudes towards AI stocks, there’s no 
better barometer than South Korea’s Kospi index. Right now it’s sending the 
message that the market is back in full-on greed mode.

The benchmark has soared 22% in the past 10 sessions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/korean-stocks-rise-22-in-ten-days-as-chip-rally-regains-steam?cmpid=081326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7649>
, putting it back in a bull market.The Kospi trades like a pure play on AI 
because two big winners from the data center boom — Samsung and SK Hynix — 
account for half the index.A pair of earnings reports late last month from big 
AI spenders Microsoft and Amazon eased concern about a bubble.

The rally comes after a 39% plunge from the all-time highs in late June. The 
market bottomed on July 30, the day after the government convened an emergency, 
late-night meeting of regulators to curb some of the excesses tied to leveraged 
exchange-traded funds.

“The market overshot to the downside during the unwinding of leveraged 
positions, and the current rebound is a natural one as flow stabilized,” said 
Kang DaeKwun at Life Asset Management. “That said, I think it will be difficult 
for the market to sustain a continued rally until we see some stabilization in 
the AI narrative and US interest rates.”

In South Korea, efforts by the authorities to cool the mania have paid off. 
Trading in leveraged ETFshas plummeted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/south-korea-s-leveraged-etf-trading-plummets-under-new-curbs?cmpid=081326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7649>
 since the introduction of a higher cash deposit. Now, regulators are requiring 
new investors in single-stock leveraged ETFs to undergo at leastfive days of 
mock trading exercises

The case for an earnings-led rally

It’s not just South Korea: In the US, too, some of the excess has come out of 
the equity market. Earnings are a big reason why.

Profits are growing at a pace rarely seen this far into an economic expansion. 
With the exception of rebounds seen after recessions, they haven’t grown so 
quickly since at least 1998.Due largely to the AI boom 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/garbage-truck-margin-boost-shows-the-ai-profit-boom-has-begun?cmpid=081326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7649>
, US earnings exceeded expectations by 29%, the most in at least two years.

The profit jump, coupled with the largely sideways move in stocks since late 
May, has reset valuations, taking off some of the froth.Some investors worry 
that the growth is so strong that the inevitable slowdown willpressure stock 
prices next year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/potential-peak-earnings-set-the-stage-for-disappointing-stocks?cmpid=081326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7649>
.

But at around 20 times estimated earnings, the S&P 500 trades in line with its 
five-year average. Even getting it back to its six-month, pre-Iran war average 
of 22.3 signals a roughly 11% gain to about 8,600, if earnings forecasts stay 
unchanged. —Tatiana Darie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASS6kq2J56U/tatiana-darie?cmpid=081326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7649>
