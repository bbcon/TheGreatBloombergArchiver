• Six months into the Iran war, global fossil-fuel importers have paid more than $330 billion in extra costs since fighting began on February 28 — roughly Finland's annual GDP — according to the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA).
• US Central Command says Navy divers and SEALs have cleared mines from the Strait of Hormuz, though allies privately warn the route remains likely mined; Goldman Sachs estimates Gulf oil exports have recovered to about two-thirds of pre-war levels.
• Gold-mining equities are having their best month on record, with the MSCI global gold miners index up 43%, as the US Treasury's plan to expand bond buybacks revives the "debasement trade" globally.
• Monetary policy is diverging sharply: the Fed's Warsh speaks at Jackson Hole today amid a divided FOMC, Japan spent a record $96.4 billion defending the yen over the past month, and markets fully price an ECB hike to 2.5% in September.

**Energy Realignment**
The war has pushed governments, companies and consumers toward renewables and EVs faster than feared, with global greenhouse-gas emissions up just 0.2% in the first half versus a year earlier, per Climate Trace — a smaller increase than many anticipated given fears of a coal rebound. China has emerged as a net beneficiary of its clean-technology manufacturing dominance, while Gulf exporters absorbed roughly $2 billion a day in export losses at the March peak, per Rice University estimates.

**Diplomacy**
Iran's foreign minister said resuming US diplomacy "isn't impossible" following "creative discussions" with mediator Qatar, even as Washington presses a new economic-pressure campaign against Tehran.
