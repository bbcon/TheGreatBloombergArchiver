Tuesday, November 03, 2026

US · Asia · Europe · LatAm · Global

US

  • US ISM Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.5 in October, beating consensus and reaching its highest level since late 2021, with new orders remaining firmly expansionary.
  • US midterm elections underway; Democrats widely expected to take the House, with Senate control more uncertain — a split result would end unified Republican government.
  • Apple shares fell 3.7% premarket after the company guided for thinner-than-projected profit margins; the Nasdaq 100 has entered correction territory, down 10.4% from its peak.
  • Fiscal pressure looms: debt ceiling expected to be breached in early 2027, with a divided Congress making bipartisan agreement both necessary and uncertain.

Manufacturing and Economic Data

October's ISM Manufacturing index rose one point to 55.5, beating consensus and posting its best production reading since late 2021. New orders remained well within expansionary territory, suggesting the industrial economy retains momentum despite broader uncertainty around AI investment cycles and geopolitical risk. The print offers a constructive counterpoint to the equity market turbulence dominating near-term sentiment.

Monetary Policy and Fiscal Outlook

With the Fed's December rate decision approaching — markets continue to price a hike under Chair Kevin Warsh — the more consequential medium-term risk may be fiscal. Goldman Sachs and Citigroup analysts both assess it as unlikely that a lame-duck Republican Congress will resolve the debt ceiling before losing its majority. A divided government arriving in January faces two hard deadlines: a government funding expiration on December 11 and a Treasury borrowing limit breach projected in mid-2027. Analysts at Goldman note the most probable resolution involves a "win-win" spending deal that boosts both defense and non-defense outlays — an outcome that leans inflationary and should concern fixed income investors.

Equity Markets

The Nasdaq 100's 10.4% drawdown from its peak reflects a confluence of pressures: Midterm uncertainty, Apple's margin warning, Palantir's weaker sales outlook, and oil-driven inflation anxiety. S&P 500 futures were modestly lower as trading commenced. ---

Asia

  • China's CSI 300 fell 0.2% and the tech-heavy Star 50 tumbled 3.8% after Beijing's revival of 2024-era market support tools — a swap facility and relending operations — failed to reassure investors.
  • The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its benchmark rate to an 18-year high in a split 6-3 decision; Governor Bullock's subsequent remarks were read as dovish, prompting the Australian dollar to fall.
  • South Korea's Kospi slid nearly 4%, dragged lower by the global AI chip selloff and midterm-related risk aversion.
  • Vietnam's October trade surplus surged to more than five times September's level, with exports to the US rising sharply and underscoring the broader strength of Asia's non-China trade engines.

China: Stimulus Credibility and Market Stress

Beijing deployed an array of market-stabilizing measures — reviving equity-support facilities used in 2024 and directing the PBOC to cool the yuan's longest winning streak in three decades — but markets were unimpressed. The CSI 300 has now declined for 12 consecutive weeks and fallen 13% year-to-date, even as most global indices have rallied. The October monthly loss of 8.5% was the index's worst in over a decade. Analysts note that durable stabilization requires demand-side fiscal stimulus reaching households and evidence that AI and tech earnings can justify current valuations — neither of which was delivered Tuesday.

Australia and Regional Central Banks

The RBA's decision to hike in a split vote signals continued concern about inflation even as growth moderates. Bullock's guidance toward a pause was interpreted as dovish by markets, with rate expectations pared back and the Australian dollar weakening. The divergence between a hiking RBA and the market's interpretation of a dovish signal illustrates the difficulty central banks face in managing expectations during a late-cycle tightening phase.

Trade and Export Momentum

Vietnam's export data and Taiwan's fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit GDP growth — the longest such run in nearly 39 years — underscore that AI-driven demand continues to channel significant economic benefit through supply chain hubs outside China. Taiwan's September exports surged 61% year-on-year to a record, beating all Bloomberg survey estimates. ---

Europe

  • No significant macro data releases were the primary driver; European equity markets were set for a weaker open, tracking the US Nasdaq correction and AI-sector concerns.
  • Sweden's Riksbank was expected to announce a potential shift to hiking on Wednesday, adding to European monetary policy complexity.
  • Switzerland reported inflation jumping to a near-three-year high, adding pressure on the SNB.

Monetary Policy Divergence

European monetary policy remains in flux. Switzerland's inflation surge to a near-three-year high complicates the SNB's stance, while Sweden's Riksbank appeared poised to join the hiking cycle — a notable shift for a central bank that had been among the more cautious in the region. The ECB's path remains constrained by weak growth in the eurozone core even as energy-driven inflation pressures persist from Strait of Hormuz disruptions.

Markets and Geopolitical Exposure

European equities were positioned for a softer open, with Brookings Institution analyst Robin Brooks noting that French debt, alongside gold, silver, and the S&P 500, has been moving in correlation with oil — reflecting war-risk premium rather than idiosyncratic domestic factors. Royal Philips rose 7.1% in Amsterdam after settling a US regulatory probe over faulty sleep apnea devices, a rare positive outlier in an otherwise risk-off session. Brookfield Asset Management and the Qatar Investment Authority agreed to invest £2.5 billion in a UK data center project with Drax Group, underscoring continued private capital appetite for energy-adjacent infrastructure despite the broader market uncertainty. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil presented a formal fossil fuel transition road map at the post-COP30 follow-up process, proposing a phased, country-specific framework to reduce dependence on oil, gas, and coal over the next decade.
  • Chilean President José Antonio Kast's approval rating fell to a record low, weighed down by a child sex offender early-release controversy compounding anger over a stagnant economy.
  • Brazil's midterm election results deepened pessimism among agricultural sector participants hoping for policy relief.

Brazil: Climate Leadership and Domestic Pressures

Brazil's COP30 organizing team released a phased fossil fuel transition road map, positioning Brasília as a constructive actor in post-Dubai climate diplomacy. The framework categorizes countries by economic archetype and proposes tailored policy levers, with findings intended to inform COP33 in 2028. The initiative is voluntary and does not require multilateral negotiation — a deliberate design choice reflecting the difficulty of achieving consensus, as seen when a similar road map proposal at COP30 failed due to opposition from major fossil fuel exporters. Meanwhile, Brazil's domestic political landscape grew more complicated as election results weighed on agricultural sector sentiment, signaling potential friction between the government's green agenda and commodity-dependent constituencies.

Chile: Political Risk

President Kast's record-low approval — just eight months into his term — illustrates the vulnerability of right-leaning governments in Latin America when economic stagnation coincides with governance failures. A sluggish economy that has failed to deliver visible improvements in living standards has left Kast with limited political capital ahead of what may become a more contentious second half of his term. ---

Global

  • Brent crude fell 11.4% on Monday — its largest single-day drop in six months — after Iran's President Pezeshkian indicated the Supreme Leader had granted the government authority to pursue peace talks, before partially rebounding Tuesday on reports the two sides remain far apart.
  • The Nasdaq 100 entered a 10.4% correction, with AI investment cycle concerns and Hormuz-driven inflation anxiety weighing simultaneously on risk assets.
  • The Binance cryptocurrency exchange suffered a $6.3 billion hack attributed to North Korean state actors; Bitcoin held near $77,000, with the market treating the event as an operational rather than systemic shock.

Oil Markets and the US-Iran Conflict

Monday's 11.4% decline in Brent — the largest in six months — was driven by Iran signaling openness to diplomacy, with President Pezeshkian invoking Supreme Leader Khamenei's authorization as a credibility marker. However, Reuters reported Tuesday that the two sides remain far apart, and oil partially recovered. The front-month Brent contract had been approaching new highs even as the December 2027 contract reached $87 — a level implying markets were pricing in prolonged disruption. Analysts at Signum Global argue that the logic of de-escalation is sound: Iran faces real re-escalation risk, mediators including China are pressing for compromise, and the Trump administration has incentive to avoid renewed hostilities. But as the 2006 Iraq war analogy illustrates, US domestic politics — including a potential Democratic House majority — does not automatically constrain a president's war-making authority.

AI Investment Cycle and Global Capital Markets

Wall Street's senior leadership remains divided on AI's trajectory. Morgan Stanley's Ted Pick described the current moment as the "second inning" of a multi-trillion dollar buildout; PIMCO's Emmanuel Roman warned that $10 trillion in financing over five years risks becoming excessive, with a downturn eventually likely. The tension between infrastructure capital demand — Oracle raised nearly $20 billion in equity and debt in a single week — and elevated valuations is sharpening. The Nasdaq correction and Apple's margin warning suggest the market may be beginning to price in some of that risk.

Crypto and Geopolitical Risk

The Binance hack — $6.3 billion stolen, attributed to North Korean state actors — triggered $4.7 billion in net withdrawals but did not produce a systemic collapse analogous to FTX. Bitcoin's relative stability near $77,000 reflects market confidence in Binance's commitment to cover losses, though the reputational damage to an exchange described as "effectively the JPMorgan of crypto" — without the regulatory backstop — remains an open question for institutional adoption.
