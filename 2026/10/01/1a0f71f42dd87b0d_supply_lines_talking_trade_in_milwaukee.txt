US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer is meeting with counterparts from 
across the globe this week at the Group of 20 trade ministerial in Milwaukee, 
Wisconsin.

It’s the first time the world’s trade ministers have gotten together since 
Washington putsweeping tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/us-proposes-broad-tariffs-of-at-least-10-citing-forced-labor?cmpid=BBD100126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9353>
 on imports from dozens of trading partners earlier this summer under 
allegations that they failed to enforce rules prohibiting forced labor in 
supply chains.

US trade policy has shifted abruptly since President Donald Trump began his 
second term, straining relationships with most partners. Milwaukee, a Lake 
Michigan brewery town north of Chicago with its own rich history in the US’s 
rise as a manufacturing power, sits in the agricultural and industrial 
heartland that Trump is trying to revive.

So in theory, a trade ministerial in the same city that hosted the 2024 
Republican National Convention seems like a fitting place to size up how 
countries are handling the tariff wall the US is rebuilding after the Supreme 
Court struck down Trump’s emergency duties in February.
Trump and his family stand on stage during the Republican National Convention 
in Milwaukee on July 18, 2024.Photographer: Al Drago/Bloomberg
Yet back on the East Coast there was a fresh legal test of the authority Trump 
is using to collect some import taxes. While Greer hosted a series of meetings 
Wednesday, the US administration’s attorneys faced tough questions in a trade 
court in New York about the legality of the Section 301forced-labor tariffs 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations>
.

Legal Scrutiny

The three-judge panel also turned a critical eye to some of the claims raised 
by the small businesses and Democratic state officials who brought the suit. 
The US Court of International Trade panel didn’t say when it intends to rule.

At the Milwaukee talks, most of the events 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/september/ambassador-greer-travel-milwaukee-wisconsin-host-g20-trade-ministerial>
 excluded press, so it’s been tough the assess the mood. Reporters did briefly 
hear from trade ministers from the European Union, Maros Sefcovic, and Japan, 
Ryosei Akazawa, on Tuesday evening.

The two ministers joined a roundtable discussion on excess capacity hosted by 
Greer. The roundtable also included executives from US-based companies 
Cleveland-Cliffs, Steel Dynamics and Dow, which emphasized how the tariffs have 
benefited their industries.

Transatlantic Relations

Sefcovic said that the work between the US and the EU to reach a trade 
framework paid off “extremely well” last year. The economies are moving in 
close cooperation on “economic security,” and ensuring that they have a 
reliable supply chain of critical minerals.

Akazawa emphasized the importance of collaborating with like-minded countries 
to strengthen economic and supply chain resilience, especially among rising 
political tensions across the globe.

Indian Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal is in the US on a seven-day visit and 
also reportedlymet with Greer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/trump-modi-discuss-trade-energy-ties-as-new-us-tariffs-loom?cmpid=BBD100126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9353>
 at the G-20 talks.

At the same time, the US is investigating whether to apply more tariffs in a 
separateSection 301 case 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/march/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity-and-production>
 involving excess manufacturing capacity on products from more than dozen 
economies — including the EU, India and Japan.

Steel Capacity

In a statement, USTR said that the topic of excess capacity itself would be a 
focus during the meetings this week.

The issue has long-standing roots in the US. Industry officials tout a 50% 
tariff on steel and aluminum as being a key solution in combating the influx of 
cheap steel, and often point to the flood of imports as reason for the downfall 
of domestic manufacturing. Critics say the duties are too high, and limit 
cooperation between trading partners.

On Wednesday, members of the Global Forum on Steel Excess Capacity released a 
pledge 
<https://us.list-manage.com/T3McxGFcQ0P?e=331128ba95&c2id=e677d0c7b376c42069e3fcd95d07f140>
 to address the issue, called the “Milwaukee Framework.” It includes vows to 
“develop and enhance supply chain transparency through the collection and 
publication of steel trade data, including ‘country of melt and pour.’”

The steel industry has been pushing for the so-called melt-and-pour standard 
to combat transshipment. The standard defines the product’s country of origin 
as the place where the raw materials were melted and poured.

 | The US merchandise-trade deficit unexpectedly widened in August, reflecting 
a jump in the value of imports of industrial supplies such as petroleum 
products. The shortfall in goods trade totaled $132.6 billion, the widest since 
early 2025, Commerce Department data showed. Imports increased 5.5%, led by a 
16.6% surge in the value of inbound shipments of industrial supplies. Imports 
of capital goods — a category that includes computers and accessories, 
semiconductors and telecommunications equipment — also rose. US exports, 
meanwhile, climbed 1.9%.
