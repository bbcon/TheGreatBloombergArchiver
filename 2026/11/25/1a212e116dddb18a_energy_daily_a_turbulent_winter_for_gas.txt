Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Elena Mazneva looks at the sharp price swings roiling Europe’s gas 
market.

It’s been a choppy week so far for the European natural gas trade.

A key gauge of volatility for the front-month contract has jumped to the 
highest since May, with prices at times swinging by more than 5%.

Those fluctuations have been driven largely by shifting weather forecasts. 
While the weather is typically a major price driver in winter, withcold snaps 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-02/energy-traders-scrutinize-record-early-polar-vortex-collapse?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
 driving up gas consumption, this year everything is amplified.

That’s because Europe started the heating season with gas inventories far 
lower than normal, after Middle Eastsupply disruptions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/iran-war-has-permanently-redrawn-global-energy-markets-iea-says?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
 hampered efforts to replenish stocks. It now has little wiggle room when 
demand spikes and ismore reliant on spot purchases 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-29/war-and-lng-shift-breaks-economics-of-european-gas-storage?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
 of liquefied natural gas in the global market.

For now, supplies remain relatively stable, with LNG imports rising as some 
tankers divert 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/europe-is-beating-asia-in-fight-for-gas-supply-disrupted-by-war?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
 away from Asia toward Europe, where prices recently soared to a four-year 
high. Yet the hefty costs for utilities are already coursing through the 
economy, and will eventually filter through to household bills and inflation.

There’s also a risk that a dip in temperatures in Asia could reverse that LNG 
flow.Spot power prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/japan-power-prices-four-year-high-cold-outlook-winter-snow-weather-gas-lng?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
 have jumped in Japan this month, making it affordable to import LNG for 
gas-fired stations for the first time since February.

In the US, where the current gas trade looks very different, volatility surged 
earlier this week too. The December contract slumped the most in almost 10 
months as forecasts for milder weather pointed to weaker demand in a market 
flush with supply. USHenry Hub 
<https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/energy/natural-gas/natural-gas.html> 
benchmark prices are just a fraction of those for European futures.

Abundant US supply could bolster exports across the Atlantic, though there’s a 
risk prices drop so low that some producers cut output.

In Europe, long-range forecasts suggest the weather may become colder later 
this winter. Either way, the market is likely to remain vulnerable to large 
price swings, especially toward the end of the heating season when inventories
will have substantially drained 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-09/europe-s-gas-storage-problems-seen-lasting-well-beyond-winter?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
.

For the region’s traders, persistent volatility can present opportunities to 
profit from large price dislocations. Yet it can also bring steep losses to 
those caught on the wrong side of the trade.

—Elena Mazneva, Bloomberg News

Chart of the day

A record three-quarters of the global fleet of large oil-product tankers are 
shipping crude 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/most-large-fuel-tankers-now-ship-dirty-crude-upending-trading?cmpid=BBBXT112526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11804>
 to secure higher earnings, according to Signal Ocean data. The vessels’ holds 
are coated to transport refined fuels such as jet fuel and naphtha, and they 
have to undergo a costly cleaning process to return to the trade after taking 
“dirty” crude. The shift in use of the fleet is helping to keep the cost of 
transporting fuels elevated.

stations, and recent projects have achieved some of the lowest levelized 
capture costs worldwide. Those costs could fall further, to $44 a metric ton by
