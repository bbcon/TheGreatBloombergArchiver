It’s judgment day for Donald Trump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/trump-faces-referendum-as-voters-head-to-polls-in-midterm-elections-2026?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
 in America. For Asian leaders, the US midterms mean they can finally start to 
imagine a world without Tariff Man.

They probably shouldn’t breathe a sigh of relief just yet, though.

When Americans vote today, they’ll be delivering a verdict on a presidential 
comeback that brought thempersistent inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/strong-gdp-unlikely-to-sway-midterm-voters-worried-about-inflation?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
 and an unwanted war against Iran that’s making affordability among the
campaign’s top issues 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-us-midterm-election-affordability/?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
. Once the polls close, the country will soon shift focus to who follows Trump 
in 2028.

If the Republicans are bruised as projected, the danger for everyone from 
China’s Xi Jinping to Japanese leader Sanae Takaichi is that for the final two 
years of his last term, Trump — hamstrung at home —becomes bolder abroad 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-31/us-midterms-trumpism-may-already-be-in-decline-worldwide?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
.

His legacy-mode laundry list could look like this: Tokyo pressured into paying 
more for US troops (a longtime bugbear for Trump); South Korea forced into 
signing up to unfavorable investment projects; and Taiwan squeezed into handing 
over more chip technology.

A fourth and final rendezvous with North Korea’s Kim Jong Un for old times’ 
sake can’t be ruled out.
Voters wait to cast early ballots in Houston, Texas, on Friday.Photographer: 
Antranik Tavitian/Bloomberg
There are immediate policy implications, too. For Asian investors already 
bleeding from the AI rout, a Democratic sweep of both chambers of Congress 
would likely bring greater scrutiny ofthe artificial intelligence industry 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/wall-street-ceos-big-global-money-managers-debate-ai-risks?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
 and data-center buildout.

South Korean and Taiwanese chipmakers will be watching closely.

Democrats also differ from Trump in supporting clean energy. That’s a boon for 
Beijing — if it means welcoming world-leading Chinese solar, battery and 
electric-vehicle companies.

Across Asia, a better scenario would be Trump focusing on projects closer to 
home 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/trump-chooses-dc-reflecting-pool-for-midterm-closing-argument?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
, perhaps building the “Great Triumphal Arch” that’s facing legal battles.

That should keep him busy while policymakers from Bangkok to Beijing figure 
out how to prepare for whoever moves into the White House next.— Jenni Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVX1HMBXAFs/jenni-marsh?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>

weapons-delivery program

 for Ukraine, sources say, a sign its defense support for Kyiv continues 
despite tensions between Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. A 
Russian air strike on a humanitarianaid center

 in a massive overnight drone attack.

The US announced plans to revoke the access of South Africa and Rwanda to a

killing of White farmers and what he calls unfair race-based investment rules, 
while Rwanda is seen backing rebels in neighboring Democratic Republic of Congo.

A Libyan legislature approved Fawzi Abukhuzam’s appointment as governor of the 
central bank, the latest in aflurry of moves 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/libya-parliament-caps-recent-moves-by-backing-new-central-banker?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>

divided between an internationally recognized government in its west and the 
rival parliament in the east, where military commander Khalifa Haftar holds 
sway.

One of Haiti’s largest and most notorious gangs is seeking peace talks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/haiti-gang-leader-calls-for-peace-after-el-salvador-sends-troops?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>

more bloodshed in the Caribbean nation. The request, in a letter purportedly 
written by crime boss Wilson Joseph and sent to United Nations 
Secretary-General Antonio Guterres, comes after El Salvador dispatched more 
than 2,000 soldiers to crush “wicked” gangs.

island’s international standing.

Chart of the Day

In Cape Town, the rich and the less well off share something in common: both 
arestruggling to find homes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/south-africa-s-cape-town-faces-luxury-home-shortage-as-millionaires-rush-in?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
. An influx of millionaires has intensified competition for a limited supply of 
luxury properties in the coastal city, pushing prices higher in one of Africa’s 
most expensive real estate markets. Meanwhile, a growing population and a 
shortage of affordable housing are putting pressure on ordinary residents — a 
hot-button issue for voters in tomorrow’s municipal polls.

And Finally

A million-barrel-per-day oil pipeline from Alberta to south of Vancouver on 
the west coast was the first project to be designated under Mark Carney’s 
Building Canada Act. Dubbed “Pacific Link,” it was awarded national-interest 
status under a law passed last year that’s a central pillar of the government’s 
strategy to grow the economy and diversify trade in response to Trump’s 
tariffs. But now the prime minister’s decision to fast-track the project facesa 
wave of court challenges 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/carney-s-push-to-fast-track-oil-pipeline-faces-mounting-indigenous-lawsuits?cmpid=BBD110326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10938>
 mounted by Indigenous groups citing inadequate consultation.
