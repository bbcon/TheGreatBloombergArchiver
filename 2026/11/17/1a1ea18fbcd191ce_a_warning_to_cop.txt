have offered blunt warnings to the COP31 climate talks about the ramifications 
of failing to deliver outcomes on key issues.

A warning from the EU

By John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>

Annual United Nations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/8243133Z:US?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>
 climate talks risk becoming “untenable” as debate over a vast agenda causes 
envoys to lose focus on how to combat global warming, according to the European 
Union’s key official.

A lengthy draft text of a final agreement published Monday, with 144 
paragraphs, laid out myriad options on contentious issues including the future 
of fossil fuels, climate finance and trade restrictions. The volume of topics 
was overwhelming, the EU’s climate commissioner said.
Wopke HoekstraPhotographer: Saeed Khan/AFP/Getty Images
“It is hugely important that at this COP, which is about implementation, we do 
not get distracted by other things,” Wopke Hoekstra told delegates Tuesday at 
the summit in Antalya, Turkey. “To be frank, that is unfortunately what the 
draft cover decision is doing, and we do run the risk to make the process 
untenable.”

Read More: Climate Summit Draft Sets Up Fights on Fossil Fuels, Electricity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/global-electrification-push-runs-into-resistance-at-cop31-climate-talks?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>

Negotiators from almost 200 nations at the talks, which opened Nov. 9 and are 
scheduled to run through Friday, should instead focus on core tasks, including 
the goal of delivering faster and deeper cuts to greenhouse gas emissions, he 
said.

“It is absolutely critical that COP31 sends the strongest possible signal on 
the clean energy transition,” Hoekstra said, addressing a session alongside 
ministers from dozens of other nations, including Spain, Japan and Singapore.

Hoekstra’s comments underscore the difficulties ahead for envoys to reach 
agreed outcomes from the talks in Turkey, and to deliver more ambitious climate 
action. Advocates have urged nations to deliver a response after a recent UN 
report showed the world will inevitably overshoot the 1.5C goal warming 
threshold set under the Paris Agreement.

Powerful pollutant

24,300

One kilogram of sulphur hexafluoride (SF₆) — used in electricity grid 
switchgear — has about 24,300 times the warming impact of an equivalent amount 
of carbon dioxide over 100 years. Germany and China are among nations 
highlighting measures to tackle the pollutant at COP31.

Wasted words

“For every COP where we spend time stalling over the semantics, millions die 
in developing countries across the world.”

Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings

Ghana’s Minister of Environment, Science and Technology. Ghana currently 
chairs the African Group of Negotiators on Climate Change.

Concerns on electrification

By Azman Usmani 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYPlpafOt_4/azman-usmani?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>
 andAkshat Rathi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOobfNzkQ/akshat-rathi?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>

A proposal at United Nations climate talks to increase electricity’s role in 
the global energy system needs detailed country plans and financing to become 
more than an empty idea, according to the head of the International Solar 
Alliance.

The presidency of COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, began by pushing for countries to 
sign onto a voluntary pledge to raise electricity’s share of global final 
energy demand to 35% by 2035, from just over 20% currently. The notion was 
included in draft language of the final deal on Monday, but there is opposition 
to retaining it in an ultimate declaration with the backing of nearly 200 
nations at the summit.
A solar array on the roof of a factory in Harohalli, Karnataka, India, in 2025.
Photographer: Prashanth Vishwanathan/Bloomberg
Even if the electrification goal makes it into the final text, implementation 
will be tough, said Ashish Khanna, director general of the India-based 
intergovernmental group. The research going into the idea was based on a global 
analysis and doesn’t represent circumstances of many countries, Khanna said.

“It doesn’t mean that just announcing will make a difference,” Khanna said. 
“The number 35% should have come from a good bottom-up assessment. That is what 
the heart of the COP is.”

Khanna said to ease differences the organizers could have assessed 40 or 50 
countries across different economic and energy-system “archetypes,” and then 
estimated how quickly each could accelerate electrification, combining those 
pathways to test the global target.

On the ground at COP31

By John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>
,David Stringer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARMqcSMt218/david-stringer?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>
 andAkshat Rathi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOobfNzkQ/akshat-rathi?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>

Japan will create a network across the Asia-Pacific region to promote 
development of early-warning systems 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/japan-to-launch-natural-disaster-warning-network-across-asia?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>
 aimed at bolstering protections against extreme weather events and natural 
disasters.

Gaps in capabilities such as remote sensing and cellphone alerts have been 
highlighted by recent events, including the devastating August floods near the 
Nepal-China border, which killed more than 1,400 people and wiped out towns, 
border posts and hydropower plants.
Damaged properties following flash floods in Nepal’s Nuwakot district, on Aug. 
27.Photographer: Prabin Ranabhat/AFP/Getty Images
“The network will share knowledge and expertise in the introduction and 
operation of the early warning systems and promote effective deployment,” 
Japan’s Environment MinisterHiroyoshi Sasagawa 
<https://www.env.go.jp/en/aboutus/profile/minister.html> said Tuesday in a 
speech to the COP31 conference in Antalya, Turkey.

The UK is among nations championing a push to add new levies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/uk-backs-push-to-explore-new-global-levies-on-climate-polluters?cmpid=BBD111726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11488>
 on polluting sectors of the global economy and direct the proceeds to bolster 
funding to manage the impacts of climate change.

A declaration launched Tuesday at the COP31 climate talks acknowledged the 
potential for “small levies” to help mobilize climate finance for developing 
countries. Other nations backing the initiative include Spain, Kenya and 
Timor-Leste, the document said, confirming an earlier report by Bloomberg News.

Solidarity levies typically refer to surcharges on business class travel, 
financial transactions and fossil fuel production. The individual levies often 
raise only small amounts of revenue, though are seen as an important tool to 
widen the pool of financing directed toward climate action.

With negotiations ramping up in Antalya, envoys need to make the best use of 
any spare moments they can grab — and preferably find some moments of calm.
A spot to rest at the COP31 site.Photographer: Akshat Rathi/Bloomberg
Tucked away in the southern part of the COP31 venue is a curated Chinese 
garden. A small pond features a Chinese pagoda, well-kept trees and ornamental 
riverbed stones. Delegates can find quiet spots to sit and talk, or appreciate 
the beautiful handicrafts produced by Turkish artist Latife Cetinkaya, who uses 
dried leaves, seeds and fruit to create collages.
