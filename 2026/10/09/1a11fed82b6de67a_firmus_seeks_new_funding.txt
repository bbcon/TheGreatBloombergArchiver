Firmus is exploring a private funding round to raise as much as $3 billion 
after the collapse of its planned IPO left the data center operator looking for 
other financing options.

Nvidia-backed Firmus and its advisers are in talks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/firmus-is-said-to-explore-raising-3-billion-after-shelving-ipo?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9918>
 with existing investors to help raise $2 billion to $3 billion in a private 
round, people familiar with the matter said. The company had been aiming to 
raise about $5 billion in the IPO.

The failed deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/nvidia-backed-ipo-s-cratering-demand-sends-warning-on-ai-funding?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9918>
 underscores growing concern over how much capital AI infrastructure companies 
are demanding from public markets at a time when borrowing costs are rising. 
Much of Firmus’ valuation was based on the company successfully building a 
pipeline of data centers across Asia serving customers such as Meta and OpenAI.

The listing was poised to be the next beat of Firmus co-founder Oliver Curtis’s
comeback story 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/ex-banker-s-redemption-story-hits-a-setback-as-firmus-pulls-ipo?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9918>
 after serving time in prison on insider trading charges. It would also have 
cemented his position among the AI boom’s ultra-wealthy. Now the question is 
where the company will find the capital to keep funding its data center rollout.
