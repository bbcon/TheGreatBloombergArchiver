Deep Dive: ‘Nonsense Rate’

A legal battle is exposing turmoil inside the Baltic Exchange, a 282-year-old 
institution responsible forone of the most important prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/baltic-exchange-s-oil-benchmark-td3c-described-as-complete-nonsense?cmpid=111026_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11212>
 in global oil markets. A legal battle playing out in London between the Baltic 
and oil trader Mercuria Energy Group has shown that numerous people involved in 
the key oil tanker rate, called TD3C, expressed reservations as the exchange 
continued to publish the price in the early weeks of the Iran war even as 
tanker traffic came to a halt.

 * “Nonsense rate”: Christopher Loveday, who helped oversee TD3C, repeatedly 
questioned his own estimates and believed the benchmark should have been 
suspended. Other staff warned it was losing credibility, while one broker 
called the whole thing “complete nonsense.”
 * Circular logic: The Baltic told brokers they could look at derivatives 
linked to TD3C when assessing the benchmark itself, prompting warnings the rate 
could become detached from the physical shipping market. Those derivatives were 
worth €17.4 billion in the first half of the year.
 * The battle: Mercuria, the biggest user of TD3C derivatives, suffered hefty 
losses as the benchmark surged while its tankers couldn’t sail through the 
Strait of Hormuz to benefit from the higher rates. It wants a court to rule 
that the Baltic should have suspended TD3C from March through July; the Baltic 
says its decision to keep publishing was justified.
