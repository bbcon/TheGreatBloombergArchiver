The selling in global sovereign bonds is worsening in a warning sign for 
central banks seeking to bring inflation under control:

The US bond market in particular is flashing red 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/us-10-year-treasury-yields-rise-to-highest-level-since-2007?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
, with the 10-year Treasury yield rising to 5.04%, the highest since 2007.

But the klaxon is sounding elsewhere: similar maturity yields in Australia — 
which is facing aproperty downturn 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-08/australia-s-housing-pain-likely-to-deepen-on-interest-rate-pressure?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 — are the most elevated since 2011. Bonds in Japan retreated after it was 
reported that the authorities are considering hiking defense spending due to US 
pressure. Yields on Indian five-year notesjumped the most 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/indian-bonds-fall-as-rbi-debt-sale-plan-worsens-supply-pressures?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 since May 2022 after the central bank said it would sell sovereign notes to 
drain cash from lenders.

Pressure is building on central banks to be more aggressive in tightening 
monetary policy.Investors expect 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-09-15/federal-reserve-has-rarely-had-a-stronger-case-for-raising-rates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 the US Federal Reserve on Wednesday to raise short-term borrowing costs for 
the first time since July 2023. The Bank of Japan looks set to hike forthe 
third time 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/japan-s-economic-comeback-faces-hardest-boj-test-in-a-generation?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 in less than 10 months on Friday. A Kotak Mahindra Bank economist predicted 
the Reserve Bank of India will raise borrowing costs by 50-75 basis points, 
with the chance of a hike at next month’s policy review now much stronger.

Traders are also diverging 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/traders-are-at-odds-with-ecb-and-boe-on-how-far-rates-will-rise?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 from central bankers on the outlook for interest-rate hikes in Europe as money 
markets turn more hawkish. Swaps now imply four more quarter-point rate rises 
from the European Central Bank over the next 12 months, and five such increases 
from the Bank of England.

With energy prices continuing to rise 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-15?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 — Brent crude is around $107 a barrel — and a surge of corporate borrowing 
likely to keep yields elevated, the path forward for policymakers has rarely 
looked more treacherous.

Uneven growth: China’s domestic consumption is showing signs of further 
weakness 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/china-s-consumption-and-investment-wilt-as-factories-power-ahead?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
. Retail sales rose 0.4% in August from a year ago, slowing from the 0.6% pace 
last month. Fixed-asset investment plunged 7.2% in the first eight months, 
while there was a 19.9% slump in property investment.

Industrial output, meanwhile, climbed 5.2% in August, accelerating from July’s 
4.5% gain, underscoring the divergence in China’s economy as exports boom.

“Thanks to exports, China’s factory activity is holding up,” said Raymond 
Yeung, chief economist for greater China at ANZ. “But investment is falling 
faster, not to mention the persistent weakness in retail sales. Domestic demand 
is very weak. The economic momentum is clearly uneven across sectors.”

Up in arms: Japan is considering a new mid-term defense spending target 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/japan-eyes-defense-spending-worth-3-5-of-gdp-amid-us-pressure?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8915>
 of 3.5% of GDP in line with NATO and other US allies, a move that could send a 
shockwave through financial markets concerned about Prime Minister Sanae 
Takaichi’s spending plans.

Japanese defense officials have already signaled a willingness to sharply 
increase defense spending in meetings with their US counterparts, according to 
people familiar with the matter. One scenario under consideration is to match a 
commitment made by South Korea to increase defense spending to 3.5% of GDP over 
10 years, they said.
