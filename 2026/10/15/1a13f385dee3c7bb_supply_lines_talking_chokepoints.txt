The banking institution that guides central bankers is thinking about how 
pressures stemming from chokepoints filter through to the global economy and 
inflation.

In a paper released Thursday 
<https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-138-chokepoint-disruptions-and-their-macroeconomic-consequences>
, the Basel, Switzerland-based Bank for International Settlements broadly 
defines a chokepoint as a single company, an industry within a country, a 
production hub or a transport route that can’t be easily replaced or 
substituted for.

“While a disruption to an ordinary node may be temporary and localized, a 
disruption to a chokepoint can spread more broadly through production 
networks,” the bulletin said. “On average, disruptions at chokepoints are 
associated with larger output losses and stronger price pressures than 
comparable disruptions elsewhere.”

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/fed-s-beige-book-shows-intensifying-price-growth-in-some-areas?cmpid=BBD101526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=9931>

The paper said the standard policy prescription 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/fed-s-goolsbee-says-inflation-discouraging-warns-of-overheating?cmpid=BBD101526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=9931>
 of looking through the initial effects when bottlenecks are strained remains 
appropriate as long as inflation expectations stay anchored.

“However, policy calibration might have to consider the disruption’s position 
and centrality in the supply chain, the ease of substitution, the state of 
buffers (inventories, spare capacity, margins) and demand,” it said.

The economic and price fallout hinges on several factors, according to the BIS 
research:

 * The nature of the disruption: “Earnings call data indicate that firms are 
more likely to seek substitutes and reallocate production in response to trade 
restrictions and other geopolitical disruptions than to natural disasters or 
health crises, presumably because the former are expected to be more 
persistent. By contrast, logistical disruptions typically prompt like-for-like 
sourcing from alternative suppliers rather than broader adjustments.”
 * Scope for substitution: “Substituting across suppliers generally limits the 
impact on quantities but often implies paying higher input prices,” the paper 
says. “The potential for substitution increases with time – and the size of the 
macroeconomic effects diminishes – as technological and organizational 
constraints ease.”
 * Inventory levels: “The oil market offers a clear illustration: The large 
drawdown of oil reserves during the current disruption of the Strait of Hormuz 
has contributed to limiting the impact on oil prices,” the paper says. 
“However, with inventories currently low and transit through the Strait of 
Hormuz limited, the risk of sharper price increases has risen.”
 * Margins: “Analysis of earnings calls indicates firms with higher margins – 
often producers of less substitutable intermediate goods – are initially less 
likely to raise prices following supply chain disruptions, but this effect 
wanes after four quarters.”
Coincidentally, the BIS’s study was released on the final day of the Bloomberg 
New Economy Forum India in New Delhi, which includedthree days 
<https://www.bloombergneweconomy.com/2026-new-delhi-forum-program/> of panels, 
interviews and speeches from some of the world’s leading experts in supply 
chains and trade policy.

Among the main themes was the price of resilience. The CEO of one of the 
world’s largest port operators said global businesses are grappling withsurging 
costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/dp-world-sees-higher-costs-with-trade-partners-losing-trust?cmpid=BBD101526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=9931>
 in a more uncertain and fragmented world.

“Where I could have invested a billion dollars in supply chains in the era 
which has just ended, I am now going to look at investing almost double,” DP 
World’s Yuvraj Narayan said. “That’s the cost of redundancy.”

, adding that the country’s relationshipwith the US

 is “very good.”

 by year end.

“With Canada there’s trust, we’re a reliable partner and there’s certainty 
there with us, and that’s what businesses need,” Canadian International Trade

conference.

Survival of the Fittest

That message drove home another lesson: A shifting global order marked by 
upended supply chains and unstable alliances is forcing so-called middle-power 
countries tochart new paths 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/-middle-powers-grapple-with-new-survival-strategies-under-trump?cmpid=BBD101526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=9931>
 of survival.

Former US Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo added AI safety standards and 
enforcement to a list of issues including the Iran and Ukraine wars that need 
urgent attention.

“I’m worried that that won’t happen until after we have a crisis,” she said on 
apanel discussing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-13/global-leaders-on-the-high-stakes-of-navigating-change-video?cmpid=BBD101526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=9931>
 how businesses are adapt to rapid technological, geopolitical and economic 
change.

US-China ‘Stability’

Meanwhile, the US and China are in “relative stability mode” after last 
month’s leaders’ summit in Washington, Asia Society chief Kevin Rudd said, 
while warning of the risk of asnapback in tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/rolex-and-gold-were-for-us-and-not-trump-swiss-trade-chief-says?cmpid=BBD101526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=9931>
.

“That’s my best descriptor,” Rudd told Bloomberg Television’s Yvonne Man at 
the forum on Wednesday. Despite diverging interests, it suits both sides to 
stay within a stable bandwidth this year and next, he said.
