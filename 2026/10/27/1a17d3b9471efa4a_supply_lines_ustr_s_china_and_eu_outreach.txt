The US Trade Representative’s office issued statements Monday aimed at two 
economies President Donald Trump counts among the most prominent purveyors of 
government subsidies that distort international commerce.

In a Federal Register notice reported here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/us-plans-two-month-extension-to-shipping-fee-truce-with-china?cmpid=BBD102726_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10562>
 by Bloomberg’s Weilun Soon, USTR General Counsel Jennifer Thornton indicated 
the US may extend by two months a pause on threatened fees on Chinese-linked 
ships that visit American ports. The Trump administration wants to revive 
domestic shipbuilding, andtaxing vessels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-17/trump-moves-to-levy-chinese-vessels-in-shipping-shakeup?cmpid=BBD102726_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10562>
 connected to China — the world’s leading builder of cargo ships — is among the 
ideas.

The proposed extension, to Jan. 9, follows a broader trade truce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/xi-gets-rare-tarmac-welcome-from-trump-after-landing-at-us-base?cmpid=BBD102726_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10562>
 between Washington and Beijing that was announced last month between the 
leaders of the two countries.

QuickTake: How Trump Is Targeting China’s Shipbuilding Dominance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-21/us-china-port-fees-can-trump-make-american-shipbuilding-great-again?cmpid=BBD102726_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10562>

“The United States is continuing to engage with China with the goal of 
obtaining the elimination of the acts, policies, and practices at issue in the 
investigation,” Thorntonwrote in the notice 
<https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/26/2026-21755/request-for-comments-on-further-suspension-of-section-301-action-chinas-targeting-of-the-maritime>
. “During the suspension period, no party would accrue liability for or be 
required to pay the fees on maritime transport services” outlined in earlier 
decrees, it said.

Public comments on the case about China’s maritime dominance are being 
compiled inthis USTR portal 
<https://comments.ustr.gov/s/docket?docketNumber=USTR-2026-0694>.

Airbus Loan

Separately yesterday, USTR Jamieson Greer issued a statement criticizing the 
European Union for failing to respond with details about a loan to Airbus that 
drew his ire back in July.

The European Investment Bank extended financing 
<https://www.eib.org/en/press/all/2026-228-eib-commits-eur3-billion-to-airbus-to-boost-europe-s-industrial-base-and-technological-edge-in-aerospace-innovation?utm_source=socialmedia&utm_medium=Social&utm_campaign=na&utm_content=na&utm_term=na_na&utm_id=2026-06-29_01_en>
 for Airbus, according to an announcement in late June, a €3 billion ($3.34 
billion) package Greersaid at the time 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-22/airbus-and-boeing-and-subsidy-debate?cmpid=BBD102726_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10562>
 he was “looking at very closely.” It was the EIB’s largest-ever corporate loan 
package “tailored to underpin enterprising innovation.”

Brussels has argued the funds were offered at market rates, but apparently the 
EU hasn’t satisfied Greer’s demand to see the fine print.

“Despite ongoing requests, USTR still has no details about this loan,” he 
wrote in Monday’sstatement 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/october/ambassador-greer-issues-statement-status-us-eu-detente-following-record-breaking-loan-european>
. “This situation injects substantial uncertainty into transatlantic trade in 
the aerospace sector. It is important this situation is resolved as soon as 
possible to ensure market-driven production in the aerospace sector and to 
avoid violation of the terms of our hard-won large civil aircraft deal.”

Greer said he “had sincerely hoped that trade-distorting European subsidies to 
Airbus were a thing of the past.”
