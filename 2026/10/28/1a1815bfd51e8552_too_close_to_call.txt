Deep Dive: Hollowed-Out UK High Streets
A series of shops are photographed on Mesnes Street at the bottom of the 
pedestrianized shopping area in Wigan, UK, on Oct. 21.Photographer: Mary 
Turner/Bloomgerg News
From the same barbershop window over almost two decades, Luke Halsall has 
watched the main street in Hindley in northwest England change beyond 
recognition. Gone are the hardware stores, pet shops and banks on Market 
Street; in their place are vape shops, fast-food outlets and convenience stores
without the same appeal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-28/can-andy-burnham-s-uk-budget-help-shopping-town-centers-high-street-retailers?cmpid=102826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10667>
.

 * “It used to be a social place to come and hang out with your mates,” the 
36-year-old said. “Over the years, it’s just gone downhill.”
 * Wednesday’s budget is the first real test of whether Burnham’s serious 
about reversing the high-street decline in many British towns like Hindley. His 
and Chancellor Healey’s decisions will get the ball rolling on an effort to 
regenerate town centers that are seen as community hubs.
 * Hit by soaring costs and changing shopping habits, once-thriving 
independent stores, retail chains and banks have closed, with the void partly 
filled by unsightly vape shops that Burnham’s cracking down on.
 * He and Healey have little financial firepower to breathe life back into 
high streets with wide-scale reform of business rates, an unpopular commercial 
property tax, or by reversing former Chancellor Rachel Reeves’ payroll tax 
rises, raising questions about what levers they can pull.
