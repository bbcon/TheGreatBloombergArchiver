An earthquake that has killed at least 130 people and reduced thousands of 
buildings 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/colombia-hit-by-strong-earthquake-that-shakes-bogota?cmpid=BBD081126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7589>
 to rubble across western Colombia presents an immediate challenge to Abelardo 
de la Espriella just as he takes office promising drastic spending cuts.

The president faces immediate pressure to direct resources to some of the 
hardest-hit areas, including parts of his own electoral base. What’s more, the 
7.4 magnitude quake struck Colombia’s coffee region, raising the prospect of 
wider economic damage.

The disaster could give his government political cover to retreat from one of 
his mostambitious campaign pledges 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/outsider-de-la-espriella-takes-power-in-divided-colombia?cmpid=BBD081126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7589>
: slashing public outlays by 40%.

How he funds reconstruction while pursuing those cuts will offer an early 
indication of how his economic pledges can survive the realities of governing.

It’s a baptism of fire for the political novice who rode a Trumpist agenda to 
power 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/trump-ally-wins-colombian-election-heralding-pro-business-swing?cmpid=BBD081126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7589>
.

The crisis also complicates De la Espriella’s push to decentralize power.
Rescuers hunt for survivors amid rubble in Pereira, Colombia, after the quake 
yesterday.Photographer: Jaime Saldarriaga/AFP/Getty Images
The first president in recent history to be sworn in outside the capital, he 
found himself away from the center of government on his first full working day, 
weighing whether to return to Bogotá or travel directly to the affected areas.

De la Espriella responded quickly on X, but it took more than five hours for 
him to appear on camera and address Colombians directly. In that time, local 
officials became the public faces of the response, providing updates on damage 
and rescue efforts.

The crisis comes less than two months after devastating twin earthquakes 
struck neighboring Venezuela, exposing the political risks ofa response seen as 
too slow 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-03/venezuelans-frustrated-by-earthquake-response-want-new-presidential-elections?cmpid=BBD081126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7589>
.

For De la Espriella, the catastrophe presents an opportunity to demonstrate 
leadership and rally the country after a particularly divisive election.

His response will show how he balances the demands of a national emergency 
with the fiscal discipline and smaller government he promised voters.

It will also provide a gauge of whether his political ties to Donald Trump 
translate into tangible US support 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/colombia-president-de-la-espriella-takes-office-as-latin-america-shifts-right?cmpid=BBD081126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7589>
. — Patricia Laya
