Monday, August 10, 2026

US

  • July payrolls contracted by 23,000, sharply missing the +80,000 consensus, marking a third consecutive summer of employment weakness.
  • S&P 500 opens the week at an all-time high, up 13.3% year-to-date, even as AI-sector credit spreads widen materially.
  • CPI for July due Wednesday; consensus expects a +0.1% monthly print after June's -0.4% decline, the first drop in six years.
  • JPMorgan raised its S&P 500 forecast for a second time in two months, citing strong earnings and AI spending payoff.

Labor Market

July payrolls fell 23,000 against a median economist expectation of +80,000 gain, a significant miss that marks the third consecutive summer of employment underperformance. A 60,000 slide in local government employment — partly reflecting the exhaustion of post-Covid federal teaching assistance — was a key driver. Critically, initial jobless claims remain spectacularly low, suggesting minimal firing accompanies the lack of hiring. JPMorgan's Michael Feroli acknowledges the miss may partly reflect a seasonal pattern that corrected in prior autumns. Santander's Stephen Stanley argues the Fed, having been "fooled" into cutting rates in the past two Septembers, will look through the weakness this time given core inflation remains well above the 2% target.

AI Credit and Markets

The S&P 500's all-time high masks growing stress in AI-sector credit. Barclays data show the top six tech groups — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, and SpaceX — account for 8.4% of the US investment-grade market's duration-times-spread risk, exceeding the 7.3% share of the six largest banks. Alphabet reported negative free cash flow for the first time since its IPO; Meta's second-quarter free cash flow plunged 91% year-over-year. JPMorgan estimates $4.1 trillion of $5.5 trillion in projected AI capex will be debt-financed, making the buildout acutely sensitive to the Fed's next move. Berkshire Hathaway spent $4.5 billion on share buybacks in Q2, its largest since 2021. ---

  • China's exports climbed ~24% YoY in July and imports ~28%, well ahead of consensus, with AI-related electronics a key driver.

China: Deflation Risk and Leverage Controls

China's July inflation data present a deteriorating picture. CPI decelerated to 0.5% YoY — the weakest since January — from 1.0% in June, while PPI eased to 3.5% from 4.1%, its first moderation since the Iran war began in February. Both prints missed expectations, raising renewed deflation concerns even as the trade account remains robust. In response to an AI-driven margin debt frenzy that pushed the balance above 3 trillion yuan in late June, Chinese brokerages including Citic Securities have tightened compliance requirements. The margin balance has since retreated to 2.6 trillion yuan. Beijing appears to be threading a narrow path: supporting AI-related equities for strategic reasons while containing speculative leverage that contributed to July's market turbulence.

Regional Central Banks

The Bank of Japan's July meeting summary flagged rising upside price risks, with one board member raising the possibility of an accelerated tightening pace, sustaining pressure on the yen. Meanwhile, Indonesia's President Prabowo nominated Destry Damayanti as Bank Indonesia governor following Perry Warjiyo's unexpected departure, with parliament expected to consider the appointment from August 14. In the Philippines, Governor Eli Remolona signaled readiness to tighten "as much as necessary" despite Q2 GDP growth slumping unexpectedly to 2.3%, as inflation at 6.2% runs more than double the government's target. ---

Europe

  • UK hiring held steady in July per a closely watched survey, ending a downturn stretching back to the Liz Truss crisis of 2022.

Eurozone Outlook

The eurozone appears to be navigating the Iran war's economic disruption with greater resilience than anticipated. A Bloomberg survey of analysts projects growth to quicken from Q2's already-surprising strength, as domestic demand holds up and export momentum — particularly in AI-related manufacturing — provides a partial offset to energy cost pressures. European equities are reflecting this fundamental shift: with earnings growth of 17% — the strongest in four years — and the most robust economic momentum since March 2023, strategists are increasingly attributing the Stoxx 600's rally to improved fundamentals rather than merely cheap valuations relative to the US. BlackRock's Helen Jewell noted that regional "resilience has surprised the market."

UK Economy and Politics

UK hiring stabilized in July, according to a closely watched survey, ending a prolonged contraction that began with the Liz Truss gilt crisis in 2022. Temporary vacancies rose for the first time in two years and pay growth ticked up, suggesting the labor market may be turning. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is touring the country to promote cost-of-living measures under the banner of "everyday fixes," though controversy over his prison overcrowding policy threatens to complicate the political backdrop. Swiss rate expectations were pushed back, with no hike now anticipated before June 2027 at the earliest. UK GDP data are due later this week. ---

LatAm

  • Bolivia's government is betting on commodity crop expansion for growth, while Brazilian deforestation enforcement is displacing agricultural activity across the border into Bolivia.
  • Canada faces a potential 50% US tariff on a wide range of goods from August 19, with negotiations ongoing.

Brazil and Regional Commodity Dynamics

Brazil's intensified deforestation enforcement has produced a notable cross-border displacement effect: as Amazon clearing has declined domestically, it has surged in Bolivia, where President Rodrigo Paz's government is actively encouraging commodity crop expansion as a growth strategy. The dynamic illustrates how regional enforcement asymmetries can redirect rather than reduce agricultural pressure on ecosystems. More broadly, Latin American commodity exporters remain exposed to the dual pressures of Iran-war-related energy price volatility and the prospect of higher-for-longer US interest rates, which continue to weigh on capital flows into the region and sustain currency depreciation pressure across several markets.

Trade Exposure

Canada's position — while not strictly Latin American — represents the most acute near-term trade risk in the Americas. Canadian negotiators are attempting to secure a trade agreement before threatened 50% US tariffs on a wide range of goods take effect on August 19. The outcome carries spillover implications for regional export dynamics, particularly as US trade enforcement posture under the current administration has demonstrated a willingness to deploy tariffs as negotiating leverage across multiple fronts simultaneously. ---

Global

  • Iran ruled out direct talks with the US, appointing a hardline ex-commander as top security official; the Strait of Hormuz remains partially disrupted with no deal in sight.
  • Brent crude is trading above $84/barrel; diesel in Europe exceeds $160/barrel as Iranian and Russian supply disruptions compound ahead of the Northern Hemisphere winter.
  • Gold surged 7.3% last week, its biggest weekly gain since late January, driven by ETF inflows of 24 tons since July 20 and reduced rate-hike expectations.

Hormuz and Energy Markets

The Strait of Hormuz remains in a state of strategic limbo. Iran has ruled out direct US talks, promoted a hardline security official, and remains short of a deal with Oman on shipping passage. President Trump signaled a shift toward patience, describing the US as "semi-negotiating" and betting that economic pressure will eventually soften Tehran's position. The practical consequence for energy markets is sustained uncertainty: oil moves through the strait via a fragile network of shuttle tankers, while UAE vessels continue to be targeted. Global oil inventories are drawing at pace, America's Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest since the 1980s, and diesel — critically important ahead of winter — is trading above $160/barrel in Europe, compounded by Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refining infrastructure. The market remains vulnerable to sharp price spikes even if the extreme tail scenarios of the war's early phase have receded.

Gold and Monetary Policy Divergence

Gold's 7.3% weekly gain — its largest since late January — reflects a confluence of the payrolls miss reducing Fed rate-hike probability and resumed ETF buying at the fastest pace since April. At roughly $4,340/ounce, bullion remains well below its pre-war peak above $5,400, but the structural case — heavy sovereign debt loads, large deficits, and substantial non-bank leverage — remains intact. Monetary policy divergence persists: the BOJ is flagging accelerating tightening while the Fed's next move is genuinely uncertain pending Wednesday's CPI print. Emerging Asian central banks are increasingly seeking to defend currencies through intervention rather than reserve drawdown as higher-for-longer US rates sustain pressure on the region's exchange rates.
