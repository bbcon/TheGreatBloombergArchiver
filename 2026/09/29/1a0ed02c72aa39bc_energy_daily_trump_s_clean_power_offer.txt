Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Mark Chediak explores the potential impact of the Trump 
administration’s offer to allow more clean-power projects.

US lawmakers have been at the negotiating table over a potential deal to help 
accelerate major energy and infrastructure projects, and the Trump 
administration has dangled a carrot for Democrats: unfreezing some solar and 
wind developments.

President Donald Trump has long made clear his distaste for clean power, 
leaving Democrats clamoring for ways around his moves to thwart the industry. 
Part of the administration’s playbook has included making it tougher to get 
approvals from the Pentagon for wind projects.

So the potential concession has drawn attention. The administration is 
offering toallow some solar and wind projects 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/trump-offering-solar-projects-for-permitting-deal-senator-says?cmpid=BBBXT092926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9467>
 to move forward, Bloomberg News reported last week. The final details on any 
deal will still need to be voted on by Congress, which may not happen until 
after the US midterm elections in November.

But even if the concession goes through, its impact would likely prove modest. 
It may not lead to an appreciable jump in clean-power capacity, thanks in part 
to separate actions.

Last week, the administration said it would prevent the stockpiling of solar 
equipment beforenew tariffs take effect 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-signed-order-on-tariffs-trade-actions-on-polysilicon?cmpid=BBBXT092926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9467>
 in December on polysilicon, a key material in most panels.
Solar panels in Tucson, Arizona.Photographer: Rebecca Noble/Bloomberg
“We would expect this and other enforcement actions to restrict supply and 
drive pricing higher,” Philip Shen, an analyst at Roth Capital Partners LLC, 
said in a research note.

Costlier solar, of course, would risk making panels less competitive against 
other electricity sources, potentially slowing its growth.

Congressional Democrats are keenly aware of the totality of Trump’s efforts to 
thwart renewables. Indeed, while solar remains a dominant source of new US 
power capacity, research groupBloombergNEF 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3470094Z:LN?cmpid=BBBXT092926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9467>
 has reduced its outlook for further growth, citing the elimination of a 
federal tax credit as well as the new polysilicon tariffs.

This awareness informs discussions in Washington on a potential deal to 
expedite the permitting of big energy projects. Members of both parties want 
faster authorizations — Republicans for oil and gas developments, Democrats for 
projects centered on clean power.

For Democrats, a key question is whether the deal would provide enough of a 
boost to renewables to be worth their support.

—Mark Chediak, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

The Iran war is reshaping how emerging Asian LNG buyers contract future 
supply, according toBNEF <bbg://news/stories/TM3MOMKGZAIW>. Shipping 
disruptions underscore the risks of relying heavily on a single supplier or 
trade route. US and West African cargoes helped plug the supply gap, with 
volumes rising 158% and 91%, respectively, from April to August from a year 
ago. A prolonged disruption could also pull some Russian LNG from 
non-sanctioned projects into Asia.
