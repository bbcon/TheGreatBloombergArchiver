departing, diplomats are getting down to the real task of negotiating an 
agreement.

of the list are electrification, climate finance, theacceleration of global

A wrap on the first week

By Laura Millan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASzdyi2b6bE/laura-millan?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>
,John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>
 andJennifer A. Dlouhy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASlQFjfALa0/jennifer-a-dlouhy?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>

Long queues, eternal commutes and a global Microsoft Corp. meltdown have all 
challenged delegates at COP31, though there were signs of progress Friday as 
the summit’s presidency published a first summary of negotiations so far.

The document 
<https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/Reflections%20on%20Proposed%20Cover%20Decision.pdf?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email>
, rounding up debate over a proposed cover decision – a final statement that 
requires agreement among the almost 190 nations at the talks – follows 
consultations on issues including the need to curb methane emissions and on 
lifting the share of global final energy demand met by electricity.
Attendees at the Ocean pavilion at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, 
Turkey, on Friday, Nov. 13, 2026.Photographer: Chris Ratcliffe/Bloomberg
It also reflected a broad array of other discussions touching on climate 
finance, the role of science and the need to transition away from fossil fuels. 
And it revealed some countries have presented unorthodox ideas such as avoiding 
references to outside scientific reports, possibly including work from the UN 
Environment Programme.

Climate envoys will offer their thoughts – and objections – in the coming 
days, ahead of a first draft cover text that’s expected to be circulated as 
soon as Monday.

It’s still early in the process, acknowledged Chris Bowen, the COP31 president 
of negotiations and Australia’s climate change and energy minister. “Nothing 
included is prejudged, locked in, or will necessarily be included in the final 
decision,” Bowen said.

Progress in talks through the first week has been slow, with a two-day 
leaders’ summit — which happened in the middle of negotiations — capturing most 
of the attention.

Now, more technical talks about how to cut emissions and adapt to global 
warming will dominate. Envoys are focused on delivering an agreed final text by 
next Friday’s deadline – or, if past practice is a guide, in an overtime 
session hours or days later.

“We need to be able to tell the world that climate change is accelerating,” 
Diana Acconcia, the EU’s lead negotiator, told reporters. “The transition is 
also accelerating and is irreversible.”
Attendees pass a giant turtle structure at the COP31 on Friday, Nov. 13.
Photographer: Chris Ratcliffe/Bloomberg
The summary document includes a reference to latest UN reports that warn the 
world can no longer prevent global warming exceeding 1.5C – a key target of the 
Paris Agreement in 2015.

It also notes that pledges on climate finance aren’t being met, and highlights 
the need for developing nations to receive more assistance to adapt to climate 
change.

In 2024, countries agreed to deploy climate finance for developing countries 
worth $1.3 trillion a year by 2035, with $300 billion coming from public 
sources.

It’s a sign of the still ongoing divisions between rich countries responsible 
for historical greenhouse gas emissions that are causing climate change and 
poorer countries that are suffering the consequences.

On boosting electrification, some debate has focused on whether the goal 
should include any reference to requirements for clean electricity. EU climate 
chief Wopke Hoekstra said the 27-member bloc isn’t seeking to add restrictions, 
and hopes the initiative can retain broad appeal.

A “certain degree of pragmatism” was warranted to bring as many countries on 
board as possible, he told reporters. While curbs on methane are mentioned in 
the summary text, that’s no guarantee the issue will make it into a final text, 
according to Acconcia.

“It’s really impossible to say what will make it and not make it in the cover 
decision,” she said.

Another potential point of contention is whether a final text will recognize 
the role of science in the UN climate process, and will seek the completion of 
the next landmark report by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change 
before COP33 in 2028.

China rules out 2028

By Lili Pike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXt16hTqABg/lili-pike?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>
 andJohn Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>

China has decided against bidding to host the United Nations climate summit in 
2028, an event that is scheduled to coincide with the next US presidential 
election.

Officials deliberated over a proposal and have now opted not to proceed, 
according to people familiar with the situation, who requested anonymity to 
discuss private discussions.

The people didn’t cite any reason for China’s conclusion, though the event 
would fall two years ahead of the country’s deadline to peak its carbon dioxide 
emissions.

Talks on the issue in recent months involved the Ministry of Foreign Affairs 
and environmental officials, and a final endorsement had required support from 
the highest levels of government, Bloomberg News reported in August. The 
Guardian first reported news that China would not formally bid to act as host.

Negotiations will be hosted by Ethiopia in Addis Ababa in 2027. Duties are 
rotated between regional blocs with the Asia-Pacific States, a group of more 
than 50 countries which also includes Japan, South Korea and Indonesia, due to 
provide a venue in 2028. A decision doesn’t need to be taken at the current 
talks in Antalya, and could come at next year’s summit.

Indonesia and Uzbekistan have signaled their potential interest to host, 
according to a person familiar with the matter. However, no country has yet 
submitted a new official bid to the Asia-Pacific group after India in April 
withdrew an offer to host the negotiations.

On the ground at COP

The watercooler talk on Friday wasn’t just on the presidency’s summary of 
cover text consultations:

 * A massive Microsoft Corp. outage left delegates without access to Outlook, 
Teams and other Microsoft 365 services. That meant environment ministers, 
negotiators and other delegates were struggling to maintain communications — 
and even unearth key files — before meetings and presentations.

 * Needing solace during negotiations? Delegates can retreat to a glass-walled 
garden filled with brilliant butterflies, including the blue morpho and other 
varieties. Inside a steamy green glass structure that once showcased the 
rainforest, butterflies can be observed emerging from their cocoons and 
flitting around lush greenery and water features. The butterfly garden at COP31 
in Antalya, Turkey.Photographer: Alex Michie/Bloomberg
 * In an exclusive meeting area for VIPs, an exhibition of stone sculptures, 
bowls and artifacts from about 9,000 BCE provided a history lesson this week. 
An archaeologist on site explained that some experts believe some communities 
in what is now Turkey may have exposed their dead to scavengenging birds before 
retrieving and burying the bones, while skulls were sometimes removed and kept 
or displayed — practices that may have reflected beliefs about death and the 
afterlife.
Canada’s slowing progress

By John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>
 andJennifer A Dlouhy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASlQFjfALa0/jennifer-a-dlouhy?cmpid=BBD111326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11387>

Canada’s environment minister defended the country’s record on tackling global 
warming at the United Nations climate summit, as the nation loses ground on its 
plan to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

Environmentalists have scrutinized Prime Minister Mark Carney’s support for 
expanding oil and natural gas production and exports. And Canada’s own auditors 
last monthcited 
<https://www.canada.ca/en/auditor-general/our-work/audit-reports/17-commissioner-environment-sustainable-development-202610-implementing-canadian-net-zero-emissions-accountability-act-2026-report.html>
 regulatory rollbacks under Carney for causing the country to fall further 
behind on its Paris Agreement pledge to cut emissions by 40% from 2005 levels 
by 2030.

But other countries, including in the European Union, are relying on Canada to 
step up with fossil fuel supplies, Julie Dabrusin, environment and climate 
change minister, told Bloomberg at COP31. “We’re in a geopolitical moment,” she 
said. “Countries are looking to Canada in this moment for more support with 
conventional energy.”

At home, Canada is working to bring down methane emissions and boost 
electrification, Dabrusin said. She touted the country’s record on climate 
finance, with the nation on Friday announcing C$116 million ($81 million) aimed 
at international projects, including early warning systems for climate 
disasters and methane monitoring.

Canada’s dual ambition to expand oil and gas output and zero out emissions by 
2050 underscores one of the key tensions at the UN climate talks: The global 
balancing act between energy security and addressing planetary warming. Wars in 
Ukraine and the Middle East have led to supply shocks and fuel price spikes, 
feeding into broader inflation that’s underpinnedprotest movements
