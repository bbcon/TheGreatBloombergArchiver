Bessent’s AI Beefs

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent regularly touts the global dominance of 
American developers of artificial intelligence. One refrain in that context 
that he rolls out regularly is that the US last year had 55% to 60% of global 
computing power, and that “we’re going to have 80% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/bessent-blasts-us-hyperscalers-for-failure-to-tout-ai-benefits?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
” by 2028.

That’s not to say he doesn’t have issues with the so-called hyperscalers 
building out the data centers on which AI runs or the software companies 
pushing the technology’s frontier. And a new one might have just emerged over 
the weekend.

Leaders of the world’s biggest AI platforms — all American — said it’s time to 
slow 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/anthropic-ceo-says-it-s-time-to-slow-pace-of-improving-ai-models?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
 the development pace of their most advanced models, citing escalating risks of 
the technology. Dario Amodei, Anthropic’s CEO, issued a lengthy blog post 
Saturday urging a slowdown, and his call was quickly endorsed by OpenAI chief 
Sam Altman and Elon Musk, who runs xAI.

But just four days prior, Bessent was arguing for going full steam ahead. “We 
can’t pause,” he said in a Breitbart News event Tuesday. “You can’t, because the
Chinese won’t pause 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-us-china-ai-race/?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
. Even the North Koreans, they won’t pause.”
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-11/openai-is-open-to-slowing-cutting-edge-ai-video?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
The Treasury secretary’s key point is that, if the US falls behind its chief 
geopolitical rival, China will have an advantage with dangerous implications. 
“If they were to pull away from us on AI, thennothing else would matter 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/bessent-warns-nothing-would-matter-if-china-wins-the-ai-race?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
.” Inremarks 
<https://charlotteeconclub.org/event/the-cec-is-proud-to-host-u-s-secretary-of-the-treasury-scott-bessent/>
 on Sept. 2, he said, “I am in charge of managing the relationship with China, 
and we are ahead on AI,” but “the Chinese are right behind us,” by about six 
months. If Beijing wins, he said, “they will be able to hack into everything.”

The Treasury didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment Sunday.

President Trump, in remarks over the weekend, emphasized the importance of 
staying ahead ofChina 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/xi-pitches-his-ai-vision-at-brics-summit-as-china-duels-with-us?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
. He also said, “We can put guardrails. We can do this and that. But I think 
you have a lot of negative forces that are bringing it up thatshouldn’t be 
bringing it up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/trump-downplays-ai-concerns-as-ceos-call-for-slowing-technology?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
.”

China showed no signs of favoring a halt in development either, with a foreign 
ministry spokesman on Monday warning against “fearmongering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/china-rejects-ai-fearmongering-after-amodei-urges-slowdown?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
.”

National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett said Sunday that AI safety a 
“solvable problem.” House Speaker Mike Johnson indicated the potential for 
summoning AI industry leaders to “one big meeting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/johnson-calls-for-meeting-with-ai-leaders-before-any-legislation?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
,” and said any federal legislation shouldn’t be rushed.

Bessent has had other beefs with the AI companies, repeatedly blasting them 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/bessent-blasts-us-hyperscalers-for-failure-to-tout-ai-benefits?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
 for failing to explain to the broader American public what the benefits to 
them will be. (For his part, Open AI’s Altman agreed, saying on Bloomberg 
Television the industry “hasdone a terrible job 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/sam-altman-says-openai-has-fumbled-at-communicating-ai-benefits?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
 of this, on the whole.”

large amounts of debt — to some degreein competition

Need-to-Know Research

Framing his return to the policymaking stage after a decade and a half, Fed 
Chairman Kevin Warsh on Aug. 31 observed that he’d departed at a time when the 
narrative was about a “global savings glut” and stagnant growth prospects, 
whereas today, “if I were to try to characterize this moment, it would be one 
of a global investment surge.”

Extending from that same observation, Torsten Slok, chief economist at Apollo 
Global Management, says this is a key reason interest rates are rising. “When 
capital is abundant and projects are scarce, projects compete for capital, and 
they compete by accepting a lower return. When projects are abundant and 
capital is scarce,capital competes for projects 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-26/artificial-intelligence-investment-now-competes-with-governments-for-capital?cmpid=BBD091426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8831>
, and it competes by demanding a higher return. The return that clears the 
market is a higher yield.”

It’s also logical that longer-dated rates are rising more than the short-end 
ones, he wrote in a note Saturday. “Data centers, power generation, 
transmission and government deficits are all long-duration claims on savings,” 
he says. They’ll be around for what’s likely to be decades to come. “The 
competition for capital concentrates at the long end of the curve, which is why 
long rates have moved more than short rates.”
