Slowing economic growth is the last thing Japan’s government needs as 
officials seek to shore up a currency near a 40-year low.

Real gross domestic product grew 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/japan-s-economic-growth-slows-as-mideast-conflict-clouds-outlook?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
 1.1% on an annualized basis in the second quarter, a Cabinet Office report 
indicated. That’s weaker than a revised 1.9% pace in the previous quarter and 
short of economists’ forecast for 2% growth. Notably, capital spending 
continued to slump and private consumption flatlined under the weight of 
inflation.

While the poor figures aren’t likely to derail the central bank from its rate 
hike path — traders see an 80% likelihood of the Bank of Japan hiking its 
benchmark rate when it next decides policy on Sept. 18 — the slowdown 
underscores the challenge facing policy makers: how to support the currency 
without hurting growth. Japanisn’t alone 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-22/asia-s-central-banks-fight-to-defend-currencies-as-oil-surges?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
 in Asia facing that conundrum — central banks inthe Philippines 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/philippine-central-bank-chief-eyes-less-aggressive-rate-hikes?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
 andIndonesia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/indonesia-economy-grows-5-29-in-second-quarter-beats-estimates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
 are also battling a similar combination.

For now rescuing the yen seems to be the priority. The Bank of Japan should 
raise its benchmark interest rateat every meeting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/boj-should-raise-rates-at-every-meeting-ex-currency-chief-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
, with the goal of taking the rate above 2% in order to narrow the rate 
differential with the US and ease pressure on the yen, a former Finance 
Ministry official said.

“Real interest rates are still negative even after Japan has raised its policy 
rate to 1%, while they are positive in every other country,” Takehiko Nakao, 
who was vice finance minister for international affairs until March 2013, said 
on a TV Tokyo program.

Such expectations are driving up government bond yields. The 10-year rate rose 
as much as 5.5 basis points to 2.93%, thehighest since 1996 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/japan-s-bond-yields-climb-on-boj-hike-bets-fiscal-concerns?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
. The question is what will the ultimate cost of defending the yen be.

It’s not just Japan. China’s economy got off to a sluggish start in the second 
half as industrial output, consumption and investmentall fared worse 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/china-s-economy-extends-slowdown-by-weakening-across-the-board?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7787>
 than expected.

Industrial production expanded at a slower rate for the first time in three 
months by gaining 4.5% in July from a year earlier, according to data released 
by the National Bureau of Statistics on Monday. Retail sales growth slowed to 
0.6%, performing less than expected. Fixed-asset investment fell more than 
forecast at a pace of 6.7% year-on-year in the first seven months.

“It looks like a slate of disappointing data across the board,” said Lynn 
Song, chief economist for Greater China at ING Bank.

knowledge of the matter. The city is home to 60% of its roughly 75 regional 
employees, and the extra space will give room to seat 25 more.
