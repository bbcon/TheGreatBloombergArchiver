France’s political and financial turmoil is creating an opening for Jean-Luc 
Mélenchon. The far-left leader hasemerged as a serious contender 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-18/french-election-jean-luc-melenchon-is-wild-card-for-voters?cmpid=111826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11555>
 for next year’s presidential election as protests, budget gridlock and 
investor anxiety deepen dissatisfaction with the political center.

 * Race to the runoff: While Marine Le Pen remains well ahead at 31%-33%, her 
far-left rival has risen to 17%-18.5% and is currently the most likely 
candidate to face her in the second round. Nearly three-quarters of respondents 
in a September poll, however, said there was no chance they would vote for him.
 * Soak the rich: His economic agenda calls for higher government spending and 
taxes on the wealthy, including a plan targeting inheritances above €12 
million. He also wants to cancel the roughly 20% of France’s €3.6 trillion 
sovereign debt held by the ECB — a move the central bank considers illegal.
 * Markets on edge: France’s fiscal troubles have already rattled investors, 
with its 10-year yield premium over Germany reaching 178 basis points in 
October, the highest since 2012. A Mélenchon-Le Pen runoff could put even more 
focus on the country’s finances as both candidates challenge the political 
establishment.
