Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Mia Gindis looks at mounting warnings over 24/7 oil trading among 
retail investors.

In recent months a debate has intensified over whether US regulators should 
allow oil futures trading to be extended around the clock.

There are signs that some US retail investors are already trading commodities 
24/7 quietly — and illegally —via virtual private networks 
<bbg://news/stories/TN9V9RKJH6V5>, or VPNs.

That method enables the circumvention of geographic restrictions to access 
offshore platforms, according to established commodity operators who are 
warning of potentially negative consequences. Some traders leverage it to buy 
and sell oil outside of traditional market hours.

The allure of this kind of access has grown with the volatility triggered by 
the Iran war, and the Trump administration’s knack for dropping news after 
incumbent exchanges have rung the closing bell.

Warnings about VPN trading trend grew louder this week. The Commodity Markets 
Council, an industry group representing regulated exchanges and major commodity 
trading firms,expressed its concerns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/exchanges-urge-cftc-to-block-kalshi-s-perpetual-oil-futures-plan?cmpid=BBBXT102326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10470>
 in a letter to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission.

The council outlined three potential consequences. First, US customers going 
offshore lose protections offered by the Commodity Exchange Act. Second, the 
CFTC has no visibility into trading positions held offshore, reducing the 
effectiveness of its market surveillance.
The CFTC headquarters in Washington, DC.Photographer: Daniel Heuer/Bloomberg
The group also warned that growing weekend and overnight trading volumes on 
unregulated offshore venues can impact the opening moments of trading in 
benchmark contracts on established exchanges, before some participants are able 
to act. The council says such a phenomenon can already be observed due to 
so-calledperpetual futures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/exchanges-urge-cftc-to-block-kalshi-s-perpetual-oil-futures-plan?cmpid=BBBXT102326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10470>
 that trade offshore.

“There’s no one policing that activity,” said Terry Duffy, the boss of CME 
Group Inc., one of the biggest regulated commodity bourses, in a Bloomberg 
Televisioninterview <bbg://news/stories/TN9V9RKJH6V5> this week. “I find that 
to be troublesome.”

The situation echoes earlier disputes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-03-27/crypto-exchange-binance-sued-by-us-cftc-for-alleged-derivatives-rule-lapses?cmpid=BBBXT102326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10470>
 between the CFTC and crypto exchanges Binance Holdings Ltd. and KuCoin. In its 
letter, the Commodity Markets Council urged the regulator to followthat playbook
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-30/crypto-exchange-kucoin-pays-cftc-500-000-to-end-civil-case?cmpid=BBBXT102326_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10470>
 and clamp down on offshore platforms allowing US customers to gain oil 
exposure.

Whatever happens, the commodities establishment appears to be resigned to the 
fact that 24/7 trading of some kind is inevitable. The council acknowledged as 
much in its letter.

“The question is whether that exposure will be governed by one consistent set 
of standards, and whether those standards will be enforced,” it said.

—Mia Gindis, Bloomberg News
