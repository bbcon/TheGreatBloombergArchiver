Ford’s surprise EV success

By Kyle Stock 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARKz8x17rNs/kyle-stock?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>

It turns out, to make Ford Motor’s electric pickup truck a sales champion, the 
company just had to stop making it.
The F-150 Lightning, which Ford stopped making in December, is in high demand 
on the used market.Source: Ford
Since the final 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-06/ford-may-cancel-its-electric-f-150-pickup-truck-wsj-reports?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>
 F-150 Lightning rolled off the assembly line in December, it has become one of 
the most coveted cars in America and a glowing ember in thered-hot market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-14/electric-cars-aren-t-just-for-the-wealthy-anymore?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>
 for used electric vehicles. The surging demand shows that there is plenty of 
appetite for electric trucks among US drivers, provided the pickups aren’t too 
expensive, said Jimmy Douglas, CEO of Plug, a wholesale electric vehicle dealer.

“When Ford was selling it brand new, the math was complicated,” Douglas said. 
“But used, the Lightning is 50% to 60% of the sticker price and that is really 
a sweet spot; it’s an incredible truck for the money.”

In addition to being put off by high prices, truck drivers have been relatively
slow to go electric <bbg://news/stories/SR5NW9DWLU68> because the machines 
aren’t very aerodynamic. The boxy build and hefty weight of a half-ton pickup 
hamstring driving range. However, combustion trucks fight the same physics when 
it comes to mileage. Pickup fans, in short, have felt thesting of 
<bbg://news/stories/TMAD38T96OSG> the high gas prices, spiked by the Iran war, 
more keenly than most drivers.

Scarcity has helped too. In a good year, Ford sells about 900,000 gas-powered 
F-150s, whereas it moved slightly more than 100,000 of its electric version in 
a little over four years.

The typical Lightning is changing hands in about 20 days in the US, versus 44 
days for the average used vehicle. The price has steadily risen to $50,000, an 
increase of 4% since the start of the year, according to Edmunds.com.

And nearly one-third of used Lightnings are being sold by dealerships that 
peddle other brands. Typically, those stores would flip a used Ford to a Ford 
dealer, according to Douglas, but many are electing to list the car directly to 
tap red-hot demand.

Chase Norris, a county planner in Colorado ski country, recently ditched his 
gas F-150 for a used Lightning. At $40,000, he considered the vehicle both a 
major upgrade and a bargain.

“It’s better for the environment and it happens to be faster than some 
Mustangs,” Norris said. “And I go camping a lot; if it gets cold at night I can 
just plug my electric blanket into the outlet.”

Part of the appeal for customers like Norris, however, is they won’t notice 
much of a difference. The exterior of the Lightning is pretty much identical to 
the gas-powered version — so is the cabin, right down to the massive gear 
selector, whichfolds down 
<https://www.instagram.com/stories/highlights/18007118495682846/> into the 
center console. The only apparent EV exception is the yawning front trunk where 
the spark plugs and cylinders used to be. In a pinch, the truck can also power 
a house.

Funds snap up LA wildfires deals

By Gautam Naik 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVyoWbluWhQ/gautam-naik?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>

Hedge funds are stepping up purchases of legal claims tied to the 2025 Los 
Angeles wildfires, after political opposition to the deals lost steam.
Destroyed homes after the Palisades Fire in 2025.Photographer: Roger 
Kisby/Bloomberg
In some cases, funds are paying as little as 3 cents on the dollar, according 
to details of transactions shared with Bloomberg. The most expensive are going 
for around 60 cents on the dollar.

California lawmakers had sought to rein in investor purchases of subrogation 
claims, as such deals are called, criticizing efforts to reap financial gain 
from the human and environmental cost of wildfires. But animpasse 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/edison-ceo-warns-wildfire-impasse-risks-higher-customer-bills?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>
 in the state legislature has now reassured investors that there’s still room 
to chase lucrative transactions.

Cherokee Acquisition 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9336697Z:US?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>
 Director Bradley Max, who spoke last year of “a chill 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-08/wildfire-bets-of-hedge-funds-rebuked-in-california-as-unethical?cmpid=BBD101226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9963>
” in the market for subrogation deals, says he now sees optimism returning.

“There’s plenty of appetite for buyers and sellers,” said Max, who acts as a 
broker between hedge funds and insurers looking to pass on subrogation claims.

“Insurance companies know that there is liquidity available and the market has 
become more mainstream,” Max said, declining to identify individual insurers or 
hedge funds by name.

The blazes that swept through LA’s Eaton and Palisades neighborhoods in early 
2025 caused about$40 billion in insured losses 
<https://www.munichre.com/en/company/media-relations/media-information-and-corporate-news/media-information/2026/natural-disaster-figures-2025.html>
, by some estimates. Today, roughly 20 months after the event, disputes over 
who should foot that bill have yet to be resolved. Verisk, a risk assessment 
firm,describes  
<https://www.verisk.com/blog/the-la-fires-changed-the-wildfire-claims-landscape/>
the LA fires as a “historical loss event” whose ongoing claims management 
challenge carries “a multiyear development tail.”
