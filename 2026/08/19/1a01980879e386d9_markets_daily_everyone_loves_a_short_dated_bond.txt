Searching for safety

It might be the most consensus trade in markets right now — the appeal of the 
short-dated bond. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/blackrock-aviva-turn-to-short-dated-bonds-as-long-debt-slumps?cmpid=081926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7888>

It’s a safe-haven play as worries about high oil prices, government debt 
burdens and the flood of AI borrowing rattle global markets. An index of bonds 
with a maturity of one to three years has gained 1% this year, versus a loss of 
4% for bonds of 10 years and more.

Since war broke out in the Middle East, BlackRock‘s James Turner in London has 
shifted toward shorter maturities and inflation-linked bonds, while Aviva 
Investors, Aegon Asset Management and Allspring Global Investments are favoring 
shorter-dated corporate credit.

“The short end of the government bond curve is probably the safest place I can 
put my money when I want to not worry,” said Vera Fehling, Europe chief 
investment officer at DWS. —Georgia Hall 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXPC76oMnbY/georgia-hall?cmpid=081926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7888>

S&P 500 holds up just fine

For all the upheaval in the long-end of the bond markets, stock investors are 
remarkably unbothered.

Sure, the speculative parts of tech are taking a beating, but the anxiety 
isn’t showing up in the average stock just yet. The S&P 500 is less than 2% 
from a record.

Why isn’t there more concern? Ed Clissold of Ned Davis Research points to the 
strength of American corporate profits.

Bond yields still have a way to rise before “destroying the equity bull 
market,” he writes. “The good news for the bulls is that the outlook for 
earnings remains positive.” —Felice Maranz 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ABoR_rb7834/felice-maranz?cmpid=081926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7888>

The first humanoid robot stock

Robots built to move, work and act like humans. It’s a global industry Morgan 
Stanley estimates could be worth $5 trillion by 2050. It’s also one of the 
frontiers in theChina-US race for technological supremacy. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/unitree-ipo-why-investors-are-betting-big-on-china-s-humanoid-robots?cmpid=081926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7888>

Companies like Unitree have already taken an early lead over the US in 
production volumes and real-world deployment. Investors are eager for a piece 
of the action. At one point today,Unitree rose as much as 629%. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/unitree-robotics-set-to-debut-after-904-million-shanghai-ipo?cmpid=081926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7888>
Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg
But the price is steep. Unitree’s IPO valued the company at 36 times sales. 
That compares with ratios of about 20 for similar stocks in Hong Kong.

“There’s clearly no fundamental basis for the share price surge,” said 
Vey-Sern Ling at Union Bancaire Privée. “Valuations are sky-high and the 
outlook is uncertain given the early stage of development.”
