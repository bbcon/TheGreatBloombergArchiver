Beijing dismissed calls from US tech leaders to slow down AI development as 
self-serving fearmongering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/chinese-state-media-dismisses-self-serving-ai-slowdown-call?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
.

It’s not that Chinese officials don’t want to discuss guardrails; they do — but
not on terms set by the US 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/china-rejects-ai-fearmongering-after-amodei-urges-slowdown?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
.

Artificial intelligence is expected to feature prominently when presidents 
Donald Trump and Xi Jinping sit down in Washington in less than two weeks.
Trump and Xi in Beijing on May 15.Photographer: Evan Vucci/Getty Images
One reason that China’s pushing back is that it still lags behind the US 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/nvidia-ceo-puts-trump-on-speakerphone-while-downplaying-ai-risks?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
, and Beijing sees AI as a once-in-a-lifetime chance to reshape the tech 
balance in its favor.

There’s also huge skepticism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/are-we-losing-control-of-ai-what-s-driving-new-fears?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
 as to the underlying intention of American calls to hit the brakes.

Chinese AI companies already face US blocks on the chips needed to train and 
operate frontier models. State-controlled China Daily accused the US AI 
executives of wanting to further blunt Chinese competition.

It doesn’t help that Anthropic’s Dario Amodei is out in front.

Beijing holds special antipathy for the American entrepreneur, who has emerged 
as a prominent advocate of tighter US restrictions on China’s access to 
advanced AI.
Anthropic Co-Founder Amodei.Photographer: Jason Henry/Bloomberg
Yet safety concerns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/obama-calls-for-ai-safety-measures-rebuking-trump-approach?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
 are also front of mind in China, even if areas Beijing considers important 
don’t always fully overlap with American worries.

One prominent fear is that AI could threaten the legitimacy of the Communist 
Party, asvoiced in an article 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/china-spy-chief-warns-of-ai-risks-as-us-tech-leaders-urge-brakes?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
 posted Sunday by the country’s top intelligence official.

China’s definition of AI security is broader and continues to expand.

Just yesterday it released an updated safety framework to address emerging 
risks, including those of “recursive self-improvement” — the same dynamic cited 
by Amodei arguing for greater caution.

Even as Trump downplays the need for guardrails 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/trump-s-ai-defense-defies-voter-unease-heading-into-midterms?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
, the US and China have shared concerns, from AI-enabled cyberattacks by 
non-state actors to keeping humans in control of nuclear decisions.

Amid all the AI-focused noise, perhaps that’s something worth remembering as 
the Xi-Trump summit approaches.— Colum Murphy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUHPtat0hJ0/colum-murphy?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/germany-to-lobby-eu-on-new-china-policy-may-seek-more-tariffs?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
 of economic-security measures to shield strategic industries from China that 
may include new tariffs on hybrid EVs. Ministries in Berlin are mapping 
vulnerabilities to the Asian manufacturing powerhouse and assessing 
countermeasures, with potential proposals including new tariffs, mandatory JVs, 
strengthened inbound and outbound investment screening, and a bolstered export 
controls regime, sources say. Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi told French

And Finally

Thailand is targeting the equivalent of more than $600 million in extra 
tourism spending witha domestic travel stimulus plan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/thailand-eyes-600-million-tourism-boost-with-travel-subsidies?cmpid=BBD091526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8918>
 aimed at shoring up a key economic engine hit by weaker foreign arrivals and 
higher energy costs. The measure reflects the Southeast Asian nation’s growing 
reliance on local travelers to cushion the sector as it struggles with the slow 
return of international visitors. Tourism is a major source of jobs and foreign 
exchange for Thailand, making the slowdown a broader concern for an economy 
already grappling with sluggish growth.
