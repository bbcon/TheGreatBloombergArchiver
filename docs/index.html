US

  • Fed expected to hold rates steady today despite September CPI coming in above consensus — markets price only a 7% probability of a hike at the FOMC meeting
  • US durable goods orders rose in September by the most in five months, on stronger bookings for aircraft and business equipment, beating expectations
  • 10-year Treasury yield sits near 5.01%, with the S&P 500 trading at its lowest forward earnings multiple in three years
  • Microsoft and Alphabet report after the bell, with investors scrutinizing cloud growth and AI capital expenditure returns

Monetary Policy

The Federal Reserve's October decision is the immediate focal point, with consensus firmly in the hold camp despite September inflation printing above expectations. Chairman Kevin Warsh's post-decision press conference carries unusual weight: market participants remain in what analysts describe as a "discovery process" regarding his communications approach, having observed only three post-decision conferences since he took office in May. Vice Chair Jefferson and New York Fed President Williams have both signaled the Fed can afford patience, but a hawkish hold message — clarifying the committee's tolerance for above-target inflation — will be the key interpretive challenge. Bond traders who have built up bullish positions face the risk of a sharp reversal if Warsh's tone disappoints.

Equity Markets

Equity market internals have deteriorated notably. Roughly 40% of the 124,750 possible stock pairs within the S&P 500 are currently negatively correlated, an unusually high figure reflecting zero-sum competition driven by AI trade dynamics and elevated oil prices. The equal-weighted S&P 500 has meaningfully underperformed, and valuation multiples have compressed to three-year lows. Robinhood fell 8% after missing third-quarter revenue and earnings estimates, while NXP Semiconductors dropped 7% on a disappointing outlook. The AI hyperscaler earnings — Microsoft up 36% since July, Alphabet down 6.3% over the same period — represent the week's critical test of whether capital expenditure is translating into returns. ---

Asia

  • Jio Platforms surged as much as 23% in its Mumbai trading debut after raising a record $3.1 billion, lifting Reliance Group past Tata Group as India's largest conglomerate by market value
  • Reserve Bank of New Zealand unexpectedly held its key rate unchanged in a split decision, citing global uncertainty — the kiwi dollar fell sharply on the surprise

Monetary Policy

The RBNZ hold was the sharpest market surprise in Asia Wednesday, with the split decision underscoring growing divergence in how regional central banks are weighing global uncertainty against domestic inflation dynamics. In Australia, the opposite dynamic prevails: above-consensus CPI has shifted rate expectations materially toward another tightening when the RBA meets next week. Meanwhile, the Bank of Japan is widely expected to hold at Friday's meeting, with the swap market pricing near-certainty of a December hike. Early indications from Japan's shunto wage negotiations suggest 2027 pay rises could approach 6%, above the roughly 5% pace seen since 2023, which would strengthen the case for continued BOJ normalization toward an estimated terminal rate of 2%.

Capital Flows

A notable structural shift in Treasury demand deserves attention: US-aligned economies in Asia purchased a net $389 million in Treasuries in the first eight months of 2026 — Japan alone, the largest foreign holder, bought just $389 million, down 99% year-on-year. The aggregate decline among US-aligned nations exceeded 60% versus the prior year. While governments have not formally announced reallocation strategies, analysts attribute the shift to weak domestic currencies, rising local yields, equity demand, and increasing unease over US policy predictability. The shift may signal a gradual erosion of the structural bid that has historically anchored Treasury demand. ---

Europe

  • GSK fell 5.4% in London after third-quarter results revealed slumping vaccine sales; Adyen dropped 13% in Amsterdam after discontinuing medium-term profit guidance
  • Israel's parliamentary election is too close to call, with Netanyahu's bloc and opposition leader Eisenkot's alliance both tracking toward 51 seats — coalition negotiations expected to be protracted

Fiscal Policy

Chancellor Healey's inaugural budget was calibrated explicitly to avoid the adverse market reactions that destabilized predecessor administrations. The government has limited financial firepower — business rates reform and reversal of payroll tax increases are effectively off the table — constraining the scale of high-street regeneration measures that Prime Minister Burnham has made a political priority. The restrained approach appears designed to preserve fiscal credibility while delivering tangible near-term household relief, though analysts will assess whether the combination is sufficient to maintain the administration's early poll momentum.

Corporate Earnings

European bank results were broadly constructive. UBS's wealth inflows of $55.1 billion signal continued resilience in private client demand even as CEO Ermotti escalated rhetoric around Swiss capital requirement proposals, issuing an unusually explicit relocation warning. Deutsche Bank's fixed-income trading met estimates. The contrast with GSK and Adyen — where earnings disappointments triggered severe single-day drawdowns — illustrates the bifurcated environment facing European equities, with a weak euro providing partial support for export-oriented sectors. ---

  • Brazilian equities stabilized following the election result, with markets apparently relieved by the outcome's clarity despite ongoing political uncertainty
  • IMF reached a staff-level agreement with Argentina on the third review of its $20 billion loan program

Brazil Political Economy

The post-election period in Brazil is entering a sensitive phase. President Lula's surprise fourth-term victory has introduced questions about policy continuity and grid infrastructure — renewable energy curtailment has emerged as a structural constraint, with investment peaking in late 2023 at $13 billion and failing to recover above $10 billion in any subsequent half-year period. Transmission capacity and battery storage shortfalls are increasingly crimping revenues for clean energy producers even as wind and solar capacity expands. A battery auction scheduled for December is unlikely to resolve the bottleneck materially in the near term.

Regional Policy

Argentina's staff-level IMF agreement on the third program review provides a degree of near-term external financing assurance, though full board approval remains pending. In Colombia, President de la Espriella's fiscal consolidation agenda faces its first congressional challenge — the outcome will determine whether Bogotá can secure IMF support and restore investor confidence in fiscal trajectories. Chile's central bank held rates steady at its latest meeting, consistent with a cautious regional easing posture amid global uncertainty. ---

Global

  • WTI crude rose 1.8% to $101.68 and Brent gained to $114.57 after the Wall Street Journal reported the US has warned Gulf states of a possible short, intense renewal of Iran strikes following next week's midterm elections
  • US-aligned nations cut net Treasury purchases by more than 60% in the first eight months of 2026 versus the prior year, with Japan's buying down 99% — a structural shift in sovereign demand
  • AI capital expenditure is estimated to add up to 60 basis points to US core PCE inflation at peak, with the wave expected to crest in Q1 2027 according to Goldman Sachs economists

Energy and Iran

Oil markets are pricing a binary outcome in the US-Iran conflict: either a negotiated de-escalation or an extended and intensifying confrontation. The WSJ report of US warnings to Gulf states regarding potential post-midterm strikes has amplified the near-term risk premium. A third scenario — a prolonged stalemate that becomes a structural feature of the market — appears underpriced. At current Brent levels above $114, energy costs are functioning as a persistent inflationary headwind across developed markets, complicating central bank signaling in the US, Europe, and Australia simultaneously.

AI Inflation and Power Demand

The AI infrastructure buildout is generating inflationary pressure through three channels: elevated memory hardware costs, pass-through to consumer electronics, and power price increases from data center electricity demand. Goldman Sachs estimates a peak 60 basis point boost to US core PCE. Separately, Sierra Club analysis projects US power sector emissions could rise 42% by 2030 if current data center construction trajectories are realized, effectively erasing 15 years of grid decarbonization progress. Trump's proposed rollback of methane emissions rules adds a further dimension to the regulatory retreat from climate commitments, with the EU also proposing to delay methane disclosure requirements for energy imports amid winter supply concerns linked to the Iran conflict.
