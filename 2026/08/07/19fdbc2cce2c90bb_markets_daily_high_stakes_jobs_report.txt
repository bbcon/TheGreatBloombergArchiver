‘A Goldilocks jobs number’

Good news in today’s July jobs report  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/bond-traders-look-to-jobs-data-that-may-tip-scale-on-fed-hike?cmpid=080726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260807&utm_content=7451>
could equal bad news for markets.

A too-strong reading may get interpreted as a hot labor market that’s fanning 
inflation and adding to concern the Fed needs to raise interest rates. 
Conversely, a weaker-than-expected report would soothe those worries.

“If you are the Fed chairman, you want a Goldilocks jobs number and not 
something too strong or too weak,” said Hank Smith at Haverford Trust.

Data releases have taken on added importance as Warsh’s Fed weans markets off 
“forward guidance,” which refers to publicly signaling interest-rate policy far 
in advance of official decisions.

JPMorgan Chase’s Market Intelligence team sees 65% odds of the economy adding 
more than 60,000 jobs in July. In the most extreme scenario, where payrolls 
rise more than 150,000, the S&P 500 would slide as much as 1.8%, they say.

Economists surveyed by Bloomberg predict:

 * About 80,000 jobs were created in July, a pickup from June’s 57,000 but 
still among the lowest totals this year.
 * The unemployment rate stayed steady at 4.2%.

Yen falters

Today’s jobs report will also play a big role in what happens next to the yen. 
A strong jobs report that pushes up US rates would make the dollar attractive, 
possibly heaping more pressure on the yen.

“The possibility of another round of intervention is high especially as 
dollar-yen approaches 160,” said Moh Siong Sim, a strategist at the 
Oversea-Chinese Banking Corp.
