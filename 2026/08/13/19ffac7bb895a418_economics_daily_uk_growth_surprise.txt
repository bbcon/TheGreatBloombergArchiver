UK Surprise

Countless column inches have been devoted to Britain’s growth problem. But, if 
Thursday’s deluge of GDP figures are anything to go by, it certainly wasn’t an 
issue in the first half of 2026.

Sizzling weather and World Cup fever helped the UK economy comfortably 
outperform expectations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/uk-economy-weathers-early-months-of-iran-war-with-0-4-growth?cmpid=BBD081326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7685>
 with growth of 0.3% in June. It meant that the economy expanded 0.4% in the 
second quarter and 1% in the first half as a whole.

While those temporary summer boosts will fade, there were some encouraging 
signs under the hood as well. Business investment was up 1.7% in the second 
quarter, while the information and communications sector drove services output 
— both possible signs that artificial intelligence is beginning to lift the 
economy.

Still, economists believe Prime Minister Andy Burnham can’t let up on trying 
to improve Britain’s longer term growth prospects. The Bank of England has 
warned the full effects of the Iran war are still feeding through to households 
with forecasters expecting GDP growth to slow in the second half of the year.

“With household utility bills jumping in July, tighter financial conditions, 
the labor market loosening and a new prime minister adding to policy 
uncertainty, demand is likely to be weak in the months ahead,” said Matt Bunny 
andDan Hanson 
<https://bloomberg.com/authors/ASReugI9vlI/dan-hanson?cmpid=BBD081326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7685>
 of Bloomberg Economics. “Growth cooled over April and May and while the FIFA 
World Cup supported consumer spending in June, the trend is toward weakening 
underlying momentum.”

A refreshed team in Downing Street will help Burnham and his Chancellor John 
Healey put together theircrucial first budget 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/burnham-strengthens-no-10-control-on-uk-economy-in-power-shift?cmpid=BBD081326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7685>
 in the autumn. As part of the shake-up, Bloomberg revealed last night that 
former BOE deputy governorMinouche Shafik 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/minouche-shafik-leaves-post-as-chief-economic-advisor-to-uk-pm?cmpid=BBD081326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7685>
 has left her post as chief economic advisor to the PM.

All eyes will now be on whether Burnham and Healey can keep the good news 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/internal-uk-forecasts-raise-risk-of-growth-downgrade-due-to-war?cmpid=BBD081326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7685>
 on the economy coming.

Need-to-Know Research

Six months on from the Citrini Research scenario of AI becoming a wrecking 
ball for the job market — whichspooked Wall Street 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-23/software-payments-shares-tumble-after-citrini-post-on-ai-risks?cmpid=BBD081326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7685>
 at the time — analysis by Bank of America finds “there are few signs that AI 
is leading to net job destruction.”

“Data to date confirms our optimistic take on AI’s labor market impact,” BofA 
economists including Stephen Juneau wrote in a note this week. “Outside of a 
modest rise in youth unemployment rates, most data show little signs” AI is a 
major jobs headwind, they said. A “granular” look at job-creation trends shows 
“little correlation between AI usage and the change in labor demand since 
January 2026.”

There are some things worth keeping an eye on, the team said. Jobless rates 
for college graduates aged 22-27 are now above 2019 levels. While some of that 
might be due to residual uncertainty created by tariff policies, it’s possible 
AI may be affecting entry-level jobs, they said.

Also, two job categories — finance and insurance, along with information 
(covering roles such as data-processing and software publishing) — “have high 
AI usage and have seen labor demand” drop in the five months through June. 
These white-collar sectors may be looking to “offset labor costs through AI 
adoption,” the BofA team wrote.
