**Political Economy**

Brazil's accusation that Washington and Buenos Aires are interfering in its October election adds geopolitical complexity to an already contested race between President Lula and right-wing Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, who has cultivated ties with Trump and Milei. The dispute risks complicating Brazil's trade and diplomatic relationships at a sensitive moment, coming as the country navigates elevated inflation and the potential for monetary easing. If Bolsonaro prevails, it would represent the most consequential shift in Latin American political alignment in years.

**El Niño and Commodity Exposure**

For the broader region, a strengthening El Niño — potentially the most powerful in 76 years — poses a material risk to agricultural output and energy systems. Peru's anchovy fishery disruption is an early and tangible economic cost. Colombia faces compounding pressure on energy infrastructure and food prices from both El Niño-related weather disruption and elevated oil import costs. Bloomberg Economics research suggests El Niño drought conditions could raise consumer prices by nearly two percentage points in the most exposed economies, adding to inflation already elevated by the Iran conflict.
