Friday, November 27, 2026

US · Asia · Europe · LatAm · Global

US

  • Nasdaq 100 futures surged 2.4% and S&P 500 futures gained 1.8%, extending a post-Thanksgiving rebound; the Nasdaq remains ~17% below its early-October peak and on track for its worst monthly decline since 2022.
  • The dollar extended its run, with the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index up 1.9% in November, heading for a third consecutive monthly gain — the longest winning streak in two years.
  • Trump accused his own administration of deliberately under-reporting Q3 GDP growth after a routine upward revision, offering no evidence for the claim.

Equity Markets

US equity futures rallied sharply on the Friday after Thanksgiving, led by AI-adjacent names — CoreWeave gained 7%, Micron 7.6%, and optical component maker Coherent 8.9%. The move appears technically driven: analysts note many sectors had reached oversold levels following the Nasdaq 100's 17% decline from early October highs, itself the product of a multi-week reassessment of AI earnings expectations. Whether this constitutes a durable inflection or a relief rally within a broader corrective phase remains genuinely uncertain.

Monetary Policy and Dollar

The dollar's sustained appreciation reflects the intersection of resilient US growth, still-elevated inflation, and elevated rate expectations. Market commentary suggests the Fed may need to tighten further — potentially at both the December and January meetings — reinforcing dollar strength into 2027 as AI-driven capital expenditure continues to attract global investment flows into US assets. The 10-year Treasury yield rose six basis points to approximately 4.91%, consistent with that rate outlook. ---

Asia

  • China's CSI 300 Index surged more than 4% — the largest single-day gain since October 2024 — after the State Council issued a rare midday statement reaffirming market support; the chip-heavy Star 50 Index jumped 15%.
  • Shanghai Communist Party chief Chen Jining, a Politburo member and rising political star, was placed under anti-corruption investigation, removing one of the country's most prominent business-friendly officials.
  • Trump announced a new 20% tariff on Chinese goods within the bounds of the existing trade truce, while threatening a 40% rate if January negotiations collapse; Beijing reserved the right to retaliate.

China Political Risk

Chen Jining's downfall — coming despite an impressive résumé spanning Tsinghua University president, environmental minister, and Beijing mayor — represents a meaningful signal about the trajectory of Xi Jinping's governance ahead of next year's Party Congress. Chen had cultivated relationships with global business leaders including JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon and Apple's Tim Cook and had reportedly told visitors he expected further promotion, commentary that may have fed Xi's suspicions about an emerging independent power base. The episode reinforces a key lesson for foreign investors: political personalities in China offer no durable anchor for strategy.

Trade and Markets

The State Council's midday intervention stabilized markets that had experienced weeks of AI-related volatility. The optimism spread to Hong Kong, where the Hang Seng Tech Index advanced more than 6%, and copper surged 6.5% — its strongest single-day move since January. The trade backdrop, however, remains fragile: Huawei's criminal conviction in a New York court on money laundering and trade-secret charges adds another complication ahead of December's G20 discussions in Miami on extending the January trade truce. ---

Europe

  • France and Spain reported CPI that slowed significantly more than anticipated, providing unexpected disinflationary relief even as ECB rate hikes remain under consideration.
  • EU leaders cut short a Brussels budget summit without agreement on the bloc's nearly €2 trillion spending plan, with countries divided over size, new taxes, and regional funding; a December deadline looms.
  • Pablo Hernandez de Cos was recommended as the ECB's next president following the summit, though the budget impasse overshadowed the announcement.

Inflation and Monetary Policy

The sharper-than-expected deceleration in French and Spanish CPI is a material data point for ECB deliberations, introducing a tension between still-elevated core pressures elsewhere and emerging evidence of disinflation at the periphery. How the incoming ECB president — former Bank of Spain governor Hernandez de Cos, regarded as a pragmatist — will navigate this divergence will be a central question for eurozone rate markets in early 2027. The euro is on track for its largest annual decline against the Chinese yuan in two decades, reflecting the cumulative weight of the growth differential.

Corporate and Defense

SAP confirmed it is preparing to cut at least 10,000 positions — roughly 9% of its global workforce — as it restructures for AI-era software delivery, following similar moves at Oracle and Microsoft. Separately, the EU is preparing tens of billions of euros in defense loans and grants to replace US military capabilities, focusing on air defense and intelligence as Washington draws down some European commitments. Spain faces a consequential election Sunday, with Prime Minister Sánchez warning that a Vox-influenced government would implement spending cuts deep enough to risk recession. ---

LatAm

  • Brazilian President Lula appointed Bradesco Chairman Luiz Carlos Trabuco Cappi as finance minister, turning to one of the country's most recognized banking figures to restore investor confidence in public finances.
  • Argentina's senate passed legislation suspending next year's primary elections, reducing a potential source of market volatility as questions persist about President Milei's reelection prospects.
  • Peru's business community expressed unusual confidence at its annual gathering, betting on political stability under President Keiko Fujimori after a decade of institutional turbulence.

Brazil Fiscal Credibility

Trabuco Cappi's appointment signals that Lula is prioritizing market credibility at a moment when investor concerns about Brazil's fiscal trajectory have intensified. The choice of a Bradesco chairman — a figure with deep ties to the financial establishment — suggests a tactical pivot toward orthodox signaling, though whether it translates into substantive fiscal consolidation remains to be seen. Brazilian assets had come under pressure amid doubts about the administration's spending discipline, and the appointment's market reception will be an early test of its effectiveness.

Regional Politics

Argentina's decision to suspend primaries removes a near-term political volatility event from the calendar, a development likely to be received constructively by markets that have been sensitive to any signal about Milei's reform durability. Peru's improved business sentiment represents a meaningful shift after years of political instability that deterred investment in one of Latin America's most resource-rich economies. Whether Fujimori's government can translate that confidence into tangible infrastructure and mining investment remains the key medium-term question. ---

Global

  • Oil posted its first weekly gain of November, with WTI futures rising to $86.19 (+3.0%), as escalating Middle East attacks on energy infrastructure heightened supply concerns.
  • COP31 in Antalya ended without an overarching climate agreement; Turkey spent approximately $700 million hosting the summit, now facing domestic political scrutiny amid 30% inflation and a housing cost crisis.
  • Europe's grid infrastructure is proving unable to absorb the continent's rapid renewable buildout — Britain's grid operator issued safety-margin warnings on four consecutive days this week, while Germany spends roughly €3 billion annually on congestion management.

Oil and Middle East

Drone attacks on Italian technicians in Kuwait — blamed by Kuwaiti authorities on Iran — and a Houthi strike on a Saudi oil refinery on the Red Sea are keeping energy markets on edge. Qatar's LNG exports through the Strait of Hormuz are flowing more freely than earlier feared, with Bangladesh importing more than 200,000 tons this month, up from zero in August and September — a supply-chain reconfiguration that is already benefiting South Asian importers facing acute cost pressures.

Energy Transition Stress

Europe's renewable buildout is exposing a structural mismatch: wind and solar farms take two to three years to build while transmission lines require up to a decade. The European Commission estimates €584 billion in grid investment is needed by 2030. Until that capacity arrives, the paradox of paying wind farms to switch off in congested regions while firing up expensive generation elsewhere will persist — and consumers will bear both costs. COP31's failure without agreement deepens uncertainty about the global policy framework underpinning that investment.
