Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
energy reporter Eamon Farhat looks ahead to the European winter as the Iran war 
continues to inflate costs for homes and businesses across the continent.

European households are bracing for another hike in energy bills once the 
heating season kicks in.

Prices for winter gas are more than double their year-earlier levels, while 
power in Germany, the region’s biggest energy market, is up 50%.

Costs have been driven higher by the slump in fuel shipments from the Middle 
East and could climb further if the war in Iran drags on.

The squeeze is focused on supplies of liquefied natural gas 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-04/iran-war-dents-qatar-s-reputation-as-lng-s-safest-bet?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
, with about a fifth of global flowsshut in 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/qatarenergy-extends-lng-force-majeure-for-european-buyers?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
 following the blockade of the crucial Strait of Hormuz.

While Asia has been most exposed to the disruption, buyers there have pulled 
cargoes away from Europe. That’s complicated the region’s efforts torebuild gas 
inventories 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/why-europe-s-gas-prices-and-storage-issues-are-causing-growing-concerns?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
 that are languishing at their lowest seasonal level on record.

Higher wholesale costs are already feeding through to consumers and 
accelerating inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/uk-inflation-climbs-to-four-month-high-as-energy-bills-rise?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
, with UK energy bills predicted to rise to theirhighest in three years 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/uk-household-energy-costs-forecast-to-climb-to-a-three-year-high?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
 come October.

The situation has echoes of 2022, when Russia’s invasion of Ukraine caused a 
severe gas supply crunch and sent markets soaring across Europe.

Current prices are far from those levels, shielded in part by a rapid 
expansion of solar 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-13/europe-solar-power-wasted-as-electricity-grids-fail-to-handle-surge?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
 and other renewables in recent years. Even so, they underscore the region’s 
continued reliance on — and sensitivity to — imported fossil fuels as grid 
upgrades fail to keep pace with the intermittency of clean energy.

The depletion of the continent’s gas storage doesn’t necessarily mean 
consumers will face a genuine loss of supply this winter, but it does mean 
countries may have to cough up more toattract global deliveries 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/europe-faces-winter-gas-reckoning-as-global-fight-for-lng-brews?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
.

With a smaller supply buffer than usual and just six weeks to go before the 
heating season officially starts, European governments are once again 
confrontingcalls to intervene 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-01/gas-importer-vng-urges-germany-to-intervene-to-refill-storage?cmpid=BBBXT081926_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7886>
 or face mounting public ire.

—Eamon Farhat, Bloomberg News
