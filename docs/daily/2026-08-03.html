Monday, August 03, 2026

US

  • No major US macro data releases today; Friday's July nonfarm payrolls report — consensus 85,000, versus June's 57,000 miss — is the week's key event.
  • Real 30-year Treasury yields have hit their highest since 2008, with 10-year yields at their highest since before Bessent took office, raising mortgage costs and adding to economic headwinds.
  • S&P 500 futures rose 0.6% on easing US-Iran tensions; Microsoft's $600 billion two-day market cap surge narrowed the market back toward mega-cap concentration after two months of broadening.
  • Fed Chair Warsh is reportedly exploring reducing policy meetings from eight to six per year, adding to existing uncertainty about the Fed's forward guidance.

Monetary Policy and Yields

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference last week left markets without clear guidance, though the projected rate path was barely changed. More consequentially, the 30-year TIPS yield has reached levels last seen only briefly during the 2008 financial crisis — a development that may signal deteriorating confidence in the Fed's inflation credibility rather than a routine repricing. Warsh is also reportedly exploring a structural reduction in scheduled policy meetings, an unconventional move that raises questions about communication strategy at a sensitive juncture.

Equity Markets

Microsoft's earnings-driven rally — adding more than $600 billion in market cap over two days to rejoin the $3 trillion club — temporarily reversed two months of equal-weight outperformance and re-concentrated market leadership in mega-cap technology. Meta and Alphabet were punished despite strong earnings on concerns about capital spending discipline. The hedge fund Situational Awareness suffered a significant unwind of AI positions and was rescued by Citadel at distressed valuations, a development that appears to have contributed to broader volatility, though its role as cause versus symptom remains unclear. ---

Asia

  • South Korea's Kospi gained 17% in a single session Friday — an extraordinary move reflecting extreme positioning and volatility rather than a durable rerating signal.
  • Southeast Asia manufacturing PMI rose to 52.8 in July from 50.5 in June, beating the trend in China and reaching its fastest expansion pace since the Middle East conflict began.
  • China's manufacturing PMI unexpectedly contracted for the first time in five months, reinforcing signs of economic softness in export-oriented sectors.
  • The yen surged as much as 1.4% intraday after Japan confirmed the first joint US-Japan currency intervention in 15 years, with South Korea also appearing to participate in coordination.

China AI and Manufacturing

Alibaba released its largest-ever AI model, Qwen3.8-Max, built on 2.4 trillion parameters and ranking above Moonshot's Kimi K3 on several benchmarks — the latest in a series of Chinese breakthroughs challenging US rivals at significantly lower computational cost. Against this, China's factory activity contracted unexpectedly in July, underscoring that domestic demand softness and export headwinds from US tariffs continue to weigh on the manufacturing base.

Japan and the Yen

The $52.8 billion single-day intervention on July 31 was the largest on record, and the first coordinated US-Japan yen-support operation since 2011. The Bank of Japan held rates at 1% last week despite Governor Ueda projecting above-2% inflation in the second half of the fiscal year — a contradiction that has undermined forward guidance credibility. Prime Minister Takaichi's growth-oriented fiscal stance constrains the BOJ's room to tighten, while Japan's high public debt caps how far yields can rise, leaving the yen structurally vulnerable. The September BOJ meeting is now the critical decision point. ---

Europe

  • Europe's major rivers — the Rhine and Danube — are falling toward record lows, forcing Hungary to shut down its sole nuclear plant; the economic disruption to chemicals, energy, and freight transport represents a meaningful headwind for eastern European economies.
  • AstraZeneca shares fell 6.1% in London after reports it explored an acquisition of Bristol Myers Squibb, a deal that would rank as the largest ever in pharmaceuticals.
  • Spanish Prime Minister Sánchez faces growing political isolation after thousands of migrants overran the Ceuta enclave, with European peers rejecting his framing of their response.

Climate and Infrastructure Stress

Europe's heat waves are producing measurable economic damage beyond the immediate human cost. Dangerously low river levels are disrupting barge transport of chemicals, oil products, and other industrial goods, while curtailing power generation at riverside reactors in France and Hungary. European natural gas storage is at its lowest level for this time of year in almost two decades, a consequence of the Middle East conflict's impact on energy prices and winter stockpiling. The combination of energy price pressure, transport disruption, and power supply constraints represents a compound supply-side shock for continental industry as the heating season approaches.

Politics and Corporate

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham's devolution agenda — devolving economic power to regions modeled on Manchester — faces structural questions. Scotland, the UK's most devolved economy, has grown more slowly than England since 2008 despite higher tax rates and spending, complicating the evidence base for the policy. In the UK labor market, AI is producing a bifurcation: IT and engineering vacancies have held up while accounting, retail, and manufacturing roles have fallen by double digits, casting doubt on the premise that broad technical qualifications will future-proof young workers. ---

Brazil Monetary Policy

The expected rate cut by the Banco Central do Brasil this week would reflect progress on disinflation relative to its regional peers, whose central banks are holding. The divergence in policy trajectories across the region — with Brazil easing while Mexico and others pause — reflects differing inflation profiles and domestic demand conditions. The political backdrop in Brazil remains fluid, with the October presidential election campaign already generating instability within opposition coalitions.

Argentina Trade Dynamics

Chinese electric vehicle sales in Argentina are surging, with BYD entering the top 10 car sellers after the Milei government implemented an annual tariff-free import quota of 50,000 EVs. The policy reflects Milei's inflation-suppression strategy — cheap imported goods serving as a disinflationary tool — though Argentina's abstinence from international bond markets continues to raise concerns about the adequacy of hard-currency reserves ahead of any future external shock. ---

Global

  • The US-Japan joint currency intervention — the first in 15 years — marks a significant escalation in currency market activity, with the US selling euros to buy yen in an operation described as unprecedented in structure.

US-Iran and Energy Markets

Oil's sharp decline follows Trump's decision to postpone military action against Iran, with the president suggesting a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz may be near. Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi described talks with Oman as being in their final stages, though those negotiations address shipping routes through the strait rather than whether it remains open — a distinction that leaves the fundamental supply risk unresolved. Brent futures had surged more than 20% in July alone; the reversal reflects headline diplomatic progress rather than a structural resolution, and the situation warrants close monitoring.

Yen Intervention and Global FX

The structural logic of the US yen intervention is that a disorderly yen depreciation risked forcing Japan to liquidate its $1.1 trillion-plus US Treasury holdings to fund unilateral defense of the currency — which would push US yields higher at a moment when the 30-year yield is already at its highest since 2007. By selling euros rather than dollars to buy yen, the US Treasury sought to avoid contributing to dollar weakness and its attendant yield consequences. The operation appears ultimately motivated by US domestic financial conditions rather than alliance management alone, though the geopolitical leverage implications — for Japanese defense spending, base costs, and China policy — remain an active secondary dynamic. China's mining M&A posture is also shifting: Beijing's failure to approve Zijin Gold's $3.9 billion Allied Gold acquisition signals a move toward minority stake-building over outright acquisitions, reflecting rising geopolitical risk tolerance constraints.
