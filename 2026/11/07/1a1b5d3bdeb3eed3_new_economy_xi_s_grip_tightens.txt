The Nov. 3 US midterm elections weren’t the only globally significant calendar 
entry of late. Days earlier, China’s ruling party met in a conclave known as 
the Fifth Plenum. And while the American event resulted in a loosening of 
President Donald Trump’sgrip on power 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/2026-midterm-elections-what-democrats-wins-in-congress-mean-for-trump?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
, the Chinese one featured theexact opposite 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/what-to-expect-from-china-s-fifth-plenum-xi-s-purges-and-tighter-party-control?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
 for his counterpart, President Xi Jinping.

Some 14 years after taking the Chinese Communist Party’s helm, Xi remains 
focused on instituting loyalty and obedience. The gathering of the CCP’s 
Central Committee, its most important meeting of the year, yielded aresolution 
<https://english.news.cn/20261101/16d34cfc97f04b8d925baf68d2963706/c.html> that 
emphasized a tightening of Beijing’s political supervision over lower-level 
leaders. And that may cause problems for the world’s No. 2 economy.

The resolution said China’s party bosses must not turn decision-making into a “
one-man show 
<https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESsAEBxCJF1xSCq2OZUOD3Kgdrod2K0Xk_zRvrfQAIjc8htNDdwT_FyN3mqO3Kt7T1tEagX1c8xBsHF03LZ7oyquckDGVXtMpJ7bBf8O-a9XRy_y-ThTePuhR1or4T5LF9fzpT4-bjN5USiX3uj4YyG32iGyAlXWTPkKshJ-LFTtQcK5_QMyeJmoOSTAzZJVYkBD_dDstunxlabzm-ag7f4YoyZBbpumroZcDOMGYiQpPi2g>
,” subordinating other cadres to their personal authority. Nobody should be 
“getting too big for their boots” and pushing their individual views on their 
teams, nor “forming self-serving cliques.” A reporting system will provide for 
team members to report up “problems” with leaders, it said.

The thing is, party leaders who succeeded in the past were the ones who took 
charge of their city or region, competing with peers to innovate andspur growth 
<>. One area might champion electric vehicles. Another chemicals. A third data 
storage, and so on. Patronage has been key to the rise of many Chinese 
companies.

“Local governments have been the unsung engine of China’s economic model in 
the last two decades,” Alicia Garcia Herrero at Natixis SAwrote 
<https://aliciagarciaherrero.substack.com/p/chinas-local-governments-from-engine>
 this week. Further back, a decentralized power structure grew out of the chaos 
of Mao Zedong’sCultural Revolution 
<https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESogEBxCJF1z5gN0Sdo5lifyR8EQv2x6KYDflL5rjLt-X7fYLBnz6jNULxRaEZf4EVdHn1F6eyKwzcpaFTE1NNxaMPXeoeDEUoHV_6vpGZwgtiSF56gsH2M1KxTxj423CMN3ZTHMU7kxHh8_kfU8T-JWeGKTIvGbeVRRh8Iz2iu1d1Iq6hqi7zV-gymD8XfEfFBw42VfuXgnF4LRFsAw2lEycB7EA>
, which wrecked central planning capacities and empowered regional authorities. 
After reform and China’s eventual opening up, showing initiative in the lower 
ranks became a path to political success.

But now, the dictate from Beijing is obedience. Christopher Beddor at Gavekal 
Dragonomics says “there’s kind of this glitch in the system as Xi tries to 
centralize it — where the system was designed for decentralization.” Which, as 
outlined below, doesn’t augur well for resolving China’s economic problems.

The four-day meeting came roughly a year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/what-to-expect-from-china-s-fifth-plenum-xi-s-purges-and-tighter-party-control?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
 before the CCP’s once-every-five-year party “congress.” Those congresses used 
to be when leadership handovers occurred, along with the designation of the 
next batch of top leaders. Xi, now 73,did away with that 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/politics/articles/2018-03-11/china-ties-future-to-xi-as-congress-scraps-president-term-limits?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
 in 2018, but political observers will nonetheless be watching a year from now 
to see who in their 40s or 50s gets named to the party’s top ranks.

Cadres will now be expected to mount “study campaigns” on the Fifth Plenum’s 
takeaways ahead of next year’s congress. State news agency Xinhua, Qiushi (the 
party’s main theoretical journal) and other outlets will be offering analysis 
on the commandments laid down. What’s clear from the resolution itself is that 
the emphasis on the party’s grip on China, and Beijing’sgrip on the party 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/xi-s-corruption-purges-upend-hunt-for-china-s-next-top-leaders?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
, is only getting stronger.

“Take strengthening the party politically as the overarching principle,” 
cadres were told. “Adhere to a top-down approach.” Party leaders who head units 
ranging from CCP committees in villages, cities and provinces, toparty cells 
<https://merics.org/en/report/serving-people-controlling-them-how-party-reinserting-itself-daily-life>
 in private-sector companies and government agencies, were instructed not to 
impose their own views. But they also were told not to “simply play safe or 
avoid offending anyone.” Hmmm.
The emblem of the People’s Republic of China displayed at the Great Hall of 
the People in BeijingPhotographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg
There also must not be “discussion with no decisions, decisions with no 
action, and action with no results,” the resolution commanded. And all the 
while, leaders will need to keep in mind that their actions could be the basis 
for party judgment down the line.

The Central Committee said it will “improve the lifelong accountability system 
for major decisions made by leading officials.” (The plenum offered an example 
of such accountability: three former chairs of the China Aerospace Science and 
Industry Corp., the nation’s largest missile maker, wereall purged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/china-names-vice-military-chair-as-new-politburo-member?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
.)

“Xi’s trying to operate in a way that’s much more centralized than what 
they’ve been used to,” Beddor, deputy China research director at Gavekal, said 
in a client webinar this week. “You have to make this decision, and yet they’re 
telling you, basically, ‘don’t screw this up,’ — so there is a mismatch there.”

Longtime China-watcher Michael Pettis observed in a Nov. 2 analysis of the 
plenum 
<https://www.globalsourcepartners.com/posts/results-of-china-s-fifth-plenum/teaser>
 that “when officials believe policy mistakes will be punished more severely 
than inaction, they may become less willing to experiment, acknowledge economic 
difficulties, or pursue initiatives that depart from central directives.”

“The result could be a bureaucracy more responsive to political instructions 
but less capable of adapting to changing economic conditions,” wrote Pettis, a 
senior fellow at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace.

On the economic front, what Xi is interested in, as this newsletter has 
written aboutmany times 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2024-09-21/bloomberg-new-economy-why-china-s-sluggish-growth-isn-t-rattling-xi-jinping?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
, is building China’stechnological prowess 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2024-03-02/bloomberg-new-economy-xi-jinping-s-new-productive-forces-don-t-add-up?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
 and advancing itsmanufacturing capacity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2025-11-29/china-wants-to-dominate-high-and-low-tech-industries-new-economy?cmpid=new-economy&utm_campaign=new-economy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261107&utm_content=11053>
, from the low end to the high. Not so much on enriching consumers.

In the wake of the Fifth Plenum, “more efficient implementation of the 
existing growth model risks making the imbalances worse,” Pettis said.
