There is little sign of a let up in Australia’s sudden housing downturn in the 
near term.

The central bank board views elevated inflation as its top priority 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/rba-s-hunter-says-inflation-top-priority-may-have-to-raise-rate?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8629>
 and may need to raise interest rates further to tackle it, Assistant Governor 
Sarah Hunter said. “They’ve been pretty clear that they don’t really have 
tolerance for inflation to continue to be” above target for an extended period, 
Hunter told a conference in Sydney.

The RBA has struggled to return inflation to the midpoint of its 2-3% target 
and only expects to do so in early 2028. Westpacnow sees 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/westpac-now-sees-rba-raising-cash-rate-to-4-6-in-november?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8629>
 the central bank raising the cash rate to 4.6% in November, which would be the 
highest level in about 15 years. The bank’s chief economist previously 
predicted a hold decision. The RBA has hiked three times this year after 
cutting in 2025.

HSBC has widened its forecast for a drop in property prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/one-of-the-world-s-longest-housing-booms-is-cracking-in-sydney?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8629>
 to 13% from 8% previously. Consumer confidence is sinking, with a Westpac 
sentiment index dropping toward “deeply pessimistic 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/australia-s-consumer-confidence-slumps-on-rate-fears-fuel-costs?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8629>
” levels. The insolvency of builder Bathla Group hasdeepened concern 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/bathla-s-2-5-billion-property-debt-crisis-faces-fresh-tests?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8629>
 about the health of the nation’s real estate and private credit markets, as 
well as spurred concern more companies could get into financial difficulty.

The situation is likely to get worse before it gets better, and that would 
mean pain for Australians who have some of the highest household debt-to-income 
ratios in the world. But price declines alone won’t increase affordability 
given rising mortgage costs, meaning no one wins.
