Daily Brief

Tuesday, August 25, 2026

US

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's "economic D-Day" against Iran, billed as the "greatest coordinated economic isolation" in history, sanctioned only dozens of small companies Monday, leaving the major Chinese oil buyers and banks untouched.
  • Stanley Druckenmiller, Bessent's former mentor, publicly rebuked the Treasury's long-bond buyback program in a WSJ op-ed, arguing "governments defending prices against fundamentals always lose."
  • S&P 500 futures rose 0.4% to 7,700 and Nasdaq 100 futures gained 0.8%, even as Nvidia extended its losing streak to seven straight sessions, the longest since 2022, ahead of Wednesday's earnings; the 10-year yield eased 2bp to 4.68%.
  • Bitcoin topped $80,000 for the first time since May, up 1.1% on the day.

Fiscal Policy and the Druckenmiller Rebuke

Bessent's next priority after the Iran sanctions is fiscal consolidation, which budget watchers say requires Congressional cooperation unlikely before November's midterms; the House has already advanced $95 billion in unoffset spending for the Iran conflict, with $350 billion more in defense outlays under discussion. Druckenmiller called the 10-year yield "the only fiscal disciplinarian the US has left," adding pressure as Bloomberg Economics warns the buybacks "won't produce a sustained decline" in yields absent a fiscal-trajectory shift.

Markets: Nvidia's Slide, Software's Rebound

Nvidia has fallen seven straight sessions despite consensus estimates for quarterly revenue near $92 billion; its forward P/E is merely in line with the S&P 500, a sign, per Bloomberg's MLIV, that traders doubt earnings growth can keep beating the broader market. Software stocks have rallied since April lows, with all 13 S&P 500 reporters so far beating estimates by an average 10%.

Asia

  • A Bank of Japan gauge of underlying inflation rose 2.3% year-on-year in July, above the 2% target, reinforcing expectations of a September rate hike as firms pass through higher energy costs from the Middle East conflict.
  • The Reserve Bank of Australia judged that three rate increases this year have brought the economy "back into balance," while signaling it remains ready to act again.
  • The US is set to impose a 7.5% overcapacity tariff on Chinese goods ahead of a Trump-Xi summit next month, restoring total duties to 20%, even as Iran sanctions threaten a separate collision with Beijing over its role as top buyer of Iranian crude.
  • Bitcoin topped $80,000 for the first time since May, and Asian refiners are on track to nearly double September purchases of US crude.

China Under Pressure on Two Fronts

Beijing faces simultaneous trade and geopolitical pressure: alongside the overcapacity tariff, the EU's proposed measures could cover roughly 27% of China's annual exports to the bloc, per Goldman Sachs, while dozens of Chinese and Hong Kong firms were hit by Bessent's latest Iran sanctions. China has defended its cooperation with Iran and warned against disruption, and separately framed its uneven 2026 growth as resilience in a rare public defense of the economy.

Markets and Regional Developments

South Korea and the US agreed to maintain coordination on reviving North Korea dialogue after Pyongyang rebuffed Trump's overtures and test-fired short-range missiles; Korea's leveraged ETFs saw a $1 billion outflow as AI fervor cools. Taiwanese prosecutors indicted a senior Nvidia manager over alleged chip smuggling into China.

Europe

  • Germany's second-quarter GDP was revised up to 0.3% quarter-on-quarter growth from a preliminary 0.2%, with net exports the primary driver; a separate survey showed business sentiment brightening for a fourth month, its highest reading since before the Iran war.
  • Volkswagen CEO Oliver Blume presented restructuring plans at the Wolfsburg plant that labor representatives warn could eventually eliminate up to 140,000 jobs, the first of several planned townhalls across Germany.
  • Ireland's fiscal watchdog warned that an €8.5 billion budget package is too large relative to the country's tax windfall, while the Czech Republic moves to loosen public-finance rules for debt-financed investment.
  • UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham plans to lobby President Trump at the UN General Assembly in September for greater access to Patriot air-defense missiles for Ukraine ahead of winter.

German Recovery Gains Traction Amid Corporate Retrenchment

Germany's upgraded GDP print and improving business outlook point to a trade-led recovery gaining traction even as the industrial base restructures; Volkswagen's cuts, alongside Chancellor Friedrich Merz's warning that a foiled drone attack at a German airport signals renewed hybrid-threat exposure, underscore the tension between cyclical improvement and structural and security pressures facing the export economy.

UK: Heat, Housing, and Fiscal Watch

Grosvenor Group, the Duke of Westminster's property firm, warned that rigid planning rules hinder adapting London's Mayfair and Belgravia luxury housing to more frequent extreme heat, with the Met Office citing a materially stronger chance of 40C readings. The UK also reportedly suspended a Thames Water administration plan.

LatAm

  • Mexican government bonds now "trade like junk" following the $130 billion state bailout of oil company Pemex, a marked reversal for a sovereign once considered a fiscal-probity benchmark among emerging markets.
  • Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella ordered the country's first major immigration crackdown, vowing to begin deporting undocumented migrants — a policy that could affect hundreds of thousands of Venezuelans — as part of a broader "Colombia First" security push.
  • Pimco has become the dominant foreign holder of Colombian local debt, having accumulated roughly 41 trillion pesos ($13 billion) in government bonds under former President Gustavo Petro while other investors retreated; the bet is now paying off amid the world's largest currency rally and elevated emerging-market yields following De la Espriella's election.
  • Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum expressed optimism about reaching a trade deal with Washington as her chief negotiator continues talks in the US, even as Canada's parallel trade negotiations have collapsed.

Fiscal Strain in Mexico, Political Realignment in Colombia

Mexico's Pemex bailout has pushed its bonds into junk-like trading territory, a credit-market signal that state-linked fiscal risk is being repriced even as Sheinbaum pursues a bilateral trade accommodation with the Trump administration. In Colombia, De la Espriella's immigration and security crackdown marks a rightward political shift following the Petro era, and coincides with a currency rally and yield compression that has rewarded early foreign buyers of local debt such as Pimco — though the durability of that rally amid a broader regional US-tariff backdrop remains to be seen.

Global

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's "economic D-Day" sanctions campaign against Iran targeted only dozens of small companies, sparing the major Chinese banks and buyers that sustain Iran's oil trade — a rollout that fell well short of its billing as history's "greatest coordinated economic isolation."
  • Oil extended Monday's decline, with WTI crude down 2.6% to $82.80, after the muted sanctions rollout spared Iran's key trading partners from harsher measures for now.
  • Gold traded near its highest level since mid-May, above its 200-day moving average, as a bond-market intervention by Bessent revived the dollar "debasement trade."
  • The US-Canada trade war deepened, with 50% US tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods in place and Trump threatening to double tariffs on Canadian autos; the Canadian dollar tumbled by the most in two months.

Iran Pressure Shifts From Military to Tariff Leverage

With munitions shortages constraining further military action, Washington has pivoted to tariffs as its primary lever against Iran, with the new pressure point being Chinese companies rather than the Strait of Hormuz. A campaign that excludes China — the top buyer of Iranian oil — would be largely ineffective, but targeting Beijing directly risks retaliation and broader damage to trade channels ahead of a planned Trump-Xi summit. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports is, separately, proving effective, with laden tankers stranded in the Persian Gulf and Iranian crude stockpiles in East Asia nearly depleted.

Climate and Trade Frictions Widen

Copernicus recorded the highest global ocean surface temperature ever measured, 21.1C, as climate change compounds a powerful El Niño, while BloombergNEF data show corporate filings referencing extreme heat at record levels. Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore reaffirmed commitments to keep the Strait of Malacca open amid rising trade tensions.
