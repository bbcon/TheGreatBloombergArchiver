Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Europe Oil Team Leader Alaric Nightingale looks at the threat of surging 
shipping costs to refineries around the world.

The soaring cost of transporting oil by sea is becoming a growing risk to the 
availability of fuels such as diesel that are already in short supply.

In its monthly report 
<https://www.iea.org/events/oil-market-report-october-2026> today, the 
International Energy Agency repeatedly flagged the prospect of “economic run 
cuts” at oil refineries outside the US. That’s becauseshipping costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/the-global-oil-tanker-shortage-is-getting-worse-by-the-day?cmpid=BBBXT101426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10074>
 are gnawing away at their margins, even though fuel prices are far higher than 
crude on paper.

The idea of refineries scaling back right now would be an alarming one for 
governments in consumer countries, several of which have been pushing for 
plants to keep running flat-out to maintain supplies.

But freight is becoming its own crisis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-19/world-running-short-of-supertankers-threatens-long-haul-oil-flow?cmpid=BBBXT101426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10074>
, with the Iran war and Tehran’sattacks on shipping 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/iran-ramps-up-ship-attacks-in-hormuz-as-oil-and-gas-flows-climb?cmpid=BBBXT101426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10074>
 in the Strait of Hormuz completely redrawing oil logistics in the Middle East.
Oil tankers and bulk carriers sit anchored around Oman’s Qaboos Port on June 
22, 2026.Photographer: Elke Scholiers/Getty Images Europe
The tanker industry is having to add days to delivery times and demanding a 
huge risk premium to export barrels from the region as Iran maintains a 
relentless pace of strikes.

Even a bypass route from the west coast of Saudi Arabia has become complicated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/saudis-oil-logistics-roiled-again-by-houthis-red-sea-threat?cmpid=BBBXT101426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10074>
 because ships have to sail all the way around Africa to avoid being targeted 
by Yemen’s Houthis.

The net result is that it now costs about $77 million to deliver a 2 
million-barrel cargo of US oil to Asia, according to data from the Baltic 
Exchange in London. That works out at about $38 a barrel, or roughly 40% of a 
barrel of West Texas Intermediate.

That in turn means that booming headline prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/us-diesel-tops-6-50-a-gallon-as-wars-worsen-global-fuels-crunch?cmpid=BBBXT101426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261014&utm_content=10074>
 for fuels aren’t always translating into large profits for refineries, which 
must deduct freight from their calculations. In Europe, the IEA said, some 
plants are experiencing negative margins because of expensive shipping costs.

“This opens the possibility of economic run cuts for a refining system 
operating at its upper limits, despite tight product markets and low product 
stocks,” the agency said.

That’s a prospect the global economy can ill afford.

—Alaric Nightingale, Bloomberg News
