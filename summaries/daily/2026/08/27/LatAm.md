• Colombia's central bank chief warned that policymakers have limited ability to curb the peso's sharp appreciation, highlighting the currency-management dilemma facing commodity-linked economies in the region.
• Five Caribbean nations - Grenada, Dominica, St. Lucia, St. Kitts and Nevis, and Antigua and Barbuda - are bracing for economic damage as the EU threatens to end visa-free access over their sale of "golden passport" citizenship-by-investment programs.

**Brazil's AI-Driven Campaign**
With Lula seen as frontrunner, AI personas are weighing in on grocery prices, crime and the government's record, adopting the language and concerns of the audiences they target. There is no evidence the influencers are coordinated with campaigns, though some of the most visible lean right. The dynamic follows Brazil's earlier $30 million fine against TikTok's owner over data use, underscoring the country's position at the leading edge of social-media-driven political regulation.

**Currency and External Pressures**
Colombia's peso strength illustrates a broader regional currency puzzle, with policymakers largely unable to counter appreciation pressure. Separately, the threatened EU crackdown on Caribbean golden-passport programs would remove a significant revenue source and visa-free travel benefit for five small island economies, a potential shock disproportionate to their size given how central the programs have become to their public finances since 1984.
