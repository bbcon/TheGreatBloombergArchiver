**Energy Markets and the Strait of Hormuz**
The conflict's impact on energy markets is best understood through disruption and dislocation rather than simple supply destruction. Brent's move above $91 reflects renewed fighting, but the more durable profit story is in refining and trading margins, as rerouted cargoes widen regional price spreads. Goldman Sachs identifies diesel as the "epicenter" of the supply squeeze, with global refinery activity at its lowest seasonal level since the pandemic. Shell's doubled quarterly profit despite losing a third of gas production illustrates how energy majors with diversified trading and refining capabilities are capturing dislocation value. Repsol's management expects healthy processing margins into 2027 regardless of Hormuz normalization.

**Geopolitics and Ballistic Missile Proliferation**
The Iran conflict has accelerated a structural shift in global military posture. Tehran has launched approximately 2,000 ballistic missiles since February, eroding interceptor stockpiles across the region and demonstrating the asymmetric cost of offense versus defense. Separately, a Russian cruise missile came down in eastern Poland during a large-scale air attack on Ukraine, raising Article 5 sensitivities. These developments are occurring alongside a broad proliferation of new ballistic missile programs across South Korea, Japan, six European nations, and the United States, with NATO committing $50 billion toward long-range strike capabilities — a structural shift in defense spending with multi-decade fiscal implications.
