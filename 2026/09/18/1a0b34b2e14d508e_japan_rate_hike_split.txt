Deep Dive: What’s the Real AI Threat?

The current round of panic over AI is based on the conviction that the 
technology hasslipped out of human control 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/existential-angst-dominates-debate-over-tech-from-anthropic-openai?cmpid=091826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9053>
 to do its own bidding in several recent incidents.

 * To some disillusioned AI researchers — and senior employees at leading AI 
companies — this is an early sign of a threat to human civilization. But other 
experts say such talk allows companies to sidestep accountability for 
real-world impacts. Critics see AI’s environmental effects, job displacement or 
the potential for mass surveillance asmore urgent targets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-13/global-capitalism-bets-it-all-on-ai-future-that-alarms-voters?cmpid=091826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9053>
 for policymakers.
 * When Jacob Coxon, a former Anthropic and OpenAI employee, accused the 
industry of “gambling with our lives 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/anthropic-worker-quits-over-ai-firms-gambling-with-our-lives?cmpid=091826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9053>
,” Evan Hubinger, a safety researcher at Anthropic, responded by saying the 
company did believe AI might kill every human on earth. He put the chance of 
that happening within the decade at over 10%.
 * AI pioneer Andrew Ng, meanwhile, called warnings about existential risks 
from AI “science fiction,” sayingthey may hinder efforts 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/ai-pioneer-andrew-ng-calls-extinction-fears-science-fiction?cmpid=091826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9053>
 to maximize the technology’s benefits to society.
 * The world was getting a glimpse into a long-running conversation within the 
field. Before there were chatbot subscriptions to sell or IPOs to price, 
researchers understood their work as a high-risk, high-reward proposition for 
civilization. For years, some AI leaders have taken these ideas to extremes, 
saying AI might eventually cure all disease, replace all human labor, kill all 
humans — or all of the above.  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-17/ai-extinction-fears-are-science-fiction-andrew-ng-video?cmpid=091826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9053>
