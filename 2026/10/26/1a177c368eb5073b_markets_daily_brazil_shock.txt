‘Caught by surprise’

Rarely in recent memory have investors been so wrong-footed by a political 
event as they were by Sunday’s presidential election in Latin America’s largest 
economy.

An ETF tracking Brazilian equities is plunging 27% in US premarket trading 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/brazil-markets-brace-for-rout-on-lula-s-improbable-election-win?cmpid=102626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10552>
, while shares of the country’s banks are tanking too: Banco Bradesco is down 
27% and Itau Unibanco is 28% lower. Wealth manager XP is down 39%. Currency 
derivatives point to a more than 10% plunge in the real.

Bolsonaro came in first in the Oct. 4 first round, triggering a rally that 
drew billions of dollars into the country from foreign investors. They were 
betting that the right-wing politician would put in place a much-demanded 
fiscal tightening that would allow for lower interest rates.

Firms like UBS Global Wealth Management, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan Chase and 
Citigroup put out bullish calls on Brazilian assets. The Ibovespa index is up 
10% in October and 27% year to date.

In the end, the 80-year-old Lula came from behind to win, halting a right-wing 
wave sweeping Latin America. Bolsonaro called for the annulment of the result, 
raising the prospect of a prolonged political dispute.

“It’s going to be a tough day. The market will reprice everything,” said Bruno 
Funchal of Bradesco Asset Management. “Everyone was caught by surprise.” —Phil
