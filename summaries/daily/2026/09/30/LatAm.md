**Brazil Labor Market**
August's stronger-than-expected job creation is a meaningful political data point for President Lula with voters heading to the polls imminently. Brazil's labor market resilience stands in contrast to the broader EM pressure environment characterized by elevated oil prices, rising US yields, and a strengthening dollar — headwinds that are weighing more acutely on current-account-deficit economies in the region. The steel investment news from ArcelorMittal, if confirmed, would represent a significant vote of confidence in Brazilian industrial capacity, contingent on commodity price and policy stability.

**Regional Policy and Politics**
Colombia's expected rate hold reflects a central bank navigating between still-elevated inflation and slowing growth, with external pressures from oil price volatility complicating the path to easing. Mexico faces ongoing cartel-related US-Mexico tension following the latest round of Sinaloa sanctions, a dynamic that adds friction to the bilateral trade relationship at a moment when the USMCA review cycle approaches. Across the region, the stronger dollar and elevated energy costs are tightening financial conditions in ways that macro data have not yet fully reflected.
