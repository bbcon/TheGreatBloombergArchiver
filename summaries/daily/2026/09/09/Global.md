**US-Iran Escalation and Energy Markets**
The US military's destruction of five IRGC tankers marks a significant escalation in a conflict now in its seventh month, with Brent crossing $100 for the first time since July. Riyadh simultaneously halted several southern energy facilities after Houthi strikes, while Iran threatened a more aggressive posture toward US forces. Pakistan's defense minister warned that attacks on Saudi Arabia could activate the Mecca Defense Alliance. The cumulative effect — Persian Gulf output disruption, Russian refinery strikes, and a tightening diesel market — is producing a supply shock that goes beyond crude headlines. Bank of England Governor Bailey specifically flagged crack spreads as an inflation indicator, signaling that policymakers are tracking refined product prices as carefully as crude benchmarks.

**Global Capital Flow Realignment**
The potential repatriation of Japanese institutional capital — from both the GPIF's anticipated reallocation and broader "whale" asset manager repositioning — represents a structural shift in global funding. Bessent's concern that Japanese bond market volatility could spill over into US Treasuries reflects how interconnected these flows have become. If Japanese institutions rotate meaningfully into domestic bonds, the rest of the world faces a gradual withdrawal of what has been a reliable source of low-cost external financing.
