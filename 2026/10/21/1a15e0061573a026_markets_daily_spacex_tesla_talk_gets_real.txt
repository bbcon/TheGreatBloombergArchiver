Musk’s mashup dreams

Elon Musk said it’s a “good idea” to combine SpaceX with Tesla, in what would 
be the biggest-ever corporate merger. What’s more, the boards of both companies 
haveformed special committees 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/spacex-profit-beats-estimates-on-big-jump-in-ai-business-revenue?cmpid=102126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10353>
 to consider the idea.

Tesla has a market value of about $1.5 trillion, while SpaceX is valued at $2 
trillion. A merger would create a conglomerate spanning electric vehicles, 
batteries, rockets and satellites. The stated rationale for bringing those very 
different businesses together lies in a shared ambition to leverage AI for data 
centers in space, driverless cars and humanoid robots.

“There’s no question that Musk wants the merger, and he wants to get it done 
sooner rather than later,” said Gene Munster of Deepwater Asset Management.
Photographer: Ethan Swope/Bloomberg
Talk of a potential deal coincides with some serious growth for SpaceX’s AI 
business, which is called Super Intelligence, or SI. Its revenue soared to 
$7.56 billion in the quarter ended September, more than nine times the level 
from a year ago. SpaceX is now a major supplier of computing power to the likes 
of Anthropic.

Ipek Ozkardeskaya of Swissquote said we are now at the point where SpaceX is 
becoming more about AI than rockets. Growth could accelerate further, she 
argues, if SpaceX is able to use its superior financial resources to “swallow 
up” other AI infrastructure businesses that are undergrowing credit stress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/coreweave-stock-wipeout-exposes-divide-between-ai-fear-and-greed?cmpid=102126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10353>
.

“As for whether a merger with Tesla is a good idea, I would say it could be if 
the two companies can prove that the synergies create value for both of them,” 
said Ozkardeskaya. “If Tesla is better off exploring AI synergies with other 
companies, the deal could be rejected by investors.”

Not so fast: While Musk endorses a merger, another billionaire is honing in on 
a key stumbling block 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/ken-griffin-says-ai-almost-certainly-in-a-bubble-right-now?cmpid=102126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10353>
 facing the AI trade. Ken Griffin, whose business empire includes hedge fund 
Citadel, said that US companies have been “very uneven” in their ability to 
extract value from AI models.

“I think all of Corporate America is looking at their bills from Anthropic and 
OpenAI and going, ‘Okay, we need to think about how to make sure we’re getting 
productivity out of this,’” he said on Tuesday.

And are we in an AI bubble? “Almost certainly,” said Griffin. —Charles Riley

Fast money power

The world’s biggest bond market is evolving.

Foreign governments, central banks and pension funds used to dominate the $32 
trillion Treasuries market. Now they are giving way to private investors, 
including hedge funds, who about 70% of federal debt.

The US government could depend on the old guard to hoover up Treasuries, which 
were regarded as risk-free. Price wasn’t so much of a factor. But the fast 
money players of today have a different attitude. Hedge funds, for example, 
have other assets to pick from, and their overriding priority is boosting 
returns.
