Donald Trump has been racking up wins across Latin America.

In Brazil, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva may be about to hand him a singular 
defeat.

The 80-year-old leftist president was heading into a lackluster final 
presidential run until it was reenergized by Trump’s tariffs andaggressive 
political strategy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/brazil-blocks-visas-of-us-envoys-over-election-interference-fear?cmpid=BBD082026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7978>
 to reassert US dominance in the region.

That isn’t Lula’s only advantage 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-20/brazil-election-lula-seen-as-frontrunner-over-flavio-bolsonaro?cmpid=BBD082026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7978>
.

He’s also benefiting from former President Jair Bolsonaro’s decision to pick 
his son Flávio as heir to his right-wing legacy. The younger Bolsonaro lacks 
experience and charisma, and his campaign hasso far struggled 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/bolsonaro-picks-congressman-as-running-mate-in-brazil-election?cmpid=BBD082026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7978>
 to broaden its appeal.
Lula during a campaign rally in São Bernardo do Campo on Sunday.Photographer: 
Maira Erlich/Bloomberg
Lula’s odds of reelection 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/brazil-election-bolsonaro-presses-forward-with-chaotic-presidential-bid?cmpid=BBD082026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7978>
 are more than 60% in betting markets. But that doesn’t mean the race is 
sealed. Brazil remains deeply polarized, and Lula’s poll lead over Bolsonaro is 
narrow.

The president has spent the past four years trying to recapture the magic of 
his first two terms, from 2003 to 2010, when he oversaw an economic boom, 
lifted millions out of poverty and left office with approval ratings above 80%.

This time it’s been harder.

Even with unemployment low and inflation under control, Lula has struggled to 
convince Brazilians he has aneffective approach to security 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-01-26/brazil-s-lula-struggles-to-convince-voters-he-s-tough-on-crime?cmpid=BBD082026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7978>
, their biggest concern.

Speculation swirls over what kind of economic face he’d show: the surprise 
Wall Street darling of the 2000s, or a more radical, free-spending version.

Lula, who endured hunger as a child and overcame social barriers on his path 
to the top, has made a career out of defying expectations andsetting records 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-10-31/lula-bolsonaro-and-the-story-of-a-historic-comeback-in-brazil?cmpid=BBD082026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7978>
. If he pulls this off, he’d become Brazil’s longest-serving president of the 
democratic era.

This week, he launched his final campaign by returning to the city where he 
rose to prominence as a union leader. There, he read a letter addressed to his 
younger self.

“It won’t be easy but let me tell you one thing,” he said. “Everything will be
