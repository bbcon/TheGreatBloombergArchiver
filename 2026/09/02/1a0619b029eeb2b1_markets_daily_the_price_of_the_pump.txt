It’s all about oil

A lot of the action in markets can be explained by the economic cost of the 
Iran war.

As Brent crude jumps to $95 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-2?cmpid=090226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8432>
 a barrel, bringing inflation and interest-rate hikes back to the forefront, 
the result has been a selloff across stocks, bonds and gold at the start of the 
month.

It’s a cycle that’s repeated over and over again this year — conflict flaring, 
oil spiking, markets dropping, and then the US backing off enough to lower 
energy prices and send markets back up.

The latest twist on that pattern seems to be US efforts to expand Venezuelan 
crude production. But whether it’ll work is another question.

Traders now put the odds of a rate hike this month at more than 50% for three 
major central banks, including around 70% for the Federal Reserve. Critically 
for Americans, gasoline prices have stayed stubbornly above $4 a gallon.

“The major driver of the market action remains the rising oil prices,” writes 
Ipek Ozkardeskaya of Swissquote.

Yes, there are other factors driving markets. Fed Chairmen Kevin Warsh 
surprised investors with a hawkish speech at Jackson Hole, and there’s renewed 
worry about government debt in Japan, the UK and France.

At a handful of financial firms, the mood has also shifted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/wells-fargo-analysts-join-jpmorgan-traders-in-turning-cautious-on-us-stocks?cmpid=090226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8432>
. Wells Fargo has warned of “broad cautiousness” as investors grow anxious 
about the sustainability of the AI-investment boom. At Citadel Securities, 
Scott Rubner voiced concern about the path for equities over the next several 
weeks.

Like the analysts at Wells Fargo, Rubner isn’t bearish. But he is looking for 
a reset in markets and sees room for long-term gains.

“The upside catalysts are becoming less obvious just as the downside catalysts 
are becoming more numerous,” he wrote. “I would use strength to reduce some 
exposure and add inexpensive protection.”—Lynn Thomasson
