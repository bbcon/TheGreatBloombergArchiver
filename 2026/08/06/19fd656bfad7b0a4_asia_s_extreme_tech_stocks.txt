It’s hard to keep up with moves in Asian tech stocks. Price swings in the past 
100 days on MSCI’s Asian Pacific tech index are the most extreme since 2009 as 
an investor frenzy in all things AI turned south.

AI-related shares were the focus of the market again on Thursday, with the 
gauge sinking 3.2% after rallying 4.2% the day earlier. SK Hynix and Samsung 
led a 4.6% retreat in Korea’s Kospi, takingits decline 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/sk-hynix-hit-by-pre-market-flash-crash-on-nextrade-bourse-again?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7433>
 since its June peak to 31%.

The latest drop 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7433>
 came after Sandisk’s revenue forecast for the current quarter trailed the 
average estimate, prompting its shares to slump. Western Digital slid after its 
results fell short of expectations.

Meanwhile, investors hanging onto SpaceX shares after a plunge below their IPO 
price are bracing for thenext potential hit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/spacex-s-101-billion-unlock-heaps-pressure-on-battered-shares?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7433>
, when $101 billion worth of stock becomes available for trading on Thursday.

Despite AI proclaimed as the future, stomach-churning volatility is likely to 
undermine the investment case for such stocks.

—Richard Frost
