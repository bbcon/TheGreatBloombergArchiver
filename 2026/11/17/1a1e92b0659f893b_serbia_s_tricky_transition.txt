Thousands celebrated Aleksandar Vučić’s ouster 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/serbian-long-time-leader-vucic-concedes-defeat-in-snap-vote?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 last month with street parties and dancing after Serbia’s long-serving leader 
conceded electoral defeat.

The confetti and firecrackers are gone, but not the questions over the Balkan 
country’s direction 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/serbian-politics-enter-new-era-with-faceless-group-taking-power?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 now that aleaderless political movement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/all-about-the-serbian-student-list-movement-that-ousted-leader-vucic?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 known as Student List won a majority due to widespread public discontent with 
the government.

As the new parliament convenes in Belgrade today, the election winners have 
yet to name their pick for prime minister, let alone a cabinet lineup.

Foreign policy priorities are taking shape as members of the new elite hold 
meetings with US, European Union and Russian diplomats.

Seismic changes have started at home with detentions of Vučić-era officials 
accused of abuse of office, corruption and fraud, echoing thecleanup in 
neighboring Hungary 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/hungary-finds-20-million-cash-stash-in-probe-on-orban-minister?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 after Viktor Orbán’s loss.
A Student List celebration in Belgrade on Oct. 26.Photographer: Oliver 
Bunic/Bloomberg
The drive for justice, making good on election pledges to fight graft and 
reverse democratic backsliding 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/serbia-s-leaderless-force-starts-dismantling-vucic-s-regime?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 under Vučić, is pushed by prosecutors emboldened to take on cases they 
wouldn’t touch before.

Serbia’s spy chief and justice minister face probes over election 
irregularities, while a media watchdog is penalizing outlets still loyal to the 
strongman who dominated the country for more than a decade.

The transition is bound to remain rocky 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/serbia-surprises-with-rate-hike-as-political-shift-weighs-on-dinar?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 for some time, with many challenges ahead.

Local lender Alta Banka, popular with state companies and Russian expatriates, 
has collapsed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/serbia-police-arrest-macura-owner-of-alta-banka-n1-reports?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
. The central bank has tapped into its currency reserves to stabilize the dinar.

The winners have pledged to favor domestic investors, with a swipe at China’s 
Zijin Mining operations suggestinga  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2024-05-06/china-serbia-and-hungary-s-embrace-of-president-xi-opens-doors-in-europe?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
break with 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2024-05-06/china-serbia-and-hungary-s-embrace-of-president-xi-opens-doors-in-europe?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
Vučić’s policy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2024-05-06/china-serbia-and-hungary-s-embrace-of-president-xi-opens-doors-in-europe?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
.

Next up is a presidential race. Vučić stepped down as head of state in 
September in a failed gamble to return as government leader.

Lawmakers will today pick a speaker who will set a date for the vote that may 
cement the historic shift. — Misha Savic

Hungarian investigators found large amounts of cash stashed in forests, barns 
and warehouses around the country, piling pressure on an opposition dogged by
allegations of corruption 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/hungary-finds-20-million-cash-stash-in-probe-on-orban-minister?cmpid=BBD111726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11498>
 implicating allies of Orbán. The discoveries totaling almost $20 million 
relate to a case involving a former national development minister accused of 
taking bribes. Prime Minister Péter Magyar, who won April elections by a 
landslide, has vowed to crack down on graft that became entrenched during his 
predecessor’s many years in power.
