More than a science experiment

By Will Wade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQKkcaEIb6M/will-wade?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>
 andMichelle Ma 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWTYlfwjEUk/michelle-ma?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>

About 10 minutes south of the Oakland San Francisco Bay Airport, scientists 
are preparing to create tiny stars, right here on Earth, in hopes of powering 
the world.

Inside an unassuming office park, Pacific Fusion Corp. 
<https://www.pacificfusion.com/> is assembling a pulser module, a tubelike 
device roughly the length of a city bus, studded with hundreds of 
phone-book-size capacitors that store electricity. The futuristic-looking 
mechanism, plus 155 more just like it, will be installed at the$1 billion 
demonstration site 
<https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2025/09/26/governor-announces-1-billion-fusion-research-and-manufacturing-campus-in-new-mexico-pacific-fusion-project-will-create-hundreds-of-jobs/>
 that Pacific Fusion just started building in Albuquerque, New Mexico. When the 
system is up and running in 2030, the capacitors will be charged with 
electricity and then discharged, creating a powerful magnetic field that will 
compress a pellet the size of a pencil eraser filled with deuterium and 
tritium, two hydrogen isotopes. This process should create conditions similar 
to those of the sun for a fraction of a second as the atoms fuse to form 
helium. In short, they’ll create energy — and perhaps prove in the process that 
fusion is more than just avery expensive science experiment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-19/nuclear-fusion-explained-why-it-remains-elusive-even-as-investors-pile-in?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>
.


Pacific Fusion will need more than 150 pulser modules.Photographer: Poppy 
Lynch/Bloomberg
Researchers have been studying fusion as a potential power source since the 
1950s <https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0029-5515/50/1/014004>. With 
stars, immense gravity and extreme heat force small atoms to combine into 
larger ones, releasing energy; scientists have long imagined the same process 
someday fueling our homes and machines. But replicating the conditions within a 
star is pretty hard. Until recently, the energy needed to set off a fusion 
reaction was greater than the energy it produced.

Then, in late 2022, at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, east of San 
Francisco, researchers used the world’s biggest laser to deliver about 2.05 
megajoules of energy to a target,triggering a fusion reaction 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-12-12/nuclear-fusion-energy-breakthrough-made-by-scientists-in-us?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>
 that released 3.15 megajoules. The test made worldwide news, boosting 
excitement for the dozens of global companies that had been laboring to bring 
fusion systems to market. Until that breakthrough, there had always been 
skeptics and doubters, even a long-standing joke in the science community: 
“Fusion is 20 years away, and it always will be.” Now there’s growing 
confidence that fusion might be viable as a power source — and possibly at a 
cost that doesn’t put it out of reach.

“Demonstrating fusion is just a small step to building a commercial power 
plant,” says Greg Twinney, chief executive officer of Canada’sGeneral Fusion 
Group Ltd. <https://generalfusion.com/>, which operates a large-scale demo 
system outside Vancouver. “It’s been proven in labs. Now it has to be proven 
that it can be commercialized.”

The payoff would be huge: Fusion offers the potential of abundant clean power. 
Compared withfission 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-08/don-t-say-nuclear-commonwealth-fusion-ceo-seeks-to-rebrand?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>
, the nuclear process that generates energy by splitting large atoms, fusion is 
expected to produce much less waste and present lower radiation risk to 
workers. And if it works on a big enough scale, it would operate around the 
clock in any weather, unlike wind and solar. Investors have already bet more 
than $14 billion on the emerging technology, with arecord $4.5 billion of that 
coming in the last year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/global-investment-in-fusion-companies-surges-69-to-4-5-billion?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>
. Startups are piling in: The Fusion Industry Association, a trade group, is
tracking more than 50 companies worldwide 
<https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-Global-Fusion-Industry-Report.pdf>
 that are seeking to develop power plants based on all of this very complicated 
physics and engineering.

UK’s food price shocks

By Tom Rees 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWI_BZS_L70/tom-rees?cmpid=BBD090926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8681>

A string of shocks from El Niño, summer droughts and war in Iran will push UK 
food inflation above 6% next year, according to forecasts by the country’s food 
producers.

The Food and Drink Federation warned that food inflation will hit almost 4% by 
Christmas, more than double the current rate, before accelerating to a peak of 
6.4% in July 2027. It would be the highest annual increase since early 2024 
when grocery costs were still being affected by the energy shock from Russia’s 
invasion of Ukraine two years earlier.

The UK trade body for food and drink manufacturers said a £100 ($135) weekly 
shop in 2020 would cost almost £150 by next summer should prices rise as fast 
as it forecasts, intensifying cost-of-living pressures that are already top of 
Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s agenda. He has signaled that more help for 
households and businesses is on its way but is facing a deteriorating fiscal 
backdrop ahead of the autumn budget.

Explainer: Why the World Is Bracing for a Rare ‘Super’ El Niño

“The persistently higher costs of energy, logistics and packaging, compounded 
by this summer’s extreme heat, mean that food prices will rise this year, and 
we believe that rise will be sustained into 2027,” said Karen Betts, chief 
executive at the Food and Drink Federation.
