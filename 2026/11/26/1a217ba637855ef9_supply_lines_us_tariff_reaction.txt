<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/trump-s-china-tariffs-raise-threat-of-new-test-for-trade-truce?cmpid=BBD112626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11743>
 from Beijing is that the government is unlikely to retaliate against the US’s 
newly proposed tariffs.

“While not happy, Beijing seems prepared to live with the new rates provided 
that they don’t exceed 20%,” said Wendy Cutler, senior vice president with the 
Asia Society Policy Institute.

But the possibility of higher American levies on Chinese imports adds another 
layer of tension ahead of President Donald Trump’s talks with Xi Jinping at 
next month’s Group of 20 summit.

The other targets of Trump’s latest tariff threats aren’t thrilled either. 
According toreporting today 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/us-partners-respond-to-tariffs-with-anger-protest-and-diplomacy?cmpid=BBD112626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11743>
 from my colleagues Philip J. Heijmans and Jon Herskovitz, major US trading 
partners responded with disappointment while holding out the prospect of 
negotiations.

Officials from Singapore to Japan are signaling a preference for negotiation 
over retaliation. China and the European Union signaled they could hit back if 
Washington pushes beyond the terms of existing trade deals.

Bernd Lange, chair of the European Parliament’s international trade committee, 
called the US’sallegations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/us-targets-china-eu-with-new-tariffs-after-overcapacity-probe?cmpid=BBD112626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11743>
 of industrial overcapacity in the 27-nation bloc “absurd.”

‘Act Swiftly’

“We’ll now see whether the announced US tariffs comply with Turnberry in 
practice,” heposted on X <https://x.com/berndlange/status/2125613332111688055>, 
referring to the transatlantic trade dealsealed last year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-27/eu-us-clinch-deal-to-avoid-trump-tariff-hike-ahead-of-deadline?cmpid=BBD112626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11743>
 in Scotland. “If Washington breaches its commitments, the EU must act swiftly. 
It has the legal means to do so.”

Economists responded with little changes to their short-term inflation or 
growth outlooks, though some see American tariffs sticking around over the 
longer term despite public opposition to them.

“US protectionism is becoming increasingly difficult to unwind,” Inga Fechner, 
a senior economist for global trade with ING, wrote in a research note. Even 
though Democrats will be in the majority in Congress starting in January, “we 
don’t think the blue wave will mark a return to free trade,” she said.

Others viewed the inflationary impact of tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/thanksgiving-dinner-costs-more-this-year-with-grocery-prices-rising?cmpid=BBD112626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11743>
 as underappreciated.

Pressure Builds

“How much of the tariff cost has already passed through to consumer prices is 
up for debate, but in our view, the full effect has yet to show up in the 
consumer inflation data,” RBC’s Mike Reid, Carrie Freestone and Imri Haggin 
wrote in a note. “But the pressure in supply chains is building.”

Explainer: What Effect Do Trump’s Tariffs Have on the Economy? 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/trump-s-new-tariffs-how-are-import-taxes-affecting-the-economy?cmpid=BBD112626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11743>

Olu Sonola, head of US economics with Fitch Rating, said the proposed changes 
could “take the effective tariff rate from around 7% in September back toward 
double digits, closer to where it stood before the Supreme Court’s IEEPA 
ruling.”

Where it ultimately ends up hinges on the US-China truce holding up and where 
talks with other trading partners wind up, he said.
