Tech bulls take a hit

The tech selloff is a reminder of the dangers in chasing the herd. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/ai-scare-threatens-fund-flows-beating-rest-of-market-13-to-1?cmpid=091526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8889>

With Nasdaq futures sliding again today, it’s a good chance to look at how 
concentrated tech bets have become this year. Since the S&P 500 bottomed in 
late March, investors have put $52 billion into tech ETFs, compared with just 
$4 billion into the rest of the market, data compiled by Baird Strategas show.

In other words, $13 has gone into IT for every $1 invested into the other 
industries.

That lopsided balance hurt yesterday, when tech stocks fell on concern that AI 
companies may rein in the pace of development out of safety concerns.

Technology megacaps have propelled the stock market’s advance this year, 
thanks to booming spending on semiconductors and the other picks and shovels of 
the AI craze. Investors have been chasing the rally, pouring almost $13 billion 
last month into an Invesco ETF that tracks the Nasdaq 100.

“If AI CEOs slow development, it will raise questions about the possibility of 
a slowdown in spending,” said Matt Maley at Miller Tabak & Co. “Just look at 
the chip stocks. Even though they reported great earnings over the summer, the 
SOX Semiconductors Index is still more than 20% below its June highs.” —

CFOs do the math

For a quick temperature check on where finance mavens see interest rates 
heading, look at the junk bond market: Borrowers there are lining upat least 
$13 billion in refinancing deals 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/junk-firms-are-refinancing-debt-early-on-fear-costs-will-rise?cmpid=091526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8889>
, in many cases earlier than they normally would, to get ahead of rate hikes.

Authentic Brands, which owns brands such as Guess and Dockers, is selling $4.2 
billion of fresh debt, whileMister Car Wash is moving ahead with a $2 billion 
deal. Europe’s high-yield market is set for its busiest week since June.

They’re reacting to expectations for central banks to embark on a round of 
monetary tightening to combat elevated inflation.Many analysts now predict the 
Fed will raise rates this week, while traders are pricing infour hikes from the 
ECB 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/traders-are-at-odds-with-ecb-and-boe-on-how-far-rates-will-rise?cmpid=091526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8889>
 and Bank of England by the end of 2027.

“CFOs are doing the maths to work out if it is better to refinance now — 
possibly with a slightly higher coupon — or to wait and risk an increased 
market rate,” said Jeremy Duffy at law firm Cahill Gordon & Reindel. “Borrowers 
are getting in front of things to avoid a rate environment that could get 
worse.” —Ronan Martin
