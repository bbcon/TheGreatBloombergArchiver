**Earnings and Corporate**
Amazon's AWS segment extended its run of accelerating cloud growth to five consecutive quarters, lifting shares 12% in early trading and reinforcing the view that enterprise AI spending remains intact despite broader market turbulence. Apple offered a contrasting signal: the company warned of chip supply shortages and guided revenue below consensus, with Tim Cook attributing pressure to memory costs — a reminder that AI hardware demand is creating upstream supply dislocations that even the world's largest consumer technology company cannot fully absorb.

**Markets and Rates**
The week's most consequential market development in fixed income was the move in 30-year Treasuries to their highest yield since 2007, a signal that long-duration investors are questioning whether the Federal Reserve under Chairman Kevin Warsh is sufficiently committed to containing inflation. Warsh's press conference on Wednesday reportedly included a suggestion that the Fed may reassess its inflation assessment framework — language markets interpreted as ambiguous rather than hawkish. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq recovered ground as leveraged unwinds in AI trades appeared to stabilize, though the week's volatility — encompassing the collapse of hedge fund Situational Awareness — underscored how concentrated and fragile positioning had become.
