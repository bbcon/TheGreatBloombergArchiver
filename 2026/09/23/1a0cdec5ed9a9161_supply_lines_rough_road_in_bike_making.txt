Trump Wants Goods Made in the USA. His Tariffs Make That Tough for Bike Makers
Trump’s tariffs are meant to reshore production. Bike-making shows it’s more

High-end bicycles may be too niche for US President Donald Trump and China’s 
Xi Jinping to discuss when they meet this week to consider meaty topics like AI 
and regional security. But a close look at the industry reveals the difficulty 
that tariffs on Chinese inputs pose for American bike makers — and the complex 
role China plays in global manufacturing.

In Wednesday’s Big Take 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-us-china-bike-tariffs/?cmpid=BBD092326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9117>
, Bloomberg News teamed up with Bloomberg Economics to tell the tale of two 
companies: a relatively new Icelandic brand with an assembly plant in Virginia, 
and a Chinese factory behemoth that’s been making bicycles for the most famous 
western brands for decades.

The US abandoned bike manufacturing long ago. Taiwan initially served as a key 
hub for frame manufacturing, but China has taken market share over the past two 
decades. Trump’s tariffs on China in his first term further altered global 
production, with Southeast Asian countries benefiting.

Related: Trump Pageantry for Xi Will Mask Feud Over US-China AI Race 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/trump-pageantry-for-xi-will-mask-feud-over-us-china-ai-race?cmpid=BBD092326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9117>

In recent years, a few intrepid producers have made the leap back into US bike 
manufacturing. One of these isLauf, the Icelandic brand famous for the 
suspension fork on its gravel bike, built in Virginia with frames, forks and 
handlebars imported from China.

Lauf was hit hard on “Liberation Day” in April 2025, when tariffs on those 
components jumped above 170%. Today, the rate for some parts can approach 40% 
according to Flexport’stariff simulator 
<https://tariffs.flexport.com/?htsCode=8714.91.20.00&name=valued-over-600-each&entryDate=2026-09-23&country=CN&value=10000&advanced=true&modeOfTransport=OCEAN&FIELD_DATE_OF_LOADING=%222026-09-23%22&FIELD_CHOSEN_HTS_CODES=%7B%2299030591%22%3Afalse%7D&FIELD_CHOSEN_SPIS=%7B%7D>
. When you factor in American labor costs and overhead, margins evaporate.

It also reflects a broader concern from American manufacturers: Right now, 
many imported parts and materials that feed US manufacturers — especially when 
the parts come from China — face higher tariffs than finished products brought 
in from abroad.

Tariff Inversion

This pattern is called “tariff inversion” and it is getting worse as overseas 
manufacturers move finished good production to Southeast Asia, Mexico and other 
countries that face relatively lower tariffs compared to China.

Meanwhile, like in other industries, Chinese companies like XDS — one of the 
biggest bicycle manufacturers in the world and longtime contract builder for 
major western brands — are now producing their own branded performance bikes.

And despite tariffs exceeding 40%, XDS’s X-LAB models are priced very 
competitively, enabled by the increasing share of them that are built in the 
company’s facilities in Cambodia, which enjoys a much lower duty on exports to 
the US.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/xi-unlikely-to-bring-ceos-to-summit-where-trump-is-seeking-deals?cmpid=BBD092326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9117>

Adding to the story is how these bikes are marketed and sold in the US. Lauf, 
in an effort to compete against bigger names, has pursued a direct-to-consumer 
model, bypassing bike shops.

Inside the Lauf Cycles plant in Harrisonburg, Virginia, housed inside a former 
newspaper printing hall, a half-dozen mechanics hover over ultra-light carbon 
fiber frames, threading chains through drivetrains, torquing down bolts and 
wrapping handlebars in tape.

“This is one-of-a-kind here. We were so happy that they came here,” said bike 
mechanic Lucy Dunaway, as she wrapped finishing tape around the handlebars of 
Lauf’s signature Seigla gravel bike.

‘Love of Bikes’

Before joining Lauf part time as a box folder, Dunaway worked as a cross-fit 
coach and gym manager. She said she appreciates the stability and insurance 
that her job at Lauf provides. “And the love of bikes. Actually, that was the 
main reason,” she said.

X-LAB, on the other hand, has gone the traditional route, offering struggling 
bike shops good margins — and higher sales by volume than more expensive, 
legacy brands.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-09-23/us-trade-policy-on-china-has-failed-by-its-own-standards?cmpid=BBD092326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9117>

In addition to drumming up name recognition with their sponsorship of the the 
XDS Astana WorldTour team in the Tour de France, X-LAB has reached new 
customers by selling through local bike shops.

“Retailers are doubling down on these brands, placing orders, consumers are 
purchasing. It’s like they’re the talk of the town right now for retailers,” 
said Heather Brown, executive director of the National Bicycle Dealers 
Association.
“It’s hard to put the two bikes side by side, and a consumer to decide to 
spend a couple thousand more for a bike that’s the same quality.”
