Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Petra Sorge looks at how the rising cost of energy contributed to a 
far-right poll win in Germany.

When Germany’s far-right party scored its best-ever result 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/merz-moves-to-contain-fallout-from-far-right-rout-in-state-vote?cmpid=BBBXT090726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8614>
 in a state election on Sunday, high energy prices played a part in the victory.

While strident anti-immigration policies are Alternative for Germany’s 
defining characteristic, the rising cost of fuel was also a big issue in the 
campaign in Saxony-Anhalt — the poorest of Germany’s 16 states.

Europe’s largest economy has been hobbled by the soaring price of energy since 
the halt of most supplies of Russian natural gas following Moscow’s full-scale 
invasion of Ukraine in 2022. In May, the averageimport price 
<https://www.destatis.de/EN/Press/2026/06/PE26_226_614.html> of the fuel was 
roughly double the level seen at the start of 2021.

The situation has been exacerbated this year by the war in the Middle East, 
with voters seeing the cost of gasoline and diesel climbing almost 40% since 
the Strait of Hormuz was closed.

Saxony-Anhalt is home to large industries that are particularly vulnerable to 
high prices for gas and power. US company Dow Inc. plans to close two 
energy-intensive chemical factories in the state by the end of 2027,citing  
<https://corporate.dow.com/en-us/news/press-releases/dow-will-shut-down-three-upstream-european-assets-in-response-to.html>
“an ongoing challenging cost and demand landscape.”

Germany’s largest ammonia producer, SKW Piesteritz, has also warned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-10/german-chemical-makers-say-carbon-costs-damage-europe-s-edge?cmpid=BBBXT090726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8614>
 that Europe’s climate regulations, particularly the high cost of carbon 
emissions, pose a bigger threat to the industry’s survival than gas prices.
Alice Weidel, co-chair of Alternative for Germany, at a news conference in 
Berlin.Photographer: Krisztian Bocsi/Bloomberg
The AfD has seized on this like no other party. Speaking in Berlin on Monday, 
its co-chair Alice Weidel said the government has “a disastrous energy policy” 
leading to some of the highest prices in the world and “breaking the nation’s 
backbone.”

The party refuses to acknowledge manmade causes of climate change and is 
copying the policy playbook of US President Donald Trump. AfD officials want to 
exit the Paris Climate Agreement, boost fossil fuels and extend the nation’s 
coal production well beyond a 2038 phase-out date.

They also propose ending sanctions against Russia so that gas imports can flow 
again through the idle Nord Stream pipelines.

A state election doesn’t give the AfD the power to implement such sweeping 
changes. However, its victory is just one part of a broader far-right 
ascendancy across Europe that is changing the direction of the continent’s 
politics.

—Petra Sorge, Bloomberg News

Europe is facing an animal-feed crunch 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/europe-s-seared-pastures-spark-animal-feed-crunch-as-prices-soar?cmpid=BBBXT090726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260907&utm_content=8614>
, with prices of grains such as corn soaring as scorched fields force farmers 
to burn through winter stockpiles. That’s upended the normal rhythm of 
livestock farming, where cattle spend the warmer months grazing outside, 
allowing farmers to build up hay and silage stocks for winter, when little or 
no fresh grass is available. The squeeze is also curbing milk output from 
stressed animals, with the impact set to reverberate further down the food 
chain to processors and consumers.

BNEF today

Asset financing for circular economy and bioplastics projects totaled $3.5 
billion from January to August, on track for the lowest total since 2020, 
according to BloombergNEF. Substitute bioplastics accounted for the largest 
share of first-half investment as producers announced plants in China, India 
and the US. Plastic recycling made up only 6% of tracked deals, a sharp decline 
from previous years.
