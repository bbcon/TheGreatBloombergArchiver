Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, New 
Delhi-based oil reporter Rakesh Sharma looks at the repercussions of shifting 
US energy policy for India.

For the world’s third-largest oil importer, buying barrels was once boringly 
predictable. During the past two years, it has been anything but, as India 
contorts itself to balance supply, price and a fickle White House.

That juggling act has just become even harder.

Indian refiners, once heavily dependent on the Middle East, reworked supply 
chains to snap up cheap Russian crude after the invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Then last year, the Trump administration publicly pressured New Delhi to cut 
back those purchases 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-15/india-officials-say-russia-oil-imports-to-drop-on-tighter-checks?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
, an effort to resolve the war in Ukraine and its own trade headache.

All that was thrown out when the US began strikes on Iran in February, forcing 
Washington torelax restrictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-16/russian-oil-price-in-india-hits-record-as-demand-for-urals-grows?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
 in order to maintain the flow of supplies and keep a lid on price gains.

Now, the US has changed course again. Under legislation approved 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/us-senate-passes-russia-sanctions-bill-championed-by-graham?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
 on Wednesday, President Donald Trump can impose tariffs of as much as 100% on 
major buyers of Russian oil, including India.
President Donald Trump has been keeping Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 
his toes with rapid shifts in energy policy.Photographer: Francis 
Chung/Politico/Bloomberg
India needs the US, its biggest export market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/india-links-exporters-with-us-buyers-in-500-billion-trade-push?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
 and, increasingly, an important energy supplier. At the same time, New Delhi 
can’t fold — both for domestic political reasons and because of the strategic 
autonomy it has long defended.

In a forthright statement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/india-warns-us-oil-sanctions-bill-could-hurt-bilateral-ties?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
 yesterday, the government said it was “firmly committed to ensuring energy 
security” for its population, warning the US that there would be implications 
for the bilateral relationship.

Translated, this means an interim trade pact 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/india-says-us-trade-deal-hinges-on-preferential-tariff-rate?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
 risks becoming void if punitive levies are applied — a development which 
serves neither side.

India has two points of leverage. One is fuel exports. The country ships more 
than 1 million barrels a day of oil products overseas. If kept at home, that 
could worsen a global crunch.

The more important one is scale. The current market squeeze is less severe for 
crude than for oil products, yet if India has to search for non-Russian barrels 
for more than 40% of its purchases — the amount Moscow’s deliveries currently 
cover — prices will rise.

That’s a worry for India’s government 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/modi-putin-meeting-how-trump-tariffs-test-india-s-russia-ties?cmpid=BBBXT091826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9055>
, which shields households, but it’s also a problem for US voters, just ahead 
of midterm elections.

That may give Trump pause, at least for now.

—Rakesh Sharma, Bloomberg News
