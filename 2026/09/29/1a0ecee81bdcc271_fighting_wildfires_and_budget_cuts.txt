Asia’s wildfire tinderbox

By Eddie Spence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJMUap-1RE/eddie-spence?cmpid=BBD092926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9453>
,Kanupriya Kapoor 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AW2mI31p98g/kanupriya-kapoor?cmpid=BBD092926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9453>
,Mary Hui 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-indonesia-wildfires-el-nino/%3Ca%20href=%22https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXDwzJNHmiM/mary-hui%22%20target=%22_blank%22%20rel=%22noopener%20noreferrer%22%3Ehttps://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXDwzJNHmiM/mary-hui%3C/a%3E?cmpid=BBD092926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9453>
 andChristopher Udemans 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWacdKsugoY/christopher-udemans?cmpid=BBD092926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9453>

On the edge of one of the largest cities in Indonesian Borneo, 
administrator-turned-firefighter Heru Hartarto surveys the hectares of scorched 
peat before him, a desolate, slate-colored landscape punctuated with burned 
tree trunks. Acrid smoke rises from the ground, creating a dense, low blanket 
of fog.

It’s a scene that Hartarto, 47, and his team of first responders have seen 
repeatedly since a blaze ripped through the thick forest outside Palangkaraya 
in the region of Central Kalimantan earlier this month. They’re back to soak 
the dry, rich and highly flammable soil here with yet more water, hoping to 
prevent it from igniting yet again.
Indonesia Police extinguish forest and land fires in Banjar, South Kalimantan, 
Indonesia, on Tuesday, Sept. 21, 2026.Photographer: Dimas Ardian/Bloomberg
“We’re exhausted. Many new fires are appearing,” said Hartarto, as his fire 
truck hummed in the background. “The impact is worse than what I could have 
imagined.”

Uncontrolled blazes stemming from the use of slash-and-burn techniques to 
clear farmland have long been a feature of Indonesia’s dry months. But recent 
decades have seen particularly severe burning seasons, and a blockbuster El 
Niño weather pattern in 2026 is threatening to break records. At particular 
risk is forested Borneo, where farming, development and a changing climate have 
dried out bogs and created tinderbox conditions.

The resulting haze is choking citizens well beyond Indonesia’s borders, 
straining relations with neighbors, as cities from Manila to Kuala Lumpur and 
Singapore are blanketed in toxic smog.

Jakarta has largely banned the use of fire to clear land and is urging farmers 
to use buffer zones; President Prabowo Subianto has called for tougher legal 
penalties. But the government has also made dramatic cuts to funds available 
for officials handling disasters and first response, taking the budget for the 
National Disaster Mitigation Agency to just 491 billion rupiah ($27.4 million) 
– a tenth of the 2024 amount. That cash has been redirected to Prabowo’s 
priority areas, which include a costly free meal plan and defense spending.
Volunteer firefighters extinguish forest and land fires in Palangkaraya, 
Central Kalimantan,on Wednesday, Sept. 23, 2026.Photographer: Dimas 
Ardian/Bloomberg
Indonesia’s Government Communication Agency, answering questions from 
Bloomberg, said emergency fire-response funding had not been reduced, as “the 
spending adjustments concern routine costs such as official travel and meeting 
refreshments,” and not operational needs.

Still, the number of fire hotspots across the country in August was at the 
highest in more thana decade 
<https://sipongi.gakkum.kehutanan.go.id/grafik-titik-panas>, and September 
looks set to trail only 2015 over that period. With rains unlikely to settle in 
until the last months of the year, relief is likely weeks away.

“The massive scale of today’s fires is the legacy of 50 years of forest 
degradation, deforestation, and peatland conversion,” said David Gaveau, an 
environmental scientist and founder of conservation group TheTreeMap, which 
focuses on tropical forests. Drought magnifies those issues, so that “fires 
that might otherwise remain contained spread out of control, and burn far 
larger areas than originally intended.”

It has been a brutal wildfire season globally, with both Europe and North 
America grappling with severe summer blazes as temperatures rise and droughts 
create plenty of fuel.

In Indonesia, however, man-made causes – and poor management – still explain 
much of the burning, according to environmentalists including Gaveau. More than 
160 people and 95 companies are being investigated for suspicion of involvement 
in the fires,the government 
<https://mediahub.polri.go.id/in/polda/%5Bpolda_name%5D/image/detail/555387-polri-tangani-219-perkara-karhutla-162-tersangka-telah-ditetapkan>
 said this month.

Solar surprise

By Rob Verdonck 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/APiCmhJJqPs/rob-verdonck?cmpid=BBD092926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9453>

Australian households — world leaders in solar panel installations — are 
adding new units at the fastest pace in five years, defying forecasts for a 
slowdown as the grid pivots at pace toward renewables.
Solar panels are installed at a residential property in Sydney, Australia in 
2021.Photographer: Brendon Thorne/Bloomberg
The number of rooftop photovoltaic units nationwide rose 35% in the first half 
of 2026 from a year earlier, the Clean Energy Council, an industry body, said 
in areport 
<https://cleanenergycouncil.org.au/news-resources/australians-backing-rooftop-solar,-home-batteries-in-record-numbers>
 on Tuesday. That’s the highest for a six-month period since 2021. For the full 
year, installations are set to rise back above 300,000, countering the 
council’s ownoutlook 
<https://cleanenergycouncil.org.au/news-resources/record-home-battery-surge-charges-up-australia-s-biggest-power-station-on-aussie-rooftops>
 six months ago that they had neared a “critical mass.”

Australia has become a bellwether for the energy transition, as it replaces a 
rapidly aging fleet of coal power stations and aims to almost doublerenewable 
generation 
<https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/major-publications/qed/2026/qed-q2-2026.pdf?rev=5cfa9aa6b7b6490ab22a13de588f9a66&sc_lang=en&hash=E46DFE92689FE582EB708D5647453DD6>
 to 82% of the total by 2030. As part of that, the uptake ofhome batteries 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/one-in-25-australian-homes-installed-a-battery-in-grid-shakeup?cmpid=BBD092926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9453>
 has boomed since the launch of a government subsidy program last year, helping 
households spread the use of solar generation to the night.

“Solar and batteries provide Australian homes with lower bills, reliable 
power, and clean energy, which is why people are opting for them in droves,” 
CEC Chief Executive Officer Jackie Trad said. Rooftop solar capacity now 
exceeded the remaining total for coal-fired power stations, Trad said.
