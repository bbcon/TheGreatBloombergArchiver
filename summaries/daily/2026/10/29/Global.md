**Energy and Geopolitics**
The Strait of Hormuz risk premium remains embedded in oil prices, even as diplomatic activity has provided a temporary release valve. The contradiction in the US position — Vance reaching out to Tehran while Trump signals renewed strikes post-midterms — sustains a high-uncertainty environment for energy markets. Shell's record-near quarterly profit, enabled by war-driven supply disruption, illustrates how the conflict has restructured global energy economics: US Gulf Coast refining margins tripled to an all-time high of $72 per barrel in September.

**Climate and AI Transition**
The EU's frank acknowledgment that COP31 climate finance discussions are effectively deadlocked — absent meaningful contributions from China, Gulf states, and others — represents a significant setback for the post-Paris architecture. Meanwhile, the AI-energy nexus continues to tighten: rising data center demand is materially affecting fossil fuel consumption trajectories, while the BloombergNEF assessment suggests COP31's AI-climate pledge risks being more symbolic than substantive. The convergence of energy price volatility, AI infrastructure demand, and stalled multilateral climate coordination is creating a structurally more complex environment for the global energy transition.
