**Corporate Governance and Structural Reform**
Japan's corporate restructuring story continues to evolve in ways that would have been inconceivable a decade ago. The Kioxia example — in which Bain Capital carved out Toshiba's memory chip unit, relisted it, and ultimately netted roughly $15 billion in proceeds — illustrates that foreign private equity can now play an active role in reshaping Japan's industrial architecture. KKR's 20x return on Kokusai Electric, generating $5.5 billion in total proceeds from a Hitachi carve-out, reinforces the pattern. These outcomes reflect the cumulative effect of corporate governance reforms championed under the late Prime Minister Shinzo Abe, which have progressively dismantled the cross-shareholding and implicit bailout conventions that suppressed capital efficiency for decades.

**Market Dynamics**
Average daily equity trading volumes have more than doubled since 2022. Foreign net inflows have pushed overseas ownership to a record 34.7%, while domestic household savings are beginning to rotate out of low-yielding deposits as inflation erodes real returns. A generational shift is underway: the cohort scarred by the 1990s bubble collapse is exiting the investor base, replaced by managers and savers accustomed to nominal GDP growth. Whether this momentum is durable depends on the pace of continued restructuring and the depth of the domestic savings rotation — both of which remain works in progress.
