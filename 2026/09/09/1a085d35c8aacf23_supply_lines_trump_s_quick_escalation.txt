The US wasted little time threatening to escalate its trade war with Canada 
following Ottawa’s tariff retaliation on Tuesday.

Washington moved to block imports of some products while slapping new tariffs 
on others, as well as seeking to bar Canadian companies from selling to 
government contractors.

The import bans for some Canadian dairy products and alcohol will take effect 
in three weeks, a senior administration official told reporters. Trump’s 
threats to increase auto tariffs to 50% on Jan. 1 remain in place. The official 
also said they expect to have a conversation with their Canadian counterparts 
in the coming days.

Full Story: US Escalates Canada Trade War With Product Bans, New Tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/trump-plans-to-cut-canadian-products-out-of-government-contracts?cmpid=BBD090926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8648>

Before the escalation was announced in Washington late Tuesday, Bloomberg 
Economics calculated the fallout from the worst-case scenario — one where USMCA 
is scrapped and Canada retaliates against high tariffs.

In that case, “we see the long-term hit to the Canadian economy reaching 2% of 
GDP,” BE economists Maeva Cousin and Rana Sajedi wrote in aresearch note <> on 
the Bloomberg Terminal.

Explainer: Why the US-Canada Trade War Is Escalating 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/why-the-us-canada-trade-war-is-escalating?cmpid=BBD090926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8648>

Prime Minister Mark Carney said he doesn’t believe in escalating the trade war 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/carney-says-tariffs-on-us-goods-meant-to-protect-canada-not-escalate-conflict?cmpid=BBD090926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8648>
 with the US, but argued it was necessary to retaliate against the most recent 
American tariffs while warning Canadians to brace for turbulent times ahead.

“This won’t be easy, and I won’t pretend otherwise,” the prime minister said 
in a video Tuesday. “But Canadians have faced difficult stretches before, and 
what’s carried us through has never ever been just one measure.”

The economic pain that Carney warned would come from a trade war is now harsh 
reality for some small businesses that export to the US. Across Canada, 
companies are losing orders, contemplating job cuts and paying higher costs. 
For some, the threat is existential. (Read the full storyhere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-08/us-canada-trade-war-has-small-businesses-struggling-with-tariff-shock?cmpid=BBD090926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8648>
.)

Meanwhile, the European Union and Canada are working toward a new, 
all-encompassing relationship 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/eu-and-canada-plan-far-reaching-alliance-to-strengthen-partnership?cmpid=BBD090926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8648>
 that will range from trade to security, as the transatlantic partners seek to 
build a new alliance to offset the global power politics dominated by the US 
and China.
