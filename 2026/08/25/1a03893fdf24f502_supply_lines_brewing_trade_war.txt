Canada’s government is set to announce programs to help workers and businesses 
ensnared in the country’s emerging trade war with the US.

Domestic measures will include expanded jobless benefits and loans for 
businesses affected by new tariffs — similar to Ottawa’s coronavirus-era 
economic support programs, Bloomberg Newsis reporting Tuesday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/canada-to-unveil-trump-tariff-response-including-loans-benefits?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
. It’s been dubbed the “One Canadian Economy Agenda.”

University of Calgary economics professor Trevor Tombe estimated that 90,000 
jobs — about 0.4% of Canada’s labor force —may be lost 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/canada-turned-down-a-us-tariff-deal-now-comes-the-economic-cost?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 if the new levies persist.

The moves follow the collapse of talks between Ottawa and Washington that led 
Donald Trump’s administration tostart imposing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/us-canada-trade-talks-collapse-triggering-trump-s-50-tariffs?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 a 50% import tax on about $20 billion worth of Canadian goods. Both sides 
blamed each other for changing demands at the 11th hour, and Prime Minister 
Mark Carney threatened to retaliate in kind starting Sept. 8.

The name-calling and threats have only escalated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-vows-to-double-tariffs-on-canadian-autos-escalating-fight?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
. Here’s a rundown of the fast-moving developments in an extraordinary three 
days for one of the world’s closest trading relationships:

 * War of words: Trump and Ontario leader Doug Ford hurled personal insults 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/-flunky-loser-trump-trades-wild-insults-with-canada-s-ford?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
, with Trump calling Ford “the less charismatic, intelligent, and overall 
unimpressive brother” and Ford calling the US president a “dictator” and 
“loser.”
 * Good cop, bad cop: The talks collapsed after last-minute pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/lutnick-s-intervention-in-canada-talks-draws-praise-and-blame?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 from Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whom Canadians blame for moving the 
goalposts after discussions were running smoothly with US Trade Representative 
Jamieson Greer.
 * Attitude is everything: Carney said his government is still working on 
options for retaliating, but added thathe’s willing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/carney-says-us-wants-to-destroy-canada-firms-examines-retaliation-moves?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 to continue trade talks if the US adopts the “right attitude.”
 * Smelter envy: Trump lamented that his northern neighbor did have something 
he wants 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/trump-laments-lack-of-us-aluminum-amid-trade-row-with-canada?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
: aluminum. In a social mediapost 
<https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117153496831869958>, Trump also 
pitched a “desperately needed” aluminum plant in Oklahoma.
 * USMCA cohesion: Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum expressed optimism she 
can reach atrade deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/mexico-optimistic-on-us-trade-deal-after-canada-talks-collapse?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 with Trump as her chief negotiator leads talks in Washington.
 * Arctic politics: Canada said it’s investing more than C$11 billion to build 
six new icebreakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/carney-commits-8-billion-for-icebreakers-with-chantier-davie?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 for the Canadian Coast Guard in a partnership with Chantier Davie Canada.
 * Market fallout: Canada’s currency tumbled 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/canadian-dollar-tumbles-as-trade-rift-threatens-growth-outlook?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 against the US dollar by the most in two months. Shares of Ford Motor dropped 
3.3% on Monday, and General Motors fell 1.1%.
 * View from Beijing: China’s nationalist tabloid Global Times praised 
Canada’s retaliation,casting Ottawa’s move 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/china-media-hails-canada-s-chinese-style-tariff-retaliation?cmpid=BBD082526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8120>
 as validating Beijing’s opposition to Washington’s protectionist trade 
policies.
