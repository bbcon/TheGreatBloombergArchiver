Great bond shakeout of 2026

The march higher in bond yields is looking like a fundamental shift 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/us-treasury-yields-hit-5-as-wall-street-faces-new-rate-regime?cmpid=092526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9313>
 to a world that a new generation of investors has never seen before — one 
where 5% is once again the norm, if not the floor.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has consistently suggested the selloff in 
Treasuries in recent weeks is a byproduct of higher oil prices — once the Iran 
war ends, prices and yields will fall, he says.

But myriad forces have combined to push government borrowing costs higher — 
from $100-a-barrel oil and the AI spending boom, to yawning US budget deficits 
adding to a record $40 trillion debt load — all against a backdrop of a Fed 
bent on cooling inflation that’s run well past target for years.

“We are in a new regime,” said Samuel Martinez at Vanguard.

A few more takeaways from our Big Take 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/bigtake?cmpid=092526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9313>
 on the Treasury market upheaval:

 * It’s not just the US. The average yield on government debt worldwide sits 
just shy of 4%, the highest since 2007.
 * The yield surge is a political problem for Trump. “This is a bad news story 
for anyone with credit card debt, auto loans, who wants to get a mortgage,” 
said Douglas Holtz-Eakin, a former director of the Congressional Budget Office.
 * More than half of 173 respondents in a Markets Pulse survey this week 
predict US 30-year yieldswill reach 6% before the end of the year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/bond-yields-at-6-will-become-reality-this-year-markets-pulse?cmpid=092526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9313>
, a level not seen since the dot-com bubble burst.
 * For savers and investors looking to put money to work now, yields at 5% 
offer a compelling source of income.
“I’m still of the view that rates are moving higher until something breaks,” 
said Jack McIntyre at Brandywine. —Michael MacKenzie

The cusp of $2 trillion

Meta is finally seeing the stock breakout 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/meta-s-best-month-since-2013-has-it-on-cusp-of-2-trillion-level?cmpid=092526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9313>
 that beleaguered bulls had been banking on.

The Facebook parent’s shares have jumped 36% in September following the 
release of its Muse app, which has muffled concerns that heavy spending on AI 
won’t pay off.

The stock is on pace for its biggest monthly advance since 2013 and a roughly 
1% gain away from joining an elite group of companies worth at least $2 
trillion.

“Muse clearly validates its AI strategy and position, after a year and a half 
where the stock was basically flat because people didn’t know if AI was going 
to be a net positive or a net negative,” said Rob Biederman at Asymmetric 
Capital Partners. —Ryan Vlastelica

Blood bonds

This has got to be one of the world’s most unusual debt-financing campaigns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/romania-blood-bonds-target-retail-investors?cmpid=092526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260925&utm_content=9313>
.

In Romania, blood donors get a chance to buy government bonds offering 
souped-up interest rates of over 7%, a full percentage point above the rate 
available to most retail investors.

The plan is working — managing to both ease the country’s chronic blood 
shortage and secure much-needed cash to help cover its substantial budget 
deficit. It’s raised roughly $604 million since the start of 2025. —Alice Atkins
