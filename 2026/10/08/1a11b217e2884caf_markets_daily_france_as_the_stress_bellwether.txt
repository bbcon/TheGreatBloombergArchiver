France, the stress bellwether

Until further notice, France is the canary in the coal mine for investors 
looking for signs of distress in the global economy and financial markets.

France’s government bonds have endured a punishing selloff, handing investors 
a 4.6% loss this year. Fund managers view the country’s fiscal mess and 
political gridlock as a bigger version of the problems facing many nations 
around the world.

In the latest indicator of the turmoil, almost €215 billion of France’s 
corporate bonds now trade as if they weresafer than the government’s 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/france-s-debt-is-now-riskier-than-38-of-nation-s-company-bonds?cmpid=100826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9834>
, a near 18-fold increase since the start of 2026.

As confidence in French government debt erodes, investors are flocking to 
corporate bonds, especially from multinationals like L’Oreal.(The finance 
minister is trying to reassure investors the country hasno problem finding 
buyers for its bonds 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/france-has-no-problem-finding-bond-buyers-lescure-tells-bbc?cmpid=100826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9834>
.)

The stress is spreading to bank stocks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/european-bank-stocks-head-for-biggest-two-day-slump-since-march?cmpid=100826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9834>
. The Stoxx 600 bank index is posting its biggest two-day drop since March, and 
Société Générale shares have lost a quarter of their value in two months.

Of course, if France passes a credible 2027 budget before year end with proper 
noises from politicians about further reining in the country’s finances,10-year 
bonds yielding close to 5% could start lure buyers.

Some fund managers are starting to add slightly to their holdings 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/france-s-battered-bonds-are-starting-to-tempt-bargain-hunters?cmpid=100826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9834>
. Others say they’re waiting for fiscal austerity to gain wider acceptance in 
France. So far, there is little sign of that, with protests by students and 
teachers demanding more money for education.

“The moment the general public understands the issue is the moment you buy the 
bonds,” said Elliot Hentov at State Street Investment Management. —Phil

Trying to call the top

France’s isn’t the only bond market under the microscope. In the biggest of 
them all, US Treasuries, investors have been surprised with the speed and 
relentlessness of the rise in yields this year.

With the world’s benchmark debt security, the US 10-year, yielding around 
5.3%, Wall Street is trying to figure out if the worst of the selloff is over. 
(Investors have taken a loss of 2.7% this year on Treasuries, a Bloomberg index 
shows.)

Juggling the outlook for oil-driven inflation, fiscal pressures, Fed policy 
and more,plenty of strategists see yields dropping to 5% or lower 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/from-goldman-to-bofa-strategists-try-to-call-peak-bond-yields?cmpid=100826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9834>
 by Dec. 31. William Marshall, head of US rates strategy at Goldman Sachs, 
forecasts the yield declining to 4.75% by year-end.

“We think underlying inflation pressures are fairly well contained,” he says, 
arguing that some of the factors pushing prices higher, like tariffs, are 
mostly in the rearview mirror.

There’s some bet-hedging going on too. Bank of America forecasts the yield at 
5% by the end of the year, but Mark Cabana’s strategy team has issued a set of 
trade recommendations based on the assumption that yields could keep rising.

“Even though our forecasts are lower, even though we’re operating off of this 
US economic base case, we judge the balance of risks as suggesting higher rates 
are more likely than lower ones right now,” they say. —Victor Swezey 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXsKzTZBV3I/victor-swezey?cmpid=100826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9834>

commitments. What happens if they can’t fulfill those commitments or all those
