Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporters Ari Natter and Lucia Kassai look at the status of America’s Strategic 
Petroleum Reserve.

The cost of fuel is climbing again as the US-Iran war widens 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-war-with-iran-looks-set-to-drag-on-for-months-over-hormuz-deadlock?cmpid=BBBXT073126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7222>
, but this time there’s little indication that Washington will tap its crude 
stockpiles to bring prices back down.

Energy Secretary Chris Wright says another major withdrawal from the Strategic 
Petroleum Reserve isn’t on the table once the US completes the172 
million-barrel release 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-11/us-to-release-172-million-barrels-for-iea-plan-to-tap-reserves?cmpid=BBBXT073126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7222>
 that was announced in March, alongside action from other countries.

“There is certainly more oil in the SPR, but we have no thoughts whatsoever of 
needing any more,” Wright said in an interview on Wednesday.

In fact, it’s uncertain whether the 38.4 million barrels still left from the 
initial March release will actually be distributed. A late-June offering, which 
came as oil prices eased and Washington and Tehran edged toward a peace deal, 
drew tepid interest.

Wright said the administration “probably will” offer the remaining barrels, 
but he stopped short of making a commitment.

Wright’s comments come as the SPR sits at its lowest level 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-15/us-oil-reserve-hits-43-year-low-as-trump-aims-to-tame-gas-prices?cmpid=BBBXT073126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7222>
 in more than four decades, in part because of a historic withdrawal during the 
Biden administration. Created after the 1973 Arab oil embargo, the reservenow 
holds <https://www.spr.doe.gov/dir/dir.html> 307.7 million barrels — a level 
not seen since the early 1980s, when it was still in the process of being 
filled.

Analysts estimate the SPR’s operational floor, the point below which it 
becomes difficult to function, lies somewhere between 150 million and 200 
million barrels. Wright, speaking separately with Bloomberg Television on 
Wednesday, said the reserve remains comfortably above that threshold.

“We are a long way from the operational floor,” he said. Because the current 
release is structured as an exchange, with companies later returning borrowed 
crude plus interest, he said the SPR will ultimately receive about 40 million 
more barrels than it initially loaned.

Meanwhile, fighting between the US and Iran continues to disrupt Middle 
Eastern energy exports, creating price pressure everywhere.

US commercial crude inventories are at their lowest since September 2018 and 
benchmark futures are back above $80 a barrel. Refineries are churning out 
record volumes of fuel, but the national average price of gasoline has risen 
above $4 a gallon again.

The first SPR release helped blunt the initial surge in fuel costs from the 
war, but maybe not this time.

—Ari Natter and Lucia Kassai, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Europe is set to enter winter with a thin gas storage buffer. July storage 
injections remained slow as liquefied natural gas imports stayed weak. As 
injection season draws to a close, gas storage across the BloombergNEF Europe 
Perimeter — Northwest Europe, Italy, Spain and Austria — is projected to reach 
65% by Nov. 1 in the base case, which assumes the Strait of Hormuz reopens in 
mid-September.
