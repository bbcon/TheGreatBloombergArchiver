Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
metals reporter Katharine Gemmell looks at China’s steel industry as the 
country’s prolonged property downturn continues to weigh on the sector.

China’s beleaguered steel mills are facing a painful winter.

The warning, made last week by the China Iron and Steel Association, might 
sound familiar to followers of the world’s biggest steel industry.

Two years ago, the chairman of China Baowu Steel Group, the country’s top 
producer, warned of a“harsh winter,” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-14/china-steel-industry-in-crisis-as-baowu-warns-of-severe-winter?cmpid=BBBXT111626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11450>
 following previous crises in 2015 and 2008.

Predictions of mill closures and industry mergers didn’t play out then because 
Beijing stepped in with stimulus measures that helped lift demand. But this 
time around, there’s reason to believe such assistance will be less forthcoming.

Chinese authorities are increasingly eschewing short-term injections to prop 
up struggling sectors, opting instead for structural reform. That could 
initially spur more steel exports, but may eventually lead to industry 
consolidation.
A steel storage warehouse in Hangzhou, China.Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg
The roots of the current crisis date to 2021, when Beijing cracked down on 
excessive borrowing by property developers, setting off a construction slump 
that the countrystill hasn’t gotten over 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/chinese-steel-market-enters-long-plateau-after-property-crash?cmpid=BBBXT111626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11450>
.

Despite output cuts, steel inventories piled up and local prices fell. More 
recently, increases in coal costs compounded the pressure on producers. Their 
ailing health has weighed oniron ore prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/chinese-iron-ore-futures-hit-four-year-low-on-weak-steel-outlook?cmpid=BBBXT111626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11450>
, which today slid to a four-year low.

Most of the country’s steel mills are unprofitable, Liu Andong, the steel 
association’s vice president, told an industry conference in Xiamen last week.

With the local market outlook bleak, exports have become a crutch for 
producers. That’santagonized some overseas governments 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trade-war-brazil-steel-billionaires-take-fight-with-china-to-peru?cmpid=BBBXT111626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11450>
, which have accused Beijing of dumping. But without a significant recovery in 
Chinese demand, such shipments look set to increase further, potentially 
bringing some trade disputes to a head.

Beijing isn’t firmly sitting on its hands. It has managed to curb nationwide 
production this year, and the steel association hasrebuked mills 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/chinese-steel-group-chides-mills-for-ignoring-output-curb-calls?cmpid=BBBXT111626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11450>
 that ignore calls to cut back. A government agency thatcentralizes iron-ore 
buying 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/china-s-iron-ore-buying-clout-makes-each-deal-a-market-event?cmpid=BBBXT111626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11450>
 has also had some success in leveraging its scale to bring down costs for 
plants.

Still, the best-case scenario looks like an industry mired in the doldrums 
until output shrinks further. In the worst case, mills will shut down.

—Katharine Gemmell, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

China’s sustainable aviation fuel exports hit a 2026 high in September, but 
weaker margins could slow shipments in the coming months, according to
BloombergNEF <https://about.bnef.com/>. Exports rose 19% from August, with 
Belgium taking 73%. Margins on Chinese SAF sold into Europe averaged about $560 
a ton in September, then turned negative in mid-October as prices softened on 
reports that much of Europe’s 2026 demand had already been purchased.
