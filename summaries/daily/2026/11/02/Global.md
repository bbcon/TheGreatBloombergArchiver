**Oil Markets and Iran Diplomacy**
The single most market-relevant development of the day was the Axios report indicating Trump is prepared to extend diplomatic engagement with Tehran before authorizing further military action. Brent crude fell more than 4% intraday to approximately $108–110 per barrel — a meaningful move given elevated war-risk premia embedded since the initial US strikes. The signal introduces genuine uncertainty about the timeline of any renewed military escalation, though Iran's Supreme Leader simultaneously ordered military commanders to prepare for confrontation and expand retaliation options, suggesting the diplomatic opening remains fragile. Goldman Sachs's decision to raise oil forecasts on the same day underscores the underlying supply vulnerability that persists regardless of near-term diplomatic tone.

**Strait of Hormuz and Emerging Market Energy Stress**
The human and economic cost of the Hormuz disruption is becoming increasingly visible in the developing world. Bangladesh, which sourced approximately 60% of its LNG from Qatar under long-term contracts, has been forced into the spot market after Qatar was unable to fulfill deliveries. A single LNG cargo now costs Bangladesh over $100 million — roughly four times the contracted equivalent — with cumulative additional costs reaching approximately $2 billion. Textile factories supplying global brands including H&M and Ralph Lauren are operating below capacity or have halted production. The dynamic illustrates a broader risk: the longer Hormuz remains disrupted, the more deeply the crisis will propagate through energy-intensive manufacturing across South and Southeast Asia.
