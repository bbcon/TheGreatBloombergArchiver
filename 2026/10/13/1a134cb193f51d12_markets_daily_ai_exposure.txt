Roll of the dice

Here are couple of AI datapoints for consideration:

1) Global investors chasing the AI-powered rally are the most exposed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/bofa-poll-shows-investors-most-exposed-to-tech-stocks-since-2020?cmpid=101326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10020>
 to technology stocks since the rebound from the Covid pandemic six years ago, 
according to a poll by Bank of America.

2) Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/broadcom-ceo-likens-many-ai-investors-to-casino-gamblers?cmpid=101326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10020>
 at an event that “a lot” of people rushing into the AI trade “have no clue 
what they are investing in” and are setting “very extreme expectations only to 
find out that it’s almost like they are in a casino.”

In that context, perhaps best to take the long view. Today’s Big Take 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-12/how-the-ai-boom-is-fueling-global-financial-risks?cmpid=101326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10020>
 does just that, tracking how a circular funding model is accelerating the AI 
boom, but also amplifying imbalances in global capital flows.

One example: The gap between surplus savers like China and Japan and deficit 
economies like the US is at the highest level since the leadup to the 2008 
global financial crisis and is nearing a 150-year peak.

“We are not in a safe space,” said Beatrice Weder di Mauro of the Centre for 
Economic Policy Research.

Still, others argue that the financial system is less exposed than it was 
ahead of the 2008 crisis because the investment is fundamentally more 
productive.

“The technology is real, adoption is real, and many of the leading companies 
are highly profitable,” said David Chao of Invesco. “What I worry about is that 
investor expectations soon may become impossible to meet.” —Shawn Donnan

Growth shield

Total US public debt recently surpassed $40 trillion and American lawmakers 
continue to shrug off calls to contend with historically wide fiscal deficits.

Yet the country has largely escaped the kind of pressure investors are putting 
on France, the UK and other highly indebted nations. Why?One answer is faster 
economic growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/blackstone-s-gray-bets-big-on-electriciy-defense-life-sciences?cmpid=101326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10020>
, according to Blackstone President Jon Gray.

“The US is going to have to face tough choices, but I think now this is more 
of a France, UK, continental Europe challenge,” he said. “Just because there 
you have much lower rates of growth,” as well as “very large deficits,” he said.

For now, Gray said the US is keeping its mounting debt problems at bay through 
faster growth that’s being spurred by the AI boom.

“When you look at the technology, the investment, what’s happening with AI, 
you’re seeing it lead to a lot of capital flows in, and that higher rate of 
growth gives you a little more room,” he said at the Bloomberg New Economy 
Forum. —Trista Xinyi Luo 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWI8tPIZrnw/trista-xinyi-luo?cmpid=101326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10020>
