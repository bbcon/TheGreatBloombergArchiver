Former Brazilian leader Jair Bolsonaro found a way to show up at the official 
launch 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/brazil-election-bolsonaro-presses-forward-with-chaotic-presidential-bid?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
 of his son Flávio’s presidential candidacy, even though he’s under house 
arrest for plotting a coup.

“What you’re seeing here isn’t me,” Bolsonaro said, his voice booming from a 
giant video screen. “It’s a simulation of my image and my voice, created using 
artificial intelligence.”

AI has reshaped politics and elections across the globe in recent years, 
fueling fundraising 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-06-26/silicon-valley-money-drives-ai-s-growing-role-in-2026-us-elections?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
 in the US while promptingwarnings 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-26/eu-warns-election-deepfakes-start-to-surface-in-member-nations?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
 about the use of so-called “deepfake” videos in European andIndian campaigns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2024-ai-election-security-deepfakes/?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
.

But Saturday’s Bolsonaro video has opened a novel debate: What to do when a 
campaign uses AI to help a high-profile figure participate in an election 
despite court orders banning him from disseminating political and electoral 
messages on social media or via other means?

Already, the 46-second clip is facing a legal challenge from President Luiz 
Inácio Lula da Silva’s reelection campaign, setting up a major test of Brazil’s 
electoral court ahead of theOctober vote 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/lula-leads-brazil-election-poll-after-shaky-start-to-bolsonaro-campaign?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
.

Lula and Brazilian authorities have sat at the forefront of the fight against 
social-media disinformation since the leftist beat Bolsonaro in 2022,battling 
with 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-10/elon-musk-meets-his-match-in-twitter-fake-news-fight-with-brazil-s-supreme-court?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
 tech tycoon Elon Musk, refusing to bow to demands from President Donald Trump 
and evensharing notes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-03/brazil-took-on-musk-and-won-now-lula-is-sharing-notes-with-europe?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
 with European leaders.

But the electoral court that took an aggressive approach to past elections — 
including by barring Jair Bolsonaro from holding office over false claims about 
voting fraud — is now led by justices the former president appointed.

That’s only deepened questions about how the court will take on AI and similar 
issues in this race, especially as the Trump administrationtakes an interest 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-06-30/bolsonaro-to-be-banned-from-public-office-for-eight-years?cmpid=BBD072926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7122>
 in the process.

One Lula campaign coordinator, Marco Aurélio de Carvalho, tells us the contest 
is shaping up to become “the most heavily litigated election since Brazil’s 
return to democracy.” — Daniel Carvalho
