**Eurozone Outlook**
The eurozone appears to be navigating the Iran war's economic disruption with greater resilience than anticipated. A Bloomberg survey of analysts projects growth to quicken from Q2's already-surprising strength, as domestic demand holds up and export momentum — particularly in AI-related manufacturing — provides a partial offset to energy cost pressures. European equities are reflecting this fundamental shift: with earnings growth of 17% — the strongest in four years — and the most robust economic momentum since March 2023, strategists are increasingly attributing the Stoxx 600's rally to improved fundamentals rather than merely cheap valuations relative to the US. BlackRock's Helen Jewell noted that regional "resilience has surprised the market."

**UK Economy and Politics**
UK hiring stabilized in July, according to a closely watched survey, ending a prolonged contraction that began with the Liz Truss gilt crisis in 2022. Temporary vacancies rose for the first time in two years and pay growth ticked up, suggesting the labor market may be turning. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is touring the country to promote cost-of-living measures under the banner of "everyday fixes," though controversy over his prison overcrowding policy threatens to complicate the political backdrop. Swiss rate expectations were pushed back, with no hike now anticipated before June 2027 at the earliest. UK GDP data are due later this week.
