US Housing Doldrums

Many things changed when the Federal Reserve switched chairs from Jerome 
Powell to Kevin Warsh this year. But there was a clear through-line on their 
reading of the US housing market. Powell in April said it “remained weak.” 
Warsh last month said it “continues to lag.”

And the latest batch of data show, if anything, that things may be heading 
further south.

July matched the second-worst level of contract signings for previously owned 
homes in data stretching all the way back to 2001, a Tuesdayreport showed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/us-pending-home-sales-slide-to-weakest-since-start-of-the-year?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
. A separate release showed that builders broke ground on the fewest new 
single-family homessince 2022 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/us-housing-starts-slow-on-drop-in-single-family-construction?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
 — back when the Fed was jacking up interest rates.

A key reason behind the drop-off in volumes: the number of people looking to 
buyhas tumbled <https://www.redfin.com/news/buyers-vs-sellers-july-2026/>, 
according to data compiled by Redfin. The online real-estate platform estimates 
there were 51.3% more home sellers than buyers in the US housing market in 
July. Redfin defines a buyer’s market as one where there’s more than 10% more 
sellers than purchasers.

So it’s well and truly a buyer’s market. Just without a whole lot of buyers.

High costs go some way to explaining the lack of house-hunters these days.

The median sales price for US existing homes hit a record high 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/us-sales-of-previously-owned-homes-decline-to-three-month-low?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
 in June, at $434,000, and held near there last month. Benchmark 30-year fixed 
mortgage ratesremain elevated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/mortgage-rates-in-the-us-edge-lower-for-first-time-in-six-weeks?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
, at 6.67%. Rates had dipped below 6% just before the US-Israel attack on Iran 
at the end of February, but surging energy costs and rising risks of Federal 
Reserve monetary tightening then drove them back up.

It’s all left homebuilders’ sentiment in the dumps 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/us-homebuilder-sentiment-barely-rises-as-costs-stymie-sector?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
. A key gauge of their “confidence” has languished below the level of 40 for 16 
months — the longest such stretch since 2012, when the US was still digging out 
of the financial crisis. The “breakeven” figure for that index is 50.

What also may be emerging is something of a K-shaped housing market. “Custom 
builders at the higher end report stronger conditions than speculative 
builders,” Yelena Maleyev, a senior economist at KPMG, wrote in anote 
<https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/july-2026-housing-starts.html> Tuesday. 
It’s “a housing market that is splitting in two, with the high end and the 
well-capitalized builders on one side and everyone else on the other,” she said.

That top end of the market doesn’t likely produce the volume needed to propel 
broader economic growth, however. Maleyev expects residential construction — 
which has contracted in seven of the past nine quarters — to once again be a 
drag on growth. “The chill in the housing market is expected to intensify in 
the back half of this year,” she warned.

Need-to-Know Research

Total public US debt is about to surpass $40 trillion, bringing the government 
steadily closer to the current statutory limit of just over $41 trillion — and 
spurring analysts to start gaming out the nextpolitical showdown 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/explainers/debt-ceiling-explained?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
 over borrowing.

Evercore ISI reckons the ceiling will be hit in the first half of 2027, by 
when Democrats may have control of at least one chamber of Congress. They would 
likely seek policy concessions in return for agreeing to an increase, analysts 
Matthew Aks, Sarah Bianchi and Karlin Gatton wrote in a note Tuesday. Possible 
items include more health care spending or a bigger child tax credit.

The deadline for a deal would likely be around five months or so after the 
debt ceiling hits — during which the Treasury would re-use longstanding 
accounting methods to avoid a breach — the team wrote. They warned that “the 
uncertainty and brinksmanship” during the likely showdown risks fuelling 
concerns about US fiscal deficits and “legislative dysfunction, adding another 
potential source of upward pressure” onborrowing costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/bond-rout-sends-long-term-borrowing-costs-to-highest-in-decades?cmpid=BBD081926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7863>
