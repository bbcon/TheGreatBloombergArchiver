Long Goodbye

The race to lead 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-27/four-ecb-scenarios-sketch-a-succession-plan-for-post-lagarde-era?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 the European Central Bankbecame official 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/de-cos-and-knot-are-top-ecb-candidates-after-early-lagarde-exit?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 this week after Christine Lagarde finally announced her long-anticipated exit.

The saga began in May last year, when the president was reported to be 
considering taking over the World Economic Forum. Shebrushed that off 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-05/lagarde-determined-to-finish-ecb-term-after-wef-job-speculation?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 but, after more than a year of speculation, it turns out she really will exit 
in February. That’s well before her term ends next October.

Why resign from such a big job? It’s a question Lagarde faced on Thursday 
after policymakers, ina decision 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/ecb-holds-rates-as-lagarde-exit-compounds-alarm-over-oil-france?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 eclipsed by her personal plans, left theirkey rate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-30/ecb-to-act-decisively-if-needed-to-tame-inflation-nagel-says?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 unchanged whilekeeping the door open 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/ecb-officials-are-ready-to-raise-interest-rates-in-december?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 to ahike in December 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-30/ecb-s-panetta-says-more-rate-hikes-may-be-needed-to-tame-prices?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
.

“I want to have the freedom of speech, the freedom of expression, and the 
freedom to engage on matters that are not monetary policy matters,”Lagarde told 
reporters 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/lagarde-quits-ecb-to-shed-neutrality-and-enter-political-fray-mvtp2d3u?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
. “We have an obligation of neutrality” and “I don’t think that I can be 
comfortable in that neutrality,” she added.

 in office.

On the plus side, Lagarde repaired a tetchy situation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-09-17/draghi-s-qe-triumph-hinged-on-ecb-colleagues-from-tiny-economies?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 leftby her predecessor 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-10-11/28-days-later-anti-draghi-revolt-at-ecb-still-refuses-to-die?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
, Mario Draghi, byresetting relations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-11-14/lagarde-musters-ecb-team-for-huddle-at-castle-fit-for-an-empress?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 with policymakers.

She also notched up a victory over inflation. Unlike peers such as the US 
Federal Reserve, the ECB actually gotprice growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-30/ecb-s-moulin-sees-policy-as-measured-with-most-prices-stable?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 back to its 2% goal, at least before events in the Middle East derailed things.

“After hesitating at first, the ECB responded decisively and brought inflation 
back to the target level without triggering a recession,” said Emanuel Moench, 
co-director of the Frankfurt-based Centre for Central Banking.

Lagarde’s creation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-06-26/lagarde-s-moment-of-ecb-crisis-reckoning-in-a-london-basement?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 of a new crisis-fighting tool in 2022 to keep fiscal worries at bay alongside 
rate hikes was arguably a decisive moment too.

But there were stumbles. Among them was, as Moench suggests, the ECB’s tardy 
action 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-05-25/goodhart-slams-ecb-s-ultra-loose-policy-while-wishing-lane-luck?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 totame inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-03-22/ecb-latest-chief-economist-philip-lane-rebuffs-willem-buiter-with-rate-defense?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 when Russia invaded Ukraine.

There was also an error in 2020 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-03-14/lagarde-s-gaffe-puts-ecb-chief-in-elite-club-for-market-stumbles?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
, shortly after she took office, when Lagarde caused bond turmoil by saying the 
ECB was “not here to close spreads.”

That appeared to undermine Draghi’s pledge to preserve the euro at any cost, 
and necessitateda bond-buying  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2020-04-06/coronavirus-lagarde-s-810-billion-u-turn-came-in-just-4-weeks?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
program that eventually reached €1.7 trillion ($2 trillion).

Lagarde’s long goodbye meanwhile caused some colleagues to observe privately 
that itwasn’t a good look 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-15/ecb-officials-get-impatient-for-lagarde-to-clear-up-future-plans?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
 for the ECB.

So what will she do now? That’s unclear. But whether she runs the WEF or 
delves into campaigning before the French presidential election in April, 
Lagarde seems likely to stay in the limelight.

“Christine is aware that the situation in France is about to get ugly 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/french-bond-rout-puts-crisis-at-heart-of-ecb-succession-endgame?cmpid=BBD103026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10707>
,” said former Finance Minister Bruno Le Maire. “She has the skills and 
expertise to get involved and play a role.”

Need-to-Know Research

Americans have seen a bigger jump in their fuel bills than consumers in any 
other country due to the energy shock caused by the US-Israel-Iran war, 
according to a study by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air.

The world has paid some $468 billion in additional costs for diesel and 
gasoline since the war began, the Helsinki-based groupfound 
<https://energyandcleanair.org/us-paying-most-for-hormuz-oil-shock/>, with the 
US accounting for more than a third, at about $160 billion. It’s also had the 
biggest per-capita rise — at $471 — closely followed by “other economies with 
high ownership and use of private cars” such as Canada, Australia and New 
Zealand.

The CREA also examined which countries have lowered their fuel consumption 
since 2023 — the year after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine caused the last big 
energy spike — helping to cushion the blow this time around. It found the 
biggest per-capita savings in northern European countries like Denmark, Germany 
and Finland, because of a rapid uptake of electric vehicles and declining 
reliance on oil products for home heating.
