• Germany's Q2 GDP was revised up to 0.3% QoQ from a preliminary 0.2%, beating expectations, with net exports the primary driver and business sentiment at its highest since before the Iran war.
• Spanish inflation surged to more than double the ECB's 2% target; French inflation quickened to its highest since May — both driven by energy costs, pointing to euro-area inflation above 3% when aggregate data are released next week.
• ECB Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel warned rates must rise further, saying inflation is likely to exceed 2% for an "extended period"; markets fully price a September hike to 2.5%.
• European natural gas prices are near five-month highs, with winter contracts costing more than double year-ago levels, overtaking oil as bond investors' primary inflation concern.

**Inflation and ECB Policy**
Schnabel's hawkish intervention clarified the ECB's direction even as growth remains uneven. Energy is the dominant transmission channel: gas accounts for roughly 21% of the EU's energy mix and 25–35% of the UK's, and the Netherlands' network operator has warned it will miss its winter storage target, leaving the region exposed entering the heating season. Goldman Sachs estimated European gas prices may need to exceed €100 per megawatt-hour in December for inventories to reach adequate levels. That combination — above-target inflation, undersupplied storage, and a resilient economy — has pushed 10-year German and UK yields to levels not seen in decades.

**Germany's Uneven Recovery**
The GDP upgrade and a four-month streak of improving business sentiment paint a picture of a trade-led recovery taking hold in Europe's largest economy. German exporter sentiment rose to a 4½-year high in August. Yet the recovery's durability faces serious structural questions: Volkswagen CEO Oliver Blume presented restructuring plans that labor representatives warn could eventually eliminate up to 140,000 jobs, and Chancellor Merz warned that a foiled drone attack at a German airport signals escalating hybrid-threat exposure from Russia.

**Political Landscape**
France presents the week's most complex political picture: presidential candidates clashed over swelling debt without agreeing on a fix, the economy narrowly avoided recession, and polls show a fragmented centrist field currently benefiting Marine Le Pen and Jean-Luc Mélenchon ahead of 2027. Germany's AfD is positioned for a historic first hold on regional power in Saxony-Anhalt. Iceland voted on whether to reopen EU membership talks, with the latest poll showing the "yes" camp ahead by just 2.6 points. UK Prime Minister Burnham signaled continuity on Ukraine support, planning to lobby Trump at September's UN General Assembly for greater Patriot missile access.
