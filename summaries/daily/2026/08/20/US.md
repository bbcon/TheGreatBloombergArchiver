• US public debt topped $40 trillion for the first time, up a third in under five years, landing hours after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Treasury would at least double buybacks of 10- to 30-year debt to arrest rising long yields.
• Fed minutes from the July 29 FOMC showed officials sharply divided over further hikes; most respondents to Bloomberg's Markets Pulse survey still expect the 10-year yield to top 5%.
• The buyback news pulled the 10-year yield down about 7bp and the 30-year 8bp to 5.19%, but the S&P 500 gained just 0.21% and the dollar posted its worst day in months.
• Moderna shares surged as much as 176% on positive melanoma-vaccine trial data with Merck, lifting the S&P 500 healthcare index over 4% and inflicting roughly $5-5.5 billion in mark-to-market short losses.
• Trump announced an "economic D-Day" against Iran, threatening penalties on any country, bank or business still trading with Tehran — putting China, Iran's top oil buyer, in the crosshairs.

**Treasury and Fiscal Policy**
Bessent's move reflects unease over debt sustainability rather than a resolution of it: JPMorgan warned the buyback expansion risks a credibility problem absent real fiscal consolidation, and the still-rising yield curve suggests investors want more than symbolism to bring borrowing costs down.

**Markets**
Equities barely reacted to the bond news, underscoring that AI earnings, not Treasuries, remain the dominant driver of sentiment for now. Gold instead posted its best day in six months and Bitcoin surged past $70,000, as investors read the buyback as a step toward financial repression rather than a durable fix.
