• Samsung Electronics reported chip operating income of 89.2 trillion won in Q2, a 250-fold surge year-on-year, driven by AI infrastructure demand — though the stock fell as investors focused on future supply risk.
• Japan's Prime Minister Takaichi announced a temporary cut in the food and soft drink sales tax to 1% from April 2027, advancing a key electoral pledge amid slipping approval ratings.
• India's RBI sold approximately $7 billion to defend the rupee last Friday, one of its largest direct interventions in months.

**Corporate and Semiconductor Outlook**
Samsung's results were operationally strong, reflecting surging demand for high-bandwidth memory from AI clients including Nvidia, which is seeking multiyear supply contracts at elevated prices. However, the company's own warning that memory supply shortages will worsen into 2027 introduces a cost and availability risk for the broader AI infrastructure buildout. The stock's decline despite the earnings beat suggests investors are weighing whether premium pricing is sustainable once new capacity comes online.

**Policy Developments**
Japan's Takaichi food-tax cut represents a meaningful fiscal signal ahead of potential political headwinds, though the Bank of Japan is expected to hold rates unchanged at its Friday meeting, maintaining a cautious stance amid global uncertainty. In India, the scale of RBI intervention — roughly $7 billion in a single session — underscores continued pressure on the rupee, likely reflecting a combination of dollar strength, elevated energy import costs, and portfolio outflows linked to the US-Iran conflict's risk-off dynamic.
