For years, Senegal was held up as one of West Africa’s more stable democracies 
and promising economies.

The government borrowed heavily to finance highways, ports and a 50,000-seat 
soccer stadium, confident that growth would make the debt manageable.

Then the numbers changed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-10/senegal-s-hidden-debt-crisis-puts-infrastructure-boom-at-risk-worries-investors?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
.

In late 2024, an audit of Senegal’s finances ordered by President Bassirou 
Diomaye Faye found billions of dollars in undeclared borrowing, almost doubling 
its debt burden overnight.

The discovery shut Senegal out of global capital markets and led the 
International Monetary Fund to suspend a $1.8 billion facility, leaving the new 
governmentscrambling to keep paying creditors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/senegal-debt-revamp-risks-inflicting-60-losses-on-bondholders?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
 while delivering on its election promises.

With huge debt outlays absorbing scarce state resources, infrastructure 
projects have been delayed, contractors have gone unpaid, and the government 
has faced some difficult choices.

For ordinary Senegalese, the consequences are increasingly tangible 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/senegal-raises-fuel-prices-as-oil-shock-drives-up-subsidy-costs?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
, with fuel subsidies cut and new taxes imposed.
Faye, Senegal’s president.Photographer: Nathan Laine/Bloomberg
The crisis is feeding into an increasingly bitter feud at the top of 
Senegalese politics.

Faye swept to power in 2024 backed by opposition leader Ousmane Sonko. In May, 
Faye removed Sonko as prime minister as debt disagreementsfueled a broader 
struggle 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/senegal-s-faye-launches-new-party-as-split-with-sonko-deepens?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
 over Senegal’s economic direction.

Sonko, now speaker of parliament, has opposed debt restructuring 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/senegal-to-negotiate-a-debt-reprofiling-in-talks-with-creditors?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
, saying it risks hurting Senegal’s reputation. This week he said he’ll 
scrutinize reforms needed to unlock a new IMF program, potentially giving him a 
powerful lever over government strategy.

Senegal has remained an outlier in an increasingly volatile region 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/niger-says-sahel-bloc-to-triple-forces-as-islamist-attacks-mount?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
, where countries like Mali and Burkina Faso have become epicenters of 
terrorist violence. A prolonged crisis risks straining that resilience.

For Faye, the challenge is no longer simply finding a way to manage Senegal’s 
squeezed finances.

It’s also about persuading Sonko and a divided government to back the choices 
needed to secure the country’s financial future — without letting the standoff 
undermine one of West Africa’s last stable strongholds.— Katarina Hoije 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATnlsGIiBZU/katarina-hoije?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>

And Finally

Over little more than a decade, the sleepy Cambodian beach resort of 
Sihanoukville was transformed by Chinese investment and property speculation 
into a bustling mecca of high-rise hotels and casinos. When an online gambling 
ban and the Covid-19 pandemic hollowed out tourism, criminal syndicates 
repurposed resorts, condominiums and casinos into compounds for 
industrial-scale online fraud until a nationwide crackdown by the authorities. 
Sihanoukville now offers a glimpse of what happens when a criminal industry
becomes embedded 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-10/cambodia-s-sihanoukville-struggles-after-scam-economy-crackdown?cmpid=BBD091126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8800>
 in a local economy and then abruptly retreats.
