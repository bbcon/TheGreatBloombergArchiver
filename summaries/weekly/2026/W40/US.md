**Monetary Policy and the Communication Breakdown**
The dominant US macro story of the week was not the data itself but the damage inflicted by clumsy official communication. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole stance and September rate hike — the first in three years — was compounded by Treasury Secretary Bessent's remarks signaling continued tightening, driving fed funds futures to price a terminal rate near 4.86%. Core PCE at 3.0% YoY and trimmed-mean PCE declining further to 2.3% offered no obvious inflation justification for this pricing. Jefferson's Thursday comments — that the Fed "may need more time" before deciding on another hike — triggered a 21bp repricing in the October 2027 contract and a 12bp intraday rally in the 10-year yield. NY Fed President Williams had earlier reinforced the hawkish tilt, stating the Fed "can't ignore supply shocks," but markets interpreted Jefferson's remarks as the more authoritative signal. September PCE and Friday's payrolls report remain the key near-term data catalysts for resolving policy uncertainty.

**Consumer Spending and Its Limits**
August personal consumption data confirmed the consumer remains the primary growth engine, but the composition raises durability questions. Real spending rose 0.6% MoM, yet after-tax nominal income rose only 0.3% — entirely offset by a matching inflation increase. Households drew down savings, with the personal saving rate falling to 4.1%, its lowest in nearly four years. Roughly half the quarter's consumption gains appear attributable to a wealth effect following a record $12.8 trillion rise in household net worth in Q2. The Conference Board's September reading — its weakest since 2014, worse than pandemic-era troughs — suggests this aggregate strength masks significant distributional stress, with elevated energy prices weighing disproportionately on lower-income households. A sustained equity correction would meaningfully impair this spending dynamic heading into 2027.

**Rates, Equity Markets, and Trade Policy**
The 10-year Treasury yield's rise to 5.30% inverted the traditional equity risk premium: for the first time in roughly 25 years, the 10-year yield exceeded the S&P 500 earnings yield, removing a structural anchor for equity valuations. Investment-grade credit spreads have barely widened, suggesting markets have not yet repriced underlying credit risk — a disconnect worth monitoring. The AI complex continued to provide index support, with Broadcom arranging $60 billion in AI chip financing and Amazon exploring off-balance-sheet structures for Nvidia purchases. Paramount Skydance's $52 billion leveraged buyout — the largest Hollywood transaction on record — saw its eight-year junk bonds immediately trade at 96 cents on the dollar, generating over $100 million in day-one losses. On trade policy, the Trump administration invoked Section 338 of the Trade Act of 1930 to ban approximately $1 billion in Canadian imports including motorcycles and alcoholic beverages, and separately faced a Court of International Trade challenge to its Section 301 forced-labor tariffs. The administration also announced a planned $4 billion loan to Vistra Corp. for nuclear plant upgrades, reflecting the administration's energy security priorities.
