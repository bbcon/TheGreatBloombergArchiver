Japan looks to be bringing out more ammunition to support its chronically weak 
currency.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s government is said to be supportive of a 
near-term rate hike by the Bank of Japan, with thenext move likely 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/japan-s-government-is-said-to-support-faster-boj-rate-hike?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7674>
 either in September or October.

The central bank’s fears over yen weakness driving up prices and the 
government’s desire to strengthen the impact of the recent US-Japan currency 
intervention are aligning them on the need for a near-term raise, people 
familiar with the matter said.

While Japan’s central bank has legal independence over monetary policy, it’s 
also required to maintain close contact with the government on economic policy 
goals. Takaichi’s cabinet can’t force the BOJ to set specific interest rates, 
but can send signals that might influence its decisions.

Efforts by officials to support the yen have so far failed, with the impact 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/bessent-and-the-fed-help-japan-reverse-months-of-yen-losses?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7674>
 of US-Japan intervention fading. Expectationsare now building 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/yen-trades-near-160-level-keeping-traders-on-intervention-watch?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7674>
 for the BOJ to join efforts to bolster the currency — a move US Treasury 
Secretary Scott Bessent has signaled he believes is necessary.

While faster rate hikes could help the currency, which is trading near a 
40-year low, they may also hinder fragile economic growth. Householdspending 
fell 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/japan-s-households-continue-to-cut-spending-despite-wage-gains?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260813&utm_content=7674>
 in June for a seventh straight month. That puts the government in the 
difficult position of balancing limiting yen depreciation with avoiding 
over-tightening.
