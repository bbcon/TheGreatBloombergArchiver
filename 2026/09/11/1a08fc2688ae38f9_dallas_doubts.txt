Donald Trump’s midterm convention was supposed to put some wind behind 
Republicans heading into November. Instead,after two days 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/trump-s-two-days-in-dallas-5-000-cash-socialists-and-opera?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8796>
 in Dallas, the president left much where he began: staring at potentially 
bruising elections and fresh questions about how much of his formidable hold 
over the Republican Party translates beyond its base. The gathering had plenty 
of warnings about “socialists” and appeals to “common sense,” but rather less 
on the gasoline prices, grocery bills and mortgage rates voters say are 
bothering them. Trump urged patience over the Iran war anddangled $5,000 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/thune-dodges-question-on-trump-s-5-000-payout-to-us-adults?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8796>
 payments to Americans if Republicans keep Congress (a roughly $1.2 trillion 
promise that quickly ran into bipartisan skepticism).

There were other signs that the old Trump electricity may be flickering. 
Vulnerable Republicansskipped Dallas 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/swing-district-republicans-set-to-skip-trump-midterm-convention?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8796>
, major donors were conspicuously absent and empty seats dotted the arena; one 
GOP strategist called the convention essentially a wash. Trump’s approval 
rating has sunk to 33%, with Democrats now well ahead in the fight for the 
House. Still, writing Trump off has rarely been a rewarding pastime: he retains 
an unusually loyal Republican base and a $400 million super-PAC war chest. The 
convention ended in suitably Trumpian fashion: A pledge of allegiance to “the 
greatest president in the history of the country,” followed by an opera singer 
and balloons.

Oil above $100 is creating a new problem for Asia at a particularly bad time. 
Brentsurged past $107 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/asia-confronts-fresh-inflation-fiscal-threats-as-oil-tops-100?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8796>
 as fighting around the Strait of Hormuz intensified, threatening another round 
of inflation across a region heavily dependent on imported energy. Japan, South 
Korea and India face steeper import bills, while governments including 
Indonesia and Thailand may have to spend more to shield consumers from fuel 
costs. Inflation is already sticky, interest rates are high and government 
budgets are stretched, leaving policymakers with fewer options than they had 
when the Iran war began. In a telling snapshot of the squeeze, one economist 
estimates that every $10 increase in Brent could push inflation higher in 
India, shaving 15 basis points off growth.
