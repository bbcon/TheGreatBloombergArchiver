Daily Brief

Tuesday, September 01, 2026

US

  • The 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.78%, the highest since January, as five consecutive sessions of selling reflect intensifying inflation concerns and rising rate-hike expectations following Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks.
  • Economists at Barclays and Société Générale added additional rate hikes to their 2026 forecasts; the US 30-year yield has been above 5% for 55 consecutive days, the longest such stretch since 2006.
  • JPMorgan's trading desk shifted to a "tactically cautious" view on US equities for the coming weeks; S&P 500 futures declined 0.6% and Nasdaq 100 futures fell 1.0%.

Fixed Income and Monetary Policy

The dominant macro signal in US markets is the bond selloff. The 10-year Treasury yield reached 4.78%, its highest since January, extending a five-day climb that reflects markets pricing a higher probability of further Federal Reserve tightening. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks — emphasizing that supply-side inflation driven by energy and food prices requires a policy response — have shifted the consensus. Barclays and Société Générale now incorporate additional hikes into their forecasts, though no Wall Street consensus has emerged; some analysts argue rate hikes cannot effectively address supply-driven inflation. The Friday payrolls report will be pivotal in clarifying the Fed's path.

Fiscal Dynamics and Markets

Treasury Secretary Bessent's bond-buyback program has failed to durably compress long-end yields, with the 30-year rate nearly retracing its pre-announcement levels. US public debt now exceeds $40 trillion, having risen by a third in under five years, and the government faces increasing competition from AI-driven corporate bond issuance for investor capital. Equity markets are adjusting accordingly, with rate-sensitive sectors under pressure ahead of what promises to be a consequential September for policy. ---

Asia

  • South Korea's August exports surged approximately 69% year-on-year, a decisive beat driven by AI-related semiconductor demand — the most significant hard data print in the region this session.
  • China's private Caixin/RatingDog manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 in August from 50.9 in July, above consensus and marking nine consecutive months of expansion, the longest such run in five years.
  • Japan's 10-year government bond yield touched 3.0% for the first time since 1996, with US Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly pressing the Bank of Japan to continue rate hikes amid renewed yen weakness.

Chinese Manufacturing and Growth Momentum

China's export-oriented manufacturers showed resilience in August, with new orders rising for the 15th consecutive month. However, the official PMI remained in contraction for a second month, and Goldman Sachs estimates GDP was expanding at roughly 4% annualised at the start of Q3 — down from 4.3% the prior quarter and below the government's 4.5–5.0% target. Separately, Beijing announced a ban on pre-sales of uncompleted homes without escrow protections, a structural reform of the property market that may reduce new project starts materially from 2027 onward, further pressuring local government land revenues.

Japan and South Korea

Japan's bond market continues its recalibration as the Bank of Japan allows yields to be set by market forces. The 3% threshold on 10-year JGBs is psychologically significant and, combined with external pressure from Bessent, keeps the pace of BOJ normalisation in focus. South Korea's export boom — 69% year-on-year — reflects surging global demand for memory chips and tech hardware. South Korean President Lee Jae-myung's cabinet reshuffled as approval ratings fell to a record-low 42%, with housing affordability cited as the primary source of public dissatisfaction. ---

Europe

  • Euro-area inflation accelerated to its highest level in nearly three years in August, coming in above consensus and cementing expectations for an ECB rate hike at next week's policy meeting.
  • UK shop-price inflation rose to a two-year high of 1.5% in August, adding to cost-of-living pressures as Prime Minister Andy Burnham's government faces a busy autumn political calendar.
  • France's 10-year spread over German Bunds widened to near the highest level since the 2012 sovereign debt crisis, reflecting political uncertainty ahead of a presidential election eight months away.

Eurozone Inflation and ECB Outlook

The August CPI print for the euro area firmed the case for ECB tightening at the September meeting. Energy prices, driven by the Iran conflict's impact on oil markets, appear to be the primary accelerant. The ECB faces a difficult balancing act: hiking into slowing growth to address energy-driven inflation risks exacerbating fiscal stress in high-deficit member states.

UK and France Political Risk

Prime Minister Burnham returns to Parliament facing a crowded agenda — an October budget, a first Labour conference speech, and a meeting with President Trump — while polling against Reform UK remains uncomfortable. In France, political uncertainty is transmitting directly into sovereign spreads. With the fiscal deficit above 5% of GDP and the presidential field fragmented between Marine Le Pen and Jean-Luc Mélenchon, markets are demanding meaningful additional compensation to hold French paper, reflecting unresolved questions about fiscal consolidation under any plausible successor government. ---

LatAm

  • The Trump administration announced what it described as the "biggest oil deal in world history" — securing US control over more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan reserves — a development that has united both Venezuelan government loyalists and opposition figures in opposition.
  • Brazil is entering its agricultural planting season against a global diesel shortage, with the fuel crunch threatening to raise costs for the world's largest exporter of several key crops, with potential ripple effects through global food supply chains.
  • Brazil's 2027 budget proposal pledged a primary surplus, though investor scepticism over President Lula's fiscal commitment persists.

Venezuela Oil Deal

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright's visit to Caracas formalises an arrangement that the Trump administration is framing as a pre-midterm political win and a tool to suppress domestic gasoline prices. The strategic logic is that expectations of future Venezuelan supply will exert downward pressure on prices even before production ramps up materially. Venezuela's acting president Rodríguez has characterised the deal as sovereignty-preserving, though opposition figures question whether an interim government possesses the legitimacy to make long-term commitments over the country's primary resource. The deal may, paradoxically, reinforce Rodríguez's political position by giving Washington a stake in her continuity.

Brazil

The diesel crunch compounds seasonal cost pressures for Brazilian agriculture at a critical moment. Whether higher farmer input costs translate into sustained food-price inflation domestically and globally remains to be seen, but the timing — coinciding with a global bond selloff driven partly by food and energy price concerns — is consequential. ---

Global

  • Two supertankers were struck by projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz, threatening to reverse a recovery in Persian Gulf oil exports that had restored flows to roughly 6–8 million barrels per day, approximately half of prewar levels.
  • Global sovereign bond yields reached their highest since mid-2008, with a Bloomberg gauge of global government debt yielding 3.72%, as oil-driven inflation concerns and hawkish central bank rhetoric compound pre-existing fiscal sustainability pressures.
  • WTI crude rose approximately 2.4% to $87.84, with energy markets highly sensitive to Hormuz developments given the chokepoint's role in global supply.

Strait of Hormuz and Energy Markets

The attacks on two supertankers — one operated by Saudi Arabia's Bahri and one by Sinokor, the firm central to the Hormuz shuttle trade — represent a material threat to the supply recovery that has helped contain crude prices. Saudi Arabia had only recently resumed loading at Ras Tanura after rerouting shipments away from the Houthi-disrupted Red Sea corridor, leaving its export infrastructure exposed simultaneously on both flanks. Trump's characterisation of the conflict as "a relatively little war" notwithstanding, the market implications are not trivial: any sustained disruption to the 6–8 million barrel daily shuttle flow could reignite the energy price pressures already feeding into global inflation.

Global Bond Markets and Monetary Policy Divergence

The synchronised rise in yields across the US, Japan, the UK, and France reflects a common driver — sticky, supply-side inflation — but divergent fiscal and monetary starting points. Japan's 3% 10-year yield marks a historic inflection point for a market long suppressed by BOJ intervention. The global bond selloff creates a feedback loop: higher yields raise debt-servicing costs for already-stretched sovereigns, potentially forcing fiscal tightening that in turn weighs on growth. The resolution of this tension remains genuinely uncertain.
