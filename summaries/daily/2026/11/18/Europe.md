**ECB Leadership**
A Franco-German deal has effectively settled the ECB's succession question. Hernández de Cos, regarded as the more dovish of the two leading candidates, will likely take the presidency — a result that appears to reflect a Macron diplomatic win despite Germany's traditional preference for hawkish nominees. Berlin secured the chief economist role for Princeton economist Markus Brunnermeier when Philip Lane departs in May. France is expected to claim the markets portfolio currently held by Isabel Schnabel, with former OECD Chief Economist Laurence Boone the leading candidate. The composition matters: should French sovereign spreads deteriorate further, Boone would likely be in operational control of any bond-market stabilization measures.

**France: Political and Fiscal Stress**
France's political situation is deteriorating along multiple dimensions simultaneously. Street protests over cost of living, a minority government facing a budget impasse, and rising borrowing costs are converging ahead of next year's presidential election. Mélenchon has risen to 17-18.5% in polls — currently the most probable opponent for Marine Le Pen in a second round — and his platform includes canceling the roughly 20% of French sovereign debt held by the ECB, a measure the ECB considers illegal. Investors are unlikely to treat this as background noise.
