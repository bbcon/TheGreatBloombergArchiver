AI Silver Lining

Amid the bloodbath 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/us-stock-futures-diverge-as-chip-selloff-extends-into-second-day?cmpid=BBD110626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11078>
 in UStechnology-related stocks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/us-stock-futures-diverge-as-chip-selloff-extends-into-second-day?cmpid=BBD110626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11078>
, with markets plunging the most since 2020, it’s worth noting that a slowdown 
in AI investment may end up being agood thing for the real economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/ai-investment-bust-seen-less-likely-with-data-center-backlash?cmpid=BBD110626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11078>
 over time. That’s because it would keep a check on a possible bubble.

Artificial intelligence has helped fuel the recent acceleration 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/how-much-does-the-us-economy-depend-on-ai?cmpid=BBD110626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11078>
 in US GDP growth, with billions of dollars poured by tech firms into computing 
power and a spurt in data-center construction. With the breakneck speed and 
scale of the investments, however, came increased concerns of overcapacity and 
a burst.

Now that Democrats have notched historic wins in states that have seen the 
fastest expansion in data centers — chief among them Texas — the expectation is 
that we’ll see more regulation and guardrails.

Paradoxically, a slowdown could safeguard the economy against a downturn by 
pacing capital investment over a longer horizon and, as Brett Ryan at Deutsche 
Bank Securities put it, killing “some projects that were already looking shaky.”

Many candidates who won in the midterm elections Tuesday have campaigned on 
restricting, and in some cases, imposing moratoriums on, new developments. But 
they don’t necessarily oppose data centers outright. Ohio Governor-elect Amy 
Acton, for one, wants all costs to be covered by the owners and their investors 
— gas, water, electricity etc. — and she wants to rein in tax incentives.

More than 45% of planned data-center capacity is in states where newly elected 
Democrats campaigned on data-center restrictions, based on Goldman Sachs 
estimates. Texas accounts for 25% of the total, and Gina Hinojosa, the first 
Democrat to be elected governor there since 1990, has vowed to halt new 
approvals.

Economists generally expect any moratorium will be temporary, giving 
regulators time to put guardrails in place. The Democratic surge, by itself, 
isn’t going to derail the AI growth trajectory.

But the equities rout, if it continues, would hit the real economy. By 
boosting the broader US stock market to record highs, AI has helped fuel a 
wealth effect that enabled Americans to keep spending in spite of rising costs 
of living.

If consumers start feeling poorer because the stock market is tanking, they 
may cut spending, and weaken a key engine of the economy.

Need-to-Know Research

The Rhodium Group published this week a deep-dive analysis 
<https://rhg.com/research/global-impacts-of-chinas-chemicals-buildout/> of the 
Chinese chemical industry for the US Chamber of Commerce that concludes this 
sector’s overcapacity threatens deep damage to other nations and will require 
international coordination to address.

Problems were baked in once the Chinese property market began to collapse in 
2021, removing a key source of demand for chemicals — “key building blocks of 
nearly all manufacturing industries,” Rhodium’s Jeremy Smith wrote in the 
report. Chinese companies that had invested in expanding their capacity had to 
send their output overseas,roiling the market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-23/germany-s-troubled-chemical-makers-risk-disorderly-decline?cmpid=BBD110626_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11078>
 for others.

Beijing’s efforts to stabilize its industry have focused on ending price wars, 
upgrading facilities and building more integrated clusters, but none of these 
measures are likely to benefit overseas producers, Smith wrote. “Effective 
policy responses to this multidimensional challenge will require a broad range 
of countries to coordinate along full value chains,” he wrote.
