• 30-year Treasury yields rose to 5.29%-5.33%, the highest since 2007, extending a global long-bond selloff that is reshaping asset allocation.
• New York's Empire State manufacturing index expanded in August by the most since late 2021, with orders growth and backlogs firm despite worsening supply availability.
• Foreign holdings of US Treasuries fell $72.1 billion in June, led by Japan and China, the third such monthly decline in four months since February's record.
• Apple shares are down 10% from their July 28 peak, among the S&P 500's 25 worst performers over three weeks, as investors reward AI-spending peers Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet instead.

**Rates and Fiscal Pressure**
The rise in 30-year yields to a 19-year high reflects the budget deficit outlook, a surge in AI-related corporate debt issuance competing with Treasuries, and a shifting buyer base, according to Barclays — rather than higher inflation expectations, which have stayed low and stable. Yardeni Research said it is not "pushing the panic button" but is watching for bond vigilantes, drawing a parallel to 2023, when a similar run from 4% to 5% ultimately proved a buying opportunity.

**Equities Diverge on AI Exposure**
Equities fell as rising yields and firmer oil dented risk appetite, even as a Bank of America survey showed bullish positioning at its highest in nearly five years. Apple's avoidance of heavy AI spending, once seen as prudent, has become a drag now the AI trade is back in favor, while Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet rallied as results eased overspending concerns.
