**Macro Data: Indonesia Outperforms**
Indonesia's Q3 GDP print of 5.5% represented a meaningful upside surprise, with domestic demand — particularly household consumption and government-led investment — serving as the primary growth engine. The result is notable given the absence of significant AI-linked export tailwinds that have benefited peers, and underscores the resilience of Southeast Asia's largest economy to external headwinds including elevated energy prices and global financial volatility.

**Banking Sector Under Pressure**
Asia-Pacific bank earnings are deteriorating. National Australia Bank reported a rise in credit impairment charges and announced cuts of up to 2,500 roles, while Singapore's DBS suffered a decline in markets trading income. UOB flagged narrowing interest margins amid intense deposit competition, and Westpac reported impairment charges more than doubling year-on-year. The common thread across Australian and Singaporean lenders is higher funding costs compressing net interest income ahead of any meaningful rate relief — a challenging setup for upcoming results from Commonwealth Bank and Japan's megabanks.

**AI and Technology Dynamics**
China's block of the ABB-SoftBank robotics deal reflects Beijing's sharpened focus on technology sovereignty in the AI era. Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs characterized the US Democratic sweep as having "major implications for AI and data center regulation," driving the rotation away from hardware manufacturers and toward Chinese internet platforms and other discounted assets. Hong Kong stocks staged a recovery as capital rotated into perceived beneficiaries of reduced US AI capital spending.
