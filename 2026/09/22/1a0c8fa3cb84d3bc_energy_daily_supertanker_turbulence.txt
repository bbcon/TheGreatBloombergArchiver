Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Senior Reporter Alex Longley looks at how record earnings are trickling down 
through the global tanker fleet.

There’s no precedent for what’s happening right now in the supertanker market.

Disrupted by 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/surging-tanker-rates-signal-a-deepening-global-energy-crisis?cmpid=BBBXT092226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9183>
 the Iran war and Houthi threats in the Red Sea, the cost of renting a ship to 
transport oil has soared to levels never seen before.

It’s beyond the wildest dreams of many shipowners, for whom last year’s 
average rate of about $58,000 a day was just fine. Today their very large crude 
carriers are earningmore than $1.2 million a day 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-09-21/oil-market-the-1-million-a-day-oil-tanker-will-drive-inflation?cmpid=BBBXT092226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9183>
 to sail from Saudi Arabia’s Persian Gulf terminals to Asia.

Even for journeys that don’t have to run the gauntlet of the Strait of Hormuz 
—where vessels are stillat risk of being attacked 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-06/iran-says-it-targeted-oil-tankers-in-response-to-us-strikes?cmpid=BBBXT092226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9183>
 — earnings have surged to about two-thirds of that amount.
A tanker passing through the waters of the Strait of Hormuz in February.
Photographer: Fadel Senna/AFP/Getty Images
Oil tanker executives often say that where the biggest ships go, other vessels 
will follow, and there are already signs that is the case.

Suezmax tankers capable of hauling 1 million barrels of crude, or half as much 
as a VLCC, are earning north of $300,000 a day. That gives an incentive to 
divide up the biggest cargoes — also known in the industry as “stems” — onto 
multiple smaller vessels.

“VLCC stems are routinely being split onto two Suezmaxes or three Aframaxes,” 
analysts at tanker brokerage BRS wrote in a note.

A knock-on effect of this cargo splitting is that even some so-called clean 
tankers, which carry refined fuels like gasoline and diesel, are starting to 
transport crude instead because it’s more profitable.

This trickle-down of record earnings from supertankers to smaller vessels is 
good news for the world’s shipowners. The largest oil tanker equities saw their 
valuation top $70 billion last week for the first time ever, according to data 
compiled by Bloomberg.

Commodity trader Trafigura Group is looking to raise $500 million by spinning 
off part of its tanker business 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/trafigura-to-list-tanker-unit-in-norway-as-shipping-rates-soar?cmpid=BBBXT092226_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9183>
, and says the boom could be here to stay as Middle Eastern producers snap up 
vessels.

It’s not so good for consumers. For example, some European refiners are now 
being offered local crude cargoes at huge premiums, with sellers cognizant of 
the fact that seeking barrels from further afield brings with it an additional 
cost.

It’s just another indication of how the waves caused by President Donald 
Trump’s decision to go to war with Iran continue to spread through the global 
economy.

—Alex Longley, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Installed data center power capacity in EMEA is poised to grow two and a half 
times in the coming decade, according to BloombergNEF’s base case. Over half of 
this growth is contained in the five largest markets — the UK, Germany, Spain, 
Finland and France. Estimated average power draw is 28 gigawatts in 2035, 
translating into 246 terawatt hours of annual energy demand.
