Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Mitchell Ferman looks at how oil majors and traders have seen profits 
soar amid the war in the Middle East.

A defining theme emerging this earnings season is that oil trading and 
refining are booming, despite the energy-market disruptions unleashed by the 
Iran war.

Shell Plc 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SHEL:LN?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
 offered perhaps the clearest example this morning: Europe’s largest energy 
company more than doubled quarterly profit from a year earlier even as gas 
production fell by roughly a third after war-related outages in Qatar.

Record refinery utilization and strong trading more than offset the production 
hit, Shell said. The same pattern echoed across the industry.TotalEnergies SE 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TTE:FP?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
 credited stronger refining and trading for liftingearnings 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/totalenergies-profit-jumps-68-as-war-upends-energy-markets?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
,Eni SpA 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/ENI:IM?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
benefited <bbg://news/stories/TIX9V4T9NJLV> from higher processing margins and 
Glencore Plc 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GLEN:LN?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
 said its trading operation ison track 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/glencore-says-its-traders-post-first-half-profit-of-3-3-billion?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
 for one of its best years ever.

The gains aren’t simply a result of higher crude prices. The conflict has 
forced oil, fuels and natural gas into unfamiliar trade routes. Increased 
shipping distances have widened regional price gaps and tightened supplies of 
products such as diesel and jet fuel.

Those dislocations have created lucrative opportunities for companies able to 
source, transport and process energy across multiple markets.
Eni SpA’s oil refinery in Sannazzaro de’ Burgondi, Italy.Photographer: 
Francesca Volpi/Bloomberg
The second quarter also showed why producers are investing further down the 
value chain. US shale firmExpand Energy Corp. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/EXE:US?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
 agreed this week to buy gas marketer Twin Eagle Holdings NA LLC, reflecting 
growing demand for trading, marketing and optimization capabilities alongside 
production.

In refining, Repsol SA 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/REP:SM?cmpid=BBBXT073026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7167>
 sees little sign the windfall is ending any time soon. The Spanish energy 
company’s chief executive said processing margins should remain “healthy” into 
2027, citing resilient fuel demand, inventory rebuilding and the lasting impact 
of lower Russian diesel supplies even if shipping through the Strait of Hormuz 
returns to normal.

The common denominator is disruption. Cargoes have been rerouted, regional 
price differences widened, fuel markets tightened and buyers scrambled for 
replacement supplies at a premium. For companies that trade, refine and market 
oil and gas, those dislocations have fueled profits this earnings season.

—Mitchell Ferman, Bloomberg News
