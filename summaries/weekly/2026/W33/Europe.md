**Eurozone Growth and Policy**
Europe is navigating the Iran war's economic disruption with greater resilience than earlier forecasts suggested. A Bloomberg analyst survey projects eurozone growth to quicken from Q2's already-surprising strength, and Citi's macro surprise index reaching a three-year high this week reinforces that momentum. BNP Paribas's Sophie Huynh described the current configuration as a "sweet spot" — an economy robust enough to support equities without forcing the ECB's hand — with the euro trading near a two-month high against the dollar on Fed uncertainty. German bunds outperformed Treasuries over the week. Banque de France expects French momentum to persist this quarter on cooler inflation and growth in key sectors. ABN Amro shares rose 6.3% in Amsterdam Monday on a profit beat; Vestas surged the most in four years in Copenhagen on higher profit guidance and a buyback announcement.

**UK: Growth, Labor, and Political Backdrop**
Thursday's GDP data confirmed the UK economy outperformed expectations through the first half, with business investment up 1.7% in Q2 and strength in information and communications services cited as a tentative early signal of AI-driven productivity gains. The labor market has also stabilized: a closely watched survey showed hiring held steady in July, ending a contraction stretching back to the Liz Truss gilt crisis of 2022, with temporary vacancies rising for the first time in two years. The Bank of England cautioned, however, that the full economic effects of the Iran war are still feeding through to households, with growth expected to slow in the second half as utility bills rise. Prime Minister Andy Burnham faces an early budget test in October, weighing a rebalancing of business taxes toward larger firms. Internal Treasury modeling released mid-week showed the economy would barely grow next year if Hormuz disruption persists through end-2026.

**Heat, Drought, and Industrial Strain**
Extreme weather is emerging as a material and recurring economic headwind. Rhine water levels at Kaub fell to their lowest since 1880, stranding barge traffic and forcing chemical and steel producers including Lanxess to shift shipments to rail and road at higher cost. Drought has been declared across almost three-quarters of England, with reservoir storage roughly 12% below average. Delayed autumn wheat sowing and record-low Danube levels threaten to compound already elevated global food prices. Oxford Economics flagged low Rhine flows as inflationary, as gas increasingly sets marginal power prices while hydropower and nuclear output are curtailed — EDF shut three reactors at Gravelines mid-week after jellyfish swarms during the heat spike. Only around 20% of European households have air conditioning versus 76% in North America, leaving a continent warming roughly twice as fast as the global average poorly equipped for this structural shift.
