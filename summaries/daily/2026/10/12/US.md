• No major US macro data releases today; Treasury markets closed for Columbus Day; S&P 500 futures gained 0.1%–0.3% with the index approaching all-time highs.
• September CPI — the week's primary event — due Wednesday, with consensus expecting headline to rise 0.6% MoM (largest in five months) driven by gasoline, while core is seen up 0.2% MoM and 2.5% YoY.
• Bank stocks remain under pressure; the KBW Bank Index is down roughly 13% since August on AI disruption concerns, though Wall Street earnings this week may provide a catalyst for re-rating.
• Goldman Sachs strategists turned positive on global equities ahead of what is expected to be a strong Q3 earnings season.

**Monetary Policy Outlook**
Wednesday's CPI print will be the dominant domestic event of the week and the primary determinant of near-term Federal Reserve expectations. The anticipated composition — a gasoline-driven headline spike alongside moderating core — would support the prevailing market view that the Fed will hold rates steady at its next meeting. Core CPI at 2.5% YoY would remain above target but represent only a modest firming, insufficient on its own to shift the policy calculus materially. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.24% ahead of the holiday closure, a level that continues to exert pressure on rate-sensitive sectors.

**Equity and Credit Markets**
With Columbus Day closing the Treasury cash market, equities set the tone. S&P 500 futures hovered near record levels as investors weighed solid earnings expectations against elevated geopolitical risk. Bank stocks will be in focus this week: JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, and Citi all report Tuesday. Analysts at Morgan Stanley characterize the sector's recent selloff as an attractive entry point, though Wells Fargo cautions that dispersion between winners and losers is likely to be wider than usual this quarter, with higher rates pressuring fixed-income trading and M&A pipelines remaining thin.
