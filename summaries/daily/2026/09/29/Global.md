**US–Iran and Energy Markets**
Iranian officials have privately concluded that reaching a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz before November US midterms is unlikely, given Washington's insistence on resolving nuclear program questions as a precondition. This assessment meaningfully reduces the probability of near-term supply normalization and reinforces elevated energy prices. European gas storage has reached its lowest recorded level for this point in the year — a direct consequence of the supply shock compounding the structural shift away from Russian pipeline gas. LNG Canada shareholders approved a capacity doubling, reflecting long-term demand expectations among Asian buyers, whose contracting behavior is already being reshaped by Hormuz disruption risks.

**Global Bond Sell-Off and EM Stress**
The synchronized global rise in bond yields is transmitting stress most acutely into emerging market currencies. The Mexican peso has weakened as the dollar carry trade unwinds — investors who borrowed dollars to invest in higher-yielding peso assets are closing positions as US yields rise. The JPMorgan EM FX index has fallen to a year-to-date low. The dollar has recovered to above its 200-day moving average, though it remains well below levels prevailing at the start of the Trump administration. The primary uncertainty is whether the bond sell-off reflects a durable repricing of the US neutral rate or whether an incoming negative US data surprise — PCE, ISM, or payrolls — could arrest the move. Until that is resolved, the transmission of US financial conditions into global markets will remain the dominant cross-asset theme.
