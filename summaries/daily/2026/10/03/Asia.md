**India Manufacturing Outlook**
India's manufacturing ambitions, championed under Prime Minister Modi's 12-year tenure, face a structurally difficult environment. Despite production-linked incentive schemes, tax cuts, and significant infrastructure investment, manufacturing's share of GDP has remained range-bound at 15–17%. Bank of America estimates that achieving even a partial step toward the government's 25% target would require manufacturing to grow at least 10% annually for 15 consecutive years — a demanding threshold given current structural constraints including high compliance burdens (estimated 1,400 obligations annually per firm), uneven labor skills, elevated input costs, and logistics inefficiencies relative to China.

**Supply Chain and Trade Risks**
India's most competitive manufacturing segment — electronics — remains exposed to dual-direction risk. Critical components and machinery sourced from China face tightening export controls from Beijing, while the US, India's largest goods export market, has imposed 126% preliminary duties on Indian solar panels. These dynamics suggest that India's manufacturing progress, while real in pockets, rests on foundations that appear fragile — dependent on government incentives scheduled to expire around 2030, after which industry competitiveness must stand independently.
