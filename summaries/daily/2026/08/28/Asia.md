• Philippine GDP growth unexpectedly slowed to 2.3% in Q2, missing the 2.9% consensus and down sharply from 5.4% a year earlier, as consumers were squeezed by inflation and a weak peso.
• Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas hiked rates for a third straight time, reversing a mid-August signal it would be "less aggressive"; Governor Eli Remolona said "we will tighten as much as we need," projecting inflation stays above target until 2028.
• The peso fell past 62/dollar to a record low on elevated oil prices, and the stock benchmark is the worst-performing global index this week.
• Japan spent a record $96.4 billion on yen intervention over the past month, while Tokyo inflation ticked up again, bolstering the case for a BOJ rate hike.
• Sydney developer Bathla Group's insolvency, tied to $2.3 billion in debt, is stoking fears about Australia's A$200 billion private-credit market.

**Inflation and Growth Trade-off**
The Philippines' combination of slowing growth, above-target inflation and currency weakness is being read as a warning sign for other oil-import-dependent economies, notably India and Indonesia, as Brent hovers near $90 and US borrowing costs stay elevated.

**China Policy and Corporates**
Sinopec's chairman became the highest-ranking Chinese oil official to declare the country has likely already passed peak oil demand, as crude imports fell an estimated 3.3 million barrels a day in Q2 year-on-year and EVs took 64% of July passenger-vehicle sales. Separately, Beijing's tax crackdown has clawed back roughly 7.7 billion yuan ($1.1 billion) from over 100 listed firms, and Alibaba shares stayed pressured after its $10 billion AI-financing placement.
