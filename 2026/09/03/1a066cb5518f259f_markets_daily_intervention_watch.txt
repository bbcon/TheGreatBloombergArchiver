Jumpy moves

This sudden rally in the yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/yen-traders-brace-for-holiday-intervention-risk-around-boj?cmpid=090326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8483>
 highlights a growing risk in markets: More than ever, it seems, traders have 
to be on alert for policy makers putting their thumbs on the scale.

When governments step in to nudge prices in their preferred direction, traders 
almost always express skepticism that it’ll have a lasting effect. Economic and 
fiscal fundamentals will always win out over short-term fixes, the thinking 
goes.

While that may be true, those fixes can cause massive moves that ripple across 
markets for days or weeks. Just look at Scott Bessent’s surprise plan to 
increase the US Treasury’s repurchases of government bonds, which succeeded in 
pushing yields lower until oil spiked again.

Then there’s also what’s happening in Venezuela. The US has taken a stake in a 
company 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/as-us-venezuela-oil-deal-takes-shape-here-are-the-key-points?cmpid=090326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8483>
 that’s developing the country’s oilfields.

In the case of the yen, traders spent weeks questioning the long-term 
effectiveness of intervention. But now they’re focused once more on the 
short-term risk of authorities wading back into the market.

“After any jumpy move, on any trading desk, the first thing anybody will say 
is ‘intervention?’” said Bart Wakabayashi at State Street Bank & Trust in 
Tokyo. “As we saw last night, I think the market will remain very sensitive, 
very jittery.” —Phil Serafino

Dollar risk

Sift through the filings of pension funds and insurers around the world and 
one thing stands out: Some of the biggest holders of US assets have little 
protection against a weaker dollar,leaving the currency at risk of steeper 
declines 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/world-s-unusually-high-dollar-exposure-risks-fueling-selloff?cmpid=090326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8483>
 if sentiment suddenly turns.

Across markets including Japan, Canada and Taiwan, these investors hedged just 
41% of their foreign-currency exposure as of June 30 — the lowest since at 
least 2015 — according to Bloomberg calculations using data from six markets 
where such figures are available.

While not a complete picture, it offers a glimpse into how the sudden rush 
last year to hedge against dollar losses triggered by President Donald Trump’s 
global tariff rollout has faded as the US currency slowly stabilized.

“Given the scale of foreign holdings of US assets, it doesn’t take a dramatic 
change in positioning to matter,” said Laura Cooper at investment manager 
Nuveen. —Ruth Carson 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS35-mFs7Fw/ruth-carson?cmpid=090326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8483>
