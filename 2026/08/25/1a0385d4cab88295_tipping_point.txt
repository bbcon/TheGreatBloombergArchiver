The most revealing moment in Scott Bessent’s briefing on the US’s “economic 
D-Day” against Iran came when the Treasury secretary was asked why he wasn’t 
yet imposing any of his advertised sanctions.

“Why would I want to blow up the global financial system 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/bessent-pledges-to-isolate-iran-from-global-economy?cmpid=BBD082526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8149>
?” he replied.

It was an admission that unwittingly echoed Iran’s parliament speaker and lead 
negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf. In a post on X, he ridiculed the US moves 
as yielding nothing more than an “Iran boomerang” that would return to hit 
Washington in the face.

The US has been sanctioning Iran for almost half a century and little has 
materially changed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/how-iran-s-economy-is-enduring-trump-s-war-us-blockade?cmpid=BBD082526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8149>
 for the power of the theocratic system over that time.

Even a joint US-Israeli war, for all the damage and death that it’s brought 
Iranians, didn’t manage to dislodge all the men who’ve been defying US and 
Western interests in the Middle East since 1979.

Repeatedly, Iran has adapted and found ways to circumvent the US sanctions 
machine. The penalties have been around so long that they’ve become enmeshed 
within the Iranian economic system and normalized.
Bessent in Washington yesterday.Photographer: Kent Nishimura/AFP/Getty Images
That doesn’t mean this latest iteration cannot inflict more pain.

The US is most likely counting on a repeat of last December, when the currency 
collapsed; that Iranians will pour out onto the streets and risk their lives 
demanding change.

There are signs in Tehran that some officials are concerned over hitting a 
tipping point 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/trump-s-iran-pivot-from-war-to-sanctions-will-hit-china-or-fail?cmpid=BBD082526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8149>
 that will deliver that sort of existential threat to the regime.

The weeks ahead will show whether Bessent has indeed thrown a boomerang at 
Iran and the wider global economy, as hardliners in Tehran may wish to believe 
— or whether he can deliver the capitulation President Donald Trump so 
desperately seeks.

The only certainty meantime is that millions of families in Iran, already 
struggling to make ends meet 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/iranians-struggle-to-afford-groceries-as-trump-squeezes-economy?cmpid=BBD082526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8149>
, are set to pay the cost. — Golnar Motevalli 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARwAJnhPlhE/golnar-motevalli?cmpid=BBD082526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8149>

Chart of the Day

California-based asset manager Pimco is now the dominant foreign player in 
Colombia’s local debt market after snapping up about 41 trillion pesos ($13 
billion) in government bonds under then-President Gustavo Petro while others 
exited or reduced their exposure during the leftist leader’s tenure. So far,
that bet is paying off 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/pimco-wins-big-from-betting-on-colombia-while-other-funds-fled?cmpid=BBD082526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8149>
, with returns turbocharged by the world’s biggest currency rally and some of 
the highest yields in emerging markets amid investor optimism over the election 
of De la Espriella, who took office this month.
