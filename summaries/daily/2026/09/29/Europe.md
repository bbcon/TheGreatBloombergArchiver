**Inflation and Monetary Policy**
Spanish CPI reached 5.0% year-on-year, and eurozone-wide figures due this week are expected to reflect similarly elevated dynamics — the fastest aggregate pace since 2023. ECB President Lagarde's testimony to EU lawmakers on Monday appeared to temper market expectations for an October rate hike, but a December move is now widely anticipated. The inflation trajectory remains heavily dependent on energy prices, which in turn are linked directly to the duration of the US–Iran conflict and its disruption of Middle Eastern gas supply.

**ECB Succession and Energy**
Three senior ECB positions are simultaneously open: President Lagarde has signaled a possible early departure, Chief Economist Philip Lane's term ends in May, and Schnabel's resignation removes a key hawkish voice from the Executive Board. The front-runners for the presidency are Bank for International Settlements chief Pablo Hernandez de Cos and former Dutch central bank President Klaas Knot, though EU leadership negotiations are inherently opaque. Separately, Europe's gas storage economics have broken down for a second consecutive year: the loss of Russian pipeline supply and elevated LNG prices driven by the Middle East conflict have flattened the summer-winter price spread, reducing the commercial incentive to inject gas into storage. Inventories are at their lowest recorded level for this point in the year.
