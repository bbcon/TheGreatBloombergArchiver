Mixed Messages

European Central Bank policymakers didn’t need to talk for long on Thursday 
when it came to their decision tokeep rates unchanged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/ecb-keeps-rate-steady-to-gauge-iran-war-s-impact-on-inflation?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
.

According to President Christine Lagarde, their meeting was already over by 
about 10:15 a.m. in Frankfurt.

But it turns out quite a lot happened all the same. On a day when oil hit $100 
a barrel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/gulf-states-seen-splurging-on-debt-to-build-new-hormuz-bypasses?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
 for the first time in two months, some of her colleagues actually asked 
whether an immediate hike might be warranted. They then opted to back a steady 
outcome.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-07-23/lagarde-says-ecb-s-decision-to-hold-rates-was-unanimous-video?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
Officials are now ready to increase borrowing costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/ecb-officials-are-ready-to-raise-interest-rates-in-september?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
 at their next meeting in September unless the inflation outlook improves 
markedly, according to people familiar with the matter.

That’s not what they’re saying in public 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/ecb-officials-avoid-clear-hints-on-possible-rate-hike-for-now?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
 though. The ECB’s formal policy is to decide things meeting-by-meeting. Both 
the chiefs of the German and French central banks declined to give clear hints 
on amove 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/ecb-still-more-likely-to-hike-rates-than-hold-simkus-says?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
.

“We will have lots of new data in September as well as new projections,” 
Bundesbank PresidentJoachim Nagel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/nagel-says-ecb-in-good-position-stays-quiet-on-next-rate-move?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
 said. “We will also reassess the outlook for inflation then.”

In truth, seven weeks will pass before that meeting, during which hostilities 
in the Middle East could eitherescalate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/tanker-exits-red-sea-dark-as-another-china-ship-heads-to-strait?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
 or subside.

Even so the mixed messaging from the ECB is notable. 

Much the same might be said for the president herself. After recent comments 
that suggested she might want toleave her job early 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/lagarde-watchers-cast-aside-doubts-to-say-she-ll-finish-ecb-term?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
, Lagarde was invited to commit to staying on for the rest of her term.

What she offered instead was a pledge to remain in her post — but only for the 
rest of this year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/lagarde-says-she-s-staying-at-the-ecb-at-least-until-year-end?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
.

“You are not going to see the back of me before 2027,” Lagarde told reporters 
on Thursday, before adding that she won’t exit in adverse circumstances. “When 
there are clouds on the horizon, the captain stays on the ship,” she said.

From that answer, it’s not clear how long she will actually stay. But the 
message to governments is clear enough — they may soon need to pick a successor.

Need-to-Know Research

The overall US trade gap has shrunk so far this year — partly due to a surge 
in imports in early 2025 to get ahead of expected tariffs, which distorted the 
base of comparison — butdeficits with Asian nations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-07/us-trade-deficit-widens-to-biggest-in-more-than-a-year?cmpid=BBD072426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6894>
 key to the AI buildout have soared, led by a 110% jump for Taiwan.

That may be a sign of things to come. The AI boom appears to match past 
patterns of “investment-specific” technology shocks that see sustained 
deterioration in the current account (the broadest measure of trade), Federal 
Reserve economists including Giuseppe Fioriwrote 
<https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/technology-shocks-the-ai-boomandthe-u-s-current-account-20260714.html>
 in a note earlier this month.

An investment-specific shock is “a process in which rapid technological 
progress makes each new generation of capital equipment significantly cheaper 
and more powerful than the last, but where reaping those efficiency gains 
requires continuous and substantial investment to acquire and deploy the new 
vintage of capital goods,” Fiori and his colleagues wrote.

“While the historical estimates suggest that the external deficit associated 
with a technology boom gradually unwinds over several quarters, the current 
episode may prove more protracted,” they said.
