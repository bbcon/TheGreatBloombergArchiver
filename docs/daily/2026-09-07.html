Daily Brief

Monday, September 07, 2026

US

  • US equity markets closed for Labor Day; S&P 500 futures little changed at 7,718, with Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.2%.
  • August jobs report (released Friday) beat consensus, delivering a hawkish surprise that pushed Treasury yields higher and reinforced expectations of a September Fed rate hike.
  • Federal Reserve policymakers sent diverging signals ahead of the September 15–16 meeting, with Chair Warsh hawkish, NY Fed's Williams dovish, and Governor Waller intermediate.
  • Core CPI for July stood at 3.3% YoY, broadly unchanged from June, with August data due Friday representing a potential swing factor for the rate decision.

Monetary Policy

The Federal Reserve's internal debate has become unusually visible ahead of the September 15–16 meeting. Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole address characterized recent inflation data as insufficient evidence that underlying trends have "meaningfully improved," setting what analysts at Barclays described as a higher bar for a hold. New York Fed President Williams offered a markedly more dovish read, citing encouraging recent data and tariff effects fading into the "rearview mirror." Governor Waller further complicated interpretation by pivoting from 12-month to 3-month trend measures of core inflation — a shift analysts at Inflation Insights characterized as a meaningful change in reaction function. The September vote now carries unusual informational value about the distribution of Fed preferences under Warsh's leadership. August CPI data due Friday may prove decisive.

Markets

With US markets closed for Labor Day, price discovery was limited. S&P 500 futures were essentially flat, though the broader backdrop remains one of resilience: credit spreads remain tight, equity volatility is subdued, and corporate earnings are growing above 20% year-on-year. JPMorgan notes deteriorating Treasury market liquidity without comparable stress in risk assets — a tension that appears sustainable for now but merits monitoring if yields continue to rise. ---

Asia

  • South Korea's Kospi surged 4.6%, reaching 6,995, led by technology and memory chip names; Goldman Sachs maintains a 12,000 target.
  • Japan's foreign reserve holdings fell a record $87.8 billion in August, consistent with the scale of recent yen intervention; the yen strengthened 1.4% to 154.06 against the dollar.
  • China announced a $45 billion recapitalization of its largest banks and insurers — the country's largest such exercise in nearly two decades — as growth slows and property sector stress persists.
  • Malaysia is actively evaluating Huawei AI accelerators for a 494 million USD sovereign AI initiative, which would be the first known instance of a foreign government officially choosing Chinese AI chips over American ones.

China: Financial System and Property

Beijing's $45 billion capital injection into major banks and insurers reflects the cumulative pressure from a prolonged property downturn. Macquarie estimates housing starts have fallen 78% from their 2021 peak, returning to 2003 levels — a contraction comparable in magnitude to the US housing bust of 2005–2009 and exceeding Japan's multi-decade adjustment in speed. Home prices have reverted to 2016 levels and rents to 2015 levels, with self-reinforcing expectations of further declines extending the bottoming process. The recapitalization diluted existing shareholders; PICC fell 3.1%, China Reinsurance 3%, and China Life 1.1% in Hong Kong trading.

Japan and Korea

Japan's Finance Ministry data confirm the country liquidated approximately $87.8 billion in foreign securities — likely short-dated Treasuries — to fund its record yen intervention. The yen has since recovered to 154.06, surpassing the post-intervention peak. In Korea, the Kospi's 4.6% gain reflects renewed conviction in the AI memory cycle; Goldman's Timothy Moe argues investors are underestimating the duration of AI-driven chip demand, maintaining a 12,000 Kospi target against a backdrop of sharp recent volatility. ---

Europe

  • Germany's AfD secured 44% of the vote in Saxony-Anhalt — its best-ever state election result — leaving it three seats short of an outright majority.
  • European bond yields have risen more than any other G7 region over the past month; German 30-year yields are demanding the highest risk premium since 2011.
  • Natural gas prices have gained more than 120% since the onset of the Iran war, reaching a three-year high last week and driving euro-area inflation expectations higher.
  • The ECB is widely expected to deliver a second consecutive rate hike on Thursday; the policy debate has already shifted toward whether a third hike is warranted.

Political Economy

The AfD's Saxony-Anhalt result — more than double its prior support — reflects compounding grievances: energy costs in the region have risen sharply, with gasoline and diesel up approximately 40% since Hormuz disruptions began, and industrial employers including Dow Inc. are closing facilities. The result reinforces European political fragmentation with material policy implications: National Rally's Jordan Bardella has vowed to oppose French tax increases needed to narrow the fiscal deficit, while expressing skepticism toward unconditional Ukraine support — developments that directly complicate eurozone fiscal consolidation and NATO cohesion.

Bond Markets and Monetary Policy

French, Italian, and UK longer-term yields have risen the most in the G7 over the past month. The proximate driver is energy-linked inflation re-acceleration, but political risk premia are compounding the move. The ECB's anticipated Thursday hike — a quarter-point increase widely pre-signaled — is increasingly viewed as insufficient, with the debate shifting to November. UK Chancellor Healey used his first major speech to emphasize fiscal discipline alongside a regional growth agenda; separately, a UK hiring survey showed full-time staff additions in August, data the Bank of England watches closely. ---

LatAm

  • No significant macro data releases for the region on this date; rate decisions from Chile and Turkey are due later in the week.
  • A Brazilian court suspended all environmental permits for Sigma Lithium's Grota do Cirilo lithium project, halting operations entirely.
  • Commodity dynamics remain the dominant regional transmission channel, with Brent at $97/barrel and Goldman warning of a potential move to $120 — a meaningful terms-of-trade shift for oil exporters including Brazil and Colombia.

Commodities and Corporate

The Sigma Lithium court order represents a material setback for Brazil's position in the global critical minerals supply chain. The Grota do Cirilo project had been among the more advanced lithium operations outside the lithium triangle, and its suspension — driven by environmental permitting disputes — highlights regulatory risk in Brazilian mining that investors in the energy transition will need to price. Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs notes a revival in Chinese oil demand is generating price spikes in crude supply chains from Congo to Brazil, a dynamic that benefits Brazilian pre-salt producers even as downstream refining margins face pressure from elevated feedstock costs. ---

Global

  • Iran and the US conducted their largest tit-for-tat tanker strikes to date over the weekend; Brent crude last traded at $97/barrel, with Goldman Sachs warning of a potential rally to $120 if shipping disruptions broaden.
  • Iran signaled a deal with Oman to manage Strait of Hormuz shipping may be days away; Qatari LNG tankers are repositioning toward the Persian Gulf in apparent anticipation.
  • Global bond yields are rising across all major markets, led by Europe, in a coordinated repricing of inflation risk driven primarily by energy.

Oil Markets and Strait of Hormuz

The weekend tanker strikes represent a continued escalation of the six-month US-Iran conflict that has fundamentally disrupted global energy shipping. Natural gas prices have gained more than 120% since the conflict began, and oil at $97/barrel remains well above pre-war levels. Goldman's commodity team recommends long positions in natural gas and diesel as instruments to capture further upside. The reported Oman-mediated deal briefly erased oil's intraday gains, suggesting markets view a genuine Hormuz arrangement as price-negative; however, whether such a deal would hold given the current trajectory of US-Iran tensions remains deeply uncertain.

Global Monetary Policy and Risk Assets

The global bond selloff is proving, so far, to have limited contagion into risk assets. Credit spreads remain tight and equity volatility subdued despite multi-year highs in sovereign yields across G7 economies. The disconnect may reflect strong corporate earnings growth — above 20% year-on-year in the US — and still-easy financial conditions overall. The ECB is set to hike Thursday; the Fed likely on September 16. The tension between resilient risk appetite and rising risk-free rates is the central macro dynamic of the moment, and its resolution — orderly or otherwise — will determine the path for global financial conditions through year-end.
