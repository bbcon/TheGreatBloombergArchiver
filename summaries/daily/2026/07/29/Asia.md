**South Korea: Market Dislocation**
SK Hynix's earnings encapsulate the tension now defining AI-exposed equity markets. A six-fold increase in quarterly profit would ordinarily be celebrated; instead, the miss versus sky-high expectations — combined with a $31 billion capital spending commitment for the year — intensified fears of structural overcapacity in memory chips. The Kospi has now fallen approximately 40% from its June peak, a decline that reflects both the global AI sentiment reversal and idiosyncratic domestic factors, notably the proliferation of leveraged single-stock products introduced in May. Officials have been careful to frame the ETF products as one factor among several rather than the proximate cause, but the timing correlation is difficult to dismiss. SK Hynix management maintained that demand remains structurally robust, but the market reaction suggests investors require more than guidance reassurance.

**Regional Data and China**
Japanese chipmaker Kioxia is expected Friday to report a roughly 30-fold jump in quarterly operating income. Its shares fell 18% Tuesday, illustrating how the AI narrative is under pressure across the supply chain regardless of underlying fundamentals. In China, Moonshot AI's fundraising trajectory — from a $1–2 billion target to $3.5 billion closed — reflects continued strong domestic and international investor appetite for frontier AI models outside the US hyperscaler ecosystem. The FCC's new restrictions on foreign-made robots and solar inverters, though not naming China explicitly, effectively constitute a targeted measure against Chinese suppliers and are likely to complicate the bilateral tech dialogue ahead of a planned leaders' meeting.
