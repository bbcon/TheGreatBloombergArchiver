**Political Economy**
The AfD's Saxony-Anhalt result — more than double its prior support — reflects compounding grievances: energy costs in the region have risen sharply, with gasoline and diesel up approximately 40% since Hormuz disruptions began, and industrial employers including Dow Inc. are closing facilities. The result reinforces European political fragmentation with material policy implications: National Rally's Jordan Bardella has vowed to oppose French tax increases needed to narrow the fiscal deficit, while expressing skepticism toward unconditional Ukraine support — developments that directly complicate eurozone fiscal consolidation and NATO cohesion.

**Bond Markets and Monetary Policy**
French, Italian, and UK longer-term yields have risen the most in the G7 over the past month. The proximate driver is energy-linked inflation re-acceleration, but political risk premia are compounding the move. The ECB's anticipated Thursday hike — a quarter-point increase widely pre-signaled — is increasingly viewed as insufficient, with the debate shifting to November. UK Chancellor Healey used his first major speech to emphasize fiscal discipline alongside a regional growth agenda; separately, a UK hiring survey showed full-time staff additions in August, data the Bank of England watches closely.
