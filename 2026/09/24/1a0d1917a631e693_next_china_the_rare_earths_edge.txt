Hi, this is Philip Glamann in Beijing, where the view of the summit between 
Chinese leader Xi Jinping and President Donald Trump reveals three main issues.

Rare earths. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-21/world-s-top-rare-earth-magnet-maker-gives-xi-leverage-over-us?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>

Rare earths. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-us-china-heavy-rare-earth-magnets-defense/?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>

Rare earths. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/china-rare-earth-magnet-exports-to-us-drop-before-xi-trump-meet?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>

Well, that and US markets.

For all the talk about possible cooperation on AI, the Iran war and other 
issues, what matters most when the two leaders sit down in the White House is 
US access to the materials critical to the production of smartphones, cars and 
fighter jets, and China’s access to the American market, the biggest in the 
world.

There have been ample signs that this is the dynamic in the run-up to Xi’s 
first visit to the US capital since 2015.

For one thing, when United States Trade Representative Jamieson Greer 
suggested this week that the one-year tariff truce set to expire in November 
might only be extended bythree or six months 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/greer-says-us-china-to-continue-talks-on-trade-truce-extension?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
, he linked it to Beijing limiting flows of rare earths.

Later, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the sides agreed to extend their 
trade truce by two months 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/xi-gets-rare-tarmac-welcome-from-trump-after-landing-at-us-base?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
. To find more of our coverage,look here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/latest/trump-xi-relationship?itm_source=record&itm_campaign=Trump-Xi_Summit&itm_content=title&sref=IjHycLlg&cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
.

Highlighting China’s use of the materials as leverage, shipments of rare earth 
magnets to the US dropped by a little over a fifth in August from the month 
before.

“Just saying we have full trust and full confidence in each other at this 
point, I think is a little naive,” Greer said.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-21/trump-xi-to-meet-amidst-friction-on-wars-tech-trade-video?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-21/trump-xi-to-meet-amidst-friction-on-wars-tech-trade-video?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>

To ensure rare earths flow, Trump’s team is dangling tariff relief. The US 
apparently decided to hold off on announcing new levies on China (and other 
trading partners) until after the summit — a move aimed at giving it leverage.

For China, getting tariffs set at a level it can tolerate would be ideal. 
That’s because aside from exports, its economy is flailing and youth 
unemployment is rising.

Easing that headache would also help it deal with a looming trade dispute 
<https://www.google.com.hk/goto?url=CAESqAEB6zswFRWJ-02VDeMUrEJdeGXp9rZZRw5vRgoDHL-O_vwSwyBeGhPU_1u2qrhBCniqwXXke-3mSOO4Xw6g3fzOF8bGOSYmh0ECEXhJcBiCHFU2LTEnHxDzSkhggO2NG9jGs57VdDaPkFf2f6gFMXpUGNTI8kqyyelW-gIOrXaa13Psd7zSbFoFBE7jnLqJ-7-kMSbFDMepVFeriA3XQYr1RiQMDYyFbOc>
, one with Europe. Underscoring China’s worry about that issue, this week its 
top diplomat, Wang Yi, made comments aimed at heading off tensions with 
Brussels.

Europe is worried about a growing trade deficit with the world’s 
second-largest economy and about potential job losses,explaining protests in 
Germany this week 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/vw-warning-deepens-german-automakers-gloom-as-workers-protest?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
. There’s mounting evidence that Europe is at least mulling tough action, and 
the two sides have set a deadline of next month to start working on the issue.


Big Take Asia

What Trump, Xi Want From Their Summit
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2026-09-22/big-take-asia-what-trump-xi-want-from-their-summit-podcast?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
For the US, getting China to send rare earths would eliminate a potentially 
serious chokehold, just as its defense industry tries to replacemunitions used 
during the Iran war 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/us-warns-allies-of-delays-in-missile-shipments-due-to-shortages?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
. Manufacturers in a range of other sectors would also be relieved.

The US and its allies have earmarked billions of dollars to develop their own 
rare earth industries, but they havea long way to go 
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u_GFP1PD9GA> and a short time to get there.

Trump has downplayed how long it will take the US to catch up. Though the US 
can cover some ground by 2030, China will likely be dominant for another decade 
in some of the most important areas of the industry.

And it’s not like Chinese companies are standing still. Firms like JL Mag Rare 
Earth are moving ever forward, expanding into the increasingly sophisticated 
components that incorporate rare earth magnets.

Other issues in the China-US relationship will certainly get aired when Trump 
and Xi sit down.

Bessent has said the two sides would create what he called an “AI dialogue,” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/bessent-hails-very-successful-china-talks-on-ai-threats-trade?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 though neither party has given details on what that would involve. The Trump 
administration also wants China to make agricultural and energy purchases — 
goods that often come from areas led by his party.

And Xi is likely to reiterate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-21/trump-xi-detente-has-taiwan-feeling-sidelined?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 Beijing’s claim to Taiwan. He may also encourage Trump to hold off on a 
planned (and record) $14 billion arms package for the democratic island defying 
Beijing’s claims.

Xi may also push to get Trump to drop a ban on Chinese EV sales in the US. 
He’d likely bemet with some pushback 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-23/slotkin-warns-against-deal-allowing-chinese-cars-into-us-video?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
, like a demand that the autos be made in America by Americans.

A who’s who of US corporate leaders — Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, OpenAI’s Sam 
Altman, Apple’s Tim Cook, Tesla’s Elon Musk and more — will appear, though it
seems like Chinese entrepreneurs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/xi-unlikely-to-bring-ceos-to-summit-where-trump-is-seeking-deals?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 are skipping the event.

Trump will host a state dinner that will add to the pageantry of the whole 
summit, color he hopes will burnish his credentials before midterm elections in 
November that seem set todeal a blow to his party 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/trump-s-grip-on-gop-slips-as-midterm-election-anxiety-sets-in?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
.

Still, expect the start, middle and end of Trump’s dealings with Xi this week 
to be about rare earths.

What AI Slowdown?

Nowhere was the divergence between US and Chinese companies on the future of 
AI clearer than at an annual Alibaba conference this week.

At the event, reps for the tech giant showed hardly any of the consternation 
that US rivals have aired aboutthe possible risks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-20/ai-boom-is-making-diversifying-investments-tough-for-wall-street?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 AI poses to humanity. Instead, they pledged to advance their models and 
hardware aggressively to meet surging demand.

“Last year, we asserted that the more capable AI becomes, the more powerful 
humanity will be,” CEO Eddie Wu said. “Today, I remain steadfast in that 
conviction.”
Eddie Wu at Alibaba’s conference in Hangzhou on Tuesday.Source: Alibaba Group 
Holding Ltd.
The company indicated it would continue to drive the development of its Qwen 
language models. It also announceda new lineup of AI semiconductors 
<https://www.google.com.hk/goto?url=CAESqAEB6zswFfEjs_J99tW8-5l9NAtNPoEqkEkOXqII2Dza540TOlpkxb4v4t1UQVZIYggp2kBCUiMsBlWShkohCS40UuCbl9XyWOT-hCJHieR_XvpSMU3Uf20_kcdK8W9bVBcBcnhV_h4jxY6yb_jMp_xUyta377ViG4u-WdPfKrSQzj0USbbQF7RX3B6PmuUwOlLejNBCBVkU7jAKZf4dwhi4fgMPi_KbyZg>
 developed by its chipmaking unit T-Head, including a new accelerator called 
Zhenwu V900 that the company says triples the performance of its predecessor.

Alibaba also set a target of 20 gigawatts of data center capacity for its 
Alibaba Cloud service globally by 2032, on the expectation of “exponentially 
rising demand.”

Known for years as a trailblazer in online retail and payment, Alibaba has 
transformed itself into the country’s leading rival to American frontier labs 
in the AI arena. This summer, Alibaba released a Qwen model that performed well 
compared to cutting-edge offerings from Anthropic and OpenAI.

The Hangzhou-based tech company is reinvesting its profits and raising funds 
from outside investors to bankroll its ambitions. The firm also just completed a
$10.2 billion share sale 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/alibaba-to-raise-10-billion-by-selling-shares-for-ai-expansion?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 in Hong Kong last month.
The Zhenwu V900 chip.Source: Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.
Alibaba will need the money. In early 2025, it pledged to spend more than $53 
billion over three years to expand its AI capabilities. That makes it a very 
big spender in China, butpales when compared 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/new-data-centers-worth-68-billion-disrupted-in-us-data-show?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 to firms like Microsoft, which had capital expenditures of $41 billion in the 
quarter ended June 30.

While showing off Alibaba’s advances, Wu didn’t quite speak to whether it can 
make enough silicon in the short term to meet surging domestic demand, 
especially with Nvidia’s cutting-edge chips restricted in China.

It is unclear whether Alibaba is working with domestic chipmakers — such as 
capacity-constrained SMIC — but US export controls largely block the country’s 
firms from using market leaders like TSMC.

Still, a lot of what Alibaba showcased sent the clearest signal yet that China 
isn’t slowing down one little bit in pursuing what the industry calls 
artificial superintelligence.

And as for those worries about the risks AI may carry? Those may eventually 
come to China.

Xu Zhijun, the rotating chairman of another tech leader, Huawei, said recently 
that given the immense computing power American engineers have at their 
disposal, only they “know how far they have pushed AI.”

“We’ll have to speed up the pace to understand the risks that American 
companies are feeling right now,” he said.

Behind the Great Firewall

A weekly look at the big water cooler news in China.

This week, some in China reminded tech titan Lei Jun that, for them at least, 
his penchant for attracting attention had worn thin.

That’s because during a livestreaming event, the CEO of EV and phone maker 
Xiaomi seemed to go off script in defense of a now-infamous breakfast.

Back in June, Lei was surrounded by a crowd in the central city of Wuhan while 
he ate some street food. Then a young girl walked past and delivered a cutting 
line: “So many people taking pictures of him just having breakfast!”

The clip then went viral. Social media users said the precocious youngster’s 
comment perfectly captured their feeling that Lei was trying a bit too hard at 
the marketing game. He was also called a copycat because a few weeks earlier 
Nvidia’s Huang was spottedeating street food in Beijing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-05-15/nvidia-s-ceo-spotted-eating-noodles-on-beijing-sidewalk-video?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
.
Lei Jun, CEO of Xiaomi, in Beijing in 2024.Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg
Lei brought up the breakfast thing again this week while touting his company’s 
latest EVs, denying he had been posing. Local tourism officials in Wuhan had 
set the event up, he said, and he brought just one cameraperson.

His comments set off another round of criticism of his showmanship, with a 
hashtag about the defense of breakfast quickly garnering more than 32 million 
views.

“He eats, ends up on Weibo trending,” one person posted on Weibo. “He 
explains, goes on trending again. The king of marketing.”

Lei is one of China’s most recognizable business figures — and one with a 
robust online presence. He co-founded Xiaomi in 2010 after a career in software 
and built a community of users who call themselves “Mi fans.”

He frequently livestreams, including once for about 15 hours straight during a 
road trip from Beijing to Shanghai in a Xiaomi. His engagement with social 
media users eased the firm’s pivot to EVs, helping itturn a profit faster 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-19/xiaomi-rides-fan-base-to-hit-ev-profitability-faster-than-tesla?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
 than Tesla did.

But that online presence has also been polarizing. Lei was accused of 
mishandling the response to an incident in which a Xiaomi burst into flames 
after a crash, killing three people. He offered an apology three days afterward 
and then stayed mostly silent on social media for about a month.

After a spate of deadly incidents involving a range of companies around the 
world, the Chinese government introduced rules to regulate EV safety, such as
banning concealed door handle designs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-02/china-bans-hidden-car-door-handles-in-world-first-safety-policy?cmpid=BBD092426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9213>
. It also issued standards for driver-assist technology.

Xiaomi didn’t respond to a request for comment.

Lei remains a tech hero in China for building Xiaomi into an industry leader, 
and many people defended him this week.

“In the eyes of critics, Lei Jun just breathing is doing something wrong,” 
another person wrote on Weibo.
