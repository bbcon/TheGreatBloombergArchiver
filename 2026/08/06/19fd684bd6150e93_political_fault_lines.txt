A week on and we still have no definitive answer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/eu-tries-to-tamp-down-divisions-after-ceuta-migration-surge?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 as to what caused tens of thousands of desperate people to storm the Spanish 
enclave of Ceuta in North Africa.

A ploy by Morocco to apply pressure on Madrid over its relations with 
neighboring Algeria? A mistaken interpretation of a Spanish court ruling 
amplified by social media? Or a rush to take advantage of Prime Minister Pedro 
Sánchez’s open-doors policy?

Perhaps all three at once 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/how-the-morocco-ceuta-crossing-became-a-mass-migration-event?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
.

What is abundantly clear is the panic that any breach of the European Union’s 
external borders instills in the bloc’s governments.
Members of the Guardia Civil oversee migrants who crossed the border between 
Spain and Morocco on July 31.Photographer: Adri Salido/Getty Images
Berlin and Rome were among those to demand that Spain do its “duty” and, in 
Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s words, “get the situation back under control as 
quickly as possible.”

Migration is a fault line in politics worldwide, with President Donald Trump 
long setting the tone with his zero-tolerance stance.

But it’s also now reverberating across Latin America and even in South Africa 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-12/south-africa-says-53-449-migrants-returned-or-deported-in-crackdown?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
, the original rainbow nation. European governments are running scared of 
anti-immigration parties that are setting the agenda.

This week saw another lightning-rod issue 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/trump-calls-el-sayed-a-man-of-hate-after-michigan-primary-win?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 emerge: support for Israel.

Progressive Abdul El-Sayed won Michigan’s Democratic Senate primary 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/el-sayed-wins-michigan-democratic-senate-primary-nbc-projects?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 against a candidate backed by the centrist party establishment. While both 
criticized the Iran war, the dividing line was over how much Israel and 
lobbying groups supporting it influence US policy.

More broadly, it was another sign following Zohran Mamdani’s election as New 
York mayor that a left-wing message based around affordabilitycan energize 
voters 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/progressives-in-congress-see-el-sayed-victory-as-change-mandate?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
.
Abdul El-Sayed.Photographer: Sarah Rice/Bloomberg
Where the US leads, will Europe follow?

Germany may offer an answer, of sorts. Polls for two state elections next 
month in the former east suggest that the anti-migrant AfD party ispoised to 
win big 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-18/german-far-right-trojan-horse-triggers-alarm-over-intelligence?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
.

Berlin is pointing the other way.

The anti-capitalist Left party polled first this week for Sept. 20 elections 
in the German capital on a platform that pledges a “social, ecological, livable 
and just city for all.”

As in the US, the direction of Europe’s divided politics is still up for 
grabs. —Alan Crawford 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOTqYzZDKuY/alan-crawford?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>

Global Must Reads

Iran says it reached a deal with Oman on a proposed route 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/iran-says-agreement-on-hormuz-shipping-route-reached-with-oman?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 for shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a potential step toward a reopening 
of the waterway crucial to the flows of energy supplies. While fighting between 
the US and Iran has paused, other issues remain unresolved — like Tehran’s 
nuclear program — and there hasn’t been an agreement to start formal 
negotiations.

Beijing leveraged its dominance over global drone production to hit back at 
widening US tech and trade curbs, unleashinga barrage of retaliatory actions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-hits-back-at-us-with-tighter-drone-export-curbs-sanctions?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 while signaling it wants to keep relations with Washington on track. In making 
the move, China cited decisions such as the US ban on some foreign-made robots 
and power inverters and the blacklisting of more than 40 Chinese companies 
accused of using forced labor.

Ousted leader Sheikh Hasina vowed to return 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/bangladesh-s-ousted-leader-says-she-ll-return-despite-threats?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 to Bangladesh despite facing a death sentence, calling it her “duty” to help 
rescue a country she described as sliding into economic distress and democratic 
decline. The government in Dhaka warned India that allowing Hasina to conduct a 
live media briefing from New Delhi undermined efforts to reset bilateral ties, 
saying it was “outraged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/bangladesh-outraged-with-india-over-ex-leader-s-media-briefing?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
” she was given a platform to make allegations against the state and its people.

Joint US-Japan intervention has supported the yen for now, but isn’t a 
game-changer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/japan-s-kishida-sees-growth-strategy-as-game-changer-for-yen?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
 for the currency or the economy, Japan’s former premier, Fumio Kishida,says in 
an interview 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-06/fmr-japan-pm-on-yen-communication-with-boj-and-markets-video?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
, touting a $2.3 trillion growth strategy to transform his country’s fortunes. 
Kishida, who is chief of a ruling-party group that provided advice to Prime 
Minister Sanae Takaichi on the plan, brushed aside concerns over how it will be 
funded.
Fumio Kishida in Tokyo yesterday.Photographer: Toru Hanai/Bloomberg
President Javier Milei’s latest push to lure private investment to Argentina

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum’s approval rating jumped

Chart of the Day

As President Vladimir Putin’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine extends for a 
fifth year, Russian businesses can no longer afford to keep pace with the 
Kremlin’s war machine inthe wage race 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/russia-s-war-economy-is-fueling-pay-gains-companies-can-t-afford?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
. Years of acute labor shortages have pushed salaries well ahead of 
productivity growth. In prior years, surging corporate revenues absorbed the 
pressure, but with the wartime economic boom at an end, businesses are shifting 
into survival mode and slashing costs.

And Finally

Hong Kong’s expat population was hollowed out by Beijing’s ever-tightening 
grip and strict lockdowns during the pandemic, with tens of thousands leaving 
including bankers, lawyers and other skilled workers who made the city Asia’s 
foremost financial hub. Now,it’s enjoying a moment  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-05/hong-kong-draws-back-expats-seeking-low-taxes-finance-jobs-after-covid-exodus?cmpid=BBD080626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7397>
as white-collar professionals make their way back, responding to a booming IPO 
market, planned tax breaks and a more vibrant city overall. For returnee 
Frenchman Theo Bertrand, Hong Kong is “the place to be for the next few years.”
Mid-Levels, a popular residential area for Hong Kong’s expats.Photographer:
