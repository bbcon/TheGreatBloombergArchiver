All eyes on Warsh

The Fed’s annual economic symposium 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/fed-s-jackson-hole-conference-is-underway-here-s-what-to-expect?cmpid=082826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8284>
 is getting underway in Wyoming, with Warsh due to speak at 10 a.m. New York 
time.

Here’s what going on:

 * Investors want a clear message. Fed watchers say Warsh has a chance to 
convince the market that managing inflation is his priority.
 * But he might stick to a tight-lipped comms strategy. “The speech arguably 
matters less than in recent years,” says Felix Vezina-Poirier of BCA Research.
 * Options are pricing in small moves. Derivatives traders don’t see this 
speech as being an inflection point. They might be onto something — with a few 
exceptions, history shows the speech isusually a dud 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/jackson-hole-history-shows-fed-chief-rarely-shocks-stock-market?cmpid=082826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8284>
. 
Given the recent selloff in long-dated Treasury bonds, there’s still a current 
of nervousness in the market about Warsh’s strategy. To MLIV strategist Alyce 
Andres, the important signal may not come today, but rather when Governor 
Christopher Waller, who has been more vocal, speaks next week.

When it comes to Warsh, she says: “All signs point to him doing exactly what 
has frustrated bond markets for months: talking less.” —Lynn Thomasson 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOdzWfuXL-k/lynn-thomasson?cmpid=082826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8284>

China’s chip giant

CXMT is getting ready for earnings that will test whether it can live up to 
its status asChina’s most valuable listed company. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/cxmt-s-580-stock-rally-raises-bar-for-first-earnings-since-ipo?cmpid=082826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8284>

As the world’s fourth-biggest DRAM memory producer, the chipmaker is a symbol 
of Beijing’s efforts to become less reliant on foreign technology.The stock has 
jumped more than 570% since going public in July.

The company, due to report earnings today, faces a high bar, and there are 
signs investors are also turning more selective on memory stocks. Shares of 
both SK Hynix and Samsung shares fell after reporting results, even though 
profits increased sharply.
