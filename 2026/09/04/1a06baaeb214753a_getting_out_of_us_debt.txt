US Treasuries may be losing their hold over some long-term institutional 
investors just as spiking yields raise concern over the country’s ability to 
service its $40 trillion public debt.

Norway’s sovereign wealth fund proposed reducing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/norway-wealth-fund-proposes-trimming-government-bond-holdings?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 the amount of government bonds in its $2.3 trillion portfolio to boost 
holdings of riskier debt, with Treasuries the most affected. Government debt 
should be cut to 50% of the bond portion of the portfolio from 70%, Norges Bank 
Investment Management said. That implies a $75 billion drop in its Treasury 
holdings.

The move comes after Norway’s Finance Minister Jens Stoltenberg said in 
January hesaw no reason 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-21/norway-s-stoltenberg-sees-no-reason-for-wealth-fund-to-exit-us?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 for its wealth fund — the world’s largest — to exit US markets in response to 
threats to seize Greenland.

Meanwhile, bets are rising that Japan’s Government Pension Investment Fund 
will buymore local bonds 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/gpif-s-unusual-meeting-fuels-speculation-over-allocation-change?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 to helpsupport the yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/carry-trade-exodus-fuels-yen-surge-ahead-of-boj-rate-decision?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
. It’s not yet clear if any such shift by the $2 trillion fund would result in 
cutting any overseas debt holdings. Japan is the largest foreign holder of US 
Treasuries with a $1.2 trillion stockpile.

This isn’t a new trend. Dutch pension fund ABP, Europe’s largest, spent much 
of last year dramaticallyscaling back 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-23/dutch-pension-abp-cut-us-treasury-stake-by-12-billion-in-2025?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 its exposure to Treasuries. Turkey offloaded almost all of its US bonds in 
March as it defended the lira. The US likely helped Japan intervene to support 
the yen in late July to ensure Tokyo didn’t sell Treasuries, suggestinggrowing 
awareness 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-01/bond-markets-signal-investor-alarm-over-debt-levels-and-inflation?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 of the risk.

While declines are marginal right now — foreign holding 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/foreign-holdings-of-treasuries-fell-in-june-led-by-japan-drop?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
s of Treasuries fell in three of the past four months, from a record high in 
February — it’s a trend to keep watch on. Especially as large fiscal deficits 
mean the Treasury will need tosell ever more 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/us-treasury-sparks-debate-over-auction-cutbacks-to-temper-yields?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 bonds.

said, as President Donald Trump ramped up pressure on the US ally over its lack 
of support for the war with Iran.

 a naval ship and maritime patrol aircraft, and began preparations to seek 
parliament’s approval, the Chosun Ilbo newspaper said. Some 150 troops would be 
required to operate the ship and aircraft, the report added.

Trump abruptly ordered

 the Pentagon to scale back annual joint military drills with the ally last

Inflation hedge: Some of the world’s biggest money managers have rebuilt their 
gold holdings after prices dropped.

Amundi, Europe’s largest asset manager, bought bullion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/world-s-biggest-money-managers-are-rebuilding-gold-positions?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 on the expectation it will return to $5,000 an ounce by year-end. Fund 
managers at Pictet Asset Management, Robeco Institutional Asset Management and 
Fidelity International also added to holdings cut earlier this year, during 
bullion’s retreat from an all-time high.

“Gold is an asset that we consider to be cheap, a good hedge and reasonably 
liquid,” said Lorenzo Portelli, head of cross-asset strategy at Amundi 
Investment Institute.

Hazy shade: Singapore’s air quality hit an unhealthy level on Friday as smoke 
from forest fires raging in neighboring Indonesia continued to spread across 
swathes of Southeast Asia.

The haze made the city-state one of the 10 most polluted major cities 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/singapore-air-unhealthy-as-haze-spreads-from-indonesian-fires?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8546>
 in the world, coming in at eight in IQAir’s ranking. The fires and 
accompanying haze are a yearly occurrence, often attributed to land-clearing 
practices. But this year’s ‘super El Niño’ weather pattern threatens to 
intensify adverse impacts on public health and commercial activity across the 
region.
