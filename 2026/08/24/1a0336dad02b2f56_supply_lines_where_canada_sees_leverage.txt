Joining dots in the global economy is always a fraught business. The trade war 
erupting between the US and Canada is materially different from the bloody 
actual war being fought by the US and Iran. And much as they may be feared in 
international finance ministries,bond vigilantes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/pimco-expects-elevated-term-premium-says-bonds-are-attractive?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 don’t drop actual bombs.

But there’s a clear link between the three emerging as summer winds down in 
Washington. Canada’s Mark Carney, hardliners in Tehran andbond investors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/bessent-s-bond-maneuvers-giving-global-debasement-trade-new-life?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 have all joined the growing ranks of those willing to defy US President Donald 
Trump and his use of American economic muscle.

That matters for a lot of consequential corners of the global economy. Whether 
it’s the oil andTreasuries markets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/bessent-has-no-easy-fix-for-what-s-really-driving-bond-yields-up?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 or the cost of US mortgages or Canadian wood and steel, plenty depends on how 
the defiance of Trump’s coercion efforts turns out these days. Never mind the 
potential broader consequences of an erosion of America’s credibility or its 
capacity to dictate events.

Read More: Canada Rejected US Tariff Deal. Now Comes the Economic Cost 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/canada-turned-down-a-us-tariff-deal-now-comes-the-economic-cost?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>

Just how Carney’s decision — to abandon talks and shun a deal that he saw as 
unfair — will turn out is of course unclear. It’s also worth remembering that 
the 50% tariffs that have taken effect on Canadian imports as a result are on 
just 5% of goods coming into the US from its northern neighbor. As far as 
deployments of American leverage go, this is a long way from even the economic 
equivalent of threatening to launch cruise missiles let alone deploying a naval 
blockade.

But Carney also notably drew a clear line and was willing to say ‘no’ to a 
Trump that many other leaders in the world have chosen to bend the knee to. In 
scathing remarks on Saturday he accused the US of waging economic war on Canada 
and offered that American promises were now best treated as temporary because 
“sometimes its signature was written in pencil.”

That may have been because he was willing to bet that Trump’s leverage was 
less than it seemed and was in fact diminishing.

Carney led both Canada’s central bank and the Bank of England. He therefore 
was certainly paying attention over the past week as the bond market shrugged 
off US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’sintervention 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 to bring down long-term borrowing costs with an eye on both domestic mortgage 
rates and the cost of servicing the US’s now $40 trillion sovereign debt.

Former Central Banker

In response to a question, Carney on Saturday denied that the bond market’s 
negative response to Bessent’s actions had figured in his calculations as he 
rejected a proposed US deal to avoid tariffs. He made clear, though, that he 
thought Canada was in a stronger position than the US when it came to bond 
investors.

“We are entering a phase where fiscal strength, discipline, focus is going to 
be very important — it’s going to be scrutinized,” he said. “Markets sometimes 
ignore these fundamentals and then all of a sudden they focus on them. And when 
they focus on them, if you don’t have your house in order, it’s too late. We 
have our house in order and we’re getting stronger.”

Read More: China Media Hails Canada’s ‘Chinese-Style’ Tariff Retaliation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/china-media-hails-canada-s-chinese-style-tariff-retaliation?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>

He’s not wrong. Canada’s fiscal deficit is expected to be around 2% of GDP 
this year versus the US’s more than 6%.

Canada’s economy, like its population, is of course significantly smaller than 
the US. But what Carney was intimating was that its fiscal position offered its 
own meaningful strength, which went beyond any patriotic appeal in standing up 
to a perceived bully.

Sagging Morale

Iran, it should by now be clear, offers its own lessons in the pitfalls of 
projecting power.

Having failed to topple the regime via the war it launched with Israel in 
February and facing depletingmunitions stockpiles 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/key-us-munitions-are-dangerously-low-bloomberg-economics-says?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 and morale issues aboardaircraft carriers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/us-commander-says-mental-health-a-priority-aboard-uss-lincoln?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 engaged in the fight, the US is now returning its offensive against Iran to 
the economy.

Washington is expected this week to roll out more measures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/trump-s-economic-isolation-plan-risks-iran-strikes-across-gulf?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 against Tehran meant to isolate it and force a capitulation it has so far 
resisted. In a Financial Times article published on Monday, Bessent repeated a 
promise to bring an economic D-Day to Iran.

“The world should understand that our objective is to sever every economic 
lifeline that sustains the tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone,”he wrote
 
<https://www.ft.com/content/cb865200-1a06-40be-86a3-10ee2bb05804?FTCamp=engage/CAPI/desktopapp/Channel_Bloomberg//B2B>
.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/iranians-struggle-to-afford-groceries-as-trump-squeezes-economy?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>

Yet Iran has for decades withstood US economic pressure including from the 
Trump administration. And if there has been a theme to emerge from the Middle 
East this year it is that Iran’s leadership has found a way to endure bombings 
and the deaths of its top leaders to continue toassert control 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/saudis-oil-logistics-roiled-again-by-houthis-red-sea-threat?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 over the Strait of Hormuz and disrupt global energy markets.

Each of these efforts is its own thing. The fights against Canada, the bond 
market and Iran are each unique and being fought in their own environments. We 
shouldn’t conflate them. Yet it’s hard to imagine any of the participants not 
noticing their common reactions.

As, undoubtedly, is China’s Xi Jinping, who has his own fight with Trump 
bubbling along and isexpected to visit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-set-to-look-past-trump-s-new-tariffs-before-xi-visit-to-us?cmpid=BBD082426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8087>
 Washington a month from today.
