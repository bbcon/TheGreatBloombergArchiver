Tuesday, November 24, 2026

US · Asia · Europe · LatAm · Global

US

  • S&P 500 surged 4.7% and the Nasdaq 100 climbed 6.9% on Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO, though futures retreated 1.0–1.9% by the following morning, suggesting the rally may lack follow-through.
  • New York Fed President John Williams declined to rule out successive rate hikes, reinforcing a hawkish monetary policy posture even amid equity market volatility.
  • Food inflation is accelerating: grocery prices posted their largest monthly gain since April, with skirt steak up 33.3% YoY and sweet potatoes up more than 9%, compounding persistent inflation above 3%.
  • America's Car-Mart filed for bankruptcy, illustrating the cumulative stress on subprime consumers from sustained high borrowing costs.

Nvidia and AI Governance

Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO — secured following direct intervention by President Trump, including a call into Nvidia's board meeting — triggered one of the most dramatic single-day moves in recent memory. Nvidia shares rose 31%, their largest gain since 2003, adding approximately $952 billion in market capitalization. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index jumped 14%. Yet the rally arrested a decline rather than reversing it: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 remain below their November peaks, and even the semiconductor index has not recovered to its pre-resignation levels. The episode has introduced a new category of political risk — Nvidia's governance is now effectively underwritten by a presidential assurance rather than a completed investigation, drawing bipartisan scrutiny and comparisons to "too big to fail" dynamics from the 2008 financial crisis.

Monetary Policy and Economic Outlook

Fed Governor Williams's comments that successive rate hikes remain on the table underscore the Fed's commitment to returning inflation to its 2% target, even as equities whipsaw. Citadel Securities strategists have drawn explicit parallels to late 2018, when resilient growth masked deteriorating prospects just as policy tightened. With roughly a third of 2026 GDP growth estimated to derive from AI-related investment and consumption, the unresolved governance questions around Nvidia and the broader AI complex carry meaningful macroeconomic implications. ---

Asia

  • Asian equities surged on the Huang reinstatement: Taiwan's benchmark gained 9%, led by TSMC; South Korea's Kospi rose 6.3%; the Nikkei 225 advanced 3.9%; Samsung and SK Hynix each added approximately 7%.
  • China extended its suspension of critical mineral export bans — covering gallium, germanium, and antimony — through end-January, choosing restraint over immediate retaliation to US chip controls.
  • China's Bloomberg Economics Credit Impulse Index is near flat, signaling a persistent liquidity squeeze despite accelerated government bond issuance; private credit demand remains subdued.

China: Macro and Trade Policy

Beijing's decision to again suspend its critical minerals export ban appears calibrated to preserve a fragile trade truce ahead of the January 10 tariff deadline, rather than reflecting any genuine easing of tensions. Chinese state media simultaneously warned of potential "witch hunts" against Chinese interests linked to the Huang saga. The macro backdrop remains challenging: private borrowing is subdued, Beijing is relying on bond issuance to approach its "near 5%" GDP target, and the yuan's sustained appreciation has not stimulated commodity demand. Macquarie analysts note that the semiconductor index's roughly 33% decline in the second half of 2026 historically leads Chinese export performance by two quarters, suggesting export momentum may fade by early 2027 — a development that would compel more meaningful domestic stimulus.

Regional Markets and Geopolitics

Asian equity gains appeared to be fading by Tuesday morning, with S&P 500 futures declining and the broader rally losing momentum. South Korea scrambled fighter jets after Russian warplanes approached its airspace, the latest in a pattern of increased Russian military activity noted by South Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. MSCI stated it had not changed its process for evaluating a potential downgrade of Indonesia's equity market status, following a meeting between its CEO and President Prabowo brokered by Ray Dalio. DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng's net worth rose 45% to $55 billion after a new fundraising round drew investment from Chinese industrial groups. ---

Europe

  • European car sales rose 3.4% YoY in October — in line with recent trend, representing a ninth consecutive month of gains — with EVs and plug-in hybrids comprising 43% of registrations, up from 31% a year earlier.
  • Germany's business outlook improved for a seventh consecutive month, cementing expectations that the economy is emerging from a prolonged stagnation.
  • Spain faces a snap election Sunday amid significant public protests over a housing crisis, even as the economy expands at more than twice the eurozone average.
  • European natural gas prices surged as a colder weather outlook intensified concerns over winter supply adequacy, already strained by Iran war-related export disruptions.

Economic Conditions

The European picture is notably bifurcated. Germany's sequential improvement in business sentiment offers the clearest sign yet that Europe's largest economy may be turning a corner, though the improvement is from a depressed base. Spain's macro credentials — above-average growth, declining unemployment, narrowing fiscal deficit — contrast sharply with public sentiment: housing prices have risen roughly twice as fast as wages for young workers, and two-thirds of adults under 34 still live with their parents. The snap election on Sunday injects near-term political uncertainty. European gas prices surging into a cold snap highlights the region's continued vulnerability to energy supply disruptions tied to the Middle East conflict.

Corporate and Policy Developments

Sanofi agreed to acquire US biotech Insmed for $41 billion, the largest pharmaceutical acquisition in three years, with Insmed shares rising 14% and Sanofi gaining 6.2% in Paris. Intesa Sanpaolo opened a €36 billion bid for Monte dei Paschi, the world's oldest bank. Commerzbank is weighing a partial exit from US Treasury trading ahead of new US clearing requirements, a decision complicated by the pending UniCredit takeover. An ECB official expressed support for a "prudent" rate increase, reinforcing policy divergence with other major central banks as inflation pressures persist. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's lower house approved President Lula's fiscal consolidation package, his most significant budget effort since re-election, though the Brazilian real weakened past the closely watched 6-per-dollar level ahead of the vote — signaling investor skepticism about its sufficiency.
  • Colombia, Chile, and regional commodity dynamics remain tied to copper price volatility; copper briefly rallied on the Huang news before resuming its decline, with prices still down more than 20% from September highs.

Brazil: Fiscal Credibility Under Scrutiny

The passage of Lula's public spending curbs through the lower house represents a meaningful political achievement — the package navigates the difficult balance between fiscal restraint and the preservation of social programs central to his coalition. However, the real's move through 6 per dollar in the hours before passage is a candid market assessment: investors appear unconvinced the measures are sufficient to durably stabilize Brazil's fiscal trajectory. The currency's behavior reflects broader emerging market sensitivity to the global liquidity squeeze — an environment in which tightening US monetary policy, a strengthening dollar, and elevated commodity price volatility collectively tighten financial conditions across the region.

Regional Political Economy

Venezuela's government and an opposition faction agreed to overhaul political party rules but stopped short of reforming the electoral authority, leaving the path to credible presidential elections unclear. Separately, a US Senate investigation into Vice President JD Vance's role in awarding a Venezuelan oil contract to commodities trader Vitol adds an additional layer of geopolitical uncertainty around Venezuelan energy flows. Across the region, the commodity outlook — particularly for copper and oil — remains the dominant macro variable, with China's subdued credit impulse representing a material headwind for metals-dependent economies including Chile and Peru. ---

Global

  • Crude oil benchmark futures have fallen more than 20% this month as the IEA projects a 2 million barrel-per-day surplus this quarter — the first since the US-Iran war began — with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and Bernstein all flagging potential oversupply of up to 2.5 million b/d in 2027.
  • The next tranche of US Section 301 tariffs — targeting 15 countries and the EU on industrial overcapacity grounds — appears imminent, with the USTR report expected by year-end and potentially before the G-20 summit on December 14–15.
  • A "super El Niño" is approaching its peak, driving the largest monthly US grocery price increase since April and generating the highest level of corporate weather-risk anxiety in a decade.

Oil Markets: From Disruption to Surplus

The pace of reversal in oil market sentiment is notable. The near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz earlier this year was widely characterized as a worst-case scenario; nine months later, Persian Gulf producers are exporting crude near pre-war levels via both tankers and pipeline alternatives. US shale, Canadian, and Latin American output has grown to fill the supply gap, while global demand has contracted more than 2% as the energy price spike weighed on consumption. Inventories remain severely depleted, limiting the system's resilience to any renewed supply shock — but with Trump signaling reluctance to escalate and Democrats' midterm victory raising expectations of a faster path to de-escalation, traders are pricing a materially looser market. European natural gas remains the exception, with prices rising sharply on cold weather forecasts and ongoing Hormuz-linked supply fragility.

Trade Policy and Geopolitical Fault Lines

The pending Section 301 tariff report, once released, will add a new dimension to already elevated US-China trade tensions — with the potential to push effective tariff rates on Chinese goods back toward 20% when the truce lapses on January 10. The G-20 meeting in Miami on December 14–15 creates a political incentive to delay the announcement, though analysts are divided on timing. China's decision to extend its critical minerals export suspension through January represents a measured, transactional response — preserving leverage without triggering an immediate escalation. Meanwhile, the Nvidia governance episode has introduced a new element to the US-China technology competition: the perception, reinforced by Beijing's state media commentary, that US export controls may be selectively subordinated to market considerations.
