• Brazilian President Lula appointed Bradesco Chairman Luiz Carlos Trabuco Cappi as finance minister, turning to one of the country's most recognized banking figures to restore investor confidence in public finances.
• Argentina's senate passed legislation suspending next year's primary elections, reducing a potential source of market volatility as questions persist about President Milei's reelection prospects.
• Peru's business community expressed unusual confidence at its annual gathering, betting on political stability under President Keiko Fujimori after a decade of institutional turbulence.

**Brazil Fiscal Credibility**
Trabuco Cappi's appointment signals that Lula is prioritizing market credibility at a moment when investor concerns about Brazil's fiscal trajectory have intensified. The choice of a Bradesco chairman — a figure with deep ties to the financial establishment — suggests a tactical pivot toward orthodox signaling, though whether it translates into substantive fiscal consolidation remains to be seen. Brazilian assets had come under pressure amid doubts about the administration's spending discipline, and the appointment's market reception will be an early test of its effectiveness.

**Regional Politics**
Argentina's decision to suspend primaries removes a near-term political volatility event from the calendar, a development likely to be received constructively by markets that have been sensitive to any signal about Milei's reform durability. Peru's improved business sentiment represents a meaningful shift after years of political instability that deterred investment in one of Latin America's most resource-rich economies. Whether Fujimori's government can translate that confidence into tangible infrastructure and mining investment remains the key medium-term question.
