Bessent’s FX Chess Game

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s maneuvers in the currency market marked new 
ground on Friday, with the first yen purchases by the USsince 1998 <>, back 
during the Asian financial crisis.

Trump confirmed the intervention on Sunday, which earlier reporting by the 
Financial Times said involvedsales of euros for yen <>. It was the latest in a 
string of steps since last year to help arrest a slide in the yen, which last 
month hit its weakest since 1986. As of September, Bessent was offering verbal 
support, agreeing to ajoint statement 
<https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0245> with Tokyo that 
highlighted “excess volatility and disorderly movements” were unwelcome.

In January, Bessent signed off on so-called rate checks, which are calls by US 
authorities to banks for quotes on the yen’s rate. Viewed as a precursor to 
direct intervention, those came as a big surprise to market participants. A 
former head of the Bank of Japan’s foreign exchange division, Atsushi Takeuchi, 
said he’dnever even thought 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-11/bessent-s-unprecedented-grasp-on-japanese-policy-tests-takaichi?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
 of such an option when in the job.

Another tool traders might not have thought of: market signaling from the 
Treasury secretary’s notepad, which emerged on Friday.
Polymarket on Twitter / X <https://x.com/Polymarket/status/2083390480088236247>
During a cabinet meeting, a Reuters photographer captured an image of 
Bessent’s notepad that showed, “Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil.” The context:
Bessent has been well aware <> of the visibility of his notepad since at least 
May, when he recognized in a White House briefing that media were able to look 
over his shoulder at his notes.

As for intervening in euro-yen, “to our knowledge, it is highly unusual – 
maybe unprecedented – for the US authorities to conduct an FX market operation 
without buying or selling” dollars, Joey Chew, head of Asia foreign exchange 
research at HSBC, wrote in a note Saturday.

Keep in mind, Bessent was a currency specialist during his decades-long hedge 
fund career. And he’s already embraced unusual ideas, such as expandingcurrency 
swaps 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-04/swap-lines-explained-how-the-us-extends-dollar-power-globally?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
 agreements and mounting a political supportinitiative 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-17/why-argentina-turned-to-trump-for-a-financial-rescue?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
 for Argentina via the foreign-exchange market.

Euro-yen trading is much thinner than dollar-yen, potentially offering a 
greater bang for the intervention buck — or, euro, in this case. The currency 
pair doesn’t register in the top 10 trades in the $9.5 trillion-a-day market.
Sources: Banque de France, Bank for International Settlements. Note: daily 
averages per currency (USD billions).
HSBC postulated the Treasury might have wanted to avoid contributing to 
downward pressure on the dollar, out of “concern that USD weakness may lead to 
higher US Treasury yields.”

That angst about yields is something Mohamed El-Erian, the former CEO of bond 
giant Pimco,also flagged <https://x.com/elerianm/status/2083891482717573565> as 
a possible explanation for Bessent’s move. If Japan is left to intervene on its 
own, at some point that likely involves disposing of a share of its $1.1 
trillion-plus stockpile of US government securities, which could send yields 
higher.

And yields on 10-year Treasuries — Bessent’s key benchmark — already are at 
their highest since before he took office. This isdriving up mortgage rates 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/mortgage-rates-in-us-rise-to-6-66-highest-level-in-a-year?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
, and adding to economic headwinds. Friday’s 10-year close was up almost a half 
percentage point from Jan. 28, when the Treasury chief had declared the US was “
absolutely not 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-28/bessent-cools-yen-intervention-speculation-touts-strong-dollar?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
” intervening to help the yen. Higher yields may be one reason he was now ready 
to take that step.

Further evidence Treasuries are ultimately Bessent’s concern came in a Sunday 
eveningpost on X <https://x.com/SecScottBessent/status/2084051676801933622> 
that mentioned the Federal Reserve’srepurchase facility 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/japan-s-use-of-fed-facility-may-ease-pressure-on-treasury-market?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
 for foreign official institutions. The so-called FIMA repo facility lets 
counterparties park Treasuries at the Fed in return for short-term cash. This 
potentially would let, say, Japan, avoid selling Treasuries as it obtained 
dollars to trade for yen. Bessent said the tool “is an important backstop” and 
encouraged it to be “upsized in the coming months.”

The Week Ahead

Employers in the US likely stepped up their pace of hiring in July, showing a 
steady appetite for workers despite the overhang of geopolitical concerns and 
elevated inflation.

Economists estimate the monthly jobs report on Friday will show an 85,000 
increase in payrolls after the lower-than-expected 57,000 gain in June, 
according to the Bloomberg survey median. The unemployment rate, which is based 
on a survey of households rather than establishments, is seen holding steady at 
4.2%.

Elsewhere, Canada’s jobs report is also due with economists expecting another 
increase. Central bankers in Brazil are likely to cut interest rates, while 
those in Mexico, India and the Czech Republic may stay on hold.

Need-to-Know Research

UK employers are creating jobs for experienced staff with artificial 
intelligence skills and cutting back elsewhere, according to new figures that 
show how the technology is starting to create atwo-track labor market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/ai-is-creating-a-two-speed-jobs-market-in-the-uk-indeed-says?cmpid=BBD080326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7033>
.

British firms posted about 10% fewer vacancies this month than in January 
2025, data from job search website Indeed shared with Bloomberg shows. The fall 
disguised disparities as firms adopt AI to cut costs and boost productivity.

Occupations such as IT and engineering, where AI enhances the value of 
experience by taking over routine tasks, are among the few that have held up 
since last summer. Elsewhere, from retail and manufacturing to white-collar 
roles that are more vulnerable to substitution like accounting or marketing, 
vacancies have fallen by double digits as employers take action to cope with 
higher employment costs and a more volatile economy.

“The UK labor market is increasingly splitting into two speeds,” said Jack 
Kennedy, senior economist at Indeed. “Demand is concentrating around 
experienced workers and roles directly connected to AI, rather than flowing 
evenly through the profession.”
