Thirty years ago, Israelis went to bed on election night believing Labor’s 
Shimon Peres was the next prime minister, only to wake up and find Likud’s 
Benjamin Netanyahu had won the most votes.

A similar dynamic has been playing out today 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/israeli-vote-too-close-to-call-between-netanyahu-and-eisenkot?cmpid=BBD102826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10640>
: Exit polls last night showed opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot defeating 
Netanyahu. With more than three-quarters of the ballots counted, Netanyahu — 
Israel’s longest-serving premier — is ahead.

There’s a long way to go since neither has a path toward forming a ruling 
majority – 61 of the 120-seat parliament — and more than half a million votes 
from army bases, hospitals and diplomatic posts are still to be tallied.

But there’s no avoiding the fact that Netanyahu, 77, known in Israel as “the 
magician” for his political skills, was able to rouse aremarkable amount of 
support 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-19/netanyahu-risks-israel-election-loss-but-his-iran-gaza-policies-will-continue?cmpid=BBD102826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10640>
 for someone who was in charge when Hamas invaded and traumatized the nation in 
2023, triggering brutal wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-28/netanyahu-moves-ahead-of-eisenkot-in-israeli-vote-count-video?cmpid=BBD102826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10640>
WATCH: Abeer Abu Omar reports on Bloomberg Television.
Backed by US President Donald Trump, Netanyahu vows to form the next 
government and return to fighting in Iran. Eisenkot says he should be given the 
first chance to put together a ruling coalition and won’t rush to war.

President Isaac Herzog will choose next week, based on coalition options.

The other big surprise is the large gains of the parties of Israel’s Arab 
minority, giving themthe chance to play a key role 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/israeli-arab-parties-claim-kingmaker-status-in-extra-tight-vote?cmpid=BBD102826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10640>
.

Netanyahu says he won’t rule with Arabs and had called on his supporters to 
counter their vote; Eisenkot says he’ll only include parties that define Israel 
as a Jewish state and label Hamas a terrorist group. The Arab parties have so 
far demurred.

A new election could be in the cards — or a deal between parts of each bloc to 
form a new group.

Either way, Netanyahu runs Israel for now. — Ethan Bronner 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASGGjEWbfUs/ethan-bronner?cmpid=BBD102826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10640>

Chart of the Day

Being one of Washington’s closest partners no longer means reliably funding 
its deficit. In the first eight months of the year, countries and regions most 
aligned with the US bought the lowest net amount of Treasuries since 2021 — 
down more than 60% from a year earlier. Several factors may be contributing, 
including weak currencies, rising yields at home, growing demand for stocks and
unease over American policy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/us-friendly-nations-slash-treasury-purchases-by-60-led-by-asia?cmpid=BBD102826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10640>
