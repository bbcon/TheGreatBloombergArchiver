France and Germany are jointly pushing for new measures to curb Chinese 
imports, a show of unity that will strengthen the European Commission’s 
position as it tries to rebalance trade ties with Beijing.

In a paper and letter to Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, the 
European Union’s biggest economies proposed quicker mechanisms to protect 
domestic industries under threat from Chinese rivals in the event of a trade 
war, Jenny Leonard of Bloomberg Newsreports on Tuesday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/franco-german-unity-on-china-trade-paves-way-for-tougher-eu-line?cmpid=BBD100626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9710>
.

The alignment fuses the stances of two nations that disagreed just a few years 
ago on how to deal with the world’s No. 2 economy. The most striking part of 
their plan: a new tool that could allow the 27-nation bloc to shut down Chinese 
firms’ access to its $8 trillion market of traded goods and services, if needed.

The move comes ahead of an important stretch for Brussels and its trade 
relations with the single biggest source of its imports.

EU Trade Commissioner Maros Sefcovic heads to Beijing later this week in an 
attempt to negotiate a framework to stabilize the commercial flows and avert a 
full-blown trade war. EU leaders are expected to discuss their China strategy 
at a European Council meeting in Oct. 15-16.
