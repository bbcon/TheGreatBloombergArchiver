The European Union imposed anti-dumping duties that factored in the impact of 
Chinese industrial overcapacity on other markets, setting a significant 
precedent as the bloc seeks to shield its economy from unfair trade tied 
indirectly to China.

The European Commission on Monday announced duties on cold-rolled flat steel 
from five economies, the EU executive said in astatement 
<https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-imposes-anti-dumping-duties-certain-cold-rolled-flat-steel-products-2026-11-16_en>
 specifying India, Japan, Taiwan, Turkey and Vietnam.

An investigation found that “certain cold-rolled flat steel products from 
these countries were entering the EU at dumped prices,” the statement said

In the case of Taiwan, the commission for the first time determined that 
Chinese imports created a market distortion that led to “downward pressure on 
domestic prices and deteriorating profitability for the Taiwanese industry.” (

The commission “considers this investigation to be of systemic importance for 
trade defence, beyond the products and industry sectors it concerns,” it said, 
underlying the importance of “credible, evidence-based and innovative solutions 
to address evolving trade-distorting practices.”

Widening Imbalance

The EU is beefing up its trade-policy toolbox 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-and-eu-risk-new-trade-clash-over-what-comes-after-car-deal?cmpid=BBD111726_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11457>
 to confront the global effects of China’s factory overcapacity. Brussels has 
said Beijing’s industrial policies and extensive support for manufacturing are 
generating excess production, which is responsible for a trade deficit that 
reached €360 billion ($396 billion) last year and could swell to a record this 
year.

Last month, 15 OECD-aligned economies including the EU and the US, signed a 
pledge to confront excess manufacturing capacity and production in certain 
sectors — a show of unity aimed at China even the joint statement 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/october/fourteen-economies-join-united-states-signing-joint-ministerial-statement-address-global-excess>
 didn’t specify that the world’s biggest exporter is the target.

The Trump administration has yet to release its industrial overcapacity” 
investigation into excess capacity, which is expected to result in higher 
tariffs on the targeted economies.
