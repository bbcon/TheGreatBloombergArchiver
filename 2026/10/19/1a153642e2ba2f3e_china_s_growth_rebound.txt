China’s economy is benefiting from its export boom, and that’s boosting the 
odds of reaching Beijing’s annual growth target 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-s-growth-rebounds-to-4-5-puts-annual-target-within-reach?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10252>
.

Gross domestic product grew 4.5% last quarter from a year ago after a gain of 
4.3% in the second quarter, data released Monday showed. That took expansion in 
the first nine months of the year to 4.6% — within the government’s annual goal 
of 4.5%-5%. More good news: China’shome prices fell at a slower pace 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-home-price-slump-eases-fueling-hopes-for-market-recovery?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10252>
 in September, spurring hopes that the battered real estate market is turning a 
corner.

The improvement should give policymakers some breathing room 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-19/china-s-growth-rebounds-while-home-price-slump-eases-video?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10252>
, after officials dialed uppro-growth support in recent weeks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-10-02/beijing-s-mini-stimulus-package-next-china?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10252>
 with a package of fresh targeted measures for the domestic economy. Still, 
frail local demand has left Chinaincreasingly dependent on exports for growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-10-14/china-s-soaring-exports-may-mean-more-pushback-from-trade-partners?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10252>
, and that’s put Beijing under rising pressure from trade partners to address 
its yawning surplus.
