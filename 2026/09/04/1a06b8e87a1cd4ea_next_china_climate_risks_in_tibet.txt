Hi, this is Dan Murtaugh in Beijing.

China started this year with plans to transform Tibet into an energy 
powerhouse due to its steep mountains and powerful rivers.

Don’t expect that to change 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-02/nepal-flood-exposes-risk-to-hydropower-in-himalayas-due-to-climate-change?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 even after it was at the center of the region’s worst disaster in years — one 
that has killed more than 1,000 people along the border with Nepal and renewed 
worry around the world about the immediaterisks of climate change 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/what-fueled-the-deadly-flash-floods-on-the-nepal-china-border?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
.

Tibet aims to expand power generation capacity from 13 gigawatts now to 60 
gigawatts in operation by 2030 with another 120 under construction. When all 
that’s eventually built, Tibet will have more power generating capacity than 
France does now.
Buildings caked in mud after floods hit Devighat, Nepal, last week.
Photographer: Arun Sharma/AP via PTI
At the center of the plan is a hydroelectric project on the Yarlung Tsangpo 
River in eastern Tibet — a project President Xi Jinpinghas linked to his legacy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-07-29/xi-s-167-billion-tibet-mega-dam-promises-to-spur-china-s-economy?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
. At an expected 60 gigawatts, it will be the world’s largest power plant when 
completed.

Beyond that are several so-called “integrated bases” where solar and wind 
power will complement smaller hydro projects.

The Himalayan mountains that feed Tibet’s rivers are great for hydropower 
because they offer two things that are rarely found together, according to Joe 
Bernardi, a project manager with Global Energy Monitor, a provider of 
open-access information.

The rivers are fed by both rain and snowmelt, allowing them to keep flowing 
outside the rainy season. They also have enormous vertical drops over short 
distances, naturally creating the force needed to spin turbines and generate 
electricity.

The problem is the terrain that makes these projects possible is undergoing 
rapid change 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-02/deadly-flooding-in-nepal-highlights-asia-s-climate-vulnerability?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
. Mountains are warming faster than the average for the rest of the planet as 
greenhouse gases accumulate in the atmosphere. Higher temperatures are melting 
snow and glaciers, making these environments more unstable and putting billions 
of people living downstream at risk.


Big Take Asia

Nepal’s Floods Expose Hydropower Risks
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2026-09-02/big-take-asia-nepal-s-floods-expose-hydropower-risks-podcast?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
Harnessing China’s rivers, which flow from the snowy peaks in the west to the 
fertile deltas in the east, has always been a prime concern of the nation’s 
leaders.

This stretches back more than 4,000 years, when a possibly mythological figure 
known asYu the Great gained eternal fame 
<https://thechinaproject.com/2020/08/24/yu-the-great-tamer-of-chinas-greatest-floods/>
 by using canals and other methods to control flooding that plagued the 
ancients.

Now the government wants large supplies of energy to power its tech ambitions, 
partly in AI 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-05-17/china-s-energy-boom-could-give-it-the-ai-edge-video?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
.

Thing is, climate change is making controlling nature more difficult. Weeks 
before the disaster along the border with Nepal,heavy rain brought by Typhoon 
Maysak 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/pacific-typhoon-season-forecast-to-be-among-most-active-ever?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 led to a fatal dam breach in the southern province of Guangxi.

The challenge — harnessing hydropower in the face of climate change — is very 
much on the minds of Chinese policymakers. In a five-year plan on climate 
policy released recently, the government called for population centers, 
infrastructure and industry to be located in areas at lower risk of hazards and 
disasters.

Climate change is likely to affect the hydropower projects that Tibet is 
building. More frequent floods will lead to building delays and cost overruns.

And a warmer world 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/un-says-global-warming-will-cross-1-5c-soon-hit-at-least-1-8c?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 leads to reduced ice storage and snowpack, meaning they might not generate as 
much power as engineers had hoped when they modeled them on historical river 
flows.

Still, it’s unlikely that Tibet will drop its hydropower plans. They’re too 
important for the nation.

While they will account for only a small fraction of the country’s total power 
output, the megadam in particular will helpfeed clean power into industrial 
hubs like Guangdong 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-21/why-china-s-167-billion-mega-dam-project-in-tibet-is-so-controversial?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
, shutting down coal plants and reducing emissions.

Perhaps more importantly, they’re too central to the economy of Tibet, a 
region known in Chinese as Xizang thatBeijing would like to better integrate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-20/xi-pushes-for-mega-dam-rail-links-to-boost-tibet-development?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 given long-standing ethnic and religious tensions. Construction will bring 
jobs and infrastructure. Electricity exports can contribute to long-term 
revenue for more development.

“From the government’s side, they’re quite supportive of these hydropower 
projects,” said Yuxi Wang, a senior research analyst with energy consultancy 
Wood Mackenzie. “They hold strategic importance not only for Xizang’s economy 
but for China’s national grid.”

Tough Timing

For a company known for churning out fashion as fast as anyone, one could 
argue that the timing for Shein’s IPO this week wasless than ideal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/shein-s-debut-shows-the-cost-of-ipo-missing-its-growth-peak?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
.

It raised some $1.7 billion in Hong Kong, giving the company a market value of 
slightly more than $26 billion, well below its peak of around $100 billion back 
around 2022.

The stock slid almost 10% in its debut 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/shein-set-to-debut-in-test-of-investor-demand-for-consumer-plays?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 on Tuesday though it ended the day nearly even. It then fell even more in the 
ensuing trading sessions.

Cautious investors were a hurdle. Right now they are keener on Chinese 
companies linked to AI and robotics than e-commerce.

The new valuation means that, at least on paper, some of Shein’s backers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/shein-s-private-backers-endured-73-valuation-drop-on-ipo?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 such as Boyu Capital, Coatue Management, Joshua Kushner-backed Thrive Capital 
and General Atlantic are sitting on losses. (Joshua Kushner is the younger 
brother of Jared, US President Donald Trump’s son-in-law.)

Shein, founded in China and now based in Singapore, had sought to list in New 
York or London, but those plans were eventually scuttled.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-01/shein-shares-plunge-in-hk-debut-after-rocky-road-to-ipo-video?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
The company was founded in 2012, and growth exploded during the pandemic. It 
became a household name in the US by using a data-driven supply chain, centered 
around the southern Chinese city of Guangzhou, to rapidly make and ship 
low-cost apparel directly to consumers.

Now it faces higher trade barriers in the US and Europe, and as a result its 
costs have been rising.

Still, Shein is one of the world’s largest listed apparel and fashion 
companies with a market cap of just slightly below Swedish retailer H&M’s $30 
billion. The big question iswhether its growth prospects 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/shein-ipo-what-to-know-about-hong-kong-listing-valuation?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 will entice investors or if the stock will be seen as overvalued and sold even 
more.

Its financial results showed notable swings of late. The company posted a loss 
of $99 million in the first quarter, versus a profit of $395 million in the 
same period a year ago. Revenue growth has slowed, its preliminary prospectus 
shows.

One thing that investors will be watching closely is how Shein fares against 
intensifying competition, especially from Temu. The firm said it plans to use 
the IPO money to strengthen its tech capabilities, expand its global brand 
presence, support corporate responsibility initiatives and general corporate 
purposes.

Shein bought American millennial clothing brand Everlane this year as part of a
global expansion plan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-26/shein-ipo-fashion-giant-bets-on-acquiring-more-brands-as-new-growth-strategy?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 centered around buying other fashion labels or partnering with them, and using 
its nimble supply chain in China to boost sales and margins. But that deal 
faces a national security check in the US.

The IPO does add to the Hong Kong bourse’s good run this year. It boosted 
proceeds from first-time share sales in the financial hub to more than $45 
billion, making 2026 the third-best year ever. The city is on track to break 
the record of $57.8 billion set in 2010, said Hong Kong Exchanges & Clearing 
CEO Bonnie Chan.

Pace Car

18

That’s how many months Chinese companies assisted by AI may soon need to bring 
cars to market, according to one estimate — down from the current two years and 
much quicker than the roughly three to five for legacy foreign competitors. The 
pace is fueling concern that rapid innovationcould outpace regulatory oversight 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/china-makes-cars-so-fast-that-it-s-starting-to-worry-regulators?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 and companies’ quality-control capacity.

Behind the Great Firewall

A weekly look at the big water cooler news in China.

The disaster along the border with Nepal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-nepal-flash-flood/?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 has been front of mind for many in China this week, raising worries about 
climate change and prompting donation drives.

On Chinese social media, the Real Madrid soccer team has come under fire from 
some users over a post commemorating the victims.

The Spanish team shared a photo on X of its team honoring victims before a 
match in Madrid. The caption read: “Moment of silence at the Bernabéu for the 
victims of the flood in Nepal and Tibet.”

The post was removed days later but not before it drew the ire of nationalist 
voices in China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/china-delays-film-on-battle-for-taiwan-after-nationalist-debate?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
, who accused the club of placing Tibet on the same footing as Nepal — that is, 
as a sovereign country — without spelling out that it is part of China. Real 
Madrid didn’t respond to messages or a phone call seeking comment.

Real Madrid didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

Some users contrasted the wording with that used by other European teams who 
honored landslide victims. FC Barcelona used “Nepal and China,” while Paris 
Saint-Germain referred to “China’s Tibet Autonomous Region and Nepal.”

Two related Weibo hashtags on the subject attracted a combined 50 million 
views, with many commentators demanding Real Madrid apologize.

Angry comments also flooded the team’s official Weibo account. Some Chinese 
users also took their criticism to X — a platform blocked in China and 
accessible only through tools like VPNs — to respond.

“As a 20-years+ Real Madrid fan, if you don’t change the word and apologize, I 
will never support Real Madrid,” wrote a user who said they were from China.
Real Madrid and Malaga players honor flood victims in Madrid on Aug. 30.
Photographer: Jesus Troyano/Real Madrid/Getty Images
Chinese state media also weighed in on the controversy. The Global Times, a 
state-run tabloidknown for its nationalistic tone 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/china-media-hails-canada-s-chinese-style-tariff-retaliation?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
, said in an online commentary that Real Madrid should take down its post and 
use the term “China’s Xizang” instead.

In recent years, the government and state media have increasingly replaced 
“Tibet” with “Xizang” — the pinyin romanization of Tibet’s Mandarin name — in 
English-language statements and reports. The shift seems to be part of China’s 
effort toreinforce its control 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-20/xi-pushes-for-mega-dam-rail-links-to-boost-tibet-development?cmpid=BBD090426_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8480>
 over the Himalayan region that it invaded in 1950 and later annexed.

“Real Madrid’s flawed caption eroded all goodwill, twisting a solemn tribute 
into an improper move that crossed the red line of China’s sovereignty,” the 
Global Times commentary said.

The criticism was echoed by readers. One comment that got more than 3,700 
likes called Real Madrid’s post “completely absurd.”
