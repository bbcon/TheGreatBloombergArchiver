**Brazil Elections**
Sunday's first-round presidential vote is the region's most consequential near-term political event. Lula's Workers' Party faces meaningful erosion in northeast Brazil, home to more than a quarter of the national electorate, as voters who were lifted out of poverty a generation ago now demand responses to modern affordability pressures and security concerns. The outcome will have significant implications for Brazil's fiscal trajectory, trade policy, and commodity sector governance.

**El Niño and Policy Risk**
El Niño is emerging as a material macro risk across Latin America. T. Rowe Price has deployed custom climate analytics to assess sovereign credit exposure, with Colombian bonds a particular focus given hydroelectric dependence and agricultural sensitivity. The Colombian central bank's apparent reluctance to tighten sufficiently in response to supply-driven inflation pressures raises the prospect of a policy error, a risk investors are beginning to price more explicitly.
