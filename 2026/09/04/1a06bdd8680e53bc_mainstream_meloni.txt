A mark of Giorgia Meloni’s success is how little the word “fascism” is 
mentioned in association with her now, if ever.

Her record speaks for itself. She presides over Italy’s longest-serving 
government 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/giorgia-meloni-enters-the-history-books-pursued-by-italy-s-far-right?cmpid=BBD090426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8552>
 since the republic was founded after World War II.

During an asphyxiatingly hot summer, UK Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch 
sought Meloni’s advice over coffee in Rome. These days, Italy looks like a 
study in stability, and Britain the basket case.

But appearances can be deceptive.

Having moved away from the political fringes, outmaneuvered rivals and 
established herself firmly in the mainstream as a G7 veteran, Meloni must now 
face the reckoning that comes from being, well, successful and part of the 
establishment.

When she didn’t do Donald Trump’s bidding, because it’s unpopular to be seen 
as subservient to the US, no European leader came to her defense as the US 
presidentviciously turned on her 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-23/italy-seeks-to-mend-ties-with-the-us-after-trump-meloni-blowout?cmpid=BBD090426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8552>
.
Meloni and Trump.Photographer: Jakub Porzycki/NurPhoto/AP Photo
And having long shed the fascist label, a former general with pro-Russian 
views (which Meloni tellingly doesn’t share) has suddenly appeared as athorn in 
her side 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/italy-s-league-faces-northern-split-as-national-future-surges?cmpid=BBD090426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8552>
. His being a Putin apologist has raised alarms given Russia’s more brazen 
attacks at the heart of Europe.

Roberto Vannacci’s self-published book-slash-anti-woke manual entitled The 
World Upside Down became an instant hit.

A far-right firebrand, he occupies the space she once occupied 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/meloni-party-official-floats-alliance-with-italy-far-right-group?cmpid=BBD090426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8552>
 as a populist insurgent, attracting headlines by praising Benito Mussolini as 
a statesman.

There was a time not so long ago when the prospect of Meloni becoming the 
first woman prime minister seemed remote — and no interviewer could resist a 
question on Mussolini.

As a teen she praised him as a good politician. In a 2020 interview with 
Bloomberg she was far more nuanced: He was a historical phenomenon who should 
be judged in the relevant context.

Yet the past is stalking Europe, in Germany as in Italy.

For every label shed on the way up, another aspiring political leader is more

Trump’s $185 billion Golden Dome defense program asks companies to do what 
Ronald Reagan couldn’t: build interceptors to shoot down missiles from space. 
But the firms must fund much of the development themselves. The effort has 
becomea high-stakes bet 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-trump-golden-dome-unproven-bet/?cmpid=BBD090426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8552>

of Trump’s term in 2028 when it’s their investors — and not taxpayers — on the 
line.
