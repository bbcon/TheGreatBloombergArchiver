As the giant plume of dust from the US midterm elections 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/republican-blame-game-starts-as-2028-hopefuls-angle-for-position?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11039>
 begins to settle, market focus is returning to more tangible concerns.

For one, banks across the Asia Pacific region are taking an earnings hit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/nab-profit-misses-estimates-as-housing-slump-weighs-on-banks?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11039>
. National Australia Bank and Singapore’s DBS are among lenders grappling with 
higher funding costs and bigger outlays for credit losses that have eroded 
their profitability, even as they await the boost to their lending income.

Melbourne-based NAB, which reported an increase in credit impairment charges, 
is cutting up to 2,500 roles as it navigates the sluggish economy and rising 
interest rates. Meanwhile, DBSsuffered a tumble 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/dbs-profit-misses-estimates-on-trading-income-names-lim-chair?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11039>
 in markets trading income and CEO Tan Su Shan cautioned that the outlook has 
become more uncertain.

Yesterday, DBS’s smaller Singapore rival UOB saw earnings hit by a drop in 
trading and investment income 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/uob-profits-miss-estimates-as-lower-trading-income-dampens-gains?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11039>
, together with narrowing interest margins as rivals compete intensely for 
deposits. And earlier this week, Westpac Banking said its impairment charges
more than doubled 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-01/westpac-profit-misses-estimates-ceo-says-economy-uncertain?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11039>
 in the year and was a key factor impacting the dip in its net profit.

This is setting up a grim backdrop for the rest of major bank earnings that 
are coming, from Commonwealth Bank of Australia to Japan’s so-called megabanks.

Benny Tai.
