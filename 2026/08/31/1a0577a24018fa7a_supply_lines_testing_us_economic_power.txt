The jamboree in Jackson Hole that concluded over the weekend will undoubtedly 
getmore attention 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/warsh-s-inflation-warning-sets-up-september-showdown-for-the-fed?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
. But if you are tracking the history of the global economy and American power, 
a meeting that starts Monday in another mountain town is probably more 
consequential.

One should never oversell the significance of the Group of 20 leading 
economies and its meetings. But as my Bloombergcolleagues and I write 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/bessent-takes-us-growth-agenda-message-to-g20-summit-in-asheville?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
, the gathering of G20 finance ministers getting underway today in the Blue 
Ridge Mountains town of Asheville is emerging as an important credibility test 
for US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and his interventionist take on 
American might. Albeit one that Bloomberg reporters alongside those from 
several other outlets have beenbarred from attending 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/us-treasury-bars-reporters-from-several-outlets-from-g20-meeting?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
.

After running into pushback from markets, Bessent now has to sell his global 
peers on his vision. The early signs are that they’re not convinced. And, if 
that holds true, in the long run of history that will matter more for America’s 
standing in the global economy and its influence than theearly stumbles 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/bond-investors-wary-after-warsh-fans-wagers-fed-poised-to-hike?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 of new Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh.

It’s not hard to make the case today that the world is signaling it has found 
the limits of America’s economic power.

There are individual and technical sticking points but the trade war with 
Canada 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/canada-s-ford-likens-trump-s-actions-to-saturday-night-live-skit?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 is ultimately one about the refusal of the US’s northern neighbor to bow to 
Washington and allow it to dictate the other relationships it can have.

The same goes for the economic campaign against Iran 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/world-mostly-shrugs-off-bessent-s-d-day-iran-sanctions-threat?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 and the threats to crack down on allies and rivals alike if they do any 
business with Tehran.

China Stability

It will only truly matter if Bessent opts to extend sanctions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/bessent-pledges-to-isolate-iran-from-global-economy?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 to major Chinese banks. Which seems unlikely at least in the near term with 
the US and China both eager to extend a fragile trade truce. It would also 
probably accelerate China’s moves to establish a credible alternative to the 
dollar system.

Bessent is carrying a “you’re either with us or against us” message into the 
G20 meeting. In bilateral sessions with his peers he is likely to reinforce 
that message. Frank talks withmembers like China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/bessent-to-push-g20-to-rethink-china-trade-terms-reuters-says?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
, India and Turkey are undoubtedly on his agenda.

Read More: German Finance Minister to Press G20 on Iran War, Trade Fights 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/german-finance-minister-to-press-g20-on-iran-war-trade-fights?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>

He’s made clear already that he is willing to use the dollar’s reserve status 
as an economic weapon to get allies and foes alike to bend to Washington’s 
will. “Our partnership now carries expectations. And, in some instances, 
non-negotiable obligations,”he told 
<https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0539> the Economic Club of New 
York in June.

There is no doubt that Bessent is entering the G20 meeting with influence and 
a spotlight on him.

Nathan Sheets, a Fed and Treasury veteran who is now Citigroup’s global chief 
economist, saysBessent’s activism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 — combined with Warsh’s newcomer status and preference for more limited 
communications — have created an unusual moment when the Treasury secretary is 
the more dominant figure.

‘Big Brother’

“It feels to me like at the moment Bessent is the big brother and Warsh is the 
little brother,” Sheets said.

Like many brothers, the two don’t seem to agree on their priorities. Bessent, 
who has to service$40 trillion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/bessent-s-treasury-twist-makes-november-refunding-a-wall-street-wildcard?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 in government debt, is seeking to bring down borrowing costs. Warsh says he’s 
focused on reining in stubbornly high inflation, a message he reiterated Friday 
at Jackson Hole, amid debate inside the Fed over the need for higher interest 
rates.

That incoherence matters for the projection of American power. When the top 
two economic policymakers in the world’s largest economy have different 
priorities, that is likely to have consequences for everyone else. Especially 
as financial markets offer a daily gauge of US credibility.

Related: Bessent Expects BOJ to ‘Do the Right Thing’ on Policy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/bessent-expects-boj-to-do-the-right-thing-on-monetary-policy?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>

You could see it in the movements of the Japanese yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/yen-weakens-past-160-per-dollar-eroding-intervention-gain?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 on Friday. A hawkish message from Warsh and a signal that higher US rates may 
be nigh led the yen to weaken again to thesymbolic 160 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/bessent-says-a-disorderly-yen-could-ultimately-raise-us-rates?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 to the dollar level that prompted Bessent’s earlier unusual intervention. 
Which in turn puts more pressure on the Bank of Japan to raise rates.

Underlying this all is the biggest question of them all. No one likes a fiscal 
deficit around 6% of GDP or the $40 trillion pile of debt. But the real 
question is, will bond traders in the days to come return from their summer 
vacations and finally declare their doubts about the US fiscal path and force 
action?

Market Pushback

Bessent has already faced pushback from financial markets. The very public 
slap-down 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/druckenmiller-bessent-s-early-mentor-calls-bond-buys-a-mistake?cmpid=BBD083126_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8290>
 from Bessent mentor Stanley Druckenmiller may well end up being a more 
consequential document than Warsh’s speech in Wyoming. Don’t mess with US 
Treasuries and their yields was its unequivocal message.

Economic statecraft is the term of the moment. It’s meant to describe the art 
and science of using tools like tariffs, sanctions and export controls to 
leverage economic power. It’s where geopolitics meets economics and becomes 
geoeconomics.

It also assumes power. But what happens when that power encounters its limits? 
It sure feels like that may be what we are about to start finding out in 
Asheville this week and beyond.

last year on a multi-decade surge in inbound shipments of capital equipment. 
The shortfall in goods trade grew 17.2% from the prior month to $118.8 billion, 
the largest since March 2025, Commerce Department data showed. Merchandise 
imports climbed 3.7%, fueled by the largest increase in capital goods since 
1993. US exports of goods fell 2.9%.
