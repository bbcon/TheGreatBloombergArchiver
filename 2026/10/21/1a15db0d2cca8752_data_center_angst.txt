Volatility around data centers is increasing and starting to have ripple 
effects on initial public offerings.

Take Singapore-based DayOne Data Centers, which is weighing options for its US 
IPO 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/dayone-is-said-to-weigh-options-for-us-ipo-amid-data-center-rout?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10345>
, including a potential delay until the market recovers, a Bloomberg exclusive 
revealed. Other possibilities include going ahead with a smaller listing that 
may raise $2.5 billion to $3 billion, roughly half of what it had originally 
aimed for, according to people familiar with the matter.

Earlier this month, Australia’s Firmus Grid dropped its own IPO plans, though 
the Information reported that Nvidia is intalks to invest hundreds of millions 
of dollars 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/nvidia-in-talks-to-back-firmus-after-ipo-flop-information-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10345>
 in the company. Meanwhile, another Australian data center operator took a hit 
Wednesday. Nextdc’sshares plummeted 42% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/nextdc-cuts-outlook-as-public-backlash-delays-data-center-power?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10345>
 after the firm said it was considering force majeure options and cut earnings 
guidance by more than a third, as community opposition delayed power to one of 
its major projects in Melbourne.

Billionaire Ken Griffin only added to the anxiety over the global AI buildout. 
Investors are “almost certainly” in themidst of an AI bubble 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/ken-griffin-says-ai-almost-certainly-in-a-bubble-right-now?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261021&utm_content=10345>
, he said, as many companies are still grappling with just how much they’ll 
spend on the technology and how it will transform the economy.

Bank Indonesia Holds Rate as MSCI Shock Threatens Rupiah

Indonesia’s central bank kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged, opting to 
continue relying on targeted measures to support the rupiah through the market 
volatility triggered by MSCI’s double-downgrade threat. The rate was held at 
5.75%, as seen by 19 of 38 economists surveyed by Bloomberg. The other half 
expected a hike.
