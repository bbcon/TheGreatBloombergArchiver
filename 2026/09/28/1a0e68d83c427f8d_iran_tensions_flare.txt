Deep Dive: A London Bridge Is Falling Down
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-28/london-s-waterloo-bridge-at-risk-of-shutdown-within-three-years?cmpid=092826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9367>
Traffic and pedestrians cross the River Thames on Waterloo Bridge.Photographer: 
Jason Alden/Bloomberg
London’s Waterloo Bridge is heading toward closure within three to four years 
unless vital repairs are carried out, the latest sign of how ageing 
infrastructure risks shutting down anotherkey artery into the capital 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-28/london-s-waterloo-bridge-at-risk-of-shutdown-within-three-years?cmpid=092826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9367>
.

 * An inspection of the bridge, which links the South Bank and London’s West 
End, found water damage and cracks as wide as 2 millimeters, Westminster City 
Council said in a funding-bid document which Bloomberg obtained in response to 
a Freedom of Information request.
 * Without further repair work by the end of the 2029-30 financial year, 
additional measures could include the introduction of weight limits “or 
ultimately full closure of Waterloo Bridge,” the council said.
 * Britain’s river crossings have been a slow-burning infrastructure problem, 
strained by heavy traffic and extreme weather. The situation has grown more 
acute over the past seven years as funding challenges push some deeper into 
disrepair, and now London’s bridges really are falling down.
 * Hammersmith Bridge, built in 1887, shut to cars during a heat wave in 2019 
after cracks were found in the cast-iron structure. In February, Kensington and 
Chelsea Council barred cars from Albert Bridge, another Victorian crossing 
along the River Thames. Transport for London imposed further weight limits on 
Vauxhall Bridge over the summer and warned that without repairs, further 
restrictions could be introduced by 2030.
 * “We are often in London dealing with infrastructure that was built for a 
city 150, 200 years ago,” David Knight, a structural engineer and adviser to 
the Institution of Civil Engineers, said in an interview. Even then, engineers 
are usually on the “back foot when it comes to maintenance budgets,” Knight 
said.
