Daily Brief

Thursday, August 20, 2026

US

  • US public debt topped $40 trillion for the first time, up a third in under five years, landing hours after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Treasury would at least double buybacks of 10- to 30-year debt to arrest rising long yields.
  • Fed minutes from the July 29 FOMC showed officials sharply divided over further hikes; most respondents to Bloomberg's Markets Pulse survey still expect the 10-year yield to top 5%.
  • The buyback news pulled the 10-year yield down about 7bp and the 30-year 8bp to 5.19%, but the S&P 500 gained just 0.21% and the dollar posted its worst day in months.
  • Moderna shares surged as much as 176% on positive melanoma-vaccine trial data with Merck, lifting the S&P 500 healthcare index over 4% and inflicting roughly $5-5.5 billion in mark-to-market short losses.
  • Trump announced an "economic D-Day" against Iran, threatening penalties on any country, bank or business still trading with Tehran — putting China, Iran's top oil buyer, in the crosshairs.

Treasury and Fiscal Policy

Bessent's move reflects unease over debt sustainability rather than a resolution of it: JPMorgan warned the buyback expansion risks a credibility problem absent real fiscal consolidation, and the still-rising yield curve suggests investors want more than symbolism to bring borrowing costs down.

Markets

Equities barely reacted to the bond news, underscoring that AI earnings, not Treasuries, remain the dominant driver of sentiment for now. Gold instead posted its best day in six months and Bitcoin surged past $70,000, as investors read the buyback as a step toward financial repression rather than a durable fix.

Asia

  • South Korea's Kospi jumped 5.9% as Samsung and SK Hynix unveiled record shareholder returns — Samsung reportedly lining up more than $72 billion and SK Hynix announcing a $29 billion buyback — evidence the AI capex boom is translating into cash.
  • Japan's exports rose 23.2% year-on-year in July, the fastest pace since 2022 and up from June's 19.3%, driven by cars, chips and a yen at a 40-year low.
  • Trump's threatened "economic D-Day" against Iran puts China, the top buyer of Iranian crude, at the center of a potential new front in US-China tension.
  • China sentenced Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan to life in prison and fined the developer 15.82 billion yuan ($2.4 billion) for accounting fraud, closing out the property collapse that has now swept up 57 defendants.

Capital Flows and Policy

India's central bank has raised roughly $57 billion of a targeted $80 billion through rupee-support measures, yet the currency remains little changed from June, even as hawkish RBI minutes put rate hikes back in play. SEBI separately barred a Mauritius-based JPMorgan unit from Indian markets over alleged manipulation of the country's new closing-auction system, one of its largest actions against an international firm since the Jane Street case.

Markets

Australia's unemployment rate unexpectedly rose to 4.5% in July, leading traders to pare bets on further rate increases even as the country's national debt briefly topped A$1 trillion. Luxury sales continued to slide in China amid a tax crackdown on wealthy shoppers, while Shein lined up Boyu Capital, UBS and Tencent as cornerstone investors for a roughly $2 billion Hong Kong IPO.

Europe

  • Sweden's Riksbank held its policy rate unchanged and reiterated it could still hike this year, one of the only explicit central-bank data points from the region on the day.
  • London could lose up to £36 billion ($50 billion) a year in GDP by the 2050s from climate-related disruption, according to a report from the Mayor's office, which urges employers to adapt working hours and infrastructure to extreme heat.
  • A separate analysis found the UK Treasury would save as much as £32 billion ($43.3 billion) a year if NHS productivity had kept pace with pre-pandemic trends; NHS resources rose 26% from 2019-2025 while output grew just 19%.
  • JD Sports fell 10% in London after cutting its profit outlook on weaker sneaker demand.
  • UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the government will prepare new targeted sanctions against Israelis involved in West Bank settlement expansion, as Germany's Chancellor Merz condemned separate comments by an Israeli minister calling for nightly assassinations in Gaza.

Growth and Fiscal Pressures

Absent a major eurozone data print, the day's economic signal came from structural cost estimates: London's climate exposure and the NHS's productivity shortfall both point to a widening fiscal drag that will weigh on UK growth over the coming decades if unaddressed.

Markets and Politics

Equity moves were driven by company-specific news rather than macro data, with JD Sports' guidance cut standing out against an otherwise quiet corporate tape. Politically, the UK and Germany's responses to developments in Gaza added to a backdrop of diplomatic friction with Israel.

LatAm

  • Brazilian President Lula's odds of reelection have climbed above 60% in betting markets, buoyed by a nationalist backlash against Trump's tariffs and by Jair Bolsonaro's decision to hand his political legacy to son Flávio, whose campaign has struggled to broaden its appeal.
  • US oilfield-services group SLB and independent producer Hunt Oil signed new contracts with Venezuela's state oil company PDVSA, with Pacific Coast Energy said to be close behind, though the bilateral, non-competitive process remains opaque and lacks clear dispute-resolution rules.
  • The tentative US-Canada tariff deal is piling pressure on Mexico's negotiators to secure comparable relief, even as the US Commerce Department made a preliminary finding that Mexican producers are dumping strawberries into the US market below cost.
  • In Colombia, only a few dozen former militia members have demobilized under a peace initiative that President Abelardo de la Espriella has effectively abandoned in favor of renewed offensives against armed groups.

Brazil's Election Economy

Lula's advantage owes as much to Trump's aggressive regional posture as to the domestic economy: unemployment is low and inflation contained, but voters remain unconvinced by his record on security, the top-ranked concern heading into the vote. A win would make him Brazil's longest-serving president of the democratic era.

Venezuela and Trade

Venezuela's push to re-attract US oil capital reflects both genuine investor appetite — shale veterans call the reserve base underexploited — and the country's poor historical record on contract enforcement, a tension that will determine whether the current wave of deals sustains momentum.

Global

  • President Trump announced an "economic D-Day" against Iran, threatening penalties on any country, bank or business trading with Tehran, after months of military strikes and a maritime blockade failed to force capitulation; WTI crude climbed to a nearly four-week high on the news.
  • Commercial transits through the Strait of Hormuz remain suppressed at around 5% of prewar throughput following the August 17 expiration of the 60-day US-Iran ceasefire, even as Chinese refiners increasingly substitute Iraqi crude for barrels no longer exiting the Gulf.
  • US refiners processed 17.4 million barrels a day last week, the highest rate since September 2019, as the industry works to keep global diesel, jet fuel and gasoline markets supplied through the conflict.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent's expanded bond buybacks rattled the dollar globally, sending it to its worst day in months, while lifting emerging-market currencies, gold and Bitcoin, which surged past $70,000.
  • A Russian missile strike on Kyiv killed at least 12 people and wounded roughly 40 more, as escalating Black Sea hostilities continue to disrupt Ukrainian grain exports, with knock-on risks to food security in some developing countries.

Iran and Oil Markets

Trump's pivot to economic warfare marks an implicit acknowledgment of the military campaign's limits, with US resources stretched and gasoline prices an increasing political liability ahead of the midterms. Analysts remain divided on whether tightening sanctions can extract political concessions from Tehran that months of force have not, and China's role as Iran's largest oil customer makes the threat as much a test of Washington's relationship with Beijing as further pressure on Tehran.
