Deep Dive: Hot Potatoes

Europe is set for one of its smallest-ever potato harvests after a succession 
of heat waves and lingering drought battered thewater-hungry staple 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/hot-potatoes-mean-higher-prices-and-shorter-fries-in-europe?cmpid=091726_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9005>
.

 * The summer heat is likely to curb the crop in Europe and the UK by about 
3.5 million tons, according to analysis from the Energy and Climate 
Intelligence Unit. That could cost farmers more than €800 million ($923 
million), given the current range of prices, it said.
 * For consumers, the squeeze could soon show up on supermarket shelves in the 
form of smaller baked potatoes, shorter fries and ultimately higher prices. The 
searing temperatures that cut yields were also compounded by a decline in 
plantings, following a potato glut last year.
 * “No one escaped this summer’s heat,” said Jan Van Luchene, the owner of 
Bruwier Potatoes, west of Brussels. “We are in a new perfect storm, with a 
considerably smaller acreage, coinciding with historically poor yields, caused 
by drought and more importantly by heat.”
 * Potato yields have been hit hard, dropping 21% in Belgium, 13% in France 
and 10% in Germany. The impact is already filtering through to prices.
