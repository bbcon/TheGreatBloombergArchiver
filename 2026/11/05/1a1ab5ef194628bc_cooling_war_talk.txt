opt for a diplomatic resolution.

Tuesday’s election laid bare just how much Americans are against the war.

The fighting was meant to last weeks, with Trump and Israeli Prime Minister 
Benjamin Netanyahu relishing the prospect of toppling the Islamic Republic’s 
leadership. But the conflict is now in its ninth month and deadlocked.

Energy prices have soared, while US stocks of interceptor missiles are depleted
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/key-us-munitions-are-dangerously-low-bloomberg-economics-says?cmpid=BBD110526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11047>

It’s still up more than 50% this year. But many traders now believe the 
election losses will chasten Trump and curb whatever remaining desire he and 
Netanyahu — for whom the midterms were a serious blow — have for escalation.

Iranians and Gulf Arab states are still on edge 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/despite-peace-talks-iranians-brace-for-next-wave-of-us-bombing?cmpid=BBD110526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11047>
. Trump might push ahead with strikes regardless. Iran, emboldened, may yet 
demand a deal the US cannot accept.

Hong Kong’s jewelry and watch sales have surged to their highest level since 
2019 despite China’s broader luxury slowdown, as the rich turn to gold and 
gemstones to preserve wealth after Beijing tightened its grip over their 
offshore investments. The shopping spree underscores how Chinese consumers are 
prioritizing wealth preservation over growth, and how gold and precious stones 
have emerged asrare safe havens
