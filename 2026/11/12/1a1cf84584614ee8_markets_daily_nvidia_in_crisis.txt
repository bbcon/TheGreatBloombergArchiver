From unthinkable to inevitable?

Nvidia and the fate of Jensen Huang is all that matters in markets this morning
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/live-blog/2026-11-11/nvidia-turmoil-board-to-weigh-ceo-jensen-huang-future-over-alleged-affairs?cmpid=111226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11325>
, and for good reason: A big chunk of the AI trade that has propelled equity 
markets over the past four years depends on the company’s chips.

Huang is the company’s co-founder, and under his watch the stock has returned 
a staggering 38% a year since its 1999 IPO. Gains have been even more stunning 
since OpenAI kicked off the AI boom in 2022 with ChatGPT.

“He is not only the face of AI but arguably the architect of the AI 
infrastructure,” said Joe Gilbert of Integrity Asset Management. “It will be 
difficult if not impossible to replicate Jensen’s vision.”

The stock — already down almost 30% from its early-October peak — is sinking 
again in premarket trading. If that holds, we’re looking at a wipeout of more 
than $241 billion that sends Nvidia’s market value below $4 trillion.

Turning less bullish

When a stock has returned 760,000%, as Nvidia has since its IPO, analysts will 
do whatever they can to stay bullish on it. At the end of last week, 79 of 82 
analysts tracked by Bloomberg had buys on Nvidia.

But cracks were emerging in Wall Street’s overwhelmingly bullish consensus, 
even before the New York Times reported yesterday that Huang had a second 
extramarital affair, this time with a subordinate.

Analysts at Bernstein and Oppenheimer have cut their ratings 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/nvidia-tumbles-as-investors-consider-future-without-ceo-huang?cmpid=111226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11325>
 this week, while Morgan Stanley removed the stock as a top pick. Baird cut its 
price target to $200 from $500. Dan Ives at Yorkville Ives put the estimated 
fair value of Nvidia without Huang at $225, versus $300 per share if he stays 
as CEO.

“If he goes, the bull case goes with him,” Jay Goldberg of Seaport Global 
Securities, the lone analyst with a sell rating, said this week. “If he gets 
ousted, the floor would fall out from under the stock.” —Subrat Patnaik 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVr07GqbP-I/subrat-patnaik?cmpid=111226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11325>
