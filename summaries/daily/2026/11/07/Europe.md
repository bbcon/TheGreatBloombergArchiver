**COP31 and Turkey**
Turkey's hosting of COP31, beginning November 9, places the country at the center of global climate diplomacy at a moment when its domestic circumstances illustrate the summit's core themes. The Turkish-Syrian border region is experiencing an intensifying cycle of drought and flooding that has materially disrupted agricultural output — honey production among Syrian refugee communities has fallen by approximately 50% over five years due to climate-related stress. Official development assistance flows have declined 23% from 2024 to 2025, constraining adaptation capacity precisely when it is most needed. The gap between climate financing pledges and on-the-ground implementation is expected to be a central tension at the summit.

**Geopolitical Headwinds**
The Iran conflict has pushed energy security up government agendas across Europe and beyond, crowding out climate action as a near-term policy priority. The US will not send an official delegation to COP31 — its second consecutive absence — ahead of its formal UNFCCC withdrawal in February 2027. For European governments that have positioned climate leadership as a strategic differentiator, the combination of US disengagement and Iran-related energy market disruption presents a difficult navigation challenge. Whether the summit can deliver concrete implementation commitments rather than aspirational language remains genuinely uncertain.
