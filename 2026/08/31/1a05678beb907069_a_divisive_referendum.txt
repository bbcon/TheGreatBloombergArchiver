Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses on sale in Rome.Photographer: Alessia 
Pierdomenico/Bloomberg
The fight over the late Leonardo Del Vecchio’s more than €40 billion fortune 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/essilorluxottica-clan-s-40-billion-rift-grows-with-heir-s-exit?cmpid=083126_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8339>
 deepened after the exit of one of his heirs from management roles at the 
eyewear company he founded.

 * Leonardo Maria Del Vecchio, the fourth son of Leonardo Del Vecchio, had 
sought — and failed — to break the deadlock at the family firm Delfin Sarl, the 
biggest shareholder of EssilorLuxottica.
 * His abrupt decision, made public Tuesday, to step down from roles at the 
eyewear company and focus on his family office LMDV Capital removes from the 
group an heir at the center of the latest attempt to reshape Delfin’s 
ownership. Differences among the eight heirs and their many advisers on the 
structure, ownership and operations at Delfin have led to a governance crisis 
at a key corporate player in Italy.
 * The consequences stretch well beyond EssilorLuxottica into the country’s 
financial sector. Delfin is the biggest shareholder of Monte Paschi, the lender 
at the center of Italy’s latest round of bank dealmaking, and also has a stake 
in Assicurazioni Generali, one of Europe’s largest insurers, alongside an 
investment in UniCredit.
 * For EssilorLuxottica, the stalemate at the family estate is becoming a 
distraction as it strives to hang on to its first-mover lead in the market for 
AI smart glasses, which it produces with Meta, and protect its profit margins. 
That’s becoming increasingly challenging as the likes of Apple, Google and 
Samsung enter the fray.
