**China's Faltering Recovery**
Beijing's September stimulus package has yet to transmit meaningfully into activity. The simultaneous weakness in consumption, investment, and industrial output suggests the economy risks losing momentum again rather than finding a durable floor. The property sector remains the structural drag: iron ore prices fell to a four-year low, and China's steel mills — the majority of which are unprofitable — face what the China Iron and Steel Association has described as a painful winter. Beijing appears disinclined toward broad demand-side stimulus, opting instead for structural consolidation, which may sustain elevated steel export volumes and intensify trade friction.

**Currency and Capital Flow Pressures**
The rupiah's record low reflects compounding pressures — a stronger dollar, hawkish Fed expectations, and domestic political uncertainty — that may be difficult to arrest. Taiwan's dollar paradox is analytically striking: despite the island's longest streak of double-digit quarterly growth in four decades and the world's fourth-largest current-account surplus, the currency is approaching decade lows as export earnings accumulate offshore rather than repatriating. Alibaba's launch of its Qwen4-Max model, which reportedly outperforms Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 on coding benchmarks, underscores that the US-China AI competition remains dynamic despite US export restrictions.
