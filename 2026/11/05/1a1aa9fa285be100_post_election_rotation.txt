For decades, Israel has been able to rely on rock-solid bipartisan American 
support for its every move 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/midterms-leave-israel-fearing-us-alliance-fundamentally-broken?cmpid=110526_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11037>
, including diplomatic cover on the international stage and hundreds of 
billions of dollars in aid. But now all of that is being called into question.

 * Michigan’s newly elected senator, Abdul el-Sayed, campaigned to end all aid 
to Israel and called its war in Gaza a genocide. Georgians reelected Senator 
Jon Ossoff, among the first Senate Democrats to vote against offensive weapons 
sales to Israel in 2024.
 * The Democratic wins followed hours after Donald Trump told Fox News that 
the US would end $3.8 billion in annual military aid to Israel — noting that 
politically, it had become “almost too easy a target.”
 * Vice President JD Vance took to social media, citing the need to “change 
our relationship to Israel” as one of the key ways of winning back Congress and 
the White House in 2028.
 * While legislation restricting US arms transfers to Israel is unlikely to 
become law under Trump, shifting public and congressional sentiment could 
eventually translate into policy that would limit Israel’s access to munitions 
and expose it to broader sanctions.
