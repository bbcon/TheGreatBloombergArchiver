Those with the clearest inside knowledge of AI frontier models are suddenly 
agreeing that slowing down the pace of development is advisable. But with the 
US and China competing in a race for tech supremacy, any deceleration risks 
allowing the rival to get ahead. Caution, in other words, could equal surrender.

Countries should promote 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/china-rejects-ai-fearmongering-after-amodei-urges-slowdown?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8879>
 “open, inclusive, universally beneficial and ethical” AI development, Chinese 
Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun said at a regular briefing in Beijing. 
“Fearmongering, confrontation and vicious competition will only disrupt the 
process of global AI governance and serve the interests of no one,” he said.

Over the weekend, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for companies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/ai-bosses-risk-clash-with-wall-street-and-trump-over-safety-cal?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8879>
 to moderate the pace at which they improve AI capabilities, warning that 
safeguards are failing to keep up. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman backed Amodei’s 
proposal to give independent evaluators employee-like access, while Elon Musk 
agreed with the Anthropic chief.

Yet Amodei also argued that any effort by US companies to moderate development 
would be constrained by the lead they maintain over China. If American labs 
slowed too much, Beijing could pull ahead and create what he described as a 
grave danger to the US and the world. State-run Global Times attacked Amodei’s 
proposals, accusing him of waging a “silent AI Cold War” to suppress China’s 
technological development.

The dispute is intensifying days before anticipated AI discussions ahead of US 
President Donald Trump’s summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping on Sept. 24.

“The boundary between ‘AI safety’ and ‘AI competition’ is becoming 
increasingly blurred,” said Sun Chenghao, a fellow at Tsinghua University’s 
Center for International Security and Strategy in Beijing.

What You Need to Know Today

All this talk of slowing AI development weighed on tech stocks. Nasdaq 100 
futures sank 1.8%. An ETF tracking key chip shares dropped 4.7% in early trading
as traders fretted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/us-stock-futures-fall-on-ai-warning-oil-gains-markets-wrap?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8879>
 that efforts to rein in AI could weigh on the boom driving hundreds of 
billions of dollars in capital spending. OpenAI backer SoftBank slid the most 
in nearly three months, while South Korea’s Kospi index dropped 3.3%. S&P 500 
contracts were down 0.8%.

The selloff in tech stocks comes at the start of a week in which both the 
Federal Reserve and Bank of Japan face pressure to raise rates as policymakers 
meet Wednesday and Friday, respectively. While a Bank of England hike on 
Thursday isn’t anticipated, the prospect of a shift toward an increase in 
November remains on the cards.

Pricier energy: Oil pushed higher as Saudi Arabia’s closure of a major crude 
pipeline following attacks jolted the market, with the move disrupting a route 
that’s been key to bypassing the Strait of Hormuz during the US-Iran war.

Brent rose as much 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-14?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8879>
 as 3.9% to above $108 a barrel, before paring gains, while West Texas 
Intermediate was near $103. Saudi Arabia said late on Friday that it had halted 
the East-West pipeline as a precaution after attacks the previous day. There’s 
been no indication of when operations will resume.

Indonesia’s Prabowo Ousts Finance Chief Purbaya After Rocky Year

Finance Minister Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa had served as finance minister since 
last September, when he replaced the long-serving Sri Mulyani Indrawati in the 
wake of deadly street protests. During his roughly year-long tenure, he pushed 
for faster economic growth while pledging to keep the budget deficit within 
Indonesia’s legal limit.

No drones: Beijing has banned the possession and use of recreational drones 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/beijing-bans-drones-in-sweeping-curbs-after-citic-tower-crash?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8879>
 as part of moves to bolster security in the capital after a light plane 
crashed into the city’s tallest skyscraper in June.

The restrictions — some of the tightest in the world on drones — come after a 
two-seat light plane flew into the 109-story Citic Tower in June, killing the 
pilot and injuring 13 others. The incident took place just several kilometers 
away from the headquarters of the ruling Communist Party and residence of top 
leaders.

LNG rethink: The loss of a fifth of liquefied natural gas supply due to the 
US-Iran war isblowing out costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/a-7-billion-gas-bill-sees-developing-asian-nations-sour-on-lng?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8879>
 for Asia’s developing markets, and forcing them to reconsider the fuel’s 
long-term future in the region.

Qatari shipments of LNG through the Strait of Hormuz have all but dried up 
since the conflict started at the end of February. That’s deprived Asian buyers 
of contracted supply of the power-station and industrial fuel, pushing them 
into the spot market where prices are surging.
