China caught investors by surprise 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/china-s-cabinet-stresses-market-support-in-midday-release?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11936>
 midway through Friday’s session. The State Council reaffirmed its support for 
the market, pledging to boost the role of the so-called national team and 
improve policies guiding long-term funds into stocks, according to the Xinhua 
News Agency. Authorities will also crack down on financial fraud while 
encouraging listed companies to step up dividend payouts and share buybacks.

The unusual timing and authoritative heft of the statement had an immediate 
impact on markets. The benchmark CSI 300 Index jumped more than 4% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-26/asian-stock-futures-signal-gains-oil-climbs-again-markets-wrap?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11936>
, the most since October 2024. Tech stocks were among the biggest gainers, with 
the chip-heavy Star 50 Index surging 15%. The optimism spread to Hong Kong, 
where the Hang Seng Tech Index advanced more than 6%. Coppersurged the most 
since January 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/copper-rebounds-from-11-month-low-on-return-of-risk-appetite?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11936>
 as risk appetite returned.

The move reflects the growing involvement of governments in markets after 
weeks of volatility fueled by concerns the AI boom is waning. South Korean 
officials are set to meet the heads of the major financial companies and 
industry associations on Monday toassess recent market conditions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/south-korea-reportedly-mulls-expanding-stock-stabilization-fund?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11936>
. The country’s stock market stabilization fund is likely to come up in the 
talks, the main financial regulator says. The government is considering 
expanding the $8 billion fund, a local newspaper reported earlier.

HSBC Reorganizes India Support Roles, Impacting Hundreds of Jobs

The bank is consolidating some support functions in India as it steps up 
efforts to rein in costs and accelerate the adoption of AI. HSBC is said to 
have recently told employees in India that it was centralizing finance 
functions across cities including Chennai, Gurugram and Bangalore and will no 
longer be housing those roles in other hubs, including Hyderabad and Pune.

Bankers Pay Up for Hong Kong Apartments as Rents Hit New Highs

Hong Kong’s booming rental real estate market is spurring gains in previously 
underappreciated neighborhoods, as bankers pay up for apartments in one of the 
world’s most expensive places to live. Ho Man Tin is luring finance 
professionals to the upscale neighborhood that’s near top schools and is close 
by to an area soon to house new office space for JPMorgan Chase & Co. Rents in 
the area are up more than 15%, almost double the pace of Hong Kong’s wider 
property market.

IMC Trading’s Internship Ad Misfire Shows Rush for Quant Jobs

An internship at a global trading firm has become such a hot ticket that 
people apply with almost no information. When IMC Trading posted a LinkedIn ad 
for a machine learning internship in Sydney, the firm accidentally pasted a 
placeholder template rather than the real thing. Instead of outlining what the 
interns would actually do, the ad instead had instructions for removing spaces, 
making sections bold and using bullet points. That was enough information to 
attract a wave of applicants, who quickly applied before IMC posted the correct 
link. The response triggered a lament about the crowded job market online — and 
a mea culpa from IMC.

Singapore’s Endless Hunt for Space Faces Rare Public Resistance

The city-state made itself 28% bigger since independence. In recent months, 
the government announced ambitious plans for more reclamation along Singapore’s 
southern coast for industrial use, as well as cutting down more forest for 
housing. A new generation is questioning the cost. The opposition comes as 
Singapore enters a new demographic phase that is leaving some citizens more 
skeptical about re-engineering nature in the name of growth.
