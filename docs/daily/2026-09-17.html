Daily Brief

Thursday, September 17, 2026

US

  • The FOMC unanimously raised the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to a target range of 3.75%–4.00%, with the dot plot signaling at least one additional hike in 2026 — confirming this as the start of a hiking cycle rather than a one-off move.
  • Fed Chair Warsh framed the decision around economic strength, citing improved hiring, business investment, and credit flows, while noting that "too many categories" of inflation continue to run above 3% annualized on a six-month and twelve-month basis.
  • The S&P 500 fell 0.45% on the day of the decision but futures recovered by 0.8% overnight; the 10-year Treasury yield peaked near 5.02% before retreating to just below 5.00%.
  • The US Senate failed to advance the Clarity Act, delivering a setback to crypto legislation and sending Bitcoin down more than 4%.

Federal Reserve

The FOMC's unanimous rate decision — the first hike in more than three years — appears to have materially strengthened Warsh's institutional credibility. The unanimity was notable given that several members were previously skeptical a hike was warranted. Rather than framing the move as a reluctant concession to inflation, Warsh positioned it as a response to underlying economic resilience, citing robust private-sector hiring and business capital investment. His language was deliberately calibrated to signal continuity: the phrase "removed a dose of accommodation" implies further doses remain in the prescription. The dot plot reinforced that reading, with the committee's median end-2027 rate projection rising 50 basis points since June. Markets priced three additional hikes to a peak below 5%.

Markets and Politics

President Trump's public response — demanding rates fall to 1% — was notable for stopping short of directly criticizing Warsh, instead attributing the decision to a "hostile" board. Bond and equity markets largely shrugged off the political noise, interpreting the unanimity as evidence that Warsh commands the committee. The 10-year yield's orderly retreat below 5.00% after briefly breaching that level suggests no disorderly repricing. The Senate's failure to advance the Clarity Act removed a near-term catalyst for crypto, with Bitcoin ETF inflows of $3.5 billion in August and $600 million in September unable to prevent a further decline. ---

Asia

  • The Bank of Japan is widely expected to raise rates on Friday, but the Fed's hawkish hike sent the yen back toward ¥156 per dollar — raising the bar for BOJ Governor Ueda to avoid renewed yen weakness toward ¥160.
  • Manus, the Chinese AI startup whose $2 billion acquisition by Meta was unwound by Beijing, is now raising $500 million at a $4 billion valuation — twice the original deal price.
  • India's National Stock Exchange opened a $2.4 billion IPO at approximately 43 times earnings, with a potential regulatory risk from its 60% dependence on options trading revenue.
  • Huawei accelerated the launch of its next-generation Ascend 960DT chip to early 2027, intensifying China's drive to reduce reliance on Nvidia.

Monetary Policy and Currency

The Fed's hawkish pivot has complicated the BOJ's calculus significantly. A 25-basis-point BOJ hike on Friday is almost entirely priced in, meaning the market reaction will depend almost entirely on forward guidance. If Governor Ueda fails to signal further tightening, the US-Japan rate differential could reassert itself, pushing the yen toward levels that previously triggered intervention. The episode illustrates a broader tension: the BOJ is hiking into a strengthening dollar environment, which mechanically undermines its efforts to stabilize the currency through domestic rate normalization alone.

Technology and Capital Markets

China's technology sector produced two consequential developments. Beijing's forced unwinding of Meta's Manus acquisition, which appeared damaging at the time, has yielded a markedly superior outcome for Manus's founders — a signal of how state intervention in M&A can occasionally produce counterintuitive results for the target company. Separately, Huawei's decision to accelerate its Ascend 960DT chip timeline reflects the urgency of China's semiconductor self-sufficiency push. The strategy of connecting large numbers of domestically produced chips to approximate Nvidia's performance represents a pragmatic workaround to export controls, though the performance gap remains material. ---

Europe

  • The Bank of England held rates at 3.75%, as expected, but warned that a hike may be required if price pressures intensify — a more hawkish tilt than its previous guidance.
  • Sweden's center-left opposition bloc led by former Prime Minister Magdalena Andersson is projected to win the general election by a margin of approximately 176 to 173 seats, effectively closing off near-term prospects for far-right participation in government.
  • Europe's potato harvest is forecast to decline by approximately 3.5 million tons relative to normal due to summer heat waves, with yield losses of 21% in Belgium, 13% in France, and 10% in Germany.
  • Marine Le Pen kept open the possibility of a no-confidence vote over France's 2027 budget, adding to fiscal uncertainty in Paris.

Bank of England

The BOE's decision to hold at 3.75% was in line with consensus, but the accompanying language was notably more cautious than prior communications. The committee scrapped plans to sell long-dated gilts — a material shift suggesting concern about absorbing additional duration risk into a market already digesting the Fed's hawkish turn and elevated global yields. The majority of MPC members have argued that the current rate level is sufficient to contain inflation, but that position is becoming harder to defend as energy-driven price pressures persist and the Fed signals further tightening. An ECB official separately indicated the October meeting is "live," suggesting European central banks are shifting incrementally in a more restrictive direction.

Politics and Food Inflation

Sweden's election result, while subject to final confirmation, appears to close a path toward far-right governmental influence that had concerned European markets. The margin is thin enough that political stability should not be assumed. On the inflation front, Europe's record summer heat has produced a material agricultural shock: the potato harvest shortfall of 3.5 million tons will filter into consumer food prices over coming months. Bond managers are already flagging food as a potential successor inflation shock to energy, with a super El Niño, tight fertilizer supplies, and shipping disruptions compounding the summer's agricultural damage. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's central bank cut its benchmark rate by 25 basis points as inflation slows and economic momentum fades, with the move coming weeks before a tight presidential election.
  • Argentina's Salta governor announced the reintroduction of a local provincial currency, directly challenging President Milei's free-market policy framework.
  • Peru's central bank president Julio Velarde was confirmed for another five-year term by President Keiko Fujimori, providing continuity at an institution that has served as a rare anchor of stability through a decade of political turbulence.

Brazil Monetary Policy

The Banco Central do Brasil's 25-basis-point rate cut reflects a modestly improved inflation backdrop, but the timing — immediately ahead of a closely contested presidential election — will invite questions about the degree to which the decision was shaped by the political calendar, regardless of the merits. A decelerating economy provides legitimate justification for easing, and the cut remains modest relative to the still-elevated level of the Selic rate. The more significant risk is whether the easing cycle becomes difficult to sustain if the Fed's hawkish pivot triggers capital outflows from emerging markets more broadly; the JPMorgan EM FX index fell to its lowest since March following the FOMC decision.

Argentina

The reintroduction of a local currency by one of Argentina's most prominent opposition governors represents a direct repudiation of Milei's economic liberalization program at the subnational level. The move is particularly significant in the current context: Milei has staked his credibility on convincing international investors that Argentina's institutional commitment to free markets is durable. Provincial experimentation with alternative monetary arrangements — however limited in scale — complicates that narrative and may add to sovereign risk perceptions at the margin. ---

Global

  • Saudi Arabia is working to restore approximately half the capacity of its East-West oil pipeline within days following drone attacks, with Brent crude falling 1.5% to $104.25 on signs supply disruptions may ease.
  • Container shipping through the Suez Canal has risen to approximately 26% of Europe-Asia capacity from 17% in early August, as Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd added four service lines through the Red Sea.
  • The Fed's rate hike drove the JPMorgan EM FX index to its lowest level since March, with the broad dollar index posting its best day since Warsh's first FOMC meeting in May.
  • Hedge funds are actively positioning around a super El Niño, with food commodity markets potentially facing a successive inflation shock following the energy-driven disruption of 2025–26.

Oil and the Strait of Hormuz

The partial restoration of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline is a meaningful near-term development for energy markets. The pipeline, which had been used to route oil exports around the severely disrupted Strait of Hormuz following the Iran war, was itself struck by drone attacks last week. Its partial resumption reduces the immediate supply risk premium, explaining the decline in Brent to $104. However, the broader structural disruption to Middle Eastern energy infrastructure remains unresolved. Houthis have captured additional territory in Yemen and are tightening control over Bab el-Mandeb, a second critical chokepoint for seaborne energy trade. The return of container ships to the Red Sea — welcomed by carriers but described by the Global Shippers Forum as a "reckless gamble" — reflects the difficult risk-reward calculation facing the shipping industry in the absence of guaranteed security assurances.

Monetary Policy Divergence and EM Stress

The Fed's hawkish pivot has meaningfully tightened global financial conditions. The dollar's broad rally following the FOMC decision directly pressures economies with large US dollar-denominated debt overhangs. The BOJ faces a particularly acute dilemma: hiking into a strengthening dollar risks appearing insufficient, while inaction allows yen weakness to persist. More broadly, the combination of still-elevated energy prices, an emerging food inflation shock from El Niño and the European summer drought, and a Fed committing to further tightening creates a challenging macro backdrop for emerging market assets heading into year-end.
