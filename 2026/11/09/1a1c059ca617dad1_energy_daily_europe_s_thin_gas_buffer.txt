Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
energy reporter Anna Shiryaevskaya explores the outlook for gas inventories in 
Europe at the end of winter.

The latest forecasts suggest that Europe’s gas-storage problems — and the 
economic challenges they bring — look set to continue long into 2027.

The continent started the cold season with inventories less than 75% full, 
well below their historical average because of the disruption to liquefied 
natural gas supplies from the Middle East caused by the US-Iran war.

If Hormuz remains effectively closed to LNG shipments until the end of March, 
which is the base case for BloombergNEF, European inventories are expected to 
fall to just 17% full, assuming weather equivalent to the 10-year normal.

If winter is colder than average, that figure could go as low as 10%, 
according to theInternational Energy Agency 
<https://www.iea.org/files/42bb838f-d20d-4815-8554-32146033967d/GlobalGasSecurityReview2026.pdf>
.

Either way, it would be the lowest level in data going back more than a 
decade, leaving Europe needing to buy even more fuel over the summer to refill 
its tanks, potentially at higher prices if the Middle East conflict lingers.

The region could still be suffering from tight markets in 2028, the IEA said.

Such a situation would be another blow for Europe’s economic competitiveness, 
which has suffered greatly from high energy prices since Russia’s full-scale 
invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The continent has already curbed industrial gas usage by about a fifth since 
2022 by switching to other fuels, according to Wood Mackenzie Ltd. “Any further 
large demand reduction would therefore only come at the cost of a recession,” 
the consultant said in areport 
<https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/need-to-know-gas-in-winter-2026-27/?utm_source=chatgpt.com>
.

Households from the UK to Germany still rely heavily on the fuel for heating, 
weakening their purchasing power when costs rise. If European storage ends the 
winter only 17% full it would create “large upside risk to prices,” Wood 
Mackenzie said.

For all the fears about Europe’s gas supply this winter, the bigger headache 
may arrive with spring.

—Anna Shiryaevskaya, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

The cost of balancing the British power grid has tripled since 2015, 
equivalent to a compound annual growth rate of 12% in real terms, according to 
BloombergNEF. Grid balancing costs rose to 4.3% of an average domestic 
consumer’s annual electricity bill in 2025/26, according to the National Energy 
System Operator. The recent increase reflects structural grid congestion rather 
than the gas-price spikes that drove the surge in 2021 and 2022.
