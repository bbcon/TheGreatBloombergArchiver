Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
energy reporter Will Wade looks at Big Tech’s rush to squeeze more power from 
existing nuclear plants — and the limits of how much more they can provide.

Interest in the US nuclear industry is at its highest in decades, driven not 
only by the AI data center boom but by broader growth in industrial power 
demand. But the country’s next new conventional reactor is still years away.

In the meantime, Big Tech companies have latched on to a faster and cheaper 
way to secure additional nuclear energy to keep data centers humming: upgrading 
existing power plants.

The industry calls these projects “uprates,” which largely entails installing 
new components 
<https://www.nrc.gov/licensing-applications/operating-reactor-licensing/power-uprates>
. Operators can swap in more efficient turbines or generators, fine-tune 
measurement systems, replace transformers, install better pumps — there are 
numerous options for eking out more megawatts.

Constellation Energy Corp. last week added to the list of US uprate projects.

Spurred on by a 20-year purchase agreement with Alphabet Inc.’s Google, 
Constellation said it’s planning to spend $4.3 billion to juice 11 reactors to 
add a total of 890 megawatts of new nuclear capacity — almost equivalent to 
what one average-size conventional reactor can deliver. The first power is 
expected to arrive as soon as 2028.
A cooling tower at the Constellation Nine Mile Point Clean Energy Center in 
Scriba, New York.Photographer: Lauren Petracca/Bloomberg
That deal came just a week after Constellation agreed to uprate a plant in 
Maryland and supply Amazon.com Inc. with 190 megawatts. There are similar deals 
to supply Walmart Inc. and Meta Platforms Inc.

If there’s a catch, it’s that there’s a finite amount of additional capacity 
to be squeezed out of the nation’s existing reactor fleet.

The remaining pickings are slim. Across the biggest US power grid, operated by 
PJM Interconnection LLC, there’s just one potential uprate project 
<bbg://news/stories/TMSECBRKV2UV> whose output isn’t yet spoken for, according 
to Bloomberg Intelligence: a 112-megawatt boost planned for the Salem plant in 
New Jersey, majority-owned by Public Service Enterprise Group Inc.

Constellation has already hit its limit on uprates. “Google basically bought 
everything that was left,” spokesman Paul Adams said last week.

Once the current fleet has been maximized, hyperscalers will have to confront 
the fact that the timeline for new nuclear capacity in the US may be several 
years at best — an uncomfortable fact for an industry looking at every option 
to meet AI demand.

—Will Wade, Bloomberg News

5–11 as the impact of summer maintenance faded and the Arctic LNG 2 project 
continued to ship cargoes, according toBloombergNEF 
<bbg://news/stories/TMU4U6KGIFQ0>. Sakhalin underwent annual maintenance from

production recovering post maintenance, Russia’s weekly exports rose to their 
highest level since January.
