First mover advantages are not always sacrosanct — just ask Netscape 
<https://www.ecommerce-digest.com/netscape-case-study.html>. Nearly four years 
ago, OpenAI’s ChatGPT generative artificial intelligence played a similar role 
to the pioneering web browser. It set in motion a once-in-a-generation frenzy 
about a technology whose capabilities have already evolved almost beyond 
recognition.

But almost 1,450 days later, Anthropic PBC — and not OpenAI — has become the 
first frontier AI lab to go from unicorn to publicly traded company, with a 
valuation of about $1.5 trillion. Its IPO priced at$83 per share 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/anthropic-is-said-to-price-ipo-at-83-per-share?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
, after promoters said the offering was almost seven times oversubscribed.

The $94.5 billion Anthropic raised in Wednesday’s pricing and the concurrent 
private placement eclipses what SpaceX raised five months ago, making it the 
biggest IPO by some margin. It concludes what was already the busiest year for 
mega IPOs since 2021, when companies like Coinbase, Roblox and Robin Hood 
Markets came to market:

It’s hard to see this blockbuster performance carrying into next year, even if 
the AI trade survives the Nvidia scandal (which at present looks likely). As 
Bloomberg Opinion colleaguePaul Davies argues 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-11-18/goldman-sachs-and-morgan-stanley-are-high-on-the-ai-trade?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
, many factors driving this outperformance will likely fade. With a December 
Federal Reserve hike all but certain, the prospects are that interest rates 
will be higher for longer. Tighter financial conditions won’t do the AI 
buildout any favors.

So even though a few big names are still to come, this year’s record for 
issuance may stand for quite some time:

Whether OpenAI’s debut, expected next year, can come close remains to be seen. 
But higher rates could make future capital raises more costly, and make staying 
private more expensive. That won’t necessarily compel Sam Altman’s company to 
go public before it believes market conditions are right. Regardless, 
Anthropic’s epochal moment will serve as a useful benchmark for its rival.

For now, these offerings revive the debate over whether they draw capital away 
from existing equities, siphon liquidity from the broader market, and add to 
volatility and valuation pressure for mid- and small-cap stocks. Nathan 
Sonnenberg of Pitcairn argues:
The concern I have with Anthropic going public, and maybe OpenAI, is the size 
of these deals. The size of SpaceX, I believe, was larger than all of the IPOs 
that happened in the late 1990s — the whole IPO internet-growth bubble. The 
markets, while they are large, can only absorb so much capital. If we, the 
investment bankers, come to market with deals that are too large, that means 
other securities need to be sold.
Regardless of the enthusiasm around Anthropic’s listing, a plethora of 
examples, most recently SpaceX, show that a strong IPO doesn’t always translate 
into lasting share price gains. Tech stocks are under enduring pressure, 
exacerbated by Nvidia’s troubles — but this gives Anthropic a shot at becoming 
thenew poster child 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-15/anthropic-s-ipo-makes-dario-amodei-the-new-face-of-the-ai-boom?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
 for artificial intelligence. How it performs from here could go a long way 
toward shaping investors’ faith in the technology’s ability to deliver returns.

—Richard Abbey

Jumbo Minutes

The Fed really is going to raise rates in December. The publication of the 
minutes for last month’s Federal Open Market Committee 
<https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcminutes20261028.pdf> 
caused little stir, as investors were already resigned to this. But the 
direction is clear.

The minutes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/fed-minutes-show-almost-all-officials-expect-more-rate-hikes?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
 don’t reveal names or exact numbers, but the careful choice of words makes 
clear that the committee is bent on tightening. Highlights include:
Most participants expected inflation to remain elevated.

The vast majority saw upside risks to this outlook.Many participants viewed 
the AI buildout as a material source of inflation risk.A majority noted the 
possibility that higher costs could be passed through more broadly and 
persistently.

A number of participants remarked that greater scrutiny of the sector’s 
returns and financing could lead to a slowing in AI-related spending.Almost all 
participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks, further action to 
raise the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate.

A couple of participants noted that an increase of more than 25 basis points 
could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were 
unfavorable.
We know that Governor Chris Waller was one of the two. He went public with his 
call to consider a “jumbo” hikein a CNBC interview on Wednesday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/fed-s-waller-says-fed-should-consider-half-point-rate-hike?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
. Bloomberg’s World Interest Rate Probability function still only puts an 8.6% 
chance on a jumbo, and rate expectations barely shifted on the day. Other 
rate-sensitive investments very much did. The bloodbath for precious metals 
continues, with Bloomberg’s index now down 37.25% from its January peak:

Precious metals, which pay no income, respond negatively to higher rates. 
Their fall came with a steady rise in yields after the outbreak of war in Iran. 
But the last few days’ price action isn’t about rate expectations. Implicit 
future fed funds rates did nothing on Wednesday, while the dollar staged a 
major surge:

What, then, is driving the dollar up and metals down? A disquieting 
possibility is that investors fear a dislocation (such as a credit event) in 
the AI trade, despite Anthropic’s successful IPO. To quote Robin Brooks of the 
Brookings Institution:
It’s clear that the sharp rise in the dollar has totally decoupled from the 
rate differential, so dollar strength is NOT about the hawkish Fed. That can be 
a sign that something is else is going on, i.e. something is breaking.
Whatever is happening, we now know that it will take something serious to 
shake the Fed out of continuing to tighten.

BIndonesia

Bank Indonesia has administered a shock. In its penultimate meeting of the 
year Wednesday, it unexpectedly raised its benchmark lending rate as the rupiah 
weakened and it sought to bolster its credibility as a central bank. The 
25-basis-point rise in the seven-day reverse repurchase rate brings it to 6%, 
the highest since January last year.

The bank’s aggressiveness came after the rupiah plunged to record lows 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/idr-usd-indonesian-rupiah-prabowo-fed-bets-boost-dollar?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
 earlier this week, but analysts had still anticipated a fifth consecutive 
pause. That consensus suggested that BI would use other monetary levers to 
defend the currency, and the great concern now is whether the bank can continue 
to tighten policy without plunging Southeast Asia’s largest country into a 
recession:

However, the decision shows that the bank is more immediately concerned by the 
rupiah and inflation. Governor Destry Damayanti and the board are targeting 
inflation within the 1.5% to 3.5% range:
BI will continue to consistently align monetary policy with rupiah stability 
to keep inflation within the target range... Macroprudential policy will remain 
accommodative to support credit financing for the real sector.
Ultimately, this should complement BI’s currency intervention. Whether it 
works will depend on the factors driving the weakness. Those include a run 
against all emerging currencies as rising yields in the US drive fresh strength 
for the dollar. JPMorgan’s gauge of emerging currencies closed Wednesday below 
its level on Election Day in 2024, and is at its lowest since last year’s 
Liberation Day tariffs selloff:

Indonesia has its own idiosyncratic problems. With investors souring on the 
government, the unexpected hike could serve a double purpose by stabilizing the 
currency as well as demonstrating policy credibility. Capital Economics’ Jason 
Tuvey, who expects two additional 25-basis-point hikes, argues that winning 
over investors will be tough:
Credibility is much easier to lose than it is to rebuild. Investors will be 
watching closely for any comments from government officials regarding today’s 
decision given their push for faster economic growth. The acid test for Bank 
Indonesia will be how it responds if the rupiah were to come under fresh 
downward pressure.
What’s driving that pressure? Domestically, President Prabowo Subianto’s 
populist policies have driven a wedge between himself and investors. Once an 
emerging-market standard-bearer, the stock market has been savagely 
disappointing since Prabowo took over two years ago:

A string of high-profile departures, including the vice president 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jokowi-son-gibran-rakabuming-raka-resigns-indonesia-vice-president-prabowo?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
 and, before that,the finance minister 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/prabowo-removes-finance-minister-purbaya-in-cabinet-reshuffle?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
, hasn’t eased concerns about the accelerating concentration of presidential 
power. Gavekal Research’s Tom Miller and Udith Sikand argue that under Prabowo, 
Indonesia has lost the reputation it won under his predecessor, Joko Widodo, 
for prudent macroeconomic management, fiscal discipline and predictable 
treatment of foreign capital. Street riots over economic hardship are growing 
commonplace, and Prabowo’s approval ratings have plunged to 45% from 81% in 
under a year.

Uncertainty is compounded by MSCI’s proposal to downgrade the archipelago’s 
equity market to standalone status 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/indonesia-s-msci-downgrade-threat-what-it-means-for-investors-podcast?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
 and remove it from the emerging- and frontier-market indexes. That could 
potentially trigger billions of dollars in outflows from benchmark-tracking 
funds, who would be forced to sell.

With MSCI’s decision not due until next June, Indonesia has time to turn the 
tide. As Miller and Sikand note, were it not for a recent flare-up in thefeud 
between Prabowo and Jokowi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/prabowo-wiretap-arrest-spills-top-indonesia-rivalry-into-public?cmpid=BBD111926_AUT&utm_campaign=authers&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11545>
, investors might have been tempted to catch the falling Indonesian knife:
With the rupiah now cheap by post-Asian-crisis standards, and 10-year local 
currency bonds yielding 7.7%, Indonesia offers potential to diversify away from 
souring AI trades in other emerging markets. But political uncertainty risks 
delaying investment and raising financing costs.
Ultimately, the central bank must insulate itself from perceived political 
interference — and its hike is a gesture of independence. For now, Capital 
Economics’ Gareth Leather argues that weakening public support could encourage 
Prabowo to step up his efforts to engineer faster economic growth.

Looser fiscal policy that pushes the budget deficit above the 3% of GDP legal 
ceiling could further undermine Indonesia’s policy guardrails and unnerve 
investors. Their confidence has already been seriously weakened.
