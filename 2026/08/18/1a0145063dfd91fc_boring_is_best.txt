A sense of calm 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/prabowo-pledges-lower-budget-deficit-strong-growth-in-2027?cmpid=BBD081826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7834>
 seems to be creeping back into Indonesia after a turbulent period of President 
Prabowo Subianto swiftly and aggressively remaking Southeast Asia’s largest 
economy.

In the past year, the ex-general has shifted billions of dollars into his pet 
projects, seized plantations, cracked down on tycoons and seen theabrupt exit 
of veteran policymakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/bank-indonesia-chief-s-shock-exit-fuels-prabowo-policy-worries?cmpid=BBD081826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7834>
 including his finance minister and central bank governor.

In that time, Indonesia went from being one of the world’s most promising 
emerging markets to one of its worst performers.

On Friday, however, Prabowo cut a pragmatic figure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/prabowo-s-pragmatic-speech-comforts-weary-indonesia-investors?cmpid=BBD081826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7834>
 when he made his annual address to parliament, extolling the values of fiscal 
prudence and proposing a modest budget.
Prabowo delivers his speech in Jakarta on Friday.Photographer: Bay 
Ismoyo/AFP/Getty Images
He also toned down a plan to centralize the nation’s top commodity exports 
under the government, after having nominated a well-respected technocrat to 
head the central bank.

Of course, one speech does not a new man make 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/prabowo-to-face-indonesia-critics-with-key-speeches-as-skepticism-grows?cmpid=BBD081826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7834>
. But perhaps the signals from investors have become too loud for the Prabowo 
administration to ignore.

Equally, a market rout is unlikely to force Prabowo to abandon his central 
project 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-13/handing-out-15-billion-of-free-meals-strains-indonesia-s-budget?cmpid=BBD081826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7834>
 of greater state control over Indonesia’s wealth and natural resources.

That’s while he seeks to raise Indonesia’s profile on the global diplomatic 
stage, using its longstanding “free and active” foreign policy to deepen ties 
with a host of nations. He was one of the first leaders to secure a tariff 
agreement with US President Donald Trump, and made Indonesia the first 
Southeast Asian nation to join BRICS.

But for markets, his bland economic speech was of more interest, with 
Indonesian stocks entering a bull market and government bond yields falling.

His policies may be starting to affect the numbers the president does care 
about: One recent survey put his approval rating at about 51% — down from more 
than 80% last year — with half of respondents viewing the economy negatively.

Boring is perhaps the best economic policy for Prabowo right now. — Claire Jiao
