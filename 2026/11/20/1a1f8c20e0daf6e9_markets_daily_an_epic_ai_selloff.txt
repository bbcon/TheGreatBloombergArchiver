Nvidia’s upbeat outlook and Anthropic’s offering weren’t enough to dispel 
concerns over stretched valuations and the sustainability of AI spending. Anna 
Wong of Bloomberg Economics points out that the IPO filing showed weakening 
pricing power for frontier intelligence.

“The Anthropic debut tells you exactly where we are,” said Maxence Visseau at 
Arkevium Capital. “For two years, good AI news pushed everything higher. Now 
even great news gets sold. The market isn’t paying for the story anymore. It 
wants proof of returns.” —Phil Serafino

Metal mayhem

The AI selloff is wreaking havoc in metals markets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/copper-rout-deepens-as-ai-selloff-wreaks-havoc-in-metals-markets?cmpid=112026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11660>
 after a week of turmoil fueled by deepening worries about global demand.

Copper prices on the London Metal Exchange tumbled as much as 4% to put the 
metal on track for one of its worst weeks in the past decade. The sharp selloff 
has numerous drivers, the most pressing of which is growing doubt about the 
sustainability of AI spending and copper’s role in the sector.

The red metal had soared to a record high near $15,000 a ton in September as 
bullish investors bet on a demand boost from data centers and other AI 
infrastructure. Now, prices are threatening to tip below $12,000.

Other metals were also hit hard this week, with tin tumbling more than 12% and 
zinc shedding about 5%. Other factors at play include weak Chinese demand, the 
dimming prospect of US copper import tariffs, and a stronger dollar, which 
makes dollar-denominated commodities more expensive in other currencies.
Precious metals are slumping too 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/gold-set-for-worst-week-since-march-on-hawkish-fed-ai-selloff?cmpid=112026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11660>
.

“We have been saying for as long as AI has been driving the price that this 
might be a bit over-egged,” said Albert Mackenzie at Benchmark Mineral 
Intelligence Ltd. “It’s not to say that AI is not important, but there is far 
more going on.” —Martin Ritchie
