**Emerging Market Resilience**
The broader EM narrative is directly relevant to Latin America. Despite 10-year Treasury yields exceeding 4.75% — levels that historically have triggered capital outflows from emerging markets — the EMBI+ spread has held stable and EM local-currency bonds have gained approximately 3.5% year-to-date, while developed-market peers have declined 1.7%. This decoupling reflects a structural shift: improved macroeconomic frameworks in emerging economies, combined with deteriorating fiscal credibility in key developed markets, have compressed the relative risk differential. Latin American sovereigns with credible policy frameworks appear to be beneficiaries of this dynamic.

**Uruguay as Bellwether**
Uruguay offers a specific illustration: investor preference is rotating from dollar-denominated holdings toward peso money market funds and local government bonds — a reversal of the traditional safe-haven dollar bias. Central bank governor Tolosa's framing — that the advanced world is no longer unambiguously the low-risk anchor — reflects a view gaining traction among EM policymakers and investors alike. Whether this resilience persists if US yields rise further or the Iran conflict escalates materially remains an open question.
