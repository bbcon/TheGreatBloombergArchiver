A reprieve for the Amazon

By Fabiano Maisonnave 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXmFWA_HFBY/fabiano-maisonnave?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASzdyi2b6bE/laura-millan?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>
Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Brazil’s president, speaks during a press

The surprise victory 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/lula-wins-a-record-fourth-term-in-brazil-in-a-surprise-comeback?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>
 of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in Brazil’s presidential election means 
the country won’t make a sharp pivot in its approach to climate change, as 
promised by Lula’s opponent Flávio Bolsonaro, who called for more development 
in the Amazon and looser environmental rules.

“Brazilians have made their choice at the ballot box, and the Amazon 
rainforest has another chance,” said Natalie Unterstell, president of the 
Talanoa Institute, a Brazilian climate think tank.

The electoral court declared Lula the winner in Sunday’s runoff, with 51.8% of 
the vote versus 48.2% for conservative Bolsonaro. That keeps Brazil on a 
climate-policy path centered on protecting forests, whose destruction is the 
country’s main source of carbon emissions. It also means Brazil will continue 
to play a prominent role in United Nations climate talks, the next round of 
which, COP31, begins on Nov. 9 in Turkey.

Lula will begin his new four-year term with the chance to fulfill his pledge 
to end forest loss. Four years ago, he vowed to achieve net-zero deforestation 
across all of Brazil’s biomes by 2030. The country has made significant 
progress toward the goal, with Amazon deforestation falling this year toits 
lowest level 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/brazil-sees-record-low-amazon-deforestation-rate-in-win-for-lula?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>
 since official monitoring began in 1988.

Shipping’s electric advance

By David Stringer 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARMqcSMt218/david-stringer?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>

When the world’s largest battery-powered passenger ferry 
<https://incat.com.au/history-made-as-worlds-largest-battery-electric-ship-powers-up/>
 hit the River Derwent in Hobart, Australia for testing, there was an 
unexpected hitch.

The 130-meter (427-foot) aluminum catamaran — designed to transport 2,100 
passengers and about 225 vehicles — moved easily through the waves in the 
January trial and comfortably hit its intended speed of about 25 knots.

“It was an exciting moment for our first electric ship, it was quite 
remarkable that the ship was so responsive,” said veteran shipbuilder Robert 
Clifford, the 83-year-old founder and chairman ofIncat Tasmania 
<https://incat.com.au/>, which built the ferry among other vessels for 
customers in more than 20 countries.
A hybrid-electric ferry under construction at the Incat Tasmania shipyard in 
Hobart, Tasmania, Australia, in May.Photographer: Matthew Newton/Bloomberg
Yet without the loud rumble of diesel engines, other noises became more 
conspicuous, Clifford said in an interview in May. “The customer complained, 
because actually you heard the air-conditioning fans,” he said. “The rest of 
the ship was so quiet.”

China Zorrilla made its first commercial journey 
<https://www.instagram.com/reel/De0AklVzhR3/> for ferry operator Buquebus last 
week on the roughly 75-minute route between Buenos Aires in Argentina and 
Colonia del Sacramento in Uruguay. The vessel — which carries more than 250 
metric tons of batteries, a system that’s roughly four times larger than any 
previous maritime installation — is regarded as a breakthrough for clean 
shipping.

While the ferry sector is only a fraction of a shipping industry dominated by 
container ships and tankers, advocates insist China Zorrilla will prove larger 
vessels can move away from polluting fuels. Greenhouse gas emissions from 
global shipping accounted for roughly 754 million metric tons of carbon dioxide 
equivalent in 2025, more than international aviation and only a little less 
than Canada, according toEuropean Commission data 
<https://edgar.jrc.ec.europa.eu/report_2026>.
Welding work for a new fully-electric ferry at the Incat shipyard in May.
Photographer: Matthew Newton/Bloomberg
“It’s quite a pronounced shift,” said Mike Corrigan 
<https://interferry.com/our-team/>, chief executive officer of Interferry 
<https://interferry.com/>, an industry group that represents more than 280 
companies across the ferry sector. “When I look at the new build orders and 
what’s coming down the pipeline for our operators, I’m not seeing very many 
vessels that are being built to operate on our traditional fuel sources.”

Going electric has become possible as battery capabilities rapidly improve and 
equipment costs decline. The energy density of typical electric-vehicle battery 
packs has jumped more than 60% since 2010, while prices have plummeted about 
93%, according to BloombergNEF data.

“This kind of cost-performance ratio improvement has really made the battery 
technology accessible,” said Shirley Meng, a distinguished professor and vice 
president (industry) at Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, and who 
jokes that her nickname is the “doctor for batteries.”

Just as electric trucks are starting to expand battery deployments on the road 
beyond passenger cars or motorcycles, ferries are the next step for vehicles at 
sea afterpowerboats 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-23/following-ev-formula-startups-electrify-recreational-boats?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>
, tugs and other small-scale vessels. The category is an ideal use case, 
because ferries move along predictable routes and stop at terminals to load and 
unload passengers and vehicles, providing regular opportunities to recharge.

Singapore’s green opportunity

By Ishika Mookerjee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJMYcOXODs/ishika-mookerjee?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>
 andSing Yee Ong 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVxUem_vTRM/sing-yee-ong?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>

At a shipyard in northern Singapore, a yellow platform towers over a maze of 
scaffolding and cranes. Resembling a 15-story building, the piece will be 
shipped halfway around the world to Europe to be installed into an offshore 
wind farm.

The 30,000-ton platform, known as a high-voltage direct current converter, is 
scheduled to leaveSeatrium Ltd. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/STM:SP?cmpid=BBD102626_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10515>
’s yard for the North Sea in 2028, where it will sit 60 kilometers offshore and 
help transmit electricity generated by wind turbines back to land. It’s the 
latest effort by Singapore to tap into Europe’s green push.
The Seatrium Batam YardPhotographer: Ore Huiying/Bloomberg
Singapore is repurposing expertise built over decades serving the largest 
offshore oil and gas companies for the energy transition. One of the biggest 
opportunities is exporting these enormous converters — known as HVDC platforms 
— to Europe, where the technology is widely used to transmit offshore wind 
power over long distances.

The specialized technology is one bright spot in an otherwise battered 
offshore wind industry. Investmentplummeted 
<https://www.bnef.com/insights/40217/view> 72% year-on-year globally to about 
$12 billion in the first half of 2026, according to BloombergNEF. Yet few yards 
have the expertise and capacity to build the platforms, threatening a supply 
bottleneck as the European Union pushes ahead with plans to expand capacity.
