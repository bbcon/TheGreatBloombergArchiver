Nvidia is already preparing for the possibility of life after Jensen Huang. 
The chipmaker’s board has beenworking with an outside firm 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/nvidia-working-with-outside-firm-to-identify-ceo-candidates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 to identify potential replacements for its founder and only-ever CEO, said a 
person familiar, and is weighing whether to appoint a temporary chief while it 
searches for a permanent successor. The extraordinary succession scramble 
follows reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including Huang’s admitted 
relationship with Alice Huang, a data-center executive alleged to have spied on 
China’s behalf, and a reported relationship with an Nvidia executive who worked 
for him.

The story is sprawling well beyond Nvidia’s boardroom. Alice Huang is now in 
Beijing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/alleged-chinese-spy-at-center-of-nvidia-firestorm-is-in-beijing?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 with the knowledge of Chinese security officials, people familiar said, after 
years at the center of a remarkable chip-trading empire: Her Megaspeed imported 
more than $4.6 billion of Nvidia products and became a major player in 
Southeast Asia just as US restrictions tightened access to advanced chips in 
China. And one of tech’s other larger-than-life founders has entered the drama: 
Elon Musk saysit would be an “honor” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/elon-musk-says-it-would-be-an-honor-to-hire-nvidia-jensen-huang?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 to hire Huang if he becomes available. The fallout is now bleeding into 
geopolitics, too.Beijing warned Washington 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/china-says-us-should-not-use-huang-scandal-to-justify-tech-curbs?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 not to use the spying allegations as a pretext for tighter tech curbs, just 
days before Donald Trump and Xi Jinping are due to meet.

Now markets are gaming out 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 what a post-Huang Nvidia could mean for the rest of the AI race. Nvidia sank 
7% in pre-market trading while AMD and Broadcom jumped, with investors betting 
that rivals could pry away business if the turmoil disrupts customer 
relationships.Alphabet, Asian custom-chip makers and even Chinese semiconductor 
companies could also benefit. That is what makes this more than a succession 
drama: Huang has been so central to Nvidia’s extraordinary rise that 
uncertainty over one man is forcing investors to reconsider the competitive map 
of the entire AI industry.

HSBC Explores Bolstering Singapore to Balance China Reliance

HSBC has been exploring ways to ease its reliance on China including Hong Kong 
and give more independence to its Singapore operations, as the bank seeks to 
diversify risk across its Asia business, according to people familiar with the 
matter.
Read more 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/hsbc-explores-bolstering-singapore-to-balance-china-reliance?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
Adani is putting serious money behind its global ambitions. The Indian 
conglomerateplans to fund as much as half 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/adani-eyes-mix-of-equity-debt-for-2-9-billion-evos-takeover?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 of its $2.9 billion takeover of Dutch liquid-storage operator Evos with 
equity, and the rest with syndicated debt, while also refinancing about €1.3 
billion of Evos borrowings, said people familiar. MUFG is underwriting the 
deal, with lenders including DBS, Barclays, First Abu Dhabi Bank and SBI 
circling the financing. Beyond another big overseas bet by Gautam Adani, the 
deal is a sign of a broader shift: Indian companies are hunting for assets 
abroad just as relaxed takeover-financing rules give the country’s banks a 
bigger role in funding their ambitions.

The prospect of a Trump-Xi-Putin summit is gathering momentum. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/us-china-call-adds-momentum-to-potential-summit-with-russia?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and China’s Wang Yi discussed the importance 
of leader-level talks, including a potential three-way meeting with Russia, 
ahead of Trump’s trip to China for next week’s APEC summit. Both Trump and 
Putin have signaled interest in sitting down with Xi. No date or venue has been 
announced, and Beijing’s account of the call didn’t mention a trilateral 
meeting. But if it happens, the gathering would put the leaders of the world’s 
three biggest nuclear powers at one table, with everything from 
denuclearization to the war in Iran potentially on the agenda, and would give 
Xi a chance to cast China as a broker between Washington and Moscow.

Japan is reminding currency traders that 160 yen to the dollar comes with 
consequences. As the yen slid back toward that level after stronger US 
inflation data and hawkish Fed signals,currency chief Atsushi Mimura warned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/japan-s-fx-chief-warns-against-forgetting-summer-intervention?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11327>
 that recent moves have been “too fast” and pointedly said it was “too early to 
forget” this summer (when Tokyo spent a record $96.5 billion over three days 
defending the currency), including its first coordinated yen intervention with 
the US since 1998. With the yen again near 160 and the US-Japan rate gap 
threatening to widen, intervention might be back on the table.

Australia’s data-center boom is running into a very human obstacle: The 
neighbors. Quantum DChas paused plans <> for a massive 1,200-megawatt 
hyperscale facility near Adelaide after residents raised concerns about its 
water and energy demands, location and impact on farmland. It’s at least the 
third Australian project to be stalled or scrapped by community opposition in 
recent weeks, after backlash delayed a Nextdc development in Melbourne and 
prompted Stockland to abandon one in Sydney in another sign that the 
infrastructure powering the AI boom is increasingly colliding with questions 
over who bears its environmental costs.
