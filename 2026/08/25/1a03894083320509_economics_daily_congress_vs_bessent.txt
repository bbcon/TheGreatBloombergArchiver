Fiscal Policy Fissures

With his Iran plan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/bessent-pledges-to-isolate-iran-from-global-economy?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
 — “Operation Economic Outcast” — announced Monday, next up for Bessent is the 
fiscal-consolidation initiative heflagged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/bessent-says-buybacks-could-be-more-than-4-billion-per-issue?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
 last week. Any truly meaningful push will requirecooperation from Congress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/bessent-s-murky-deficit-plan-faces-grim-prospects-in-congress?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
, budget watchers warned. And lately, lawmakers have been eyeing measures that 
wouldadd to the federal deficit.

Tightening the fiscal belt is hardly a popular idea in most political 
climates, and certainly not with the midterm congressional election little more 
than two months away. That was already on display earlier this summer, when 
House and Senate Republicansdropped plans 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-10/dhs-shutdown-trump-senate-republicans-agree-on-spending-bill-outline?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
 to make hundreds of billions of dollars in spending cuts amid pushback from 
swing-district GOP lawmakers.

The House even pushed ahead with a deficit-expanding measure calling for $95 
billion in extra spending this year with no offsetting cuts, largely to fund 
the Iran War. And there are bigger plans for an additional $350 billion in 
defense outlays.

Then there’s the fast-looming annual appropriations process, for the fiscal 
year that starts Oct. 1. Congress is on the verge of extending current agency 
budgets through mid-December. If the Democrats win either house of Congress in 
the midterms, they’re likely to press for at least some domestic agencies to 
get a year-over-year boost.

Should Republicans retain the House, GOP leaders have said they’ll propose 
legislation targeting what they call fraud in Medicaid health insurance. 
Bessent last week also indicated that fraud will be a major target of his 
fiscal plan. But past attempts to rein in malfeasance in government spending 
have failed to make a big dent inUS deficits 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
.

Meanwhile, Bessent’s former mentor, Stanley Druckenmiller, penned a stinging 
criticism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/druckenmiller-bessent-s-early-mentor-calls-bond-buys-a-mistake?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
 of the secretary’s effort to tame long bond yields with buybacks. “The 
long-term Treasury yield is the most important price in the world. It is also 
the only fiscal disciplinarian the US has left,” he wrote in a Wall Street 
Journal opinion piece, calling for deep entitlement reforms both parties have 
avoided. “Term out the debt honestly and pay the price the market sets.”

Sarah Bianchi at Evercore ISI also points out that the Trump administration 
“has shown a tendency to recycle fiscal savings into new priorities.”

When tariff revenues were flowing in to the Treasury last year, President 
Donald Trump even floated sharing them via $2,000 checks. And before that, he 
mused about distributing some of the would-be savings from the DOGE 
government-efficiency effort to Americans viadividend checks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-19/trump-floats-giving-taxpayers-cut-of-doge-savings-amid-criticism?cmpid=BBD082526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260825&utm_content=8127>
. Ultimately, that Elon Musk-led effortfailed to yield <> the savings expected. 
No checks went out.

“This is not an administration that has sprinted towards difficult policy 
choices when it comes to fiscal options, and this is a particularly politically 
unappealing time to talk about them,” said Maya MacGuineas, president of the 
bipartisan Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget. But signaling and 
symbolism aren’t going to prove sufficient to shrink the deficit, she said.

Need-to-Know Research

Any effort by Bessent to tap the government’s nearly $1 trillion Treasury 
General Account to buy longer-dated bonds would do nothing to address the 
fiscal forces contributing to elevated long-term rates, according to Bloomberg 
Economics’ Andrew Sacher.

While tapping the TGA would give Bessent bit more flexibility over the timing 
and scale of bond purchases, it won’t stem the country’s large deficits, $40 
trillion-and-rising debt load and heavy supply of Treasury bonds.

“We think buybacks can move yields at the margin — but won’t produce a 
sustained decline without a change in the underlying fiscal outlook,” Sacher 
wrote.

The buybacks also “creates and awkward tension” with Warsh’s policy approach 
for the US central bank of taking cues from the market.

“It becomes harder for the Fed to listen to long-term yields — or rely on them 
to restrain demand and inflation — if Treasury is actively trying to push those 
yields lower,” he wrote.
