Singapore has had a fraught relationship with Indonesia practically since 
independence in 1965.

The neighbors have been going through another round of ructions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/prabowo-to-meet-singapore-envoy-on-mission-to-ease-relations?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
 in recent weeks.

In the 1960s, Singapore’s federation with Malaysia was seen as a 
British-orchestrated, neo-colonial effort to stifle Indonesia. While the 
violence of those times is far behind, the sprawling resource-rich archipelago, 
with a population almost 50 times larger, still often looks suspiciously at the 
city state.

The latest tensions were sparked by blazes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/el-nino-fires-test-limits-of-southeast-asia-s-cloud-seeding-bet?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
 across Borneo, Sumatra and other Indonesian islands burning through forests 
and peatlands, the result of a particularly bad land-clearing season fueled by 
El Niño dryness.

After nearly two months of haze wafting through the region — closing schools, 
canceling flights and angering residents — Singapore startedtargeting locally 
registered firms 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-24/singapore-expands-haze-investigation-to-10-locally-based-firms?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
 that operate in Indonesia.
Singapore’s Marina Bay Sands Hotel shrouded in haze on Oct. 16.Photographer: 
Edwin Koo/Bloomberg
That appeared to touch a nerve in Jakarta, as President Prabowo Subianto and 
others began to rail against unnamed countries.

The smaller, and per-capita richer, neighbor is an easy target.

Among Prabowo’s biggest irritants is what he calls “leakages” of the nation’s 
national-resources wealth by tycoons and traders, with Singapore often seen as 
the financial conduit for that flight of money.

It’s also seen in Jakarta as a banking haven for an estimated tens of billions 
of dollars in Indonesian wealth the government would like to see brought back 
home.

That all spilled over last weekend, when Indonesia suddenly trimmed 
natural-gas exports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/singapore-shields-itself-from-energy-risks-as-indonesia-cuts-gas?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
 to Singapore. The volume was relatively small — and the explanation over 
domestic needs plausible — but it set off alarm bells.

Singapore’s foreign minister got on a plane to Jakarta to meet with Prabowo 
and cool things down.

It’s likely no coincidence that this unfolds while Prabowo’s popularity has 
started to sink 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/prabowo-approval-sinks-below-50-as-economic-gloom-grows?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
, with anger rising over corruption, economic anxiety and the haze.

But bashing Singapore can only vent so much 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/indonesian-students-protest-over-forest-fires-prabowo-spending?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
.— Ramsey Al-Rikabi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQqzH2_FRwI/ramsey-alrikabi?cmpid=BBD102926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261029&utm_content=10728>
