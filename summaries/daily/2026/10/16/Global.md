**Energy and Geopolitics**
The Iran conflict continues to function as the primary inflationary impulse in the global economy, elevating energy costs, disrupting LNG supply chains, and generating freight and insurance premia that are rendering approximately 10% of global seaborne iron ore supply unprofitable, according to Vale estimates. South Korea's decision to increase coal consumption this winter as a substitute for disrupted LNG supplies illustrates how the conflict is materially complicating energy-transition timelines in energy-import-dependent economies. The Houthis' strike on the Yemeni internationally recognized government's presidential palace represents a further escalation in a theater closely connected to Strait of Hormuz risk.

**Global Monetary Policy Divergence**
The IMF meetings in Bangkok crystallized a significant tension: the US — through its fiscal stance and Iran-driven inflation — has been the primary exporter of yield pressure to the rest of the world, yet US Treasury Secretary Bessent's absence from Bangkok allowed China to partially reframe the global imbalances debate. IMF Managing Director Georgieva explicitly questioned whether US growth-led fiscal consolidation can deliver adjustment fast enough, a rare public challenge to Washington's framework. Copper markets reflect the same policy uncertainty: the US import premium — driven by threatened tariffs — has generated a historic arbitrage trade, but its durability depends entirely on whether the threatened tariff is ultimately imposed.
