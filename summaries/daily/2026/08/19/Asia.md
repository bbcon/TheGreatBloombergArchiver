• Asian tech shares tumbled on rising long-term borrowing costs, with the Kospi down 5.8%, ChiNext down 6.3% and Japan's Topix down 3.1%; Samsung and SK Hynix both fell more than 7% in Seoul.
• SK Hynix announced a 40 trillion won ($29 billion) buyback and lifted its shareholder-return target above 50% of free cash flow to ease AI-spending durability concerns; its US shares rose as much as 7.1% in premarket trading.
• India displaced Indonesia as Asia's least-favored equity market in BofA's fund manager survey, with 32% of respondents net underweight on scant AI exposure and weak growth; Indonesia's underweight eased to 27% from 32% in July.
• Unitree Robotics surged as much as 629% in its Shanghai debut, becoming mainland China's first publicly traded humanoid-robot maker, at a valuation of roughly 36 times sales versus about 20 times for comparable Hong Kong-listed peers.

**AI Funding Costs**
The regional tech selloff tracks a broader repricing of AI infrastructure financing. Alphabet paid just under 7% — a record cost for the company — on a longer-dated Australian dollar bond, and Robeco's head of Asia fixed income described AI hyperscalers as "almost price insensitive" to funding costs given the scale of their planned capital expenditure, adding to bond supply just as global 30-year yields climb toward multi-decade highs.

**Markets and Policy**
Elsewhere, the Philippine peso hit a record low as oil prices rose, adding pressure on the central bank to act, while China's government bonds bucked the global selloff on a policy divide with peers now easing rather than tightening. South Korea's household loan growth jumped to a near five-year high alongside an upgraded state growth forecast, and Hong Kong's exchange posted record earnings on an IPO and trading boom.
