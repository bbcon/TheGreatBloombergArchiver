The cost of an AI slowdown

A national conversation is underway about the need to tap the brakes on AI. 
Investors may not appreciate justwhat that means for their net worth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-20/ai-s-wobbly-house-of-cards-puts-markets-and-us-economy-at-risk?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>
.

AI-related spending accounts for about half the growth in US gross domestic 
product, by some estimates. Almost $33 trillion in market value has been added 
to the S&P 500 since late 2022, the vast majority from companies with futures 
tethered to the technology.

Those gains have been built on the assumption that AI will keep growing at 
breakneck speed for years and years to come.

“People may not fully grasp just how wound up the market and the economy is in 
all of this,” said Jim Morrow at Callodine Capital Management. “There are just 
so many things to unravel if it starts.”

Trying to diversify has proven to be difficult, too 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-20/ai-boom-is-making-diversifying-investments-tough-for-wall-street?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>
, for fund managers looking at everything from private equity to corporate 
bonds to Treasuries: The depth and breadth of the AI revolution has turned the 
usual diversification they rely upon into an illusion. —Jeran Wittenstein 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APXySqfZPFI/jeran-wittenstein?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>

Turkey recap

If you missed the meltdown in Turkey’s stock market last week, here’s what you 
need to know:

 * Investors piled into high-profile funds in recent years that offered 
triple-digit returns or more by investing in illiquid stocks,fueling suspicion 
they were being artificially inflated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/turkey-investment-funds-how-pusula-tera-crisis-sparked-market-panic?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>
.Regulators took a closer look and last week, the concerns morphed into a 
full-blown panic as investors fled.
 * One of the leading players is asset manager Tera, which boasted this year 
that it would becomethe Goldman Sachs of Turkey 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/turkish-fund-s-goldman-dreams-collapse-in-ponzi-like-scheme?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>
. The company’s chairman has been arrested and executives’assets have been 
frozen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/turkey-freezes-assets-of-fund-executives-in-redemption-crisis?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>
.
 * One of the stocks that soared thanks to Tera’s investment is finance 
company Destek. That made its chairmanone of Turkey’s richest people 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/turkey-fund-rout-erases-4-billion-from-a-rapidly-won-fortune?cmpid=092126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9083>
. He’s been detained too and his $13.8 billion fortune is melting away. 
Regulators have seized dozens of funds and are liquidating them. But as they 
try to sell the thinly traded shares, prices are plunging.

“These assets would fetch pennies on the dollar in the event of an arms-length 
sale to a third party,” said Murat Gulkan at OMG Capital Advisors. —Phil 
Serafino

Word from Wall Street

“By the end of next year, the amount of tech-related debt in the system is 
going to be like a systemic issue.”

James Durance

Bond fund manager, Fidelity International

Durance, who oversees $14 billion, has kept tech exposure across his income 
strategies below 2%. Lending to the sector could grow so large that its size 
becomes a risk in itself, he says.
