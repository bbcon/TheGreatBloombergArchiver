**Monetary Policy and Capital Markets**
The Bank of Korea's rate hike — delivered alongside a materially higher growth forecast — signals that AI-driven investment is generating enough demand-side pressure to warrant tightening, even as global uncertainty rises. The move contrasts with the more cautious posture of other regional central banks. In Japan, the yen faces a structural headwind: life insurers have reduced currency hedges to record lows, reflecting the wide US-Japan rate differential and costly hedging economics. Intervention can smooth volatility but cannot engineer a durable yen recovery when domestic institutional investors are positioned against it.

**Corporate and Trade**
New US chip access restrictions are rippling through Asia's AI financing ecosystem. Lenders have paused or slowed due diligence on billions in loans to Chinese technology firms, with the $6.6 billion Bridge Data Centres facility — whose Malaysian operations reportedly serve ByteDance — a prominent example. Nvidia separately instructed AI hardware manufacturers to halt server shipments to Southeast Asia. These curbs threaten to stall the region's data-center investment cycle. In South Asia, Adani Enterprises is preparing a public bond offering of at least $220 million, deepening India's nascent corporate debt market, while a governance dispute over Tata Sons' potential listing is escalating toward litigation.
