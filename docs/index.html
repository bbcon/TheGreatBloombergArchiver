China Energy Transition

China appears on track to meet all incremental power demand growth with clean energy by 2030, according to a report from the Academy of Macroeconomic Research cited by state broadcaster CCTV. Wind and solar are transitioning from marginal to primary sources of new capacity addition, a structural shift with meaningful implications for global energy markets and China's industrial competitiveness. The pace of this buildout — renewable generation already exceeding 40% of output through September — suggests the 2030 target carries credibility, though grid integration and storage constraints remain variables worth monitoring.

India Services Export Surge

India's services export trajectory is drawing increasing attention from economists as digitally delivered services — software development, back-office processing, legal document handling enabled by AI translation — accelerate. HSBC's Frederic Neumann and Abanti Bhaumik describe the recent export trend as near-vertical. The structural driver is the intersection of deepening international communications infrastructure and increasingly capable large language models, which are expanding the range of tasks that can be cost-effectively relocated to lower-wage, English-proficient economies. The macroeconomic implications for India's current account are constructive; the political implications for developed-market services workers are less so. ---

The Services Trade Frontier

Economist Richard Baldwin's new NBER paper frames the defining structural shift in global trade with clarity: globalization's frontier is moving from manufacturing to services, with the "global office" increasingly replicating the dynamics that the "global factory" produced over the prior two decades. Services already constitute $21.2 trillion of global GDP versus $17.7 trillion for manufacturing. The political economy implications may prove more consequential than the economics: middle-class workers in developed economies who felt insulated from the first wave of manufacturing offshoring are now exposed. HSBC's Frederic Neumann warns that the combination of AI displacement and services offshoring represents a "double-whammy" for white-collar employment, with political radicalization — left and right — a plausible consequence. Tariffs are poorly suited to restricting services flows, but data localization requirements framed around privacy and national security offer governments a credible regulatory lever. Whether policymakers deploy such tools aggressively remains uncertain, but the political incentive structure is becoming increasingly visible.

COP31 Dynamics

The Antalya summit is proceeding with an unusually large negotiating agenda — roughly 160 items — with climate finance for developing nations remaining the most contentious unresolved question. The Turkish presidency's decision to grant the country's principal business federation a formal advisory role inside the blue zone represents a meaningful shift in conference architecture, continuing a trend begun at COP30 in Brazil. Civil society groups have expressed concern about differential access. Alberta's decision to cut oil sands royalties to spur a doubling of production capacity sits in direct tension with global decarbonization trajectories, illustrating the gap between national energy economics and international climate commitments that COP31 has yet to bridge.
