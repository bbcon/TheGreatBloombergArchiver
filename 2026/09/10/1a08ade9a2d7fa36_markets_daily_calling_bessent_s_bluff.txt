Market 1, Bessent 0

Scott Bessent’s challenge to traders is off to a rocky start.

The Treasury’s bond buyback announcement yesterday, part of Bessent’s effort 
to push yields lower,had the opposite effect 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/us-more-than-doubles-long-dated-debt-buyback-size-to-6-billion?cmpid=091026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8698>
. The 10-year note jumped to a fresh three-year high of 4.85%, extending a 
two-week selloff.

The reaction exposes the danger inherent to Bessent’s approach 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/traders-brush-aside-scott-bessent-s-bet-against-me-tough-talk?cmpid=091026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8698>
, which mirrors that of his boss, Donald Trump.Verbal interventions only work 
as long as traders have faith they’ll be backed up by strong measures.

And when it comes to perhaps the two most critical factors lifting bond yields 
— the US budget deficit and the war in Iran — Bessent has limited influence.

(Trump’s pledge of that $1 trillion-plus payout 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/trump-urges-voters-to-let-him-finish-the-job-at-gop-convention?cmpid=091026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8698>
 for a Republican mid-term victory isadding to investor concerns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/trump-payout-pledge-adds-to-bond-market-jitters-as-auction-looms?cmpid=091026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8698>
 about Washington’s fiscal discipline and erratic policymaking.)

The massive federal deficit, coupled with concerns about the Fed’s ability to 
tame inflation, are overshadowing Bessent’s maneuvers, said Mark Spindel at 
Potomac River Capital.

“The market has called his bluff,” he said. “Those are all issues that I think 
weigh more heavily on the market, notwithstanding Bessent’s bravado.” —Enda 
Curran

AI bellwether

If any company crystallizes investors’ concerns about the AI buildout, it’s 
Oracle. The software and cloud computing company faces a high hurdle to ease 
those fears intoday’s earnings report 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/oracle-earnings-to-test-market-s-tolerance-for-ai-spending-risk?cmpid=091026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8698>
.

Oracle’s stock has lost half its value in the past year. Meanwhile, the cost 
of protecting debt against default has risen more than 40% in 2026.

Investors are balking at the massive debt load Oracle has taken on to build 
out AI infrastructure.Will the payoff from those investments be enough to repay 
that borrowing?

“Oracle is right at the center of the market’s concerns about AI financing, 
and a lot would have to go right to change that,” said Greg Halter at Carnegie 
Investment Counsel, which owns Oracle shares. —Ryan Vlastelica 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AT3hUmpnSRM/ryan-vlastelica?cmpid=091026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8698>
