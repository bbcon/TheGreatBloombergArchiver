Election scenarios

The US midterm election is less than two weeks away, and the conventional 
wisdom on Wall Street is that stocks will fall if Democrats regain control of 
both houses of Congress.The reality is more nuanced than that 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/us-midterm-elections-2026-what-results-could-mean-for-wall-street?cmpid=102326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10458>
, as our explainer lays out.

A blue sweep is seen as bad for stocks in part because Democrats are viewed as 
more likely to support regulations that would slow down the AI boom.But Donald 
Trump will still wield a veto, and Democrats are unlikely to hold enough seats 
to override it, so they will have a hard time implementing tax increases or 
regulations that markets tend to disfavor.

Wall Street pros tend to be just fine with gridlock, and that’s what they can 
largely expect if Democrats take back just one chamber of Congress. It will be 
hard to get anything done with the House and Senate split and Trump able to 
veto any legislation the parties do agree on.

If Republicans manage to keep control of both chambers of Congress, an outcome 
polls suggest is unlikely, it should leave intact the current favorable 
environment for AI stocks, said analysts.

Investors sifting through the various scenarios, though, are really looking at 
short-term market swings. Longer term, history points to gains for stocks no 
matter the election outcome. Wells Fargo found that the S&P 500 has risen in 
the 12 months after every midterm election in the post-World War II era. —
Matthew Griffin

‘Paralyzed’ market

The debate over AI is keeping IPOs stuck on the launchpad 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/-paralyzed-us-ipo-market-asks-if-there-s-life-after-anthropic?cmpid=102326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10458>
, with October on track for the slowest month of the year by volume for US 
stock market listings.

It’s a marked turn away from a roaring pace that saw $162.6 billion raised 
through the end of September.

AI infrastructure companies including Nscale and DayOne Data Centers are 
pitching investors on their importance to the sector’s buildout, yet with 
shares of peers such as CoreWeave plunging, investors’ appetite for risk-taking 
has evaporated.

Hanging over it all, of course, is Anthropic’s debut, expected next month. 
Companies gauging investors’ willingness to back their IPOs are clamoring for 
the firm behind Claude to deliver a win that would clear the decks.Billions of 
dollars of capital and months of preparations are hanging in the balance.

“People are truly paralyzed,” said West Riggs at Truist Financial. “The market 
has to sort itself out.” —Bailey Lipschultz 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS35PgNdMGI/bailey-lipschultz?cmpid=102326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10458>
