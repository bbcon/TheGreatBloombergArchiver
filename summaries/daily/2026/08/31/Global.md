• The Bloomberg Agriculture Spot Index rose more than 13% in August, its steepest monthly gain since July 2012, as wars and weather disrupted wheat, sugar and cocoa supplies; wheat hit a three-year high after Black Sea port attacks curtailed shipments.
• The US and Iran exchanged strikes for the first time in about a month, with American forces hitting Iranian rocket launchers and Iran firing missiles at US bases in Jordan, which intercepted eight; Brent rose more than 2% above $90.
• Crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz hit a multi-month high before fighting resumed, but LNG traffic remains almost entirely halted since a Qatari tanker was attacked in early July, pushing spot gas prices near war-era highs even as Brent sits roughly 30% below its April peak.
• G20 finance ministers convened in Asheville in what Bloomberg framed as a credibility test for Treasury Secretary Bessent's interventionist "economic statecraft," with early signals peers remain unconvinced.

**Energy Markets**
The oil-gas divergence is structural: LNG relies on specialized, often state-controlled tankers — roughly 850 vessels worldwide versus more than 7,000 crude carriers — making the fuel harder to reroute after an attack, and cargoes cannot be transferred ship-to-ship as crude can. Goldman estimates Gulf oil exports have recovered to 15-16 million barrels a day, still 7-8 million below pre-war levels but above a March trough, and more than doubled its diesel-margin forecast on tightening global refining capacity.

**Geopolitics**
The SCO summit in Bishkek, bringing together Xi, Putin, Modi and Iran's president, tests whether the bloc can translate its scale into collective action as a member remains under attack, with analysts expecting largely symbolic solidarity.
