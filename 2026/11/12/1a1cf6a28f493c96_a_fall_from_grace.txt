As the US experienced a Democratic sweep 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/2026-midterm-elections-what-democrats-wins-in-congress-mean-for-trump?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 last week, South Africa had its own, smaller electoral surge.

The Democratic Alliance gained ground in major cities in municipal voting, 
retaining outrightcontrol of Cape Town 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-15/cape-town-mayor-pushes-for-affordable-housing-in-reelection-bid?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 and taking the most support in Johannesburg.

The once-revered African National Congress, which governed the country with a 
parliamentary majority for three decades after vanquishing apartheid, saw its 
ballot share plummet to 31%.

Forced to look for partners 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-08/south-african-cities-set-for-coalition-rule-as-voters-punish-anc?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 even to run some of its old strongholds, the ANC was left a shadow of its 
former self.
President Cyril Ramaphosa and his wife Tshepo Motsepe vote in Johannesburg.
Photographer: Phill Magakoe/AFP/Getty Images
The damage started well before last week. Jacob Zuma’s nearly nine years as 
president saw widespread corruption and the weakening of public institutions.

Cyril Ramaphosa, his successor, has struggled to restore the ANC’s standing 
with voters desperate for jobs, reliable water and electricity, and functioning 
roads. The party has also splintered, withthree breakaways 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-07-29/south-africa-s-anc-expels-zuma-over-formation-of-rival-party?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 since the late 2000s.

In Johannesburg, Africa’s richest city, the DA’s 35% isn’t enough to govern 
alone. So the ANC is considering working with smaller parties to keep it out of 
City Hall.

That may solve one problem for the ANC. It could create another in Pretoria, 
where at national level it has governed alongside the DA and eight smaller 
rivals sincelosing its majority 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-14/south-african-elections-ramaphosa-reelected-as-president?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 in 2024.
ANC representatives outside a polling station in Durban’s Umlazi township.
Photographer: Marco Longari/AFP/Getty Images
Investors applauded when the parties agreed to share power. Now some ANC 
officialsblame the partnership 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/south-africa-s-anc-to-review-coalition-ties-after-voting-setback?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 for costing them votes.

The DA has its own calculation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-17/south-african-politics-da-leader-committed-to-ruling-coalition-despite-friction?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
: walk away now, or stay and defend its record in office in 2029 general 
elections.

The ANC’s decline has tracked the trajectory of liberation movements in other 
southern African nations that ruled after independence only to fall out of 
favor.

Power has changed hands in Zambia, Malawi and Botswana 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-01/botswana-s-president-masisi-concedes-defeat-in-election?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
, while ruling parties in Zimbabwe and Mozambique have held on through disputed 
elections.

The hard lesson is that a proud legacy can only carry a political party so far.

When Brazilian agents searched a truck at the border with Paraguay, they found 
a hidden compartment with counterfeit medicines, along with illegal drugs, 
cheap electronics and guns. Mixed in with the pills and weight-loss pens was a 
less traditional type of contraband: 150 bottles of pesticides. The bust 
offered a window into anillicit global trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-banned-chinese-pesticides-smuggling-brazil/?cmpid=BBD111226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11285>
 that still kills Brazilians years after the government tried to rid the nation 
of the most dangerous chemicals, powered by an increasingly fractured 
international regulatory environment.
