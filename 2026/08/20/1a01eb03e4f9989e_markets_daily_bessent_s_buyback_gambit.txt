It’s all about the bond market

Yesterday was further evidence that everything in finance revolves around 
Treasury bonds. The Treasury’ssurprise announcement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
 that it would ramp up buybacks of longer-term debt moved asset prices in a 
matter of minutes.

Here’s a round up of global markets:

 * Treasuries: Long-end yields dropped sharply yesterday, with the 30-year 
sliding to 5.19%. Today, analysts warn theplan risks being a short-term fix 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/bessent-s-plan-at-best-circuit-breaker-for-global-bond-slump?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>

Bessent vs the markets

Bessent is rapidly cementing his position as the most interventionist Treasury 
secretary in financial markets in decades 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
, putting hiscredibility on the line 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/jpmorgan-team-sees-credibility-risk-in-treasury-s-bond-buybacks?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
 in his effort to bring yields down.

He has a lot of reasons to take that risk. Total US public debt surpassed $40 
trillion for the first time 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
, the Treasury said yesterday, and has now surged by a third in less than five 
years.

Much of that borrowing goes to financing the government’s deficit spending. US 
lawmakers continue to shrug off calls to deal with the deficit, and some argue 
they’ll only act if forced by a financial-market disruption.

And indeed, rising yields in the bond market this year show that investors are 
growing uncomfortable. Higher borrowing costs alsothreaten to slow the economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/why-high-yields-on-government-bonds-are-causing-alarm?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
 by raising rates for businesses and consumers, and making it more expensive 
for the US to pay its interest bill.

Investors expect rates to climb further, too. The majority of respondents to 
Bloomberg’s latestMarkets Pulse survey 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-10-year-treasury-yield-will-top-5-this-year-markets-pulse?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
 predict the 10-year US Treasury yield will soon exceed 5%. —Chris Anstey 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AF7uNtxCFaA/chris-anstey?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
 andJorgelina Do Rosario 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AThOFgBL0B8/jorgelina-do-rosario?cmpid=082026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260820&utm_content=7901>
