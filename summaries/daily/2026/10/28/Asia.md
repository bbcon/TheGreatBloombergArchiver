**Monetary Policy**
The RBNZ hold was the sharpest market surprise in Asia Wednesday, with the split decision underscoring growing divergence in how regional central banks are weighing global uncertainty against domestic inflation dynamics. In Australia, the opposite dynamic prevails: above-consensus CPI has shifted rate expectations materially toward another tightening when the RBA meets next week. Meanwhile, the Bank of Japan is widely expected to hold at Friday's meeting, with the swap market pricing near-certainty of a December hike. Early indications from Japan's shunto wage negotiations suggest 2027 pay rises could approach 6%, above the roughly 5% pace seen since 2023, which would strengthen the case for continued BOJ normalization toward an estimated terminal rate of 2%.

**Capital Flows**
A notable structural shift in Treasury demand deserves attention: US-aligned economies in Asia purchased a net $389 million in Treasuries in the first eight months of 2026 — Japan alone, the largest foreign holder, bought just $389 million, down 99% year-on-year. The aggregate decline among US-aligned nations exceeded 60% versus the prior year. While governments have not formally announced reallocation strategies, analysts attribute the shift to weak domestic currencies, rising local yields, equity demand, and increasing unease over US policy predictability. The shift may signal a gradual erosion of the structural bid that has historically anchored Treasury demand.
