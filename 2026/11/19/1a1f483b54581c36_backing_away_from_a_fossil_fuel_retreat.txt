Climate deal scrubs fossil fuel push

By Jennifer A Dlouhy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASlQFjfALa0/jennifer-a-dlouhy?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYPlpafOt_4/azman-usmani?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXt16hTqABg/lili-pike?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>

A revised outline for a deal at the annual United Nations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/8243133Z:US?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>
 climate talks dropped calls to phase out fossil fuels and put the summit on 
track to endorse proposals to boost electrification of the global economy.

By offering potential compromises on charged issues including climate finance, 
trade curbs and a timetable for a set of key science reports, the document 
raises the prospects for the COP31 talks in Antalya, Turkey to win agreement on 
a broad deal among the nearly 200 nations represented.

The draft decision text released Thursday said nations recognize 
electrification has significant benefits to emissions reduction and energy 
security, paving the way for an increased focus on shifting sectors like 
transportation and heavy industry away from the use of polluting fuels.
Scales and the COP31 logo at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey.
Photographer: Chris Ratcliffe/Bloomberg
While whittled down to nine pages, half the size of an earlier document 
circulated Monday, the latest draft will still be subject to further debate 
among negotiators and the summit is likely to extend beyond Friday’s scheduled 
close. Ministers and blocs were due to convene later Thursday to continue 
deliberations.

“We have an obligation to make COP31 a success,” Murat Kurum, Turkey’s 
environment, urbanization and climate change minister, and the summit’s 
president, told reporters Thursday. “We are also addressing areas of 
disagreement with openness and sincerity.”

The new draft shifts decisively away from aggressive wording floated Monday 
that had called for “accelerated action to phase out fossil fuels, including 
through the immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems.” 
Calls on countries to do more to pare emissions of methane, a particularly 
powerful greenhouse gas associated with the energy and agriculture sectors, 
were also watered down.

A retreat on fossil fuels at the summit came hours after the leaders of the 
US, China and Russia agreed to boost the supply of refined petroleum products 
as wars strain oil and gas flows.

Organizers of the summit have been working to build support for a voluntary 
pledge to boost electricity’s share of global final energy demand to 35% by 
2035, from just over 20% currently. They are also seeking to include the goal 
in the final COP31 declaration, which would put the ambition on a more formal 
footing.

That’s drawn stiff objections from a bloc of developing countries, including 
India, that oppose enshrining top-down targets in a formal cover decision. 
Though the new draft includes a call for cooperation on achieving the target, 
the initiative is likely to continue to be disputed as attendees head into 
final negotiations.

While final outcomes at the United Nations-convened summits are adopted by 
consensus — an approach that effectively means even tiny countries can block 
agreements — some nations have extra heft in the talks. Seasoned diplomats can 
leverage wordsmithing or coalition-building expertise to firm voluntary pledges 
into adopted action, or to kill off outcomes that aren’t in the interests of 
their nation or bloc.

A last push for a firm agreement will be led by Turkey’s Environment Minister 
Murat Kurum, who holds the COP31 presidency, and Australia’s Climate Change and 
Energy Minister Chris Bowen, who’s overseeing negotiations.

Here are some of the key figures they’ll have to deal with in negotiating 
rooms in Antalya:

Wopke Hoekstra, European Union
EU Climate Chief Wopke HoekstraPhotographer: Wiktor Dabkowski/Bloomberg
Hoekstra, the European Union’s climate chief, sometimes cuts a lonely figure 
as the main negotiator for a bloc that is pushing ahead with the climate 
transition, yet — with the US absent — is also exposed on issues of trade and 
finance.

Ilana Seid, Alliance of Small Island States
Chair of the Alliance of Small Island States Ilana Victorya Seid.Source: AOSIS
A former investment banker at Lehman Brothers and Nomura Securities Co., Seid 
brings both a sophisticated understanding of financial markets and a sharp view 
of the limits of international diplomacy to her role as chair of theAlliance of 
Small Island States <https://aosis.org/chair/>, a grouping of 39 
climate-vulnerable nations.

Li Gao, China

On the ground at COP31

By John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>

Germany 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3413Z:GR?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>
 and theEuropean Union 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/345300Z:BB?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>
 are advancing a global initiative to tackle the world’s most potent greenhouse 
gas, after warnings that temperature increases can no longer be prevented from 
crossing a crucial threshold.

Sulfur hexafluoride, or SF6, traps about 24,000 times more heat than carbon 
dioxide over its first 100 years in the atmosphere and is used extensively in 
power-grid infrastructure. The impact of the gas is a rising concern as nations 
seek to build out electricity networks to meet rising demand, and with climate 
advocates promoting efforts to boost electrification of sectors like transport 
and heavy industry.

The pledge announced Thursday, which also has the backing of countries 
including the UK, France and Nigeria, aims to use SF6-free switchgear where 
possible and to combat leaks in infrastructure.
Read more 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/nations-take-aim-at-a-climate-threat-hiding-in-electricity-grids?cmpid=BBD111926_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11616>
The European Union’s climate chief said a deal struck between the US, Russia 
and China Thursday to expand the production and supply of fuels was an 
unfortunate reality.

The world shouldn’t fall into another energy “trap,” Wopke Hoekstra said at 
the COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey. Instead, the focus should be on 
doubling down on domestic clean energy sources that are “dirt cheap” to avoid 
another energy crisis like the one that followed the war in Iran and the 
blockage of the Strait of Hormuz, he said.

Chinese President Xi Jinping hosted what he called a “groundbreaking” 
discussion in Shenzhen with Presidents Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin. The 
three leaders announced a plan to “increase the production and supply of 
refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations” and reaffirmed the 
importance of stable energy flows. They didn’t provide details on how the 
proposal would work.
