‘Wild rates volatility’

Just when global bond investors were starting to believe the worst of this 
year’s selloff was over,surging Treasury yields are making them think again 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/treasury-yield-surge-shatters-hopes-worst-of-bond-rout-is-over?cmpid=111326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11379>
.

Stubborn inflation and hawkish rhetoric from Fed policymakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/fed-s-williams-says-inflation-far-too-high-still-work-to-do?cmpid=111326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11379>
 have traders bracing for more aggressive interest-rate hikes, while growing 
debt supply adds to the upward pressure on yields. The selloff persisted even 
as turmoil around Nvidia rattled stocks and risk sentiment, undercutting hopes 
that Treasuries had reclaimed their traditional haven role.

“This is a tougher environment than during the Covid pandemic and the global 
financial crisis,” said Kellie Wood, a bond investor at Schroders who is short 
Treasuries. “Those were big directional moves — today it’s about rolling supply 
shocks and wild rates volatility. We still have to be on alert for higher 
yields all the way till year end.”

Treasuries have had a bruising week. The combined jump in two-year US yields 
on Tuesday and Thursday — with cash markets closed Wednesday — was the biggest 
since March 2023, while the increase in 10-year yields was the most since April 
2025.

It’s a sharp U-turn from just a week ago, when a Democratic sweep in the US 
midterms and jitters over AI sent investors flocking to Treasuries for safety. —
