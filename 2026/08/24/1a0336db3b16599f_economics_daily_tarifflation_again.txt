Tarifflation Returns?

Back in March, then-Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell said the inflationary 
impulse from tariffs that had pushed US inflation to 3% — a percentage point 
above the 2% target — were expected topeter out substantially 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-03-19/powell-pushes-back-against-trump-on-rates-and-legal-investigation?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
 in “the middle parts of the year.”

Sure enough, the latest reading on the Fed’s preferred price gauge, the core 
PCE, is expected on Wednesday to show a tame 0.2% monthly rise for July after 
an even tamer 0.1% for June. Figures like that would eventually pull inflation 
back to target. (For now, the year-on-year core measure, which strips out 
volatile food and energy costs, is still forecast at 3.3%.)

But new moves by President Donald Trump indicate tariff pressures are at risk 
of a return. One is a new chapter in thetrade war with Canada 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/us-canada-trade-talks-collapse-triggering-trump-s-50-tariffs?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
, and the other is thepotential for new levies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/bessent-to-detail-us-plans-to-isolate-iran-s-economy-on-monday?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
 on US trading partners that still transact with Iran.

On Saturday, US tariffs of 50% kicked in on hundreds of items from America’s 
second-largest source of imports, after trade talks broke down. Those total 
about $20 billion in value, and include products such as plywood, liquor, 
electrical equipment and hockey gear.

Ottawa plans retaliatory measures. Given Trump’s history of being prepared to 
escalate, that suggests some danger of a period of spiraling duties — though as 
Canada’s tariffs aren’t set to take effectuntil Sept. 8 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/canada-unveils-20-billion-counter-tariffs-to-mirror-trump-levy?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
, a window for restarting talks appears open.

On the Iran front, Bessent is expected to speak at a Monday press conference 
and lay out what Trump’s new effort at economic isolation of the Middle East 
nation will involve.

Given the depth and breadth of American sanctions on Tehran stretching all the 
way back to the Islamic regime’s institution in 1979, meaningful measures would 
have to include actions against other countries. China, a major Iran partner, 
would be the big one. Analysts find it difficult to fathom the Trump team would 
be prepared for the blowback from Beijing, however, after China in 2025 
demonstrated its willingness to cut the US off fromvital industrial supplies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-14/how-xi-is-using-rare-earths-against-trump-in-the-us-china-trade-war?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
.

Whether Bessent’s announcement affects China, India, or other Middle Eastern 
nations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/trump-s-economic-isolation-plan-risks-iran-strikes-across-gulf?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
, any so-called secondary tariffs or sanctions escalate the danger of a new 
round of trade tensions. Which all could reverberate back to American prices.

The Week Ahead

Investors are looking for Kevin Warsh to clarify his views on how the US 
central bank should address stubborn inflation when the Fed chairman speaks on 
Friday at the annual gathering in Jackson Hole, Wyoming.

It’s not entirely clear he will deliver.

The chairman’s communications strategy is off to a rocky start 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/fed-s-warsh-rebuked-by-investors-craving-a-real-inflation-fight?cmpid=BBD082426_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8084>
. After the July policy meeting, Warsh offered little insight on his views of 
the economy and avoided offering forward guidance on interest rates. Investors 
interpreted his comments as showing a lack of resolve to bringing inflation 
back to target, and long-term bond yields subsequently climbed to a two-decade 
high.

For the first time as leader of the world’s top central bank, Warsh will offer 
keynote remarks at the Kansas City Fed’s annual Economic Policy Symposium. With 
Treasury yields still elevated by sticky inflation and fiscal deficit concerns, 
he’s under pressure to act more like his predecessors and give clearer guidance 
of how the Fed might react over the remainder of the year.

Need-to-Know Research

China’s economic slump may be deepening, but one sector is bucking the trend: 
tourism. Foreign visitor arrivals have surpassed their pre-pandemic levels, and 
new reforms should sustain the trend, according to research by Bank of America.

The relaxation of entry restrictions has unlocked demand from a broad range of 
countries, including Russia, Canada and the UK, wrote analysts Yvonne He, Anna 
Zhou and Helen Qiao. Visa-free arrivals surged 30% year-on-year in the first 
half of 2026.
Average travel expenses in China are only slightly above Thailand, but notably 
below South Korea and Japan. (Bank of America)Bloomberg
The expansion of VAT refunds — now eligible for purchases as small as $30 — 
has also helped trigger a shift beyond just sightseeing to holiday shopping. 
That coincides with the growing popularity of Chinese brands like Labubu 
collectibles and Songmont bags. China holidays also cost less on average than 
most regional peers.

To be sure, China’s inbound tourism remains nascent, accounting for just 0.3% 
of GDP, compared to 1.3%-1.4% in South Korea and Japan. But it provides a 
welcome boost to the nation’s services exports and consumption, at a time when 
domestic demand remains soft.
