Circuit breaker

By any measure, Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia unleashed a remarkable 
reaction in the stock market. The stats:

 * Nvidia shares soared 31%, the most since 2003, adding $952 billion to the 
company’s market value.
 * The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index jumped 14%.
 * The Nasdaq 100, which had been close to a bear market, climbed 6.9%.
 * The S&P 500 rose 4.7%. 
Huang is now back at the center of the AI trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-23/why-did-nvidia-reinstate-jensen-huang-as-ceo?cmpid=112426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11791>
, leading a company that startups, computing providers and model developers 
around the world have come to depend on for multibillion-dollar investments and 
financial backstops.

That doesn’t mean this story is over. There are still a dizzying number of 
unresolved questions about the events that led to Huang’s resignation.

He will soon face queries — under oath — about his affair with a client 
suspected of spying for Beijing, with Democrats unlikely to buy assurancesfrom 
the Trump administration 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/nvidia-s-board-was-no-match-for-trump-or-jensen-huang-s-backers?cmpid=112426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11791>
 that he committed no wrongdoing.

Nvidia’s board is also likely to come under scrutiny for how it handled the 
scandal and its interactions with President Trump. The industry as a whole 
still faces the prospect of tighter regulation when Democrats control Congress.

“The hype settles and the emotion fades and everybody starts thinking again,” 
said Jacopo Ceccatelli at Banca Finanziaria Internazionale. “It’s going to be 
very, very important to see what happens in the next two to three days.”

For now, one way to think about the Huang’s return is that it has acted like a 
circuit breaker for the AI stock slump. But as today’s action suggest, it’s 
possible the enthusiasm is short lived. —Charles Riley 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXaNtvarAGI/charles-riley?cmpid=112426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11791>

Ghosts of 2018

Eight years ago, a resilient US economy masked deteriorating growth prospects 
just as the Fed turned hawkish.

Now, strategists at Citadel Securities are warning of a repeat 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/citadel-securities-says-2018-playbook-warns-of-more-market-pain?cmpid=112426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11791>
. Like in 2018, the dollar is rising and cyclical stocks have been sinking. The 
Fed’s determination to keep monetary policy tight is helping to keep bond 
yields elevated.

“There is a natural temptation to regard the recent correction as having 
already discounted much of the deterioration in the outlook, but the experience 
of 2018 offers little reassurance on that front,” said Frank Flight, head of 
macro strategy.

Back then, the Fed was also in a tightening cycle, and Jerome Powell rattled 
markets by saying rates were “a long way” from neutral. By December, the S&P 
500 had fallen almost 20% from its September peak.

The Fed is now vowing to bring inflation — currently above 3% — down to its 2% 
target in a timely matter. Even during last week’s stock rout, two central bank 
officials reiterated the need to boost borrowing costs further.

One potential outcome: Long-dated Treasuries look primed to outperform as 
markets turn their attention to slowing economic activity. —Ye Xie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/citadel-securities-says-2018-playbook-warns-of-more-market-pain?cmpid=112426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11791>
