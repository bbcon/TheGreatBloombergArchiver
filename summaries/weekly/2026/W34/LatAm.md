**Brazil's Fiscal and Political Crosscurrents**
Finance Minister Dario Durigan acknowledged investors have "legitimate questions" about the spending trajectory less than two months before the October vote. BCA Research warns that a Lula win could dash hopes of fiscal tightening: a weaker currency might force the central bank to reverse its easing cycle, higher borrowing costs would point to a 2027 growth slowdown, and Brazilian equities — already decoupled from rising local yields since early this year — could face a valuation correction. Citi's Dirk Willer noted that unlike outcomes this year in Hungary and Colombia, in Brazil "the less market-friendly candidate is the favorite," a distinction that could deepen downside risk if uncertainty about the spending path intensifies.

**Trade, Commodities and Regional Flows**
Capital Economics argues the region entered the Iran conflict with strong external balance sheets — high reserves, resilient exports and narrowing current-account deficits — positioning Latin American currencies and dollar debt to weather dollar strength better than in past cycles. Copper has provided a tailwind for regional assets, though returns continue to lag technology-driven gains elsewhere in emerging markets. Venezuela's push to re-attract US oil capital — with SLB and Hunt Oil signing new PDVSA contracts — reflects genuine investor appetite for an underexploited reserve base, but poor historical contract enforcement and a bilateral, non-competitive award process remain material risks. Mexico's Interoceanic Corridor promises faster freight transit times than the Panama route, but its 2035 container target remains a fraction of Panama's traffic and power-infrastructure gaps continue to limit factory investment.
