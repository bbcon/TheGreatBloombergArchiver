Copper prices have surged since Donald Trump returned to the White House, 
fueled by a seismic reshuffling of global inventories linked to the risk that 
he’ll impose tariffs on the metal. But as the halfway mark on Trump’s second 
term approaches, the market’s many bulls are wary that prices could crash if he 
doesn’t follow through.

Until the past few weeks, copper futures in New York had traded at persistent 
premiums to the global benchmark set on the London Metal Exchange. That’s 
enabled traders to make unprecedented profits shipping copper to the US before 
any levies arrive. In turn, inventories outside the US have plunged, with 
ongoing US imports fueling a feedback loop that helped drive LME prices to a 
record near $15,000 a metric ton last month.

As midterm elections approach, investors across markets are questioning 
whether a strong showing for the Democrats will disrupt Trump’s trade agenda — 
or whether it might even prompt him to double down on tariffs.

For copper, one of the world’s most important commodities, the tariff trade 
has created a bubble in the market that could soon burst if tariffs are ruled 
out, analysts at Natixis warned. Macquarie Group says prices could crash toward 
$10,000 a ton in that scenario — or they could rally as far as $18,000 if 
tariffs are announced with a lag that would allow traders to ship even more 
metal to the US. (Click here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/traders-grow-wary-of-copper-bull-market-with-no-sign-of-tariffs?cmpid=BBD102026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10113>
 for the full story from Bloomberg’s Julian Luk, Mark Burton, Alfred Cang and 
James Attwood.)

Copper traders have been badly wrong-footed by Trump on tariffs before, most 
painfully in July last year, when he introduced 50% levies on downstream copper 
products while leaving the commodity-grade form of the metal traded in futures 
markets untouched.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-30/trump-imposes-50-tarriff-on-semi-finished-copper-products?cmpid=BBD102026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10113>

That sent Comex prices crashing, but until recently they’ve remained at a 
substantial premium to the LME because Trump left the door open to imposing 
them in the future. Ever since, the copper market has been in the grips of the 
polarizing debate over whether he will.

While tariff-related trade flows have been the main driver of record copper 
prices this year, mining problems are another critical factor, particularly in 
Chile, Attwood alsoreported 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/copper-boom-belies-a-deepening-mining-recession-in-top-producer?cmpid=BBD102026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10113>
.

Chile’s aging copper mines have been battered by deadly accidents, extreme 
weather and operational missteps. That’s raising questions over how quickly the 
world’s biggest producer can recover from its lowest output in more than two 
decades — just as global demand from data centers and power-grid expansion 
accelerates.

The recent spate of extreme weather is exposing a longer-term dilemma for an 
industry built around Chile’s historically arid conditions. It also offers a 
glimpse of what could lie ahead for mines and infrastructure as a powerful El 
Niño develops. Higher summer temperatures risk causing ice masses to shift at 
high-altitude operations, threatening pit stability and transport routes.

Companies big and small are scrambling for ways to extract more copper from 
aging mines. A new episode ofBloomberg Primer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-19/can-microbes-solve-the-copper-problem-video?cmpid=BBD102026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10113>
 looks at some of the innovators trying to solve the copper bottleneck by 
taking potential solutions from laboratories to some of the world’s largest 
pits.

To extend the lifespan of Arizona’s Carlota Mine, for example, owner KGHM 
recently brought in millions of microbes supplied by Denver-based Endolith. The 
unconventional miners are acid-loving microorganisms thatliberate copper from 
ore 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-19/copper-shortage-drives-innovation-by-mining-startups?cmpid=BBD102026_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10113>
 by digesting surrounding iron and sulfur compounds.

While Endolith is tight-lipped about its estimated additional copper output 
because the experiment is still in its early days, the microbial miners could 
help squeeze more copper out of the heaps and reduce the use of sulfuric acid 
at a time when the war in Iran has sent the feedstock’s price sky-high.

 | The European Union looks to be relaxing its opposition to oil and gas 
production in polar areas, while still calling for all fossil fuels to be kept 
in the ground in its forthcoming Arctic strategy. A draft of the strategy, seen 
by Bloomberg, contains no references to a moratorium on new oil and gas 
drilling in the Arctic, marking a shift in wording from the EU’s 2021 stance. 
The wording has been a topic of intense discussions between the trade bloc and 
Norway, which has lobbied hard for the EU to abandon its climate-based 
opposition to oil and gas exploration in the high north.
