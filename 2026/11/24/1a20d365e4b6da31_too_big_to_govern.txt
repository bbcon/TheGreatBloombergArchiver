affairs not only with employees, but also witha possible Chinese spy

The trend is becoming clearer. Sam Altman defied his own board to reclaim 
OpenAI. Samsung leader Lee Jae-yong was convicted of bribery and embezzlement — 
only to receive a presidential pardon explicitly so he could return to lead the 
company.

Elon Musk not only shrugged off the Biden administration’s efforts to rein him 
in, he also spent millions to help get Donald Trump elected as his successor. 
The world’s richest man then exercised his right to turn his Starlink satellite 
system on or off for Ukraine.
Trump and Huang at the White House last year.Photographer: Ken 
Cedeno/UPI/Bloomberg
The message to world capitals is unmistakable: the architects of the tech boom 
are now too big to govern.

In an era where compute power and chip supply define national strength, 
governments increasingly act less like regulators holding executives to account 
and more like supplicants desperate to keep their champions on the field.

The Nvidia board’s rapid retreat 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/nvidia-s-board-was-no-match-for-trump-or-jensen-huang-s-backers?cmpid=BBD112426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11790>
 was rewarded by an immediatesurge in the stock price 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/ai-bulls-in-asia-get-huang-back-but-the-harder-questions-remain?cmpid=BBD112426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11790>
.

“We have to protect our geniuses,” Trump declared in a television interview.

He had personally spoken to Nvidia’s board to advocate Huang’s return. When he 
met with Chinese President Xi Jinping — whose country has been working hard to 
get its hands on Nvidia’s most advanced chips and the underlying design 
technology, despite US export controls — they both apparently concluded that 
Huang was a “brilliant man.”

In other words, when a brain is considered a national security asset, the 
standard rules of corporate governance — and counterintelligence —simply bend 
around it

failure to deal with a housing crisis, which has become asymbol for broader 
anger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-24/spain-election-2026-angry-generation-feels-left-behind-as-economy-booms?cmpid=BBD112426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11790>

election. It’s a mood at odds with the image of Spain as a model economy, 
expanding more than twice as fast as the eurozone, cutting its budget deficit 
and reducing unemployment.
