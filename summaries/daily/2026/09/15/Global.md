**Energy and the Iran Conflict**
The Saudi East-West pipeline, which provides a critical alternative route bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, has been offline since last week's attacks, removing a significant buffer from global oil supply. Saudi Arabia's reported effort to compensate by increasing Strait of Hormuz flows is constrained by the security environment. With Brent at $107/barrel, the IEA has again reduced its oil consumption forecast, acknowledging demand destruction — though that destruction has so far been concentrated outside the United States. California diesel prices have set fresh records, with one San Diego station posting $9.99 per gallon. The Pentagon's confirmation of strategic munitions shortfalls introduces a material variable into the conflict's trajectory, with resupply bottlenecks potentially constraining US operational tempo.

**Trade Architecture**
The WTO's Geneva symposium crystallized the degree to which the multilateral trading system is under structural pressure. With 72% of global goods trade still conducted under most-favored-nation principles, the institution retains meaningful relevance — but its own modeling suggests that continued fragmentation could reduce real global GDP by 5-7% and exports by nearly 27% relative to a cooperative baseline by 2050. The near-term backdrop is consistent with the pessimistic scenario: US-Canada tariff friction, the EU approaching a critical juncture in its China economic-security response, the BRICS bloc raising concerns about unilateral trade measures, and the Trump-Xi summit in under two weeks carrying significant implications for the fragile trade truce and critical minerals access.
