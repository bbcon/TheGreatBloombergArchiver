**Strait of Hormuz and Energy Supply**
The conflict has entered a structurally more dangerous phase. Tanker transits through the Strait of Hormuz, which carries approximately 20% of global seaborne oil trade, fell to near zero on Tuesday — compared with a pre-war average of approximately 80 per day. Persian Gulf flows have reportedly dropped below 45% of pre-war levels, per Goldman Sachs. The US Strategic Petroleum Reserve sits at its lowest level since 1983, materially limiting the buffer available for a second intervention. Thursday's Houthi strikes on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea introduced a second chokepoint: Bab el-Mandeb, through which container traffic flows to the Suez Canal. Polymarket odds assign a 33% probability to effective Bab el-Mandeb closure by year-end. Bloomberg Intelligence estimates a sustained Red Sea blockade could lift energy shipping rates by at least 30%. US gasoline crossed $4 per gallon and diesel exceeded $5, while refining crack spreads hit records. Asian LNG imports rose 12% month-on-month in China as Qatari supply disruptions via Hormuz forced buyers to spot markets — Pakistan's state buyer paid approximately $21.88 per million BTU for a late-July shipment, among the highest prices since 2022. Morgan Stanley warns European diesel inventories are on course for multiyear lows by year-end.

**Monetary Policy Divergence**
The global rates landscape is growing structurally more complex. Japanese two-year yields reached their highest level since 1995 at just below 1.5%, with a majority of economists still expecting the Bank of Japan to hold until December even as yen weakness to multi-decade lows intensifies pressure for acceleration. UK 30-year yields are near multi-decade highs. US 30-year Treasuries have sustained their longest run above 5% since 2007. The ECB is privately leaning toward a September hike driven by energy-price inflation, even as Thursday's euro-area activity and inflation expectations data complicated that posture. The Federal Reserve faces market pricing of a one-in-three probability of a hike next week while economist consensus still points to the next move being a cut. The divergence reflects fundamentally different domestic inflation dynamics layered on a shared energy shock — a configuration that typically produces meaningful currency volatility and cross-border capital flow realignment.
