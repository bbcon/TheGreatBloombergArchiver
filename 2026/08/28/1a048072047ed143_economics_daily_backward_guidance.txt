‘Backward’ Guidance

Everyone, as they say, deserves a second chance. Federal Reserve Chairman 
Kevin Warshgets his on Friday morning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/jackson-hole-offers-warsh-high-profile-slot-to-rebut-his-critics?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 at 8 a.m. local timein Jackson Hole 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/fed-s-jackson-hole-conference-is-underway-here-s-what-to-expect?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
, Wyoming (10 a.m. New York).

While views are hardly universal, Warsh bore the brunt of a wave of criticism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/fed-s-warsh-rebuked-by-investors-craving-a-real-inflation-fight?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 for his July 29 press conference, where his remarks were tied to a selloff in 
the US Treasury market that then tanked equities.

Not only did Warsh fail to explain why he opposed raising interest rates that 
day, he declined to offer any “reaction function” tips — hints as to what would 
make him change his mind, or at least what data points were most important to 
him. He even suggested the central bank could switch the price gauge it uses 
for its inflation target, potentially opening the door todiluting that targeting
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/clarida-urges-clear-fed-inflation-target-as-warsh-floats-changes?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
.

It was hardly the first time that a new policymaker got a negative, “Bronx 
cheer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-30/kevin-warsh-delivers-a-perplexing-fed-briefing-and-gets-a-bronx-cheer?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
” reaction. In fact it might even be par for the course. Jerome Powell saw his
share of criticism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-11-06/powell-policy-lost-in-translation-as-fed-blamed-for-market-woes?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 in his first year. Janet Yellen was charged with a “rookie mistake 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2014-03-19/janet-yellens-rookie-mistake-speaking-too-clearly?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
” in 2014. Over at the Treasury, newly installed Secretary Timothy Geithner
bombed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2009-02-11/geithner-leaves-questions-and-markets-make-him-pay?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 with the initial rollout of his financial-rescue plan in 2009.
Visitors in the Jackson Lake Lodge ahead of the Kansas City Fed’s Jackson Hole

Warsh’s keynote speech at the Kansas City Fed’s annual economic symposium 
offers him a chance to rewrite the script — all the more so since the remarks 
will be wholly his. In the July and June press conferences, he was charged with 
speaking for the rate-settingFederal Open Market Committee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/fed-officials-remain-divided-on-inflation-ahead-of-warsh-speech?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 as a whole. This time, he’ll own the message.

Given that the new Fed chief has so roundly rejected offering “forward 
guidance” about future policy decisions, it would be a major surprise if he 
gave any heads up about the Sept. 15-16 policy meeting. But at least,market 
participants say 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/an-inflation-focused-warsh-speech-can-give-long-bonds-a-boost?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
, he ought to offer some “backward” guidance. Whytalk tough 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-15/kevin-warsh-talks-the-talk-on-inflation-while-leaving-the-walk-in-question?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 on inflation butno rate hike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/ex-rbi-chief-rajan-says-fed-should-raise-rates-to-curb-inflation?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
? What has he made of thelatest inflation data 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/key-us-inflation-gauge-posts-muted-advance-spending-stalls?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>

Need-to-Know Research

The unusually hot summer that affected the UK didn’t just spark debate over 
building designs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/londoners-complain-of-unbearable-heat-in-city-s-modern-housing?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 andregulations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-19/rich-londoners-balk-at-hearing-no-in-efforts-to-install-acs?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 around air-conditioning. It also affected business investment decisions, 
according to findings from Barclays.

Some 60% of companies are now investing or plan to invest in technologies to 
help them adapt to extreme heat, aBarclays study 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/barclays-says-heat-is-shaping-spending-plans-of-uk-businesses?cmpid=BBD082826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8252>
 showed this week. Consumers surveyed made clear that temperatures above 25.1C 
(77.2F) are too hot for shopping; at mercury readings above 24.2C, they no 
longer want to commute.
A worker uses an umbrella to shield from the sun during a heat wave in the 
City of London.Photographer: Chris J. Ratcliffe/Bloomberg
The Barclays survey, which was carried out at the end of July, also found that 
small businesses are more vulnerable to heat-related disruption. More than a 
quarter said that hot weather had negatively affected productivity. Among 
larger firms, meanwhile, almost half reported a positive effect.
