Thursday, September 10, 2026

US

  • Treasury's tripled bond buyback program failed to cap yields; the 10-year rose to 4.85%, a fresh three-year high, missing Bessent's stated objective.
  • Brent crude topped $101–$102 per barrel, with diesel at a record $5.94/gallon — a mechanical inflation headwind that could push headline CPI toward 4.9% if sustained.

Rates and Fiscal Policy

Treasury Secretary Bessent's bond buyback program — tripled to $6 billion in longer-dated debt — produced the opposite of the intended effect. The 10-year Treasury yield extended its two-week selloff to reach 4.85%, the highest in three years, as market participants viewed the intervention as insufficiently large. Bessent's public posture of asymmetric-information bravado appears to have invited rather than deterred testing by investors. Analysts at Wells Fargo and Potomac River Capital argue that the fundamental drivers of elevated yields — the structural federal deficit, elevated inflation expectations, and war-premium energy prices — remain beyond Bessent's direct influence. The 5% threshold on the 10-year, last breached in 2023, is now proximate enough to be a credible scenario.

Equities and Credit

The S&P 500 steadied after a three-day losing streak, with AI-linked earnings optimism continuing to cushion equities from the full impact of higher discount rates. Oracle, whose stock has lost roughly half its value over the past year amid investor skepticism about AI debt financing, reported after the close and represents a live test of market confidence in the AI buildout thesis. In private credit, over $138 billion in buyout debt is expected to come to market in coming months — US issuance at its highest since 2007 — raising questions about absorption capacity as floating-rate defaults rise. ---

Asia

  • China signaled willingness to hold AI talks with the US, but vowed retaliation against proposed restrictions on Chinese firms accused of systematic technology theft from American labs.
  • Huawei raised prices on its Ascend 950DT AI accelerator by roughly 60% to 250,000 yuan ($37,300), as domestic demand for alternatives to Nvidia accelerates.

Technology and Trade

TSMC's 53% revenue increase is the most consequential data point from the region this period, confirming that AI-driven semiconductor demand remains robust and supply-constrained — a macro-relevant signal for capital expenditure and inflation in the technology supply chain. Separately, Huawei's sharp price increase on its top AI accelerator reflects both surging domestic Chinese demand and the supply constraints facing China's homegrown chip ecosystem. DeepSeek's planned deployment of at least 160,000 Ascend 950DT units underscores the scale of Chinese AI infrastructure investment proceeding independently of US export controls.

Geopolitics and Credit

The China-US AI dispute has escalated, with Beijing's Commerce Ministry rejecting US intelligence claims of industrial-scale model theft ahead of an anticipated Xi-Trump summit. Meanwhile, a notable convergence in credit markets has emerged: SK Hynix bonds maturing 2031 now yield only nine basis points above comparable Amazon debt, a fraction of the spread from a year ago. Risk premiums on emerging-market corporate debt indexes are converging with US benchmarks — a development that reflects both Big Tech's heavy borrowing and the relative resilience of select Asian balance sheets. ---

Europe

  • The ECB is expected to raise its deposit rate by 25 basis points to 2.5% — its second hike since the Iran conflict sent energy prices higher — in line with consensus per Bloomberg polling.
  • Bloomberg Economics quantified EU market-share losses to Chinese manufacturers at 0.7% of GDP in 2025, approximately $150 billion, up from 0.5% the prior year.
  • French bond yield premiums widened to their highest level since 2012, a market signal of renewed sovereign stress within the eurozone periphery.

Monetary Policy

The ECB's anticipated rate increase reflects a eurozone economy facing a difficult combination: energy-driven inflation materially above target and, paradoxically, growth proving more resilient than feared. New quarterly projections are expected to reinforce the case for tightening by projecting higher inflation alongside firmer activity. The ECB's decision follows a period in which Brent crude has re-crossed $100 per barrel, adding mechanically to headline inflation across member states already contending with elevated gas prices and thin energy buffers heading into winter. Denmark is expected to follow the ECB; Turkey held at its most recent meeting.

Structural Competitiveness

The $150 billion Chinese market-share shock — quantified by Bloomberg Economics and set to be addressed at a Brussels summit next month — represents a material and growing drag on eurozone growth. Chinese export gains are concentrated in automotives and chemicals, sectors central to Germany's industrial base. German exports unexpectedly declined in the most recent data. EU officials are debating new tariff tools and investment screens, but internal divisions persist, with member states weighing short-term retaliation costs against longer-term industrial preservation. Serbia's October snap election and the Mladić funeral controversy add political noise to an already complex EU enlargement picture. ---

LatAm

  • Brazil's President Lula unveiled additional fuel tax cuts to shield consumers from surging oil prices, weeks ahead of October's presidential election, a fiscally expansionary measure that may complicate the central bank's inflation management.
  • Brazil's Supreme Court chief justice removed the court's most prominent judge from a seven-year "fake news" probe, deepening an institutional crisis with direct implications for the electoral environment.
  • Colombia and other commodity exporters in the region face a mixed backdrop: oil above $100 provides revenue support, but tanker freight at record levels raises import costs for non-producers.

Political Economy

Brazil remains the dominant story in the region this period. Lula's pre-election fuel tax reduction is politically legible but fiscally consequential, adding to concerns about medium-term debt sustainability at a moment when global bond markets are already under pressure from US fiscal concerns. The Supreme Court's internal conflict over the fake news investigation has broader institutional implications, creating uncertainty around judicial independence in an electoral year. Meanwhile, the convergence of emerging-market credit spreads toward US investment-grade benchmarks — visible in the SK Hynix/Amazon example — is benefiting select Brazilian and regional corporate issuers, even as local political risk remains elevated.

Commodity Dynamics

The return of Brent crude above $100 per barrel is a double-edged development for Latin America. Oil exporters — notably Colombia and, to a lesser extent, Brazil — benefit from higher prices, while energy importers face renewed inflation pressure. Record tanker freight rates, with supertanker earnings nearing $800,000 per day on the Middle East-to-China route, raise the effective cost of crude for regional refiners sourcing from the Gulf. Brazil's power trader salaries — with top earners exceeding $1.2 million annually — reflect the premiums being attached to commodity price expertise in an era of sustained volatility. ---

Global

  • Brent crude crossed $102 per barrel, with Iran signaling readiness for escalated conflict and prediction markets pricing out near-term Strait of Hormuz resolution.
  • Global tanker freight rates hit records, with supertanker earnings reaching $800,000 per day on the Middle East-to-China route — nearly double historic norms.
  • A simple inflation model from Absolute Strategy Research suggests US headline CPI could reach 4.9% if gasoline holds at current levels for six months, a level that would likely compel Federal Reserve rate increases.

Oil and the Strait of Hormuz

The re-crossing of $100 per barrel by Brent crude is the single most consequential macro development globally this period. Iran's statement that it is prepared for a more intense war — and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz that prediction markets now view as unlikely to resolve before year-end — has compressed the range of benign scenarios available to energy markets. China's refiners, who had earlier restrained purchases when prices surged, are now buying actively from Russia to Argentina; analysts at the Singapore conference questioned whether Beijing retains either the willingness or the ability to restrain demand a second time. Sinopec projects Chinese oil consumption will fall 600,000 barrels per day this year — partly accelerating EV adoption — but this structural shift cannot offset a Hormuz-scale supply disruption.

Monetary Policy and Inflation Transmission

The oil shock creates a genuinely difficult policy environment for central banks globally. The ECB is raising rates today; Fed officials have signaled readiness to hike if inflation fails to improve. The risk — acknowledged explicitly by analysts at Absolute Strategy Research and TS Lombard — is that elevated energy prices are simultaneously a growth brake and an inflation accelerant, forcing central banks to tighten into a slowdown. Coal consumption is set to hit a record this year per the IEA, further complicating the inflation outlook. The AI investment cycle remains the principal counterweight in equity markets, sustaining earnings optimism despite the rising cost of capital — a tension that, as multiple analysts note, may not be fully appreciated by investors pricing the two dynamics in isolation.
