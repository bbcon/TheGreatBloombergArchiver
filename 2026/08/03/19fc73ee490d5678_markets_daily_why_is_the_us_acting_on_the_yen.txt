What’s at stake with the yen

The US action in bolstering the yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/yen-traders-brace-for-more-intervention-with-us-at-japan-s-side?cmpid=080326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7273>
 is a big deal. Direct and explicit American involvement in currency markets is 
rare outside of a crisis and carries a lot of sway.

The joint intervention was the first since 2011, and the first time the two 
countries worked to support the yen since the late 1990s.

The stakes are clear for Japan. The currency has been under pressure from 
rising oil prices, persistent budget deficits and a wide interest-rate gap with 
the US and other major economies. It’s causing a lot of pain at home, with 
businesses and consumers paying a steep price for imports.

Washington has a lot riding on the outcome too. Japan is the largest foreign 
holder of US Treasuries, and the easiest way to sell dollars and buy yen is to 
liquidate some of those bonds.

But the big risk is that could drive bond yields even higher, and the last 
thing Scott Bessent and Donald Trump want to see are higher borrowing costs. 
The 30-year yield is already at the highest since 2007,

That’s why Bessent is touting a facility at the Fed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/japan-s-use-of-fed-facility-may-ease-pressure-on-treasury-market?cmpid=080326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7273>
 that enables overseas central banks to use their Treasury holdings as 
collateral to access dollars, rather than sell the bonds on the open market.

“The prospect of a larger, more lasting allocation shift could pose a larger 
threat to Treasury yields,” said Rebecca Patterson at the Council on Foreign 
Relations. “It’s in Bessent’s interest to convince Japan not to take that step.”

The stronger yen also helps with one of Trump’s biggest bugbears, his 
contention that other nations are abusing the US in their trade relationships. 
A stronger yen will allow Japan to buy more American goods.

In another odd twist, the US sold some of its euros 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/us-uses-euros-to-buy-yen-to-avoid-weaker-dollar-strategists-say?cmpid=080326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260803&utm_content=7273>
