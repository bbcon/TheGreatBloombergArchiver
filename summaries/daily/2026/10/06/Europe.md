**Economic Data**
German factory orders delivered a sharper-than-expected decline in August, falling by the most since January. The data is difficult to dismiss as noise: it arrives as the Merz coalition struggles to agree on budget consolidation measures, with internal disputes over tax hikes threatening coalition stability. The combination of weak industrial momentum and fiscal paralysis is weighing on the euro area's largest economy. In France, the picture is similarly troubled — a selloff in French sovereign debt has accelerated beyond what markets anticipated, with spread widening reflecting concern over missed deficit targets, political gridlock, and now student-led social unrest that is broadening into a wider labor movement.

**Politics and Fiscal Policy**
Marine Le Pen's National Rally published a counter-budget Tuesday, targeting a fiscal deficit below 3% of GDP by 2032. The credibility of the savings identified remains contested, but the document forces a market assessment of an increasingly plausible 2027 electoral scenario. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Sánchez of Spain called a snap election for November 29 after parliament rejected a housing plan, adding a third major European political uncertainty to an already crowded calendar. The ECB faces a difficult backdrop: Le Pen called publicly for rate cuts to ease sovereign financing costs, while France's finance minister pushed back, and euro-area September inflation came in above consensus on Friday.

**Markets**
The Stoxx Europe 600 gained 1.0%, helped by falling oil prices and some stabilization in risk sentiment, even as French assets underperformed. Alstom rose 5.6% after securing a deal to supply 200 train sets to Hanoi's planned urban rail network.
