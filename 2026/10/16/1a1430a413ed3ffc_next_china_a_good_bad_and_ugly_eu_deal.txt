Hi, this is Allen Wan in Beijing.

China may have averted a bruising trade war with the EU — or not.

In a last-minute deal aimed at preventing harsher trade retaliation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-11/china-moves-to-stave-off-wider-eu-trade-war-with-hybrid-deal?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 from the 27-nation bloc dealing with a massive trade deficit with China, 
Beijing agreed to limit its hybrid car exports to the EU.

Beijing has yet to confirm the cap of 350,000 cars 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-11/eu-to-cap-chinese-hybrid-vehicle-imports-at-350-000-each-year?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 per year put forward by the EU side, or offer a full picture on the 
compromise, which is aimed at maintaining access to one of China’s biggest 
markets as well as protecting European automakerslosing market share 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/legacy-carmakers-seen-losing-mass-market-to-chinese-rivals?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
.

China hopes that the calculated one-time concession will shut down further 
threats of retaliation, or at the very least, kick the can down the road by 
extending talks — perhaps giving it enough time for its automakers to start 
producing en masse in Europe to avoid tariffs.

But the move could backfire given the EU may now want concessions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-and-eu-risk-new-trade-clash-over-what-comes-after-car-deal?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 in other industries. The deal has both positive and negative ramifications for 
China, which I have broken down into three categories —the good, the bad and 
the ugly — a nod to Sergio Leone’s classic spaghetti western film 
<https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20160210-the-lasting-legacy-of-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly>
.
A production line for plug-in hybrid sedans at a Geely plant in Shenyang,

The Good

Negotiations to renew the US trade truce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-27/us-china-trade-truce-leaves-key-issues-unresolved-video?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 are looming and exports are propping up the economy, so China can ill afford 
to wage another trade war. The EU is one of its top trading partners and the 
Asian country posted a record trade surplus with the bloc last month.

The hybrid-cars deal may have done just enough to avert a clash by showing 
Beijing’s willingness to compromise even when it has retaliatory tools at its 
disposal such as tightening rare earth curbs or imposing anti-dumping measures 
on European goods like liquor.

More compromises may now be possible. The EU is apparently in talks to remove 
trade restrictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/eu-set-to-lift-sanction-on-chinese-firm-to-save-asml-supplier?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 on an unnamed Chinese firm in exchange for relief from sanctions that 
threatened the survival of a small German company in ASML’s supply chain.

“If Europe perceived the negotiations as a failure, it could move them more 
closely to the US position,” said Martin Sebena, a lecturer at the University 
of Hong Kong. “Which is something China would want to avoid.”

The deal also represents an increase from last year’s shipments of 302,000 
units and the arrangement allows for 6% growth annually. The cap enables “
export space and steady growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/china-says-hybrid-car-deal-with-europe-to-ensure-export-space?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
” for China’s companies, the Commerce Ministry in Beijing said.

The pact, which has echoes of the US-Japan managed trade of the 1980s, may 
sacrifice only a tiny fraction of China’s more than $560 billion worth of 
exports to the bloc last year. According to Bloomberg Economics, it would shave 
about 1.5% off China’s overall sales to Europe and 0.2% from its global exports.

Another plus: Europe’s new curbs may offer local automakers some relief 
without stopping their Chinese rivals from expanding, according to Oxford 
Economics. The restrictions could accelerateChinese carmakers’ shift toward 
local production 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/china-s-byd-chery-expected-to-speed-up-europe-factories-to-evade-hybrid-limit?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
, which may be more palatable for the EU’s more hawkish policymakers if it 
involves more investment and hiring.

BYD, for instance, said it’s planning to go on a hiring spree in Europe and 
increase its workforce there to about 40,000 people by the end of the decade.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-s-trade-surplus-nears-940-billion-as-export-growth-surges?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
The Bad

The hybrid-cars cap may embolden the EU to seek more concessions from China 
despite some signs that’s not what Beijing plans. A social media account 
affiliated with China’s official broadcaster saidChina is prepared to act 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/china-state-media-warns-eu-against-new-tricks-after-car-deal?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 if the EU introduces new trade restrictions after reaching the new arrangement.

The problem lies in the divergence of views on what the deal means. For the 
Europeans, it is only the first step as trade asymmetry goes far beyond cars, 
according to the bloc’s economics chief, Valdis Dombrovskis.

The EU has also identified products such as furniture and scooters where 
surging Chinese imports threaten local manufacturers, and may impose similar 
limits on shipments in industries including chemicals and mechanical 
engineering.

The push is partly coming from Germany 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/germany-to-push-eu-to-impose-caps-on-additional-chinese-imports?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
, which has traditionally been more cautious given its export-driven economy 
and deep commercial ties with Beijing. It’s seeking EU safeguard measures to 
limit the import of goods from additional sectors, including potentially 
pharmaceuticals, according to people familiar with the matter.

The Ugly

The deal does little to ameliorate China’s trade surplus with Europe, which 
grew to a record $34.8 billion in September, about a fifth higher than the same 
month a year ago.

That increase creates ever more motivation for Europe to escalate, and for the 
bloc’s leaders to come back asking for concessions that go beyond cars. It 
suggests negotiators still have a lot of heavy lifting to do. But withits 
economy struggling 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-s-producer-prices-jolted-by-100-oil-as-inflation-rebounds?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
, making the hybrid pact a precedent may be a tough pill to swallow for China.

“Other governments may conclude that threatening restrictions can extract 
concessions,” said Sun Chenghao, a fellow at Tsinghua University’s Center for 
International Security and Strategy in Beijing. “China should take that 
political incentive seriously.”

Taking the Lead

Shenzhen, a bustling city of 18 million, feels at times like a vast robotics 
workshop where engineers rub shoulders with startup founders, component 
suppliers and electronics manufacturers.

Thanks to a deep supply chain that runs through the southern city, robotics 
has emerged as one of the strategic high-tech fields whereChina has seized an 
early lead over the US 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-13/china-s-robotic-hands-key-to-5-trillion-ai-humanoid-development-race?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
.

While Silicon Valley still controls the most advanced AI models 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/us-lead-in-ai-over-china-narrows-after-deepseek-gains-bi-says?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 and an abundance of top-tier engineering talent, Chinese companies accounted 
for 97% of global humanoid shipments in the first half of this year.

China is staking its claim to the humanoid industry that may help dictate the 
path of modern economies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-08/china-s-delivery-robots-are-learning-to-tackle-the-night-shift?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
. Morgan Stanley estimates the market for the devices could top $5 trillion by 
2050, as labor forces shrink and age, and AI adoption swells.
Technicians tune up robots before the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing in 
August.Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg
What’s unsettling Washington is not any single breakthrough, but the 
possibility that Beijing could end up writing the blueprint for how the 
machines enter daily life.

Kevin Dong, who builds algorithms for robots, traded a promising career in the 
US to work for an AI startup in Shenzhen that moves at “China speed” andbuilds 
at the China price <>.

It wasn’t always this exciting for Chinese robotics companies. When Beijing 
released its Made in China master plan in 2015, many were forced to use foreign 
suppliers for motors, reducers and sensors that could power their machines.

Just a few years later, an EV boom driven by BYD 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-11/ford-s-f-150-electric-truck-is-dead-and-more-popular-than-ever?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 and XPeng swept over Shenzhen, resulting in a mature supply chain that 
overlaps with humanoids. Robotics firms began to find the parts they were 
looking for on their doorstep.

Today, these suppliers are so vital they underpin all global production, 
including for American companies. An analysis by Morgan Stanley shows the cost 
of Tesla’s Optimus humanoid would nearly triple if Chinese components were 
excluded from its supply chain.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-13/why-china-wants-to-master-the-robotic-hand-video?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>

Experts say US dependence is now so great that a move by the Federal 
Communications Commission tocurtail the growth of Chinese humanoid robots 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 and some related materials has actually hindered development for many American 
startups because they struggle to get the components elsewhere.

China’s chokehold over rare earths 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-03/china-s-critical-minerals-project-vault-and-other-efforts-to-challenge-lead?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
 gives it a unique position to grow this dominance. Beijing controls up to 90% 
of permanent magnets needed for rotary actuators, according to research by 
McKinsey, a consulting firm.

The country also accounts for 40% of precision bearings, 40% of sensors and 
30% of all the power electronics used in the components, which act as joints 
for humanoids.

American robot companies and industry groups are now lobbying Washington for a 
national strategy to better compete with China.

One startup, Agility Robotics, recently urged the US government to provide 
something like the support that Beijing extends to its robotics sector, 
including the formation of an American supply chain.

“Without targeted US government action to support the domestic humanoid 
robotics industry, it appears likely that the gap between the US and China will 
grow,” says Sam Bresnick, a researcher at Georgetown University.

Behind the Great Firewall

A weekly look at the big water cooler news in China.

Besides making tea, this week Chagee showed it knows how to turn lemons into 
lemonade.

The tea chain’s abrupt announcement of a nationwide, one-day BOGO deal after 
tennis star Zheng Qinwen’s surprise win at the China Open turned into a 
debacle. Its app crashed due to heavy demand for the buy-one-get-one-free 
promotion and the company had to turn away customers hoping to take advantage 
of the offer.

Chagee has been celebrated for supporting Zheng, a brand ambassador for the 
company whom many fans call Queen Wen. It did so during some difficult times, 
especially after the former Olympic gold medalist and world No. 4 had elbow 
surgery last year and dropped to 160.

But it was also widely criticized for not better preparing for the BOGO 
onslaught.

Chagee announced the promotion after Zheng’s victory as a wildcard entry in 
the Beijing tournament on Sunday, saying it would be available between certain 
hours. That caused something of a stampede to its stores, with some customers 
waiting for an hour or more for their drinks. Photos of workers awash in paper 
cups went viral.
Zheng Qinwen at the award ceremony after her victory at the China Open on 
Sunday.Photographer: Lintao Zhang/Getty Images
“Betting big on Zheng Qinwen demonstrated good judgment, but failing to 
prepare a proper contingency plan means that, while I think the campaign was a 
very clever marketing move, it was still a rather basic mistake from a 
corporate management standpoint,” said Weibo user Internet Third Master, who 
has almost 1 million followers on the social media platform.

What was initially a marketing disaster turned into a win of sorts after 
Chagee, which bills itself asa purveyor of premium products 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-22/tea-chain-chagee-is-sticking-to-high-prices-despite-sales-plunge?cmpid=BBD101626_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10121>
, reportedly acknowledged its mistake and announced 13.5 million yuan ($2 
million) in rewards for its employees.

It also offered a coupon to customers who were unable to take advantage of the 
initial offer. This time, the company extended the deadline to Oct. 21, 
preventing another mad dash to stores.

“It’s truly commendable that the brand took customers’ perspectives 
seriously,” said Who Is A Ke, a tech blogger with 828,000 followers on Weibo. 
“Rather than shying away from problems, they addressed them head-on and 
reassured them with tangible benefits — such an approach is sure to win over 
loyal fans.”
