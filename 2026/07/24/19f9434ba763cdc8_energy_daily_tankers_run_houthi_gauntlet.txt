Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, Oil 
Strategist Julian Lee looks at how the escalation of the war in the Middle East 
has affected Saudi oil flows.

Threats to oil tankers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/houthis-open-new-front-in-iran-war-by-targeting-ships-in-red-sea?cmpid=BBBXT072426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6948>
 in the southern Red Sea from Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen appear to be 
slowing, but not stopping, Saudi Arabia’s crude shipments.

The Red Sea has become a crucial waterway for the kingdom since Tehran 
effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz in March. Riyadh boosted flows through 
its east-west pipeline to the terminal at Yanbu,keeping exports flowing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/saudi-crude-flow-from-red-sea-hit-record-before-houthi-warning?cmpid=BBBXT072426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6948>
 throughout the US-Iran war.

But that workaround has come under threat after the Houthis warned tankers not 
to call at the kingdom’s ports, then followed up on those threats with strikes 
on two Saudi-owned ships that had been delivering crude to the refinery at 
Jizan, close to the Yemeni border.

Several tankers that previously loaded at Yanbu paused their southbound 
voyages. At least one reversed course and headed north toward the Suez Canal, a 
route that nearlydoubles the distance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/asian-oil-buyers-in-talks-with-saudis-to-reroute-red-sea-flows?cmpid=BBBXT072426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6948>
 to China.

But after the initial disruption, activity appears to have resumed.

China- and Russia-linked oil tankers have been passing through the Bab 
el-Mandeb unhindered, despite the Houthi warning.

Two Chinese-owned very large crude carriers, both fully laden with about 2 
million barrels of Saudi crude,crossed the Bab el-Mandeb 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/china-tankers-head-to-red-sea-chokepoint-despite-houthi-attacks?cmpid=BBBXT072426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6948>
 chokepoint at the southern end of the Red Sea on Thursday afternoon, clearly 
showing their positions throughout their transit.

Tankers hauling Russian barrels through the Red Sea to India and China 
continue to make regular crossings in both directions, as they did throughout 
earlier Houthi attacks on shipping.

Separately, a smaller Aframax tanker, hauling about 700,000 barrels of Saudi 
crude to India, reappeared the same eveningentering the Arabian Sea 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/tanker-exits-red-sea-dark-as-another-china-ship-heads-to-strait?cmpid=BBBXT072426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6948>
, having last been seen on Sunday after loading at Yanbu.

Other ships have been more hesitant to run the Houthi gauntlet, with at least 
two VLCCs and two smaller tankers idling in the Red Sea, according to 
tanker-tracking data compiled by Bloomberg.

While activity at Yanbu doesn’t appear to have slowed, it has become more 
opaque. Tankers appear to be switching off their position signals as they 
approach the loading jetties.

Satellite images from the European Union’s Sentinel 2 orbiter from Wednesday 
show tankers moored at four of the seven crude berths across the two Yanbu 
terminals. Only two of those ships were visible on automated tracking systems.

Going forward, the situation in the Red Sea may mirror that in the Persian 
Gulf, with crude loading activities and chokepoint transits mostly hidden from 
view. That’s going to add more uncertainty to an already opaque global oil 
supply picture.

—Julian Lee, Bloomberg News

A global competition 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/europe-faces-winter-gas-reckoning-as-global-fight-for-lng-brews?cmpid=BBBXT072426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6948>
 for liquefied natural gas supplies threatens to unravel Europe’s strategy of 
delaying winter purchases until the Strait of Hormuz reopens. Asian buyers from 
China to Pakistan have been snapping up available shipments to replace lost 
Qatari supply, effectively pulling cargoes away from Europe. A key question is 
whether governments — particularly in the largest market Germany — will 
intervene to support purchases, potentially fueling a bidding war.

BNEF today

The latest support package for electric vehicles unveiled by the state 
government of Delhi in India will improve the economics of electric two- and 
three-wheelers relative to gasoline models. Combined with federal subsidies, 
the incentives could substantially narrow the upfront price gap between EVs and 
internal combustion engine two- and three-wheelers, particularly in segments 
where top speeds exceed 25 kilometers (15.5 miles) per hour where adoption has 
been comparatively slow, according to BloombergNEF.
