Daily Brief

Monday, August 31, 2026

US

  • Fed Chair Kevin Warsh reiterated at Jackson Hole that inflation is not moving "clearly and at sufficient speed" toward 2%, prompting swaps traders to price a September hike as more likely than not; Barclays and Societe Generale joined the hike camp.
  • The US merchandise-trade deficit widened 17.2% in July to $118.8 billion, the largest since March 2025, on the biggest jump in capital-goods imports since 1993 while exports fell 2.9%.
  • August payrolls, due Friday, are expected to show a 55,000 gain after July's surprise dip, with unemployment seen steady at 4.1%.
  • US and Iran exchanged strikes for the first time in a month, sending WTI up nearly 4% to $86.60 even as S&P 500 futures slipped 0.2% to 7,705.

Fed and Rates

Warsh's speech is the pivot point for markets heading into the September 16-17 FOMC meeting. He said summer inflation data "do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved" and confirmed the PCE gauge remains the Fed's operative target, closing off any suggestion policymakers could lean on a slower-moving measure. The Treasury curve flattened in response, the 2-year/10-year spread narrowing to its tightest since July 28 — a mirror image of the steepening that followed his more dovish July 29 press conference. Some investors remain unconvinced a hike follows through, arguing the rhetoric has outrun the data.

Trade and Markets

The widening deficit, alongside record capital-goods import growth, points to elevated corporate investment even as exports soften. Equity futures were little changed despite Iran-driven oil gains. Apple's transition proceeds against this backdrop, with Ternus taking over as CEO while Cook stays on to manage Trump administration and China relations.

Asia

  • China's official August factory PMI edged up, a hopeful signal even as the broader manufacturing rebound masks underlying risks to domestic demand.
  • The yen slid back through 160 per dollar, putting traders on intervention watch a month after Japan spent a record $96.4 billion, with US help, defending the currency; swaps now price a 90% chance of a BOJ hike in September.
  • South Korea's leveraged-ETF craze collapsed after new mandatory investor-education rules took effect August 19; assets in single-stock ETFs tied to Samsung and SK Hynix fell to $5 billion from an $11.4 billion peak.
  • Modi faces a harder balancing act between Washington and Moscow as Russian crude purchases hit a record 2.8 million barrels a day in July, even as the US threatens tariffs up to 100% on Russian oil buyers.

Currencies and Rates

The yen's slide has proven expensive to fight: it has surrendered more than half its July gains despite record intervention, as a stronger dollar and higher US rate expectations keep pushing the other way. Strategists see 161 and then 162-163 as levels where the risk of action rises. Bessent said he expects the BOJ to "do the right thing," fueling bets Tokyo moves faster on rates.

Markets and Politics

Korea's benchmark remains up 61% year-to-date but 25% below the record reached two months ago, with retail traders citing regulatory friction and a fading AI-trade narrative. South Korea named a career technocrat as finance minister in a surprise reshuffle, while Modi's meeting with Putin at the SCO summit underscores the strain Trump's tariffs place on India's strategic-autonomy posture.

Europe

  • Icelanders voted to reject reopening EU membership talks, with "no" winning 52.8% against 47.2% for "yes" — a larger margin than polls projected — and the government pledged to honor the result, a symbolic setback for the EU's enlargement push.
  • Euro-zone inflation is set to accelerate to its highest since 2023 on upcoming data, with German price growth also seen picking up.
  • A €40 billion succession battle at EssilorLuxottica's controlling shareholder Delfin deepened after heir Leonardo Maria Del Vecchio stepped down from management roles, with spillover risk to Delfin's stakes in Monte Paschi, Generali and UniCredit.
  • Chancellor Friedrich Merz warned that an AfD win in Sunday's Saxony-Anhalt election, as polls predict, would deter regional investment.

Politics

The Iceland result, driven by voter concern over fisheries control, reinforces resistance to EU enlargement momentum. In Germany, Merz's warning reflects coalition concern over far-right gains translating into economic costs at the state level. In Spain, Sánchez's Socialists are losing ground ahead of next year's election, per the first major poll since the Ceuta migration crisis, while UK PM Andy Burnham moved to block more serious offenders from early-release sentencing changes.

Corporate Italy

The Delfin governance crisis is becoming a distraction as EssilorLuxottica defends its early lead in AI smart glasses against Apple, Google and Samsung. Because Delfin also anchors stakes in Monte Paschi and Generali, the unresolved dispute among Del Vecchio's eight heirs carries implications reaching into Italy's banking and insurance sectors. Separately, Mercedes-Benz announced a €1 billion share buyback.

LatAm

  • Colombia's government formally ended talks with several armed rebel groups, halting the peace process begun under former President Gustavo Petro, days after authorities bombed a rebel position and ordered alleged drug traffickers extradited to the US.
  • Trump's plan to seize direct control of Venezuela's oil reserves is drawing warnings the country risks becoming a "modern-day resource colony," with economists flagging contract-repudiation risk for investors under any future government.
  • The EU-Mercosur trade deal, creating a free-trade zone of 780 million consumers, is stoking concern among some Mercosur producers about fiercer competition from European goods in their home markets.

Political Economy

President Abelardo de la Espriella's Trump-aligned administration issued a series of resolutions formally terminating negotiations with Colombia's rebel groups, a sharp reversal from the Petro-era peace process and one that follows a more confrontational posture toward armed groups, including recent bombing operations. In Venezuela, the prospect of direct US control over oil reserves has revived comparisons to early-20th-century resource extraction arrangements; University of Denver economist Francisco Rodríguez noted that investors must now weigh whether a future government would repudiate contracts signed under such an arrangement, a meaningful overhang for capital committed to the sector.

Trade

The EU-Mercosur agreement's implications remain two-sided for the region: it opens European market access for regional exporters but simultaneously exposes domestic producers to increased competition from European goods, a tension likely to shape the deal's ratification politics across member economies in the months ahead.

Global

  • The Bloomberg Agriculture Spot Index rose more than 13% in August, its steepest monthly gain since July 2012, as wars and weather disrupted wheat, sugar and cocoa supplies; wheat hit a three-year high after Black Sea port attacks curtailed shipments.
  • The US and Iran exchanged strikes for the first time in about a month, with American forces hitting Iranian rocket launchers and Iran firing missiles at US bases in Jordan, which intercepted eight; Brent rose more than 2% above $90.
  • Crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz hit a multi-month high before fighting resumed, but LNG traffic remains almost entirely halted since a Qatari tanker was attacked in early July, pushing spot gas prices near war-era highs even as Brent sits roughly 30% below its April peak.
  • G20 finance ministers convened in Asheville in what Bloomberg framed as a credibility test for Treasury Secretary Bessent's interventionist "economic statecraft," with early signals peers remain unconvinced.

Energy Markets

The oil-gas divergence is structural: LNG relies on specialized, often state-controlled tankers — roughly 850 vessels worldwide versus more than 7,000 crude carriers — making the fuel harder to reroute after an attack, and cargoes cannot be transferred ship-to-ship as crude can. Goldman estimates Gulf oil exports have recovered to 15-16 million barrels a day, still 7-8 million below pre-war levels but above a March trough, and more than doubled its diesel-margin forecast on tightening global refining capacity.

Geopolitics

The SCO summit in Bishkek, bringing together Xi, Putin, Modi and Iran's president, tests whether the bloc can translate its scale into collective action as a member remains under attack, with analysts expecting largely symbolic solidarity.
