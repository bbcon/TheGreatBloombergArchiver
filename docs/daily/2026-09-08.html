Daily Brief

Tuesday, September 08, 2026

US

  • August nonfarm payrolls came in well above forecasts, reinforcing expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike at next week's FOMC meeting; specific consensus figures were not provided, but the beat was described as meaningful.
  • US CPI data for August is due Friday — the single most consequential near-term data point for Fed policy, with core three-month inflation running at 3.05% through July.
  • S&P 500 futures slipped 0.2% as rising commodity prices reinforced rate-hike fears; the 10-year Treasury yield edged to 4.79%, near its highest level since before the Global Financial Crisis.

Labor Market and Fed Outlook

August payrolls surprised to the upside, pushing rate futures markets to price in a live hike at the September 16–17 FOMC meeting. Fed Governor Chris Waller provided some offset, noting that three-month core inflation has fallen steadily from 4.76% in February to 3.05% through July — still above the 2% target, but trending in an encouraging direction. Chairman Kevin Warsh struck a hawkish tone at Jackson Hole, and swaps markets now imply at least two Fed increases by mid-2027, though Deutsche Bank's Henry Allen argues markets remain meaningfully underpriced for the tightening ultimately required. Friday's CPI print will be decisive.

Bond Markets and Equities

The 10-year Treasury yield is consolidating near levels last seen before the Global Financial Crisis, though it remains below the 5% mark briefly reached three years ago. Analysts characterize the move as an orderly normalization of term premium rather than a disorderly shock. Forward earnings multiples on the S&P 500 have compressed to 19.4 from a peak of 23 last October, consistent with rising discount rates. Equity markets have absorbed this adjustment, supported by AI-driven earnings growth, though Deutsche Bank warns that if rate pressure persists, risk assets face a meaningful correction. A Novartis clinical-trial setback dragged biotech shares lower in premarket trading. ---

Asia

  • Japan's economy grew at a faster pace in the latest reading while wages jumped the most in nearly three decades, materially strengthening the case for a Bank of Japan rate hike next week.
  • China's August exports surged 25% year-on-year, setting the country on course for a trade surplus exceeding last year's record $1.2 trillion; the bilateral surplus with the US widened 44% to $29 billion, the widest since Trump's return to office.
  • The yen broke through 155 per dollar, triggering stop-loss orders and pushing the currency toward its strongest level of 2026; dollar-yen traded around 153–154 in Asian hours.

Japan: Growth, Wages, and BOJ

The combination of above-trend GDP growth and wage increases not seen in nearly three decades gives the Bank of Japan tangible cover to raise rates at its meeting next week. The yen's sharp appreciation — breaking a technically significant level and prompting stop-loss cascades — reflects markets pulling forward BOJ tightening expectations. Six-chart analyses circulating among traders point to Japanese bond yields also at multi-year highs, consistent with a broader global repricing of duration risk.

China Trade and BYD

China's export machine continues to generate friction ahead of the Xi-Trump summit later this month. August's $119.1 billion monthly surplus, 6% wider than July, reflects continued manufacturing dominance across the global value chain that Brussels and Washington view as structurally destabilizing. BYD separately raised its 2026 overseas sales guidance to 1.9–2.0 million vehicles from 1.5 million, with exports more than doubling in August to a record 43% of total deliveries. Australia's housing market deteriorated further, with Westpac now forecasting the RBA cash rate rising to 4.6% in November — a 15-year high — and HSBC widening its house-price decline forecast to 13%. ---

Europe

  • The ECB is expected to raise rates by 25 basis points on Thursday, per economist consensus, against a backdrop of French and German bond yields at 18- and 15-year highs respectively.
  • The AfD secured 44% of the vote in the Saxony-Anhalt state election, falling just short of an outright majority and delivering a significant political shock to Chancellor Friedrich Merz's coalition.
  • German exports unexpectedly fell in July, snapping five months of gains, while industrial production posted its sharpest monthly decline in nearly a year.

German Politics and Growth

The AfD's Saxony-Anhalt result is the most politically significant domestic event in Germany this cycle, though its direct economic implications remain limited given the state accounts for under 3% of national population. The more consequential risk is institutional: Morgan Stanley's chief European economist warned the result will slow the pace of pro-growth reforms and may intensify questions around coalition leadership, precisely as the Ifo institute raised its 2026 German GDP growth forecast to 1.4% — which would be the fastest non-pandemic expansion since 2017. Further state elections on September 20 will test whether the political damage spreads.

UK and France Fiscal Stress

Gilt yields are at their highest since 1998, with the UK facing a late-October budget under severe fiscal pressure. France faces analogous bond market stress before next year's presidential election, with French yields at 18-year highs. The UK's new Prime Minister Andy Burnham moved to ban trade with Israeli West Bank settlements, a gesture to the Labour base that risks complicating relations with Washington. Novartis shares fell 10% after a third clinical-trial setback in a week, dragging European healthcare stocks lower. ---

LatAm

  • Copper hit a fresh all-time high on the London Metal Exchange, with benchmark three-month LME futures reaching $14,533 per ton, providing a material tailwind to Chilean and Peruvian fiscal and external positions.
  • Flávio Bolsonaro has drawn level with President Lula in polling as corruption allegations involving Lula's son and a weakening economy erode the incumbent's standing ahead of 2027 elections.
  • Argentine President Milei escalated claims to the Falkland Islands following Trump's statement that the US is re-evaluating its historically neutral position on the archipelago.

Copper and Regional Markets

The AI infrastructure buildout's insatiable demand for copper, combined with constrained supply, has driven prices to records and materially improved the investment case for the region's mining exporters. Petrobras and Grupo Mexico together accounted for 40% of Latin American equity returns year-to-date through August. EM equity ETFs recorded $3.6 billion in net inflows in the first seven months of 2026, nearly double the full-year 2025 figure. A Brazilian court ordered a halt to mining at Sigma Lithium's Grota do Cirilo project, sending the stock down 11%, though the company stated operations remain unaffected pending appeal.

Brazil and Argentina Political Risk

Brazil's political landscape is deteriorating for Lula as the Supreme Court crisis — centered on Justice Alexandre de Moraes, who oversaw the case that imprisoned former President Jair Bolsonaro for an attempted coup — provides the right with fresh mobilization material. Colombia's inflation jumped more than expected to a two-year high, increasing pressure on Banco de la República to resume rate increases. Milei's Falklands gambit carries meaningful diplomatic risk, with a Chilean shipping company simultaneously preparing new maritime routes to the islands despite Santiago's nominal support for Argentina's sovereignty claim — illustrating the tensions within Trump's hemispheric alignment. ---

Global

  • Brent crude approached $100 per barrel after Saudi Arabia reported energy facility attacks linked to Houthi militants acting in response to Riyadh's siege of Sana'a; WTI futures gained 2.3% to $93.58.
  • A Qatari LNG tanker transited the Strait of Hormuz for the first time since July, while Iran signaled an imminent deal with Oman to manage Hormuz shipping — a development traders are treating cautiously given it could tighten Tehran's de facto control.
  • The Bloomberg Commodity Spot Index has surged over 15% since July, tightening monetary policy across commodity-importing economies while strengthening fiscal positions in exporting nations.

Oil, Hormuz, and Energy Markets

The Hormuz situation remains the central node of global macro uncertainty. The partial resumption of Qatari LNG flows provides modest relief, but European gas storage is tracking 67% full heading into winter — 14 percentage points below last year — with BNEF's base case assuming Hormuz reopens in early November. If that timeline slips, European inventories could fall to 21% by winter's end. Saudi facility attacks attributed to Houthi forces represent a further escalation, with meaningful tail risk to supply. Vitol's CEO noted that 9 of the 10 million daily barrels transiting Hormuz are crude, underscoring the chokepoint's systemic importance.

Global Bond Markets and Policy Divergence

The simultaneous rise of Treasury, Bund, gilt, and JGB yields reflects a structural repricing of the post-GFC era's suppressed term premiums, amplified by large fiscal deficits across major economies. Unusually, emerging market currencies have strengthened for 11 consecutive weeks — the longest streak since 2007 — even as the dollar fails to benefit from widening rate differentials in the normal fashion. High real rates, stronger EM fiscal positions, and commodity windfalls appear to be driving idiosyncratic resilience. Canada's escalation of tariffs on US goods to 15–50% on hundreds of products introduces a fresh vector of trade fragmentation, while the approaching Xi-Trump summit carries significant implications for whether the existing tariff truce extends or fractures.
