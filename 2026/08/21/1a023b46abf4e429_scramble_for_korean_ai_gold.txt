Korean memory giants Samsung and SK Hynix face intensifying pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/samsung-plans-as-much-as-79-billion-in-shareholder-returns?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 to share windfall profits from the AI boom.

In June they agreed to invest 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-28/samsung-sk-reportedly-to-invest-1-3-trillion-over-10-years?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 at least 1,350 trillion won ($880 billion) in chips and data centers as part 
of a multi-year government plan to bolster the country’s technological edge. 
This week they announced plans to return record amounts of cash to shareholders
to support 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/sk-hynix-announces-28-6-billion-share-buy-back-on-ai-boom?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 their share prices. Samsung alone expects to return up to $79 billion this 
year. On top, Korea plans to use additional tax revenue generated from the chip 
boom to fund future growth engines, including AI.

The companies have the funds: SK Hynix’s net cash 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/sk-hynix-announces-28-6-billion-share-buy-back-on-ai-boom?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 is estimated to have topped 100 trillion won. The scale of global demand for 
chips means such largesse isn’t a problem — for now.

Yet the size of these commitments, along with massive increases 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/sk-hynix-s-record-profit-misses-investors-lofty-ai-expectations?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 in capital expenditure — with SK Hynix earmarking at least $31 billion this 
year, up 50%, and Samsung planningto invest more 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-19/samsung-to-spend-73-billion-on-chip-expansion-research-in-2026?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 than $73 billion — reflect a boom being milked on all sides. If spending 
levels by AI firms start to fall, these companies may wish they had kept more 
in reserve.

Hedge Fund Stars Spark Race to Cut Taxes in Hong Kong, Singapore

On Wednesday evening, as most of Singapore’s workers headed home, several of 
its leading financiers crowded into a Sofitel Hotel ballroom. The guest of 
honor was Chee Hong Tat, the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s deputy chairman. 
The topic du jour: slashing the taxes paid by hedge fund stars and other 
managers.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/hedge-fund-stars-spark-race-to-cut-taxes-in-hong-kong-singapore?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 A Hong Kong court convicted two former organizers of the city’s annual 
Tiananmen Square vigils of inciting subversion, in a closely watched test of 
the national security law imposed by Beijing.

The Court of First Instance found Lee Cheuk-yan, 69, and Chow Hang-tung, 41, 
guilty on Friday over the Hong Kong Alliance’s longstanding call to “end 
one-party rule.” They face up to10 years in prison 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/hong-kong-convicts-tiananmen-vigil-leaders-of-subversion?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
. The sentences will be determined at a later date. Prosecutors had argued that 
the alliance’s advocacy sought to incite others to overthrow the leadership of 
China’s Communist Party through unlawful means.

India, the world’s second-biggest sugar producer, has allowed mills and 
refiners to import duty-free raw sugar in an effort to tame record local prices.

The government will allow imports of as much as 1 million tons of sugar until 
Oct. 31 free of any taxes, according to a notice from the commerce ministry. 
Inbound shipments earlier faced a levy of 100%. India’s move stands to tighten 
global supplies andmarks a rare step 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/india-allows-tax-free-sugar-import-in-rare-move-to-cool-prices?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8028>
 by the South Asian nation, which last imported the sweetener for domestic use 
almost a decade ago.
