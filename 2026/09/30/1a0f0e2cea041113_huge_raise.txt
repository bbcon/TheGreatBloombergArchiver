Deep Dive: Sydney Slump
Radford located in Sydney’s elite suburb of Point Piper.Source: Sotheby’s 
International Realty
A waterfront mansion in Sydney’s exclusive suburb of Point Piper just sold at 
a steep discount, underscoring thedramatic shift underway 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-29/a-75-million-point-piper-home-shows-housing-slump-hitting-sydney-s-elite?cmpid=093026_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260930&utm_content=9516>
 in Australia’s property market.

 * The 14,703-square-foot (1,366-square-meter) house known as Radford was put 
on the market in August by real estate tycoon Robert Magid and his wife Ruth 
for A$85 million ($59 million). This month, they slashed the asking price of 
the Wolseley Crescent property by 12% to A$75 million. It sold for about that 
level, Michael Pallier of Sydney Sotheby’s International Realty said late on 
Tuesday, declining to give the exact price.
 * Sydney’s wealthy are grappling with the prospect of falling house prices 
after decades of supercharged gains. Higher-value homes are leading declines, 
with values at the top end falling 10.7% in the past 12 months, compared with a 
citywide drop of 7%, according to property consultancy Cotality.
 * Homeowners are being squeezed from each side. The central bank hiked 
borrowing costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/australia-raises-key-rate-to-15-year-high-to-restrain-inflation?cmpid=093026_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260930&utm_content=9516>
 to 4.6% on Tuesday, the highest level since 2011 — and up sharply from 0.1% 
just over four years ago. In May, the government withdrew tax incentives that 
had long encouraged property investment. The one-two punch is raising concern 
about how the market will fare without structural drivers of the housing boom.
