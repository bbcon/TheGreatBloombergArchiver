Extreme weather trading

By Gautam Naik 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVyoWbluWhQ/gautam-naik?cmpid=BBD091726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9015>

A former Balyasny Asset Management trader who now runs a boutique hedge fund 
in London says he expects to be able toamplify returns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/ex-balyasny-trader-bets-el-nino-can-turbocharge-hedge-fund-gains?cmpid=BBD091726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9015>
 by anticipating how an evolving super El Niño will affect markets.

The cyclical climate phenomenon associated with warming across the equatorial 
Pacific Ocean is already reshaping weather patterns across the globe. Zulfiqar 
Ali, founder and chief investment officer of energy commodities trading hedge 
fund ZAMS Asset Management, says that as El Niño gets “stronger and stronger,” 
his fund “is positioned to take advantage of dislocations as they happen, with 
a quick turnaround.”
Zulfiqar AliSource: ZAMS Asset Management
More opportunities are likely to emerge toward the end of the year, when El 
Niño is expected to produce an “unusually alpha-rich environment,” he said. So 
far, the strategy helped ZAMS deliver its clients a 5.7% return in August and 
13% year-to-date.

The 34-year-old Cambridge University mathematics graduate says he and his team 
of half a dozen traders, quants and weather strategists have been analyzing 
energy futures contracts and weather models to find price anomalies. ZAMS buys 
climate models from specialist weather-forecasting companies like MetDesk, and 
then tries to improve on them using in-house expertise.

It’s an idea that’s gaining traction. Bloomberg reported in June that Moreton 
Capital Partners is targeting $500 million to bet on all the ways in which El 
Niño will upend food prices. At Man Group, the world’s largest publicly traded 
hedge fund firm, the assessment is that the full range of contagion risks 
associated with El Niño has yet to be priced in.

Germany’s renewable developers struggle

By Eva Brendel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AW_I5phUliY/eva-brendel?cmpid=BBD091726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9015>
 andLibby Cherry 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AU0ur5AUlUs/libby-cherry?cmpid=BBD091726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9015>

Germany’s renewable-energy developers are entering a shakeout after years of 
rapid expansion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/germany-s-green-power-boom-leaves-developers-fighting-to-survive?cmpid=BBD091726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260917&utm_content=9015>
, with worsening project economics pushing a growing number into financial 
distress.

Hamburg-based solar developer Enerparc AG, one of Europe’s largest solar park 
operators, filed for insolvency this month with around €3 billion ($3.5 
billion) of debt across the group. That follows the insolvency of wind 
developer Sowitec Group GmbH, while others including BayWa r.e. and ABO Energy 
have embarked on restructuring efforts.

The difficulties mark a reversal for an industry that expanded rapidly on 
cheap financing and supportive subsidies as Germany accelerated its transition 
away from fossil fuels. Developers now face higher borrowing costs and weaker 
returns on new projects — pressures that are also weighing on the industry 
across Europe.

Germany’s rapid buildout has created problems of its own. Wind and solar 
capacity has expanded faster than the power grid and energy storage needed to 
accommodate it, making some new projects harder to connect. And when renewable 
generation surges, the glut of electricity can push prices below zero or force 
producers to curb output, undermining the economics of both new and existing 
projects.
