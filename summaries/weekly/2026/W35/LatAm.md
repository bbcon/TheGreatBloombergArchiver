**Fiscal Strain and Sovereign Credit**
Mexico's Pemex rescue has repriced sovereign risk in ways the bailout itself did not resolve. Investors remain skeptical of the broader fiscal trajectory even as President Sheinbaum pursues a bilateral trade accommodation with Washington; her chief negotiator continued talks in the US this week, and Mexican officials expressed optimism on relief for autos and steel. The contrast with Canada — whose parallel trade negotiations collapsed entirely — illustrates how differently the Trump administration is engaging its two NAFTA partners.

**China's Deepening Regional Foothold**
Ecuador's President Noboa, one of Trump's closest regional allies, concluded a weeklong China trip securing support including an AI-powered meteorological system, underscoring that political alignment with Washington does not translate into economic decoupling from Beijing. Chinese firms are increasingly working through governors, mayors, and local business groups rather than national governments, expanding into critical minerals, infrastructure, EVs, and financial services in areas the Trump administration largely shuns.

**Political Economy**
Colombia's rightward turn under President De la Espriella — including the country's first major immigration crackdown targeting hundreds of thousands of Venezuelans — has coincided with a currency rally that is rewarding early holders of local debt such as Pimco, which accumulated roughly 41 trillion pesos in Colombian government bonds during the Petro era. Brazil's October presidential race is being shaped by AI-generated influencer personas targeting Lula and Bolsonaro supporters, prompting the electoral court to tighten disclosure rules for synthetic political content. Venezuela's prospective OPEC exit and deepening US oil ties would further fragment the cartel's cohesion as a price-setting body.
