While the US and Canada’s fledgling tariff war 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/canada-set-to-hit-back-at-trump-tariffs-risking-wider-trade-war?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>
 dominates the economic headlines, another equally big development is adding 
fuel to concerns about the stability of the global trading system: China’s 
widening trade surplus.

Chinese exports in August topped imports by $119.1 billion, a gap that was 6% 
wider than it was in July, data showed Tuesday. (Read the full storyhere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/china-export-growth-rebounds-as-ai-boom-offsets-extreme-weather?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>
.)

So after posting a record $1.2 trillion surplus last year — and ringing alarm 
bells among policy makers in Washington and Brussels — China is on pace for an 
even bigger imbalance in 2026.

Related: Chinese Manufacturing Gauge Clocks Longest Upswing in Five Years 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/china-manufacturing-gauge-surprises-with-faster-august-expansion?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>

“The odds are rising we’ll see China’s trade surplus hit a new record high 
this year, despite the major pickup of imports,” Lynn Song, chief economist for 
Greater China at ING Bank, told my Bloomberg News colleagues.

China’s trade surplus with the US surged almost 44% from a year earlier to 
more than $29 billion — the widest gap since Donald Trump returned to the White 
House in January 2025.

Trump is supposed to meet with his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping later this 
month to discuss plans to extend atariff truce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/us-eyes-china-overcapacity-tariffs-of-7-5-before-xi-trump-talks?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>
.

A separate report Tuesday showed German exports unexpectedly dropped 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/german-exports-unexpectedly-drop-for-first-time-since-january?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>
 in July, snapping five months of gains and muting hopes for a quick recovery 
in Europe’s largest economy.

EU officials are increasingly worried about an influx of Chinese competition 
hurting domestic manufacturers,especially automakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/vw-board-gives-ceo-blume-unanimous-backing-to-overhaul-carmaker?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>
 likeVolkswagen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/vw-weighs-selling-motorcycle-maker-ducati-in-review-of-holdings?cmpid=BBD090826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260908&utm_content=8624>
