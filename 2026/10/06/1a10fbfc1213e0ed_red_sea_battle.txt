Deep Dive: Under Pressure From Ebola Outbreak

Work starts around 5:30 a.m. for Abdou Sebushishe. Sebushishe leads the 
frontline emergency operations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-06/drc-ebola-outbreak-virus-spreads-as-health-workers-battle-exhaustion?cmpid=100626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9748>
 for the International Medical Corps with his teams scattered across eastern 
Democratic Republic of Congo who are working on the response to the country’s 
Ebola outbreak. By 8 a.m., he finds himself in Bunia checking in on patients, 
staff and supplies with a cluster of meeting and operational problems to follow 
until his 9 p.m. meal which tends to be his only meal of the day.

 * Sebushishe’s long days are representative of one of the biggest problems to 
Congo’s Ebola response with such a limited number of experienced people to 
tackle the issue. “The numbers that you are seeing being reported every day, 
these are real people,” he said.
 * Twenty weeks into the outbreak, health workers have managed to tamp down 
some of the early hotspots.But the virus is still spreading 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/health-worker-contracts-ebola-in-congo-evacuated-to-netherlands?cmpid=100626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9748>
. What began in three health zones in one province has expanded to 64 across 
seven provinces.
 * The breakout is putting further strain on a humanitarian response weakened 
by major aid cuts. The longer responders take to get ahead of the virus, the 
higher the likelihood that Ebola crosses Congo’s borders
