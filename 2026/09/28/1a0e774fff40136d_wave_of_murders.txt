For a president who came to power promising to clean up South Africa, there’s 
an unwelcome familiarity to the latest headlines for Cyril Ramaphosa.

Eleven women’s bodies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-26/runner-s-murder-in-south-africa-reignites-push-to-stop-femicide?cmpid=BBD092826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9377>
 have been discovered in and around Ekurhuleni in eastern Johannesburg over the 
past two months.

Two separate mass shootings over the weekend left 28 people dead near 
Johannesburg and Cape Town. The assaults came days after 11 people were killed 
at a house in the port city of Durban.
A portrait of Elizabeth Moselakgomo, one of the women murdered, during her 
funeral service in Mpumalanga on Sept. 20.Photographer: Emmanuel 
Croset/AFP/Getty Images
The multiple deaths put Ramaphosa under renewed pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/ramaphosa-seeks-to-overturn-cash-in-sofa-report-in-high-court?cmpid=BBD092826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9377>
 to demonstrate that his government can protect South Africans from the kind of 
violent crime that has long scarred the country.

After the Ekurhuleni killings, the president said that the investigations would
leave no stone unturned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/ninth-woman-found-dead-as-police-probe-johannesburg-killings?cmpid=BBD092826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9377>
. He’s promised the public will be kept up to date and that the government will 
soon set out its response to gender-based violence.

It’s only in November that gender-based violence and femicide were classified 
as a national disaster, giving the government primary responsibility for 
coordinating the response. Then this year, Ramaphosa appointed members of a 
statutory National Council on Gender-Based Violence and Femicide.

Yet the bodies keep turning up and women are anxious to leave their homes.

Police statistics show 569 women were murdered between April and June, an 
average of more than six a day. That doesn’t include the Ekurhuleni killings.

Separately, the government has deployed 2,200 soldiers to assist police 
operations against illegal mining and gangsterism in five provinces.

The issue of crime is now colliding with politics.

South Africans will vote in municipal elections 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/south-african-poll-shows-anc-leading-in-johannesburg-before-vote?cmpid=BBD092826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9377>
 on Nov. 4, putting local policing, street lighting and basic services squarely 
back into the political conversation.

For Ramaphosa, the challenge is no longer simply pledging to get tough on 
crime.

He needs to persuade a country used to hearing such promises that, this time, 
something will actually change.— S’thembile Cele 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AU8puCGvjMA/sthembile-cele?sref=LlL4jr17&cmpid=BBD092826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9377>

launched the biggest raid for back taxes on local companies in years and set 
their sights on the more than $1.7 trillion of wealth that Chinese people hold 
in Hong Kong alone. They’ve also formed a series of special squads dedicated to 
tracking and taxing the rich, with some of these units focused on just one 
billionaire, sources say.

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and his Workers’ Party have long dominated northeast 
Brazil, a region home to more than a quarter of the country’s voters. Yet there 
are signs that support is weakening, partly because Lula and the left have 
struggled to meet the modern demands of Brazilians they once helped lift out of 
poverty. That points to a risk for Lula, who is locked in a tight race with 
Flávio Bolsonaro for Brazil’s presidency in Sunday’s elections. Read more about
