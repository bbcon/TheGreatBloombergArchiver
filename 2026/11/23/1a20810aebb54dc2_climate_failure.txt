The future of international efforts to combat climate change is in doubt after 
a public spat over its primary cause.

Two weeks of UN climate negotiations, dubbed COP31, in the Turkish resort city 
of Antalya descended into chaos yesterday after dozens of countries rejected a 
deal theysaw as pandering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-22/eu-rejected-un-climate-deal-due-to-backsliding-on-fossil-fuels?cmpid=BBD112326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11730>
 to the fossil fuel industry.

It would have omitted all references to the need to transition away from oil, 
gas and coal, while pledging to pursue the use of carbon capture and storage — 
a climate solution favored by crude and gas producers that has done little to 
pare planet-warming pollution.

“We will not accept a COP that greenwashes fossil fuel,” says Darragh O’Brien, 
the climate minister for Ireland, which holds the rotating presidency of the 
European Union.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-11-23/climate-talks-collapse-on-disputes-over-fossil-fuel-video?cmpid=BBD112326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11730>

The rejection leaves the summit’s Turkish hosts red-faced and fundamental 
doubts over whether the UN’s consensus model is able to address the root cause 
of a problem that’s leading to extreme weather events hitting harder and more 
often than scientific models had predicted. Multilateralism has beendealt its 
latest blow 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/failure-of-cop31-talks-shows-hard-limits-of-climate-cooperation?cmpid=BBD112326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11730>
.

All the glitz and headline-grabbing announcements couldn’t hide the fact that 
theglobal divide 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-11-22/cop31-climate-summit-fails-when-it-can-t-even-say-fossil-fuels?cmpid=BBD112326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11730>
 over combating climate change is growing larger.

Delegates blamed interference 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/nations-blast-fossil-fuel-sector-s-influence-on-un-climate-deal?cmpid=BBD112326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11730>
 from Saudi Arabia for failing to get stronger references to fossil fuels in 
the final decision, even after a landmark pledge made three years ago at COP28 
in Dubai to make the shift away from them.

The US, the world’s largest economy, is no longer a participant after 
President Donald Trump pulled Washington out of the Paris Agreement.

That accord committed nearly 200 countries to keeping global warming ideally 
below 1.5C, yet breaching the threshold isnow inevitable 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/un-says-global-warming-will-cross-1-5c-soon-hit-at-least-1-8c?cmpid=BBD112326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11730>
.
