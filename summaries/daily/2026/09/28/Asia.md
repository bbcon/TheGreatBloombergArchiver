**Trade and Economic Data**
The most tangible deliverable from last week's Trump-Xi summit — a mutual tariff-reduction list covering roughly $60 billion in bilateral trade — disappointed commodities markets. China's list of 1,600-plus products eligible for reduced duties includes grains, meat, seafood, coal, and medical equipment, but notably excludes soybeans beyond seed varieties. Grain futures fell across Chicago, reflecting trader skepticism that Chinese state-backed purchases will be replicated by private buyers. China's August industrial profit data, rising only 4.2% year-on-year, underscores that the AI-and-commodities-driven recovery has yet to transmit into broad domestic demand strength.

**Markets and Corporate**
GoTo shares tumbled in Jakarta after Indonesia's stock exchange removed the 50-rupiah price floor, releasing pent-up selling pressure. Australian gold miner Northern Star rejected a A$38.7 billion ($27.1 billion) takeover approach from Gold Fields. A former Bank of Japan official indicated an October rate hike remains a genuine possibility, a signal markets will watch closely given the BOJ's recent policy normalization trajectory.
