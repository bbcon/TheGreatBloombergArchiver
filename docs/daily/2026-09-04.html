Daily Brief

Friday, September 04, 2026

US

  • August payrolls data due today; equity futures held steady ahead of the print, with traders assessing whether the report would give the Fed sufficient cause to hold rates.
  • Fed Governor Waller signaled his next rate decision will be "heavily influenced" by August CPI data due next week; remarks pared market bets on an imminent hike.
  • US construction spending fell for the 12th consecutive month on a year-on-year basis, down nearly 5% from its February 2025 peak — a soft-side surprise that challenges the AI stimulus narrative.
  • High-grade corporate bond yields rose above 5.5%, a two-year high; 30-year mortgage rates approaching 7% as Treasury selloff extends.

Macro Data

July construction spending marked the 12th straight year-on-year decline, with the pace of contraction the steepest since the end of the 2006 housing boom when data-center activity is excluded. Data centers account for only 3.5% of total construction spending and carry significantly lower labor intensity than residential building, undermining the administration's argument that AI infrastructure investment will serve as a broad blue-collar stimulus. Manufacturing construction is down roughly 21% year-on-year. Thirty-year mortgage rates are creeping back toward 7%, perpetuating the residential downturn.

Monetary Policy

Fed Governor Waller struck a cautiously hawkish tone in prepared remarks before tempering his language in Q&A, saying he expects next week's August CPI print to come in at a "reasonable" level. Markets interpreted the comments as reducing — though not eliminating — the probability of a September rate increase. The signal reinforced a broader dynamic in which Fed communication is becoming a key near-term volatility driver.

Markets

S&P 500 futures were modestly firmer ahead of the jobs report. High-grade corporate yields above 5.5% are incentivizing front-loaded debt issuance. Lululemon plunged 18% in premarket trading after cutting its profit outlook for a second consecutive quarter. ---

Asia

  • Yen carry-trade unwinding drove the currency to a one-month high against the dollar; dollar-yen fell to ¥156.37, with markets pricing increasing probability of further Bank of Japan rate increases.
  • Shein's Hong Kong IPO raised $1.7 billion at a $26 billion valuation — well below its $100 billion 2022 peak — with shares declining in subsequent sessions after a volatile debut.
  • South Korea began preparations to deploy a naval support vessel and approximately 150 troops to the Strait of Hormuz, following US pressure over Seoul's limited support for the Iran conflict.

Currencies and Capital Flows

The yen's move to a one-month high reflects accelerating unwinding of carry positions funded in Japanese currency, as Nomura analysts outlined a scenario in which the Bank of Japan could raise rates at three consecutive meetings through December. Japan's Government Pension Investment Fund is also facing growing market speculation that it will rebalance toward domestic bonds to support the yen, which could indirectly reduce overseas debt exposure. The broader implication — marginal reduction in foreign demand for US Treasuries — warrants monitoring given Japan's $1.2 trillion stockpile.

Corporate and Markets

Shein's subdued Hong Kong listing reflects investor preference for AI- and robotics-linked Chinese equities over e-commerce names facing rising trade barriers and margin pressure. The company posted a first-quarter loss of $99 million versus a $395 million profit a year earlier, and comparable China sales fell 8% in the most recent quarter. Hong Kong IPO proceeds for 2026 have nonetheless surpassed $45 billion, on pace for a record year. Lululemon's 2% revenue decline in mainland China in Q2 — the first such decline since the company began reporting the segment — adds to evidence of softening consumer demand in that market. ---

Europe

  • Volkswagen's supervisory board approved a restructuring plan cutting 50,000 additional jobs and reducing model count and manufacturing footprint, the most significant restructuring in the company's modern history.
  • Economists surveyed expect the ECB to raise rates at next week's meeting, though the consensus view is more dovish than market pricing, anticipating no further increases beyond that point.
  • Germany's factory orders advanced more than expected for a third consecutive month, a tentative sign that Europe's largest economy may be regaining traction.

Corporate Restructuring

VW's board approval gives CEO Oliver Blume his clearest mandate yet to reshape the company, which faces simultaneous pressure from Chinese EV competition, softening European demand, and an elevated cost base. Shares rallied 6.4% in Frankfurt on the news, with Porsche SE gaining 3.4%, suggesting markets view the restructuring as necessary and overdue rather than a signal of distress. The scale of the job reductions — affecting tens of thousands of workers across Germany — raises obvious labor-relations and political sensitivities, particularly ahead of the Saxony-Anhalt regional election, where the AfD holds a wide polling lead.

Climate and Political Economy

Europe recorded an estimated 32,600 heat-related deaths this summer across the region, with France entering its fifth heat wave of the season. The UK Met Office has signaled that 2027 could rival 2024 as the hottest year on record if El Niño intensifies as forecast. Governments are beginning to respond — Spain announced additional climate-shelter funding, Germany's environment minister is pursuing constitutional changes on climate adaptation — but hospital infrastructure upgrades and broader structural reforms are unlikely to materialize before next summer. Italy's Meloni government continues to demonstrate political durability, though the emergence of Roberto Vannacci as a far-right challenger indicates the populist space she vacated is being contested. ---

LatAm

  • Argentina's President Milei escalated the Falkland Islands dispute with the UK, announcing accelerated sanctions against firms operating in the disputed area without Buenos Aires' approval — a move that may complicate future investment ties.
  • Brazil's Senate delayed Lula's flagship proposal to reduce the working week without a pay cut, a legislative setback as the president enters a tight October election campaign in a virtual polling tie with Flávio Bolsonaro.
  • Venezuela is emerging as a geopolitical flashpoint, with the Trump administration framing US control of Venezuelan oil output as a mechanism to exclude China and Russia from the hemisphere.

Brazil Political Economy

The Senate delay on Lula's workweek bill removes a potential pre-election boost for the incumbent and signals difficulty in converting populist policy proposals into legislative wins. Brazil's Planning Minister has argued that Lula can improve the fiscal position without deep spending cuts if reelected, though that framing requires sustained growth and revenue performance. Polling showing a virtual tie with Bolsonaro raises the probability of a contested second round.

Argentina and Regional Dynamics

Milei's Falklands posturing appears partly driven by domestic political calculation — his approval ratings have declined — rather than strategic foreign policy. Accelerating sanctions against energy companies in the disputed zone introduces new uncertainty for any potential hydrocarbon development in the region. Venezuela's trajectory as a Trump administration priority — with US Energy Secretary Wright explicitly stating China will be excluded from Venezuelan oil revenue — adds a great-power dimension to what had been a primarily regional political story. ---

Global

  • Brent crude rose above $95 per barrel, on track for its largest weekly gain since July, as renewed US-Iran hostilities and Israeli signals of potential resumed fighting raised concerns about sustained Strait of Hormuz disruption; Brent is up approximately 60% year-to-date.
  • Norway's $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund proposed cutting government bond allocations to 50% of its fixed-income portfolio from 70%, implying a roughly $75 billion reduction in US Treasury holdings — the latest signal of marginal but accumulating pressure on foreign demand for US debt.
  • Global money managers including Amundi rebuilt gold positions, with Amundi targeting $5,000/oz by year-end; spot gold traded near $4,471.

Energy and Geopolitics

The renewed flare-up in US-Iran hostilities is the primary driver of the week's oil move, with Brent's approximately 7% weekly gain reflecting market concern that Strait of Hormuz shipping disruptions could persist or intensify. US retail diesel has risen to a record, introducing a new channel through which the conflict feeds into domestic inflation — directly relevant to the Fed's policy calculus. Vice President Vance's effort to characterize the conflict as something other than a war, despite ongoing military strikes, creates a communication tension that adds uncertainty to any deescalation timeline.

Treasury Market and Sovereign Demand

The Norway sovereign wealth fund's proposal crystallizes a broader trend: marginal but real erosion in institutional appetite for US Treasuries. Foreign holdings have fallen in three of the past four months from a February record high. Dutch pension ABP reduced exposure significantly through 2025; Turkey offloaded most of its holdings in March. Meanwhile, Japan's GPIF may shift incrementally toward domestic bonds. These moves remain individually modest relative to the $40 trillion Treasury market, but they coincide with a structural increase in US issuance needs driven by large fiscal deficits, creating a supply-demand dynamic that may sustain upward pressure on yields independent of Fed policy.
