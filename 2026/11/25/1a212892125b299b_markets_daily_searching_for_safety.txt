Bonds shine

In the wake of the tech selloff, investors are racing to the safety of 
Treasuries.

Ten-year Treasury yields have fallen to about 4.85% — despite the risk of 
higher interest rates as inflation still runs above the Fed’s target. 
(Meanwhile, gold is acting like the opposite of a haven, with prices sinking 
closer to $3,500 an ounce.)

“If you want to de-risk your portfolios, Treasuries are quite a natural place 
for investors to park cash,” said Mark Preskett at Morningstar Wealth. “Yields 
are pretty attractive at these levels, even in this inflation environment.”

One scenario that’s being talked 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/bond-traders-rush-to-hedge-risk-of-fed-hikes-stalling-economy?cmpid=112526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11842>
 about among bond traders is that the Fed may end up overshooting, causing an 
economic slowdown as the tighter monetary policy takes effect. While it’s still 
seen as unlikely, the discussion goes some way to explaining recent moves in 
the market.

The theory among some traders works like this: The Fed hikes and keeps rates 
high in an attempt to bring down inflation, and a tumbling stock market adds to 
pressure on the economy. In that environment of slowing growth, the Fed could 
be forced to shift back to cutting rates as soon as late 2027.

Over the past week, the possibility that growth will stall has driven a surge 
in open interest — or new risk — in call options that wager the outlook will 
shift significantly over the next few months. The positions will pay off if 
markets start bracing for the Fed to cut rates to as low as 3% by early 2028.

The latest read on inflation comes later today, and could help investors gauge 
how much the Fed is likely to hike. Its preferred inflation gauge, the personal 
consumption expenditures price index, is projected to rise 3.8% from last year, 
roughly double the goal.

Some think that rate hikes would be a mistake. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent
has warned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-22/bessent-warns-fed-risks-overtightening-as-us-markets-swoon?cmpid=112526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11842>
 that the Fed risks jeopardizing the US economy by overtightening. Billionaire 
Bill Ackman suggests the 2% inflation target is “misguided” and that 3% is 
“more realistic.” —Edward Bolingbroke 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS7t6j42_Cw/edward-bolingbroke?cmpid=112526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11842>

Too slow

Not even quant funds can keep up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-24/quant-funds-are-having-a-hard-time-keeping-up-with-this-market?cmpid=112526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11842>
 with the quick-shifting market narrative.

One group of fast-money traders — commodity-trading advisors — are expected to 
unload $68 billion worth of global stocks in the next five days, according to 
Goldman Sachs.CTAs cue off price trends rather than fundamentals, and once they 
start selling, a feedback loop can take hold.

CTAs are short both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100, with positioning sitting in 
the bottom-fourth percentile of readings going back a year ago, per Barclays.

“With the Nasdaq 100 seeing moves of 3% to 6% over each of the past four days, 
usually in different directions, it makes it almost impossible for fast money 
managers to keep up,” said Matt Maley of Miller Tabak + Co.—Natalia Kniazhevich 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOpcrZfmo/natalia-kniazhevich?cmpid=112526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261125&utm_content=11842>
