**Energy Crisis and Economic Competitiveness**

Europe's energy situation has deteriorated materially. With natural gas prices up roughly 200% since February, diesel at a record, and kerosene prices nearly doubled — all driven by Iran war disruptions to Strait of Hormuz LNG transit — European industry faces a second major energy shock within five years. Wood Mackenzie estimates industrial gas consumption has already been cut by roughly one-fifth since 2022, leaving limited further demand-reduction capacity without triggering recessionary output losses. Spain has spent €12 billion since March subsidizing household energy bills ahead of a November 29 election; Poland's fertilizer industry is appealing for government relief; a German porcelain factory that survived Nazi and Communist rule is shutting after 182 years. Orlen's exit from its Qatari LNG contract — Poland's oldest such arrangement — marks the first formal contract termination resulting from QatarEnergy's sustained force majeure declarations since March, and US LNG with its Henry Hub-linked pricing and cargo flexibility is the likely beneficiary as buyers reassess geographic concentration risk. With more than half of European citizens voting in elections over the next twelve months, the political ramifications of sustained energy-cost pain appear significant.

**UK Economic Momentum and Politics**

Wednesday's UK GDP print — the strongest in two and a half years — provides a constructive counterpoint to the energy narrative. Business investment tied to AI infrastructure appears to be a material growth driver, though the divergence from the RICS housing survey pointing to its softest reading since 2023 warrants monitoring. Bailey's remarks on trade barriers carry an implicit Brexit dimension, framing expanded EU market access as a macroeconomic rather than merely political question. Prime Minister Burnham attended COP31 in Antalya as the only G7 leader present and is pursuing EU-UK summit commitments that include a reportedly near-finalized emissions market linking deal. Germany's coalition cleared its 2027 budget by deferring new taxes rather than cutting spending, while the Dutch coalition faced potential collapse over a wealth-tax amendment — a source of European political uncertainty that has not yet produced material asset-price moves. Bloomberg reporting revealed that Russian-seized subsidiaries of Rockwool, AkzoNobel, Nestlé, and Japan Tobacco have been channeling billions of rubles to Putin's war effort via the People's Front, with European and Japanese firms disproportionately affected as Moscow pursues warmer ties with Washington.
