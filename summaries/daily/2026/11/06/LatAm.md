• Pope Leo XIV, the first American pontiff, began an 11-day, 19,000-mile tour of Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru today, entering a politically charged regional environment.
• Paraguay's President Santiago Peña submitted his resignation following the collapse of a major commercial bank with government ties, though he vowed to contest a Senate removal vote to preserve potential immunity from criminal prosecution.
• Brazil's President Lula defeated MAGA-aligned Flávio Bolsonaro, halting a broader regional conservative sweep and reshaping the political landscape ahead of Pope Leo's visit.

**Political Economy**
Paraguay's banking crisis represents the most acute near-term institutional stress in the region. The collapse of a systemically connected commercial institution — and the resulting political crisis — highlights the fragility of governance structures in smaller economies with concentrated financial systems. The legal distinction between resignation and removal carries direct consequences for Peña's criminal exposure, making the Senate process a high-stakes proceeding. Meanwhile, Brazil's election outcome reinforces a bifurcated regional political map: right-leaning governments in Argentina and Peru are aligning closely with Washington, while Lula's Brazil maintains an independent posture. Emerging market external debt reached a record $9.4 trillion in 2025 per World Bank data, with lower-income nations paying average rates of 8.2–9.4% on new borrowing — a structural constraint on regional fiscal space.

**Pope Leo's Tour**
The pontiff's Latin American visit carries geopolitical as well as pastoral dimensions. His public stance against the Iran war positioned him in implicit opposition to Trump's foreign policy during a period when Washington is actively seeking to deepen influence in the region. Both Fujimori and Milei have welcomed his arrival, though their immigration and foreign policy positions sit in tension with Leo's stated priorities.
