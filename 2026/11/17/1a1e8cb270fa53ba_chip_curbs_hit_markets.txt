Xi-Trump-Putin Has World Worried About Great Power Stitch-Up

At Yalta in 1945, the leaders of three dominant military powers gathered to 
craft a plan that would bring stability to the world – or conspired to carve it 
up among themselves. Both readings of that famous World War II meeting are 
still alive and well, eight decades later. And both play into parallels getting 
drawn ahead of this week’s summit in Shenzhen, where Chinese President Xi 
Jinping is due to host his US and Russian counterparts, Donald Trump and 
Vladimir Putin.

HSBC sold a roughly $300 million piece of a loan to build a troubled New 
Mexico data center project 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/hsbc-sold-300-million-troubled-data-center-loan-at-discount?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11508>
 at a steep discount, underscoring deepening anxiety surrounding the AI boom. 
The bank sold the slice of an $18 billion financing package — which funds 
construction of the campus in Doña Ana County tied to Oracle — at prices in the 
mid-80s, according to people familiar with the matter. Lenders are weighing 
their exposure to AI debt alongside renewed regulatory pushback following a 
Democratic sweep in US midterm elections, while Jensen Huang’s abrupt departure 
from Nvidia, a linchpin of AI’s rapid expansion, has also triggered concerns 
about the pace of AI growth.

Pimco says spreads on bonds financing the AI build-out may tighten as a 
slowdown in data-center construction curbs issuance,easing some of the pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/pimco-says-ai-bond-spreads-may-tighten-as-data-center-boom-slows?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261117&utm_content=11508>
 that a flood of AI-linked debt has put on credit markets. The call marks a 
shift from earlier concerns that AI borrowing was overwhelming investor demand 
and driving up financing costs. The same Democratic surge has since revived 
doubts over the payoff from massive AI investments, and Pimco expects some of 
that spending to be cut or pushed out.
