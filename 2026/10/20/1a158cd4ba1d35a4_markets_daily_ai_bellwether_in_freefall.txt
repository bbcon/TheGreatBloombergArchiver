‘Fear and pessimism’

For a look at just how skeptical investors are about the staying power of the 
AI frenzy these days,consider the South Korean stock market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/chipmakers-lead-korean-stock-selloff-on-yield-spike-ipo-stumble?cmpid=102026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10272>
.

It’s not just Elice’s spectacular IPO flop. The Kospi index has plunged almost 
40% from its all-time high in June, including a 3.7% drop today.

South Korea is a bellwether for AI sentiment because two companies — Samsung 
and SK Hynix — account for almost half the Kospi’s market value. They’ve sunk 
40% and 56%, respectively, since the market peaked.

The two companies make memory chips, a notoriously boom-and-bust business, and 
demand has been off the charts thanks to the AI buildout — Samsung earned a 
record $80 billion last quarter.

Yet investors aren’t willing to pay up for those earnings, because they’re 
afraid the money will dry up.Samsung sells for less than five times this year’s 
estimated earnings, while SK Hynix has a PE ratio of less than four, in each 
case about half of their average valuations over the past decade.

“Valuation-wise it doesn’t make sense to be selling,” said Shawn Oh at NH 
Investment & Securities, but there’s “a lot of fear and pessimism.”

The same dynamic is afflicting the MSCI Emerging Markets Index, in which 
Samsung, SK Hynix and Taiwan Semiconductor have huge weightings — the benchmark 
is priced at less than nine times earnings,its biggest-ever discount to the US 
stock market 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/emerging-stocks-are-trading-at-their-biggest-ever-discount-to-us?cmpid=102026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10272>
. Profit estimates go up, but the share prices don’t.

What might turn sentiment around? Earnings this week and next from some big 
builders of data centers — Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet, among them — could 
convince investors that the spending spree has longer to run.

“US technology companies have generally been raising their earnings guidance 
to analysts,” said Mark Matthews at Bank Julius Baer in Singapore. “They are 
the biggest customers of the Asian semiconductor and associated hardware 
companies.” —Phil Serafino

Netflix slump

Despite the jitters around AI, the US stock market is still having a great 
year, which makesNetflix’s stock slump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/netflix-needs-blockbusters-to-reverse-its-135-billion-wipeout?cmpid=102026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10272>
 all the more surprising.

The stock is down 26% in 2026, putting Netflix among the 10 worst performers 
in the Nasdaq 100 Index and on track for its biggest annual drop since 2022. 
The reason is no secret to anyone following the company — user engagement has 
stalled due toa lack of popular content 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/netflix-trying-to-please-everyone-misses-the-buzzy-shows?cmpid=102026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10272>
.

When the streaming giant reports earnings after the close today, investors 
will likely be more more attuned to what new shows and movies are coming over 
the next six months than the company’s third-quarter numbers.

“The content’s getting very expensive, and there’s just still too many 
players,” said Ken Mahoney of Mahoney Asset Management. “I think there’s so 
many better tech stories out there than Netflix that have sticky customers.” —
Carmen Reinicke
