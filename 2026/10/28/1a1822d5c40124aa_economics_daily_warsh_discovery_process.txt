Still in Discovery

In American law, the concept of “discovery 
<https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_education/resources/law_related_education_network/how_courts_work/discovery/>
” is tied to the preparatory stage of a legal case, where attorneys review 
information and depose potential witnesses to get a full picture of the 
evidence.

Neil Dutta, head of economics at Renaissance Macro Research, says the Federal 
Reserve-watching community is effectively still in that phase when it comes to 
Chairman Kevin Warsh. He has only held three post-decision press conferences 
since taking office in May, and made a handful of other public appearances.

“We’re still in the discovery process with Warsh,” Dutta wrote in a note this 
week. “I’m unsure of his communications approach.”

Wednesday brings a further learning opportunity, with Warsh set to brief after 
theUS central bank’s latest rate decision 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/fed-seen-holding-rates-with-nod-to-december-decision-day-guide?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
. Both economists andtraders 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/interest-rate-decision-will-hold-steady-ai-fed-model-predicts?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
 largely expect the Fed to forgo a rate hike — despite ahotter-than-expected 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/us-consumer-prices-climb-by-most-since-april-on-fuel-costs?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
 September inflation report. Warsh will then have the opportunity of either 
laying out why the committee stood pat or, as in July, leaving the rationale 
vague.

In his last public remarks before this meeting, Warsh emphasized that “I don’t 
prejudge 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/warsh-boosts-market-expectations-of-fed-hold-in-october?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
 meetings.” Dutta noted that, for some, this was a tacit endorsement of 
guidance previously delivered by the other two members of the so-called Fed 
troika — Vice Chair Phillip Jefferson and New York Fed President John Williams. 
Each had said the Fed could afford totake its time 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/fed-s-jefferson-says-officials-may-need-more-time-for-next-move?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
 in contemplating when next to raise rates — suggesting an October pause.

Assuming the Fed does hold, signals from Jefferson and Williams may well gain 
even greater importance going forward. In the case of a surprise hike, the 
conclusion would be that “the ‘troika’ is dead and that this really is the 
Warsh Fed now,” Dutta wrote.

As for Warsh, the challenge on Wednesday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/fed-meeting-focus-turns-to-warsh-messaging-with-december-in-mind?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
 if the Fed holds will be to avoid the fumble in July, when investors concluded 
he was all talk, and unwilling to actuallywalk the walk 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-30/kevin-warsh-delivers-a-perplexing-fed-briefing-and-gets-a-bronx-cheer?cmpid=BBD102826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10624>
 on reining in inflation.

Some argued that Warsh’s language in his August Jackson Hole speech would have 
done the job nicely the first time around. That’s when he said he and a 
majority of his colleagues “thought the wiser course was to await new 
information” before “deciding whether a change in interest rate policy was 
advisable.” He also referred retrospectively to “our joint readiness to act as 
circumstances might require.”

That could offer a template for potential language to use again on Wednesday. 
One way or another, Dutta says, “Warsh needs to come up with a compelling 
reason why the Fed skips.”

Need-to-Know Research

The artificial-intelligence investment wave is pushing up inflation <> in 
developed economies in three ways, with the wave likely to crest in the first 
quarter of 2027, according to Goldman Sachs economists Megan Peters and Joseph 
Briggs.

First off is the jump in the cost for computing memory hardware, which is 
having the biggest impact in the US, the duo wrote. Second is the pass-through 
of memory price hikes to broader consumer electronic devices such as phones and 
computers, with the US and Japan likely to see the biggest boosts. Third is the 
pressure on electricity prices from the increased power demand of data centers. 
Again, the US has the biggest impact, with Australia also affected.

All told, the Goldman economists see “an average boost to year-over-year core 
inflation of 25 basis points” through the first quarter for developed markets 
other than the US, they wrote in a note this week. That compares with a peak 
for US core inflation of 60 basis points, using the PCE measure, they said. The 
impact will fade through 2027 as memory price pressures diminish, they said.
