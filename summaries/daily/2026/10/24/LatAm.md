• The Amazon land-use divide — environmental enforcement versus settler property rights — has emerged as the central fault line of the campaign.

**Political Economy**
Brazil's presidential runoff presents a structurally important policy fork. Incumbent Lula has anchored his second term to environmental enforcement and Paris Agreement commitments, delivering an 86% reduction in deforestation — a result that resonates internationally but has generated significant domestic friction in agricultural frontier communities. Challenger Flávio Bolsonaro has pledged to eliminate only illegal deforestation, allow legally authorized forest-clearing, and privatize land within currently protected zones in Pará state — a platform that commands overwhelming support in commodity-producing municipalities such as Novo Progresso, where he captured 84% in the first round.

**Market Implications**
The policy divergence carries direct consequences for Brazil's agricultural export sector, sovereign ESG positioning, and foreign direct investment flows tied to sustainability criteria. A Bolsonaro victory could accelerate land regularization and expand the agricultural frontier, supporting near-term rural output but potentially triggering ESG-linked capital outflows and trade friction with the European Union, which has conditioned market access on deforestation commitments. The outcome remains genuinely uncertain, and markets should treat the binary risk accordingly.
