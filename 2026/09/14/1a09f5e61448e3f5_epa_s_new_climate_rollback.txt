Pollution shift for power plants

By Zahra Hirji 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVypnhGtqak/zahra-hirji?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>
,Ari Natter 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATB37Z6gk7c/ari-natter?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>
, andJennifer A Dlouhy 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASlQFjfALa0/jennifer-a-dlouhy?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>

The US Environmental Protection Agency is expected to formally rescind carbon 
pollution standards 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/trump-epa-to-rescind-carbon-standards-for-coal-gas-power-plants?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>
 for fossil fuel-fired power plants as soon as Monday, according to people 
familiar with the matter.
Stacks from a coal-fired power station in Kentucky, US.Photographer: Jeff 
Swensen/Getty Images
The Trump administration’s latest major climate rollback is expected to come 
on the sidelines of a Group of 20 energy ministers’ meeting in Houston, said 
the people, who asked not to be named because the details aren’t yet public. 
Earlier this year, the EPA rescinded similar climate standards for vehicles and 
the 2009 endangerment finding, a landmark ruling which held that greenhouse 
gases threaten human health.

Under Trump, the EPA first proposed rolling back the power-plant climate rules 
last summer. This week, the agency is finalizing a piece of the initial 
proposal — scrapping Biden-era mandates requiring existing and future US fossil 
fuel-fired power plants to deploy specific emissions-reduction technologies, 
including carbon capture and storage, to sharply cut their greenhouse gas 
emissions in the coming decades, according to the people.

Agency officials also plan to propose a separate rule repealing the federal 
finding that greenhouse gases from power plants specifically pose a threat, 
effectively a separate endangerment finding for power plants under the Clean 
Air Act.

Power plants are the US’s largest industrial source of greenhouse gas 
emissions. If the US power sector were a country by itself, it would rank as 
the world’s sixth-largest emitter of greenhouse gases, according to a New York 
University School of Law’s Institute for Policy Integrity analysis of 2022 
emissions data.

The EPA didn’t respond to requests for comment outside business hours Sunday.

Heat on UK High Streets

By Olivia Rudgard 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWBnLoYN_tg/olivia-rudgard?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>
 andMaddie Parker 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWY9PNBczAI/maddie-parker?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>

Britain’s obsession with a nice cup of tea is famous around the world. But as 
the climate changes, that love affair is wilting in the heat.

A scorching summer dampened demand for tea 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/so-long-sausage-rolls-heat-upends-britain-s-high-street-habits?cmpid=BBD091426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8869>
, according to Associated British Foods Plc, the owner of tea brand Twinings 
and malt-drink brand Ovaltine. Hot beverages fell out of favor with customers 
as temperatures rose as high as 38C (100.4F).
A shopper shelters from the sun under an umbrella on London’s Oxford Street, 
in August.Photographer: Richard Baker/In Pictures
This summer has been a record-breaker. It was western Europe’s hottest summer 
on record, with heat waves in many countries that began early and persisted 
from May to August. Spain endured 61 days of heat-wave conditions, Germany’s 
temperature hit a new all-time high and the UK had an unprecedented 40 days of 
temperatures over 30C.

British retail sales struggled in blistering temperatures. Even England’s 
consumer-confidence-boosting progression to the World Cup semi-final couldn’t 
balance out shoppers’ unwillingness to brave the high street.

Heat is becoming a bigger consideration for British retailers, says Steve 
Fergus, national head of retail and wholesale at Barclays UK’s corporate bank, 
with growing evidence that shoppers’ behavior changes as the temperature rises 
into increasingly uncomfortable territory. “Adapting to hotter conditions is 
becoming a commercial as well as an operational priority,” he says.
