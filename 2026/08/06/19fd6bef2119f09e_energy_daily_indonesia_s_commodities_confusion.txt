Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Indonesia-based reporter Eddie Spence takes a look at the lack of clarity 
around Jakarta’s plan to nationalize raw material exports.

In late May, Indonesia’s President Prabowo Subianto unveiled a plan to 
centralize control 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/prabowo-to-tighten-controls-of-indonesia-s-commodity-exports?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
 of the country’s vast commodity exports.

Almost three months on, producers, traders and officials are still figuring 
out how to turn this radical vision into reality. The paralysis, should it 
continue, leavesworrying question marks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-22/why-indonesia-president-prabowo-s-commodity-export-controls-are-spooking-markets?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
 over a major seller of coal, metals and palm oil — and an economy already 
strained by its leader’s policy swerves.

Prabowo, a former army general, wants a homegrown Glencore 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-23/indonesia-plans-to-beat-global-trading-giants-at-their-own-game?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
. Executives at the sovereign wealth fund in charge of executing the plan have 
a far narrower blueprint, under which exports by private companies would be 
monitored rather than nationalized.

The idea is to curb a practice known as under-invoicing, where producers shift 
profits to lower-tax jurisdictions offshore by declaring exports to be worth 
less than their true value. Prabowo says it costs Indonesia $150 billion a year.
Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto.Photographer: Dimas Ardian/Bloomberg
The debate between the president’s all-encompassing vision and the more 
limited mandate isgoing on behind closed doors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/indonesia-weighs-export-rule-exemptions-for-commodity-traders?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
. Shipments are largely carrying on normally for now, but the next steps could 
be a threat to those flows, with potentially global ramifications.

The Southeast Asian country is the biggest exporter of thermal coal, nickel 
and palm oil, and any disruption can impact prices paid by consumers around the 
world. Aprolonged period of uncertainty 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/indonesia-s-radical-export-experiment-upends-its-commodity-trade?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
 may also slow market activity and crimp Indonesia’s export revenues.

The first major test for the policy will come next month, when initial 
implementation begins. But even now, there’slittle clarity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/prabowo-s-radical-commodity-export-plan-faces-dueling-visions?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
 on what exactly that will mean for the huge array of private traders who 
handle the country’s sprawling commodity supply chains.

Prabowo’s state of the nation address and budget speech next Friday will also 
be closely watched for any updates on the plan.

The president has not been afraid to play rough with Indonesia’s natural 
resources sector to achieve his goals,hiking mining royalties 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-16/indonesia-hikes-mining-royalties-in-bid-to-fund-prabowo-policies?cmpid=BBBXT080626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7428>
 and seizing millions of hectares of palm oil plantations from the business 
elite.

But the wholesale nationalization of the country’s top-earning exports may be 
a step too far.

—Eddie Spence, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Solar photovoltaic manufacturers are major buyers of silver, driving 80% of 
the growth in global demand over the past decade. But a jump in the cost of the 
metal has prompted companies to slash per-unit consumption. Solar is on track 
to take about 14% of worldwide demand for silver in 2026, down from 18% last 
year, according toBloombergNEF <https://about.bnef.com/>.
