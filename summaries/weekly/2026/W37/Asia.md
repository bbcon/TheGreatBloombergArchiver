**Japan: Macro and Monetary Policy**
The combination of above-trend GDP growth and wage increases not seen in nearly three decades gives the Bank of Japan tangible cover to raise rates at its September 18 meeting. The yen broke through 155 per dollar on Tuesday, triggering stop-loss cascades and pushing dollar-yen to approximately 153.2 by Wednesday — its strongest monthly performance among 31 major currencies. The move appears qualitatively different from prior intervention episodes: it has occurred without reported reserve drawdowns, driven instead by unwinding of leveraged short positions and growing conviction that Japanese fundamentals — current account surpluses, rising real wages, and five-year inflation breakevens above 2% — justify appreciation independently of rate differentials. Japan's August foreign reserve holdings fell a record $87.8 billion, consistent with July's intervention scale. Market participants are additionally monitoring a possible simultaneous GPIF reallocation announcement toward domestic government bonds, a potential structural shift in global capital flows.

**China: Trade, Technology, and Banks**
Beijing announced a $45 billion recapitalization of its largest banks and insurers Monday — the largest such exercise in nearly two decades — as property sector stress compounds. Macquarie estimates housing starts have fallen 78% from their 2021 peak, with home prices reverting to 2016 levels. The bank capital injection diluted existing shareholders, with PICC falling 3.1% and China Life 1.1% in Hong Kong. On technology, Huawei raised prices on its Ascend 950DT AI accelerator by roughly 60% to 250,000 yuan on Thursday, reflecting surging domestic demand as China's AI infrastructure investment proceeds independently of US export controls. TSMC's 53% revenue beat confirms that AI semiconductor demand remains supply-constrained — a macro-relevant signal for capital expenditure globally. BYD raised its 2026 overseas sales guidance to 1.9–2.0 million vehicles, with exports more than doubling in August.
