Crystal Balls

For a baffled perspective on Trump’s role as a global disruptor, just ask 
anyone whose job it is to make forecasts.

Economists at the International Monetary Fund for example had some early 
exasperationin April last year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-22/imf-slashes-global-growth-forecast-says-downside-risks-dominate?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
 when the US president’s “Liberation Day” tariffs forced them to throw out 
almost completed projections and start again.

This month, JPMorgan Chase oil analysts admitted that the on-off nature of the 
Middle East war that Trump started has made forecasting effectively futile. “We 
simply don’t know how to model the endgame,”they told clients 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/jp-morgan-analysts-unsure-how-to-model-oil-as-iran-war-drags-on?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
.

Central banks have tried to respond by offering investors different scenarios 
to help navigate the fog. Meanwhile anyone who does produce forecasts tends to 
couch them with multiple provisos.

The Paris-based OECD did much the same on Wednesday. Its latest predictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/oecd-warns-of-faster-global-inflation-sees-rate-hikes-at-fed-ecb-boj?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
 foresee faster inflation next year than previously anticipated, underscoring 
how the economic impact of Trump’s war is set to spill into the second half of 
his term.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-23/oecd-central-banks-must-be-vigilant-to-inflation-risks-video?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
“There is a huge level of uncertainty about the evolution of headline 
inflation,” OECD Chief Economist Stefano Scarpetta told Bloomberg Television. 
“This is very much related to the evolution of the conflict in the Middle East.”

With such difficulty in forecasting near term, maybe it’s intellectually 
easier to look further into the future, given that some underlying trends are 
too powerful to be dislodged.UK lawmakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/obr-needs-new-10-year-forecasts-uk-treasury-committee-says?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
, for example, just called for a new 10-year horizon to be incorporated into 
fiscal projections.

Meanwhile Bloomberg Economics attempted this week to imagine how the world 
might evolve in the coming three decades. Its forecasts anticipate that the 
triple burdens of demographics, de-globalization and debt will curb growth, with
artificial intelligence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-26/artificial-intelligence-investment-now-competes-with-governments-for-capital?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
 both a potential boon to productivity and a possible cause of higher 
unemployment.

Global annual expansion in the next decade will average 3.2%, down from 3.5% 
in the years before the pandemic, BE says, envisaging an ever greater presence 
of India during that time span.

“That would be a transformation in the balance of global activity rivaled in 
modern history only by the emergence of China itself,” BE’s chief economist,Tom 
Orlik 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APScahppEq4/tom-orlik?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
, wrote in the report, available to Bloomberg Terminal subscribershere 
<bbg://news/stories/TLQFTEKIP3IZ>. 

Among other predictions, the analysts reckon an environment of greater 
interventionism and protectionism will emerge. While more countries may 
experience authoritarianism, that trend is seen ultimately bottoming out.

Aside from India 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/india-economic-activity-climbs-in-september-shows-flash-survey?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
, will there be any winners in the long run? Among rich countries, not many, 
according to BE.

“Bloomberg Economics’s long-term forecasts show the majority of advanced 
economies growing more slowly in the coming decade than in the last 10 years,” 
Orlik wrote. “Only a few — including Japan and Sweden — look likely to escape 
this fate.”

Need-to-Know Research

In a fresh summary of views on the outlook for major economies, central banks 
and financial markets, Goldman Sachs economists and strategists say that 
inflation should be slowing over the coming year, with key central banks 
shifting from rate hikes now to reductions by the latter part of 2027.

“The news should turn less hawkish over the next several months,” Goldman’s 
chief economist, Jan Hatzius, wrote in a note Tuesday. Part of that is 
predicated on a retreat incrude oil prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-22?cmpid=BBD092326_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9197>
, with Goldman’s commodity team seeing Brent down to $85 a barrel by December 
from roughly $100 levels now. Already, Middle East oil exports “have recovered 
to nearly 80% of prewar volumes.”

As for surging bond yields, most of that can be explained by the repricing 
higher of monetary policy expectations.

“Under our forecast that some of this repricing will reverse, our rates 
strategists see long-term yields also coming down modestly,” Hatzius said. He 
sees the Federal Reserve hiking in October, then standing pat until cutting in 
late 2027. The Bank of England will do a “one and done” November hike and the 
European Central Bank will deliver a final increase in December, Goldman 
expects.
