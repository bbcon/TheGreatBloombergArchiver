Hi, this is Philip Glamann in Beijing, where everyone is of course fascinated 
with the spy/affair saga that led toJensen Huang leaving Nvidia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jensen-huang-face-of-the-ai-boom-ousted-by-nvidia-board-as-ceo?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
.

Not long ago, some in China were asking who in the US would be the next Henry 
Kissinger — as in someone who was friendly to Beijing and also influential in 
the US, allowing for the two sides to come together on certain issues.

Then, during a trip to Beijing, the Chinese-speaking Huang called for closer 
business ties between the two nations. He also championed granting China access 
to the best American tech, and even joined President Donald Trump on his flight 
to Beijing back in May.
Jensen Huang speaks in Stockholm on Oct. 15.Photographer: Erik Flyg/Bloomberg
Even if Huang was leery of politics, for a short while it did appear that 
China had that unofficial ambassador it wanted.

The fact that he had led the company at the very heart of the tech 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-08/nvidia-board-to-probe-spying-case-after-ceo-jensen-huang-admits-infidelity?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 that is defining our times only made his role more promising. American and 
Chinese companies being at the forefront of AI — while their governments try to 
sort out the rules of the road for the tech — only added to the possibilities 
that Huang could play a larger role in ties.

No more.

Huang’s ouster and admission he was having an affair with a suspected Chinese 
spy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/alice-huang-suspected-china-spy-spent-years-in-nvidia-s-orbit?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 — Alice Huang, the founder of a tech company at the center of a US probe — 
ended that.

In fact, it’s now difficult to see who could ever take up even a lesser 
version of the role anytime soon.

As Juliana Liu, a columnist for Bloomberg Opinion, wrote this week: “Huang was 
the last tycoon standing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-11-10/huang-was-the-last-tycoon-standing-on-us-china-ties?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 among high-profile American corporate titans advocating for closer engagement 
with Beijing in the Trump 2.0 era.”

The Future of AI Without Jensen Huang
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/audio/2026-11-12/big-take-the-future-of-ai-without-jensen-huang-podcast?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
Liu pointed out that Apple chief executive Tim Cook has stepped down, 
scratching his name off the list of potential unofficial ambassadors. Cook had 
a nearly cultlike following among Apple fans in China and a talent forhandling 
his company in the nation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-04-21/apple-ceo-tim-cook-wrote-a-playbook-for-success-in-china?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
.

And Elon Musk? Fair to say he has other concerns right now 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-12/elon-musk-powers-ahead-despite-a-costly-midterms-rout?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
. Tesla sales are stalling in China due to tough competition. And Democrats 
returning to power in Congress have little desire to embrace the tycoon after 
he gave Republicans hundreds of millions of dollars in backing in the US 
midterm elections.

Officials in Washington never seemed to think the next Kissinger was terribly 
important to find.

Still, Huang’s situation serves to “complicate prospects for American 
companies seeking business with China more broadly,” Liu wrote.

And right now, it’s very difficult to see US officials being able to reverse 
that, even asTrump prepares to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/nvidia-fallout-puts-trade-truce-to-the-test-as-xi-welcomes-trump?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 next week at an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation event in the southern city 
of Shenzhen.

In an interview with Bloomberg TV this week, the American ambassador in 
Beijing, David Perdue,summed up ties with China as “bumpy.” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/us-envoy-to-china-says-relations-are-bumpy-after-nvidia-affair?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 One country fearing another is spying on one of its top executives will do 
that for relations, even if China hasrepeatedly denied the accusation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/china-says-us-should-not-use-huang-scandal-to-justify-tech-curbs?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
.

Additionally, some in the Trump administration have recently highlighted 
frustration with how things stand with China.

While Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent hailed improved understanding between 
Xi and Trump going into their third meeting this year, US Trade Representative 
Jamieson Greer called on Beijing tofollow through on pledges 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/bessent-hails-us-china-engagement-sees-more-talk-on-ai-models?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 regarding “agricultural purchases, rare earths, and other critical minerals.”

Underscoring that the vexation cuts both ways, in the same video call with 
Greer, the Chinese side voiced “serious concerns” about recent US trade and 
economic restrictions.

Looming on the horizon is Democrats taking control of Congress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/2026-midterm-elections-what-democrats-wins-in-congress-mean-for-trump?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 early next year, and figures on both sides of the American political divide 
have indicated theywant to take a tougher line on China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/openai-joins-groundswell-of-support-for-ai-chip-control-measures?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
, especially on issues like flows of advanced semiconductors.

Expectations for the next Xi-Trump meeting were already low, partly because 
they have one planned for next month in the US they can fall back on toextend a 
trade truce expiring in January 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-extends-pause-on-24-us-tariffs-to-january-keeps-10-rate?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
.

But with Huang’s troubles, the two sides will have one less resource for 
reaching agreements.

A Global Emporium

And hello, this is Allen Wan in Shanghai, fresh from visiting the China 
International Import Expo.

The CIIE is one of the events 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/at-china-s-big-import-fair-sellers-try-to-crack-a-tough-market?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 that I look forward to every November,like Thanksgiving 
<https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/planes_trains_and_automobiles>.

Besides giving me a chance to check out the latest in everything from food and 
medical gear to consumer and industrial goods, it is also an opportunity to get 
in my steps because the event is enormous, with more than 4,000 companies in 
booths spread out over an area the size of 50 soccer fields.

Despite a slowing Chinese economy and sluggish consumer spending, foreign 
companies are still eager to come here to showcase their wares and mingle with 
locals.

After all, China has one of the world’s biggest consumer markets, even though 
consumption as a percentage of GDP trails countries like the US.

The good news is that after years of Beijing giving short shrift to the idea 
of importing more foreign goods, it appears serious this time. There have been 
renewed pledges of market openness and access for financial services firms.

Soaring exports and rising trade frictions are pressuring Xi’s government 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-s-economy-is-nearly-20-trillion-but-its-citizens-remain-relatively-poor?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 to reduce the ballooning trade surplus — which recentlytopped $1 trillion for 
the second time ever 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/chinese-trade-surplus-surpasses-1-trillion-for-second-time-ever?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
. Even though imports spiked almost 42% last month from a year earlier, the 
most since 2021, that is still not enough as far as trading partners are 
concerned.

The European Union is planning to cut imports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/eu-takes-the-trade-fight-to-china-and-braces-for-the-backlash?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 of products from sectors such as furniture, chemicals and machinery.

The bad news is that foreign companies are looking to make more inroads just 
as the Chinese consumer market has matured, with domestic companies getting 
more competitive, while younger consumers are proving to be more discerning 
than previous generations.

One area where the Chinese are willing to open up their wallets is health.

After a wave of consumer-health scandals, Chinese shoppers have put more faith 
in foreign standards, giving foreign brands an edge. That’s especially true for 
outdoor gear or baby products, said Mark Tanner, managing director of 
Shanghai-based marketing firm China Skinny.
Exhibitors dish out food at one of the US booths at the expo in Shanghai.
Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg
That perception is helping companies like Jamieson Wellness, a Toronto-based 
maker of vitamins and nutritional supplements. China has become its 
second-largest market because of confidence in what’s in the bottle, said Chief 
Executive Mike Pilato.

“The Chinese consumer is the most discerning consumer on quality, on trust, on 
heritage and on efficacy,” he said.

Other exhibitors at the expo indicated that Chinese buyers are also still 
willing to shell out for niche imports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/coach-ralph-lauren-defy-china-retail-slump-as-badge-of-new-cool?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 like Almond Roca candy, handmade scarves using natural dyes, and Himalayan 
goat wool.

Products with a technical edge that can’t easily be made at home (yet 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/can-china-upend-commercial-aviation-emirates-says-be-prepared?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
) are also in demand. That means Siemens Healthineers’ $10.5 million PET/CT 
scanner, which can handle a patient in 20 seconds, and Nikon’s precision optics 
used in photo equipment.

“We have certain sophisticated technologies which China cannot produce at this 
moment,” said Japan’s ambassador to China, Kenji Kanasugi. But he warned that 
sales were “a race against time” given the track record of the nation’s 
manufacturers in catching up.

Behind the Great Firewall

A weekly look at the big water cooler news in China.

Many in China were also gripped by the turmoil involving Jensen Huang 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/inside-jensen-huang-s-final-hours-as-the-ceo-of-nvidia?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 and Nvidia this week. Much of the attention was focused on Alice Huang — no 
relation — who US officials suspectmay have been a spy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/singapore-says-warns-alice-huang-lacks-evidence-of-offenses?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
.

From school photos to clothing choices, internet users have been dissecting 
nearly every aspect of her life. Purported pics ofAlice Huang, also known as 
Huang Le 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/alice-huang-suspected-china-spy-spent-years-in-nvidia-s-orbit?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
, dating back to her school days have surfaced online.

So have screenshots from her WeChat feed, including posts featuring Jensen 
Huang and her travels in Europe.

Her clothing choices have become a particular obsession. On Xiaohongshu, 
fashion bloggers have pored over clips and photos of Alice Huang, identifying 
the dress, coat and shoe brands she preferred — and digging into the cost.

One outfit attracted extra scrutiny: a sleeveless pink dress she wore to a 
dinner with the former Nvidia CEO in Taipei last year that was captured in 
footage that has gone viral. Online fashion sleuths suspect the dress was made 
by French luxury house Dior, with a price tag of about $3,500.
Alice Huang at a dinner hosted by Jensen Huang in Taipei in 2025.Source: 
TVBS/AP Photo
Yet internet users also seem skeptical of the espionage allegations, echoing 
dismissals by the Foreign Ministry in Beijing.

“Huang Le has been so high-profile, repeatedly posting photos with Jensen 
Huang on WeChat Moments — would a ‘spy’ really act like that?” one Weibo user 
said.

Some influential commentators went further, portraying the allegations as part 
of a broader campaign against Jensen Huang, driven partly by his Chinese 
ethnicity. (Though it should be said thatAlice Huang is facing a US inquiry 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/us-probing-if-alice-huang-helped-smuggle-super-micro-products?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
 into whether entities linked to her were involved in efforts to smuggle tech 
products to China, and that Jensen Huang’s ouster also came amid reports he had 
a separate affair with a subordinate.)

“What makes the targeting of Jensen Huang so shrewd is that it sits at the 
intersection of anti-China, anti-Chinese sentiment, anti-Trump politics, 
anti-Big Tech sentiment and anti-AI backlash,” wrote Ren Yi,an online 
commentator better known as Chairman Rabbit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-03-18/as-china-targets-jack-ma-s-media-empire-chairman-rabbit-thrives?cmpid=BBD111326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261113&utm_content=11280>
, who has 2 million followers on Weibo.

“Yet his greatest fault, or original sin, is being a person of Chinese descent 
with countless intricate ties to China while sitting in such a critical 
position.”
