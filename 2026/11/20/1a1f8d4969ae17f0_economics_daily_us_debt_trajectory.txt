Debt Trajectory

Economists and policymakers have long agreed that the US is on an 
unsustainable trajectory for government debt. In the wake of the November 
congressional election, fears are rising the situationcould even deteriorate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-03/the-two-big-2027-challenges-looming-for-a-divided-us-government?cmpid=BBD112026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11626>
.

To help focus attention, a new proposal 
<https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/p/2026-10-26-fiscal-consolidation-2026-an-illustrative-package-of-tax-and-spending-reforms/>
 from Penn Wharton Budget Model shows what it would take to narrow a outsize 
fiscal deficit-to-GDP ratio that’s been running at an outsized 6% or so for 
several years now.

Among other things, the proposal calls for eliminating many of the Trump 
administration’s signature tax cuts; taxing capital gains and dividends at 
ordinary rates; and imposing a carbon tax.

The policy package also includes gradually raising the Social Security 
retirement age to 70 and Medicare eligibility to 67 from 65. It would reduce 
deficits by $11.3 trillion over ten years. (For context, the Congressional 
Budget Office projects deficitswill total 
<https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105> $23.1 trillion through 2035 if current 
spending and tax policy remains in effect).

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent came into the role in 2025 vowing to lower 
the deficit ratio to 3%. But in the fiscal year ended in September, the gap 
still stood atabout 6% 
<https://www.crfb.org/blogs/us-ran-2-trillion-deficit-last-year-we-estimate>.

Democrats, who swept control of Congress in the midterms, have laid out a 
100-day agenda 
<https://jeffries.house.gov/2026/11/04/dear-colleague-announcing-candidacy-for-speaker-of-the-house/>
 that would reverse cuts to the Medicaid health insurance program and restore 
funding for food aid known as SNAP. That could add $4.9 trillion to the debt 
through 2037 without offsets, according to the non-partisan Committee for a 
Responsible Federal Budget.

While the Penn Wharton Budget Model plan is likely a non-starter for both 
political parties, it provides a roadmap for stabilizing a government debt load 
that’s soared to roughly 100% of GDP.

Penn Wharton’s Kent Smetters <https://bepp.wharton.upenn.edu/profile/smetters/>
, a former Treasury and CBO official who came up with the proposal, is 
realistic about its chances of being adopted.

“The odds of this going through are very low — but what’s the odds of current 
policy being sustainable? Even lower than zero,” he said. “So policymakers have 
to make a choice between a bunch of totally implausible, unrealistic decisions. 
And it ultimately comes down to, are they going to make decisions with much 
delay that will require the economy to potentially crash.”

Need-to-Know Research

JPMorgan Chase’s outlook for 2027 keys off expectations for a moderation in 
the “once-in-a-generation” capital spending boom, with modest, positive job 
growth and an inflation rate that remains above the Federal Reserve’s target.

“When the economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/us-economy-grows-a-faster-than-expected-3-4-most-in-a-year?cmpid=BBD112026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261120&utm_content=11626>
 flies on one engine, concerns about the functioning of that engine are 
magnified,” Michael Feroli, JPMorgan’s chief US economist, wrote in a note this 
week. “However, we expect 2027 will see only a benign moderation in AI-related 
spending.” Business investment will rise an inflation-adjusted 8% next year 
versus 10% in 2026, the bank expects. “Similarly, wealth effects should 
moderate,” causing a slowdown in consumer spending gains.

A fading of temporary price pressures from tariffs, energy and demand for 
technology products means core inflation should be in the “mid-to-high” 2% 
range, Feroli wrote. “Progress beyond that may require good luck, or bad labor 
markets.” The bank sees the Fed taking its key interest rate up to a 4.5% to 
4.75% range, or three quarters of a percentage point above the current level.
