Shopping Surprise

China’s consumers have been in shelter-in-place mode this year, but at least 
some have decided it’s safe to venture back to the shops.

Among the raft of statistics published Monday – an event known locally as the “
data dump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-home-price-slump-eases-fueling-hopes-for-market-recovery?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
” – the biggest surprise was September’s retail sales, which rose at more than 
twice the forecast pace. That helped lift overall economic growth to 4.5% in 
the third quarter, keeping it on track to meet Beijing’s annual target.

To be sure, a year-on-year increase of 1.1% in sales hardly suggests Chinese 
households are splurging – and even that number may overstate consumer 
strength. The holiday calendar likely accounts for some of the bump, and so 
does a recentpickup in prices 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-s-producer-prices-jolted-by-100-oil-as-inflation-rebounds?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
, especially for items like phones. Overall growth remains heavily reliant on 
industry and above all the country’spowerhouse exporters 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/china-s-trade-surplus-nears-940-billion-as-export-growth-surges?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
.

Still, the retail figure was the highest since March – and, if you strip out 
car purchases, the best since October, as my Bloomberg colleaguesreport 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-s-growth-rebounds-to-4-5-puts-annual-target-within-reach?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
. The National Bureau of Statistics said after the release that domestic demand 
has been improving steadily.

What China’s trade partners would doubtless like to see is action by Beijing 
in the near term to speed that up. They may be in for a disappointment.

Calls for a rebalancing of China’s economy toward the consumer front – which 
would relieve countries under pressure from its surging exports — have been 
mounting for years. The refrain was widespread at last week’s International 
Monetary Fund meetings in Bangkok. “China needs to spend more, the US has to 
save more, and Europe has to invest more,” was fund chief Kristalina 
Georgieva’s formula for getting theglobal economy back in sync 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/us-china-blame-game-over-global-economy-tilts-in-beijing-s-favor?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
.

But the overall takeaway from the data dump was that China will hit its growth 
target this year, with the help of its recent “mini-stimulus 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/china-s-mini-stimulus-seen-securing-gdp-target-not-much-more?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
,” and so won’t feel the need for more near-term action. Economist Larry Hu at 
Macquarie calls it the do-just-enough rule.

The next question: how much will be enough for next year? In the coming 
months, writeChang Shu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATwU2T7qXnU/chang-shu?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
 andDavid Qu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AT63ATn0yGw/david-qu?cmpid=BBD101926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261019&utm_content=10251>
 at Bloomerg Economics, “attention will increasingly turn to policy plans for 
2027.”

Need-to-Know Research

Artificial intelligence has reduced hiring by nearly 10% in exposed industries 
since ChatGPT’s release in late 2022, according to a new International Monetary 
Fundworking paper 
<https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2026/english/wpiea2026220-source-pdf.pdf>
. But the decline is now leveling off as firms seek hires who are adept in the 
technology.

Demand for those skills is rising and is concentrated in occupations with 
higher AI exposure, the IMF study found, drawing on over 700 million online job 
postings across 110 economies. Larger firms are more likely to require AI 
skills in their job vacancies — though even there, fewer than 20% of firms seem 
to be setting that as a requirement, the paper showed.

“AI’s overall impact thus reflects two forces at once: less hiring in 
occupations where AI is more useful, and new opportunities for the workers who 
can put it to use,” the IMF said. “Offsetting opportunities have begun to 
emerge already for workers with the right skills.”
