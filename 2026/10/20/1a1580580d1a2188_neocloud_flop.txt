Deep Dive: Chile’s Copper Recession
The Codelco El Teniente mine in Machali, Chile.Photographer: Cristobal 
Olivares/Bloomberg
Chile’s aging copper mines have been battered by deadly accidents, extreme 
weather and operational missteps, raising questions over how quickly the 
world’s biggest producer can recover from itslowest output 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/copper-boom-belies-a-deepening-mining-recession-in-top-producer?cmpid=102026_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10306>
 in more than two decades.

 * At least seven workers have died at Chilean mines in the past month and 
severe rain and snow have repeatedly disrupted mines and infrastructure, while 
simmering labor tensions are adding another layer of uncertainty.
 * The troubles are playing out as traders, producers and investors gather for 
LME Week in London, where faltering mine supply is likely to be a key topic as 
participants negotiate contracts for 2027.
 * While tariff-related trade flows have been the main driver of record copper 
prices this year, mining problems are another critical factor, particularly in 
Chile, which accounts for more than 20% of global mined supply.
 * The setbacks are increasing the likelihood of the first annual decline in 
global mine output since 2017 — just as demand from data centers and power-grid 
expansion accelerates.
