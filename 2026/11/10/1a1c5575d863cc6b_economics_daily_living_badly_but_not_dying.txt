‘Living Badly, But Not Dying’

Typically, when a business enjoys soaring sales, its market value increases 
alongside. A key reason why Anthropic’s forthcoming public listing is able to 
target a valuation up toaround $1 trillion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/anthropic-targets-64-4-billion-ipo-nearing-spacex-s-record?cmpid=BBD111026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11211>
 is its rapidly rising sales trajectory.

China’s carmakers, however, are proving an exception — at least, when looking 
at their overseas sales. Their 2026 exports are hurtling toward 10 million 
vehicles. (As a measure of comparison, the US had total sales of just over16 
million 
<https://www.nada.org/nada/nada-headlines/december-2025-market-beat-new-light-vehicle-sales-totaled-162-million-units>
 last year.) And that’s with the US largely shut to them. In Europe, where they 
until recently hadfew limits 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-11/china-moves-to-stave-off-wider-eu-trade-war-with-hybrid-deal?cmpid=BBD111026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11211>
, they surpassed their Japanese rivals for the first time last quarter.

Are investors rewarding them for this success on the global market? No. 
China’s automakers have lost around $270 billion from their peak valuations of 
late 2025, Bloomberg reporters Charlotte Yang, Chunying Zhang and Linda Lew 
writehere 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-chery-BYD-china-automakers-export-boom-cost/?cmpid=BBD111026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11211>
.

The reason: punishing competition at home. With more than 100 brands battling 
for buyers in China, and domestic consumers constrained by weak wage and job 
growth along with the demise of a subsidized trade-in program, the Chinese 
market has become a grave for profits.
The BYD booth at the Beijing Auto Show in April.Photographer: Qilai 
Shen/Bloomberg
What it’s not become is a grave for underperforming companies. That’s in part 
because local governments prop up their local manufacturers to safeguard 
employment. After all, “officials’ own jobs are tied to performance indicators 
that measure their ability to keep employment steady,” analysts including 
Esther Goreichy wrote in apiece 
<https://merics.org/en/comment/beijing-attempts-trim-overcapacity-steers-clear-structural-issues>
 by Berlin-based MERICS last month.

Local governments and investors have put so much into their local champs that 
they’re loath to let them go under, the think tank wrote. They’re also “banking 
on either a breakthrough or their ability to outlast the competition.” It’s 
created a situation where firms are “living badly, but not dying,” or 活不好、死不了 
in Chinese.

The national champion, BYD, is profitable but its results showcase the 
difference between the home andoverseas markets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-12/china-s-byd-chery-expected-to-speed-up-europe-factories-to-evade-hybrid-limit?cmpid=BBD111026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11211>
: last quarter, average profit per vehicle sold abroad was 16,000 yuan ($2,400) 
according to calculations by Morgan Stanley. Even so, overseas margins remained 
significantly higher than in the domestic market, where BYD earned about 2,000 
yuan per vehicle.

That’s just $300. As more Chinese manufacturers now chase the same customers 
overseas, analysts expect competition to erode some of the premium margins they 
currently earn abroad — while also walloping the earnings power of foreign 
competitors.

“In China, they’re killing each other 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-15/china-steps-up-price-war-scrutiny-as-cars-keep-getting-cheaper?cmpid=BBD111026_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11211>
,” said Edmund Harriss, chief investment officer at Guinness Global Investors 
in London. “Outside China, they’re killing the incumbents.”

Need-to-Know Research

The Fed was created more than a century ago to put an end to the series of 
financial “panics 
<https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/banking-panics-of-the-gilded-age>
” that had afflicted the US economy in the late 1800s and early 1900s. So did 
the central bank’s conduct of monetary policy improve the nation’s economic 
path? A newpaper 
<https://www.geertmesters.com/_files/ugd/8ac201_56f578942c794469bc4f1c0931b74119.pdf>
 from the New York Fed takes stock.

To answer the question requires separating the role of policymaking from the 
economic environment. After all, it could just be luck that economic conditions 
happened to have improved since the Fed’s creation. “Our framework addresses 
this challenge by comparing policymakers with the best policy response that was 
feasible given the shocks they faced,” Regis Barnichon of the San Francisco Fed 
and Geert Mesters of the New York Fedwrote 
<https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/has-u-s-monetary-policy-really-improved-a-150-year-perspective/>
.
Paul VolckerPhotographer: Pete Marovich/Bloomberg
“Has the conduct of monetary policy steadily improved over the past 150 years? 
Our first finding suggests that the answer is no,” the duo wrote. It’s true 
that monetary policy today performs substantially better, but most of the gains 
came only after Chair Paul Volcker quashed inflation by the early 1980s. From 
that point, meaningful improvements can be seen thanks to “substantially better 
responses to financial shocks and supply shocks.”
