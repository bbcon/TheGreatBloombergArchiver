‘No Brainer’

Christine Lagarde may or may not 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-05-23/ecb-rate-hike-delay-didn-t-make-a-massive-difference-lane-says?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
 serve out her term as European Central Bank, but a clear legacy is already 
emerging from her presidency.

The institution she took over in 2019 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-09-28/i-hesitated-over-taking-mario-draghi-s-job-at-ecb-lagarde-says?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
 stood out then as one of the world’s most dovish, with negative borrowing 
costs and asset purchases under way to stoke inflation seen as too feeble.

This week, when the ECB delivered its second rate hike since the outbreak of 
war in the Middle East, its hawkish posture compared with peers such as the Fed 
was plain to see.

The move on Thursday was a “no brainer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/ecb-lifts-rates-for-second-time-as-iran-war-drives-inflation?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
,” Lagarde told reporters. Investors expect more tightening, even if 
policymakers wonder ifbets on three hikes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/ecb-may-need-mildly-restrictive-rates-to-tame-prices-nagel-says?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
 in the next year are overdone. But an increase as soon as Oct. 29is in play 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/ecb-officials-expect-more-tightening-and-october-is-in-play?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
, according to people familiar with the matter.

One reason for the ECB’s aggressive stance is Europe’s huge dependence on 
energy imports, meaning that the Middle East conflict is hitting consumer 
prices in the region far more than economies such as the US.

Despite new forecasts that assume average oil costs of less than $90 per 
barrel this year and even lower thereafter, officials won’t have missed the 
news thatcrude hit $105 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-10?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
 on Thursday before then rising even higher.

Then there’s the legacy of 2022 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-05-23/ecb-rate-hike-delay-didn-t-make-a-massive-difference-lane-says?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
, when the ECB wasslow to hike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-03-22/ecb-latest-chief-economist-philip-lane-rebuffs-willem-buiter-with-rate-defense?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>

Inflation ultimately exceeded 10%.

Others such as the Fed or the Bank of England were also tardy, and got 
criticized for it, but euro-zone policymakerslooked especially late

Either way, the central bank Lagarde will soon leave

Now it has emerged that her memoir, something famous people tend to publish at

Need-to-Know Research

With the expected summit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/trump-plans-state-dinner-for-xi-during-china-leader-s-us-trip?cmpid=BBD091126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260911&utm_content=8781>
 between Presidents Donald Trump and Xi fast approaching, Barclays economists 
previewed the likely demands and offers from the Chinese delegation.

Job one is extending the one-year tariff truce that the two sides declared in 
November last year, economists including Yingke Zhou wrote in a note this week 
ahead of the Sept. 24 state dinner. The Chinese side is “likely to push for 
further tariff relief” beyond that, because Beijing faces the highest effective 
rate among major US trading partners. Barclays puts that levy at 23% for China, 
against 7% for the overall average.

Under what’s emerging as the “Board of Trade” framework, the focus is on a 
potential $30 billion of less-sensitive goods that negotiators might be able to 
settle on for reciprocal tariff cuts. Beyond that, “the scope for a broad deal 
remains limited, in our view. Any agreement is more likely to focus on targeted 
reductions,” the Barclays team said.
