Amid all the noise, several trends have remained consistent in recent weeks.

Investors are demanding more for government bonds. The average yield globally 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/tumbling-global-government-bonds-put-yields-on-brink-of-4?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9267>
 is close to 4%, the highest level since 2007. The rate on 30-year Treasuries 
rose on Thursday to 5.44%, alevel unseen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/us-30-year-yield-hits-highest-since-2004-as-bond-selloff-deepens?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9267>
 since 2004.

“People are running out of superlatives for the yield on the 30-year bond,” 
said Ed Al-Hussainy, a portfolio manager at Columbia Threadneedle. “Investors 
are saying, ‘Look, if we’re going to lock up our money for 30 years, we need 
much higher compensation.’”

Pressure for central banks to tighten continues to increase. Swaps now fully 
reflect three quarter-point hikes by the Federal Reserve over the next year, 
with significant hedging for a fourth. That is buoying the dollar, which is 
heading for its longest winning streak since May.

Oil prices are persistently elevated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-24?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9267>
. Global benchmark Brent jumped more than 3% on Thursday to touch a session 
high above $106 a barrel, after a military adviser to Iran’s supreme leader 
said Tehran may expand the war to the Indian Ocean if the US or Israel attacks 
again.

And yet stock investors aren’t too worried, as the AI trade rolls on. The 
Nasdaq 100 closed at a record this week, although futures are down today. It’s 
hard right now to see what could realistically shift these trends, which are 
pressuring indebted governments, forcing central bankers’ hands and driving up 
inflation.
