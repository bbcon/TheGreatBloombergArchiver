Deep Dive: Germany Prepares for Expanded War
A soldier opens the entrance doors to the bunker at the Bundeswehr Hospital in 
Ulm.Photographer: Jana Islinger/Bloomberg
At first glance, the hospital in the southwestern German city of Ulm looks 
much like any other. Patients sit in reception areas waiting to be called, 
doctors roam the halls looking at reports or their phones. Only the uniformed 
soldiers among the staff hint atmilitary ties 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-16/germany-s-hospitals-prepare-for-war-and-mass-casualties-as-russia-threat-looms?cmpid=091626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8956>
. Yet many meters beneath its busy wards lies a Cold War-era bunker that has 
suddenly become relevant again.

 * The underground medical facility was designed to let doctors continue 
treating injured soldiers in the event of a nuclear strike, and can house up to 
2,000 people for 90 days. For the better part of three decades, the facility 
sat dormant. Now, the German military is considering reviving it.
 * Across the country, government bodies are surveying Germany’s healthcare 
systems and infrastructure to see how — or if — they would stand up in a 
defense scenario.
 * Since Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, Germany has seen a surge in hybrid 
attacks, such as an attempted strike at an airport by an explosive-laden drone 
in Leipzig last month and various cyberattacks targeting hospitals.
 * German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius has warned that Russia may be in a 
position to attack NATO territory by 2029. If that were to happen, Germany 
would be obligated to come to the alliance’s defense, and given its position in 
the heart of Europe, the country would likely become a key transit hub for 
soldiers.
