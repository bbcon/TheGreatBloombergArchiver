Warming limit at risk

By Eric Roston 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQdgGkZhizQ/eric-roston?cmpid=BBD072726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7008>

A powerful El Niño already beginning to roil weather around the world may push 
the monthly global average temperature past 2C of warming for the first time on 
record, according to projections published by a University of Miami-based ocean 
and atmospheric research center.

The probability that a single month in early 2027 passes 2C has reached 35% to 
40%, said Ben Kirtman, dean of the Rosenstiel School of Marine, Atmospheric, 
and Earth Science at Miami.

“That’s a large probability,” he said. “That’s really large, and maybe that’s 
what people don’t appreciate.”
Smoke from wildfires near Lege-Cap-Ferret, France, on July 23.Photographer: 
Julien De Rosa/AFP
The odds of a month that’s 2C hotter than pre-industrial levels increased 
significantly in recent weeks as meteorologists upped their estimates for the 
current El Niño, which is likely to bethe strongest 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/el-nino-likely-strongest-seen-in-75-years-us-forecasters-say?cmpid=BBD072726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7008>
 in modern history. The hot El Niño is coming on top of a possible acceleration 
of global warming that scientists are still trying to unpack.

This summer has brought record heat and severe wildfires to Europe. As well as 
pushing up temperatures, the building El Niño is expected to worsen drought and 
strain food production in some parts of the world.

The earliest suggestion that 2C might be a reasonable upper limit for 
manageable climate change goes back to the mid-1970s. Four decades later, 
nearly 200 countries agreed in Paris to set it as their goal to limit climate 
change — while striving to keep heating below 1.5C as a long-term average of 20 
years or so.

The world may eclipse that stretch goal by the end of this decade 
<https://climate.copernicus.eu/rapid-approach-15degc-global-warming-threshold-paris-agreement>
, according to the EU’s Copernicus Climate Change Service.

Raging fires

By Sabrina Nelson Garcinuño 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXeC6LJ3eas/sabrina-nelson-garcinuno?cmpid=BBD072726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7008>
 andFrancois de Beaupuy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ABoiKtlFuOI/francois-de-beaupuy?cmpid=BBD072726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7008>

Wildfires in France and Spain that forced the evacuation of more than 300,000 
people have stabilized, but the onset of the summer’s fourth heat wave means 
little respite for emergency services.

There were no major changes overnight in the fire in the Gironde region on 
France’s southwest Atlantic coast, according to the regional government. That 
blaze had threatened the city of Bordeaux at the weekend. In Spain, authorities 
reported no significant overnight flare-ups or progression in the huge fires to 
the south and west of Madrid, but the blazes are yet to be contained.

“Yesterday we saw a positive trend, although it’s true that the situation 
remains very, very complex,” Spain’s Environmental Minister Sara Aagesen said 
in an interview with RNE.

Spain and France have been at the epicenter of Europe’s wildfire season, which 
started early this year and accelerated in recent weeks as successive heat 
waves baked vegetation, making it easier for flames to spread rapidly. Now 
firefighters are rushing to bring the blazes under control before another heat 
wave sears Western Europe from Tuesday.

These are not isolated incidents, but “the most painful expression of a 
climate emergency that is making sixth-generation wildfires increasingly 
ferocious,” Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said during a presentation at the 
Climate Refuge Network on Monday in Madrid. “It is the new normal: summers that 
are increasingly difficult, destructive, and, consequently, deadly.”
