Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the economy. Today, 
Tokyo-based correspondent Yusuke Maekawa explores how Japan’s trading houses 
are poised for record profits as they navigate global supply disruptions.

Japan’s trading houses spent decades securing resources around the world. In 
times of upheaval, that strategy is delivering a windfall.

Four of the nation’s five biggest lifted their profit forecasts last week, 
with Mitsubishi Corp., Sumitomo Corp. and Marubeni Corp. each projectingrecord 
earnings <bbg://news/stories/TNYSTQT96OSG> for the fiscal year through March. 
Surging copper, oil and liquefied natural gas prices are supercharging results.

Yet the boom highlights something more fundamental about the sogo shosha, as 
they’re known in Japanese: the power of combining ownership of vital resources 
with global networks built to move them wherever they’re needed.

The trading houses helped propel Japan’s emergence as an industrial powerhouse 
in the 20th century, scouring the globe for the raw materials that the 
resource-poor nation lacked. Their vast networks made them indispensable 
middlemen between Japanese companies and overseas suppliers.

Over time, that role came under pressure as globalization made commodities 
easier to source and customers worked directly with suppliers. The traditional 
trading function was diminished, leading to predictions that thesogo shosha 
might be entering their twilight years.
Mitsui & Co. Chief Executive Officer Kenichi Hori told Bloomberg News in May 
that the company is looking to invest in liquefied natural gas projects.

The response was transformational. By plowing billions of dollars into mines 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-19/mitsui-to-buy-australia-iron-ore-project-stake-for-5-3-billion?cmpid=BBBXT111026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11219>
, oil andgas fields 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-16/mitsubishi-to-buy-us-gas-assets-from-aethon-in-5-2-billion-deal?cmpid=BBBXT111026_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11219>
 and LNG projects, they became some of the biggest owners of resources without 
abandoning the global networks and trading expertise that first fueled their 
rise.

Today, the crisis in the Middle East is demonstrating that value. The trading 
houses have ownership stakes in resources from the Americas to Australia, and 
offices in more than 200 cities around the world. That makes them ideally 
placed to step into the gap left by supply disruptions in the Middle East, 
which supplied around 90% of Japan’s crude before the war began.

The bumper profits are also giving the trading houses firepower to expand.

Marubeni has boosted planned investment and other spending under its current 
three-year strategy, while Itochu Corp. earmarked ¥1.5 trillion ($9.5 billion)

As competition for resources intensifies, the trading houses are drawing again 
on the attributes that first defined them.

BNEF today

China’s natural gas storage capacity is expected to reach 109 billion cubic 
meters by 2035, according to BloombergNEF. Underground storage is seen driving 
most of the increase, with working gas capacity rising to Europe Perimeter 
levels, driven by a government push for supply security.
