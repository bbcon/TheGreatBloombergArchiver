• The ECB delivered its second consecutive 25-basis-point rate hike on Thursday, raising the deposit rate to 2.5% in line with consensus, with President Lagarde describing the move as a "no brainer"; markets are pricing a further hike as early as October 29.
• UK GDP growth beat expectations in July, with AI-linked activity cited as a contributing driver, contrasting sharply with France, which cut its 2026 GDP forecast and acknowledged it will miss its deficit target.
• Germany's AfD secured 44% of the vote in the Saxony-Anhalt state election — its best-ever result — falling three seats short of an outright majority and delivering a significant political shock.
• French bond yield premiums widened to their highest level since 2012, while German 30-year yields reached their highest risk premium since 2011, as energy-linked inflation and political risk compounded the move.

**Monetary Policy**
The ECB's Thursday hike reflects two reinforcing pressures: acute eurozone exposure to energy import costs, which amplifies the Middle East conflict's inflationary pass-through far more than in the US, and institutional memory of the 2022 episode when late tightening drew sustained criticism. ECB officials have acknowledged that their own forecasts assumed average oil below $90 per barrel, a baseline now materially stale with Brent above $100. Core inflation has continued to ease and services price growth has moderated, leading Oxford Economics to argue Thursday's move will be the last — but overnight index swaps imply a terminal rate near 3% by end-2027, and Deutsche Bank raises the possibility that AI investment and German defense spending may be lifting the neutral rate. Questions around Lagarde's tenure, with her memoir scheduled for January, added institutional uncertainty.

**Political Economy and UK Fiscal Stress**
The AfD result in Saxony-Anhalt — achieved against a backdrop of gasoline and diesel prices up approximately 40% since Hormuz disruptions began, and industrial closures by employers including Dow Inc. — reinforces European political fragmentation with direct policy implications. Further state elections are due September 20. UK Chancellor Healey emphasized fiscal discipline in his first major speech, but the government faces a deteriorating fiscal outlook heading into an autumn budget under pressure from food inflation forecast to exceed 6% by July 2027. Gilt yields are at their highest since 1998. Prime Minister Burnham's move to ban trade with Israeli West Bank settlements risks complicating relations with Washington. A UK air traffic control technical failure on Tuesday caused more than 1,750 flight cancellations. Novartis shares fell 10% after a third clinical-trial setback, dragging European healthcare stocks lower.
