Daily Brief

Friday, November 06, 2026

US

  • October nonfarm payrolls expected to show 65,000 increase; data due today, with consensus framing the print as a critical read on labor market resilience amid market volatility.
  • Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% Thursday — its worst session since 2020 — wiping $2.3 trillion in value as post-midterm AI regulation fears overwhelmed the market; futures recovered +1.3% by Friday morning.
  • Democrats' "blue tsunami" midterm sweep — capturing Congress and governorships in Texas, Ohio, Georgia — raised immediate fears of data center moratoriums affecting roughly 40–45% of planned US AI infrastructure capacity.
  • Secretary of State Rubio is said to be weighing departure from the administration; impeachment proceedings against Defense Secretary Hegseth were under discussion in the new Congress.

Post-Election Economic Implications

The midterm results introduced a meaningful near-term downside risk to US AI-driven capital expenditure, the primary driver of GDP acceleration in recent quarters. Goldman Sachs estimates more than 45% of planned data center capacity sits in states where newly elected Democratic governors campaigned on construction restrictions or moratoriums. Texas alone — 25% of total planned capacity — elected its first Democratic governor since 1990. Economists broadly expect any moratoriums to be temporary, designed to allow regulatory frameworks to form rather than halt the AI trajectory outright. Deutsche Bank's Brett Ryan noted the pullback could constructively eliminate "projects that were already looking shaky," potentially extending the investment cycle rather than truncating it. The more acute risk is a negative wealth effect: the S&P 500's decline, if sustained, could erode consumer confidence and weaken household spending, which remains a primary growth engine.

Labor Market and Fed

October payrolls, due today, are expected to show 65,000 jobs added — a figure that would represent a marked deceleration from prior months. Market expectations for further Federal Reserve rate hikes have faded materially in the wake of the equity selloff. The Fed's John Williams signaled a data-gathering posture, and fewer scheduled meetings are under consideration, returning the institution to a more deliberate pace.

Equity and Credit Markets

The Thursday session was indiscriminate: Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, and memory names all fell sharply before rebounding 4–9% in Friday premarket trading. ING moved to sell a $500 million data center loan portfolio, an early signal that credit markets are repricing AI infrastructure risk. Airbnb (+12%) and Cloudflare (+14%) were notable exceptions, reporting strong earnings. Hedge funds, already under pressure in October, faced forced deleveraging as crowded AI positions were unwound rapidly, amplifying the move. ---

Asia

  • South Korea's Kospi Index fell as much as 8.8%, triggering a circuit breaker; the MSCI Asia Pacific Index tumbled 4% and Japan's Nikkei 225 declined 6%, approaching bear market territory.
  • Taiwan's benchmark index fell 4.5%, entering a bear market, as Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each lost more than 6%.
  • Indian equities were the lone regional bright spot: the NSE Nifty 50 rose Friday, on track for its best weekly gain in more than five years, as investors rotated into markets with minimal AI exposure.
  • Twenty-two Asian billionaires lost a combined $18–21 billion in wealth, with SoftBank's Masayoshi Son shedding $3.9 billion.

Equity Markets: AI Rout Reverberates

The post-US-midterm selloff struck Asian markets with particular force given the region's heavy concentration in semiconductor and AI-infrastructure equities. South Korea's $1.7 trillion market decline — which had been elevated in part by government promotion as a political success — now presents a meaningful test of public confidence in President Lee's economic stewardship, with retail investors bearing disproportionate losses. Wealthy clients across Taiwan and Hong Kong faced margin calls on leveraged positions, and major hedge funds including ExodusPoint and Millennium sustained significant losses. The breadth of damage across strategies designed to perform independently of market direction underscores the degree of crowding that had built up in the AI trade.

China: Policy Support and Structural Doubts

China's CSI 300 fell more than any month in over a decade in October, with the tech-heavy Star 50 losing 24% and erasing more than $3 trillion in value. State-backed "national team" funds have stepped in as buyers of last resort, and Beijing this week enhanced rescue tools first deployed in 2024. The Star 50 posted its best session since July mid-week before retreating again. The structural tension remains unresolved: Chinese equities have historically served as fundraising vehicles rather than vehicles for household wealth creation, and persistent governance concerns limit the effectiveness of state support. Japan's nominal wage growth accelerated to its fastest pace in nearly three decades, reinforcing the Bank of Japan's hawkish posture. ---

Europe

  • Bank of England raised rates to 4% on Thursday; Governor Bailey stated a further hike is "probably more likely than not" if elevated energy costs return.
  • Stoxx Europe 600 fell 1.8% Friday but is outperforming global peers significantly — on course for its largest quarterly outperformance versus global equities since early last year.
  • Nominations for ECB President to succeed Christine Lagarde closed Friday at 5 p.m. Brussels time; Spain proposed Pablo Hernández de Cos, the Netherlands proposed Klaas Knot.

Bank of England Policy

The Bank of England's decision to raise rates to 4% came despite emerging optimism earlier in the week that potential de-escalation of the Iran conflict could ease energy price pressures. Bailey acknowledged that a jump in inflation is "quite baked in at this point" even under a rapid de-escalation scenario, reflecting the degree to which energy cost pass-through has already been embedded in the price level. The forward guidance — that another hike is more likely than not if energy costs re-accelerate — leaves the BoE in a tightening posture that diverges from fading Fed rate-hike expectations.

ECB Succession and European Markets

The ECB leadership contest entered a formal phase Friday with the nomination deadline. The two leading candidates — de Cos and Knot — represent meaningfully different policy temperaments, with Knot associated with a more hawkish tradition. The outcome will carry substantial implications for the ECB's pace of normalization heading into 2027. European equities have benefited from relative insulation from the AI selloff, and Barclays' Emmanuel Cau noted that "indiscriminate selling looks overdone" and that Europe may benefit further if Middle East energy tail risks continue to recede following Qatar's diplomatic outreach to Washington. ---

LatAm

  • Pope Leo XIV, the first American pontiff, began an 11-day, 19,000-mile tour of Uruguay, Argentina, and Peru today, entering a politically charged regional environment.
  • Paraguay's President Santiago Peña submitted his resignation following the collapse of a major commercial bank with government ties, though he vowed to contest a Senate removal vote to preserve potential immunity from criminal prosecution.
  • Brazil's President Lula defeated MAGA-aligned Flávio Bolsonaro, halting a broader regional conservative sweep and reshaping the political landscape ahead of Pope Leo's visit.

Political Economy

Paraguay's banking crisis represents the most acute near-term institutional stress in the region. The collapse of a systemically connected commercial institution — and the resulting political crisis — highlights the fragility of governance structures in smaller economies with concentrated financial systems. The legal distinction between resignation and removal carries direct consequences for Peña's criminal exposure, making the Senate process a high-stakes proceeding. Meanwhile, Brazil's election outcome reinforces a bifurcated regional political map: right-leaning governments in Argentina and Peru are aligning closely with Washington, while Lula's Brazil maintains an independent posture. Emerging market external debt reached a record $9.4 trillion in 2025 per World Bank data, with lower-income nations paying average rates of 8.2–9.4% on new borrowing — a structural constraint on regional fiscal space.

Pope Leo's Tour

The pontiff's Latin American visit carries geopolitical as well as pastoral dimensions. His public stance against the Iran war positioned him in implicit opposition to Trump's foreign policy during a period when Washington is actively seeking to deepen influence in the region. Both Fujimori and Milei have welcomed his arrival, though their immigration and foreign policy positions sit in tension with Leo's stated priorities. ---

Global

  • Crude oil fell sharply from near $120/barrel to around $91–98/barrel — a decline of approximately $20–25 — as Democratic midterm victories reduced perceived probability of further US-Iran escalation.
  • The global AI-infrastructure equity trade unwound violently: the Nasdaq 100's worst session since 2020, Nikkei approaching bear market, and Kospi triggering circuit breakers collectively signal a synchronized repricing of AI-related risk assets.
  • Qatar's emir spoke with Trump by phone on de-escalating Middle East tensions; oil pared earlier gains on the news, suggesting markets are closely tracking diplomatic signals.

Oil Markets and Geopolitics

Crude's $25 decline from last Friday's near-$120 high represents a sharp but incomplete reversal of the Iran-war risk premium. Middle East producers — Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Iraq, the UAE — have maintained combined exports near prewar levels, with flows through the Strait of Hormuz and bypass routes holding relatively stable. Executives at a major UAE energy conference this week described supply as robust, with Persian Gulf export volumes surging even as demand in China remains well below prewar levels. The critical uncertainty is Trump's next move: lacking a clear diplomatic off-ramp from the Iran conflict, and with his domestic political standing materially weakened, the range of foreign policy outcomes has widened. Traders who priced in escalation last week were wrong; those pricing in durable peace today may be equally mistaken.

AI Infrastructure and Global Capital Allocation

The midterm results have simultaneously triggered an equity rout and introduced a structural question about the pace of AI capital deployment globally. Goldman Sachs estimates place 45% of planned US data center capacity in states with newly elected governors opposed to current construction timelines. The IEA separately reported that global renewable power capacity additions are set to fall 11% in 2026 — the first decline in over two decades — driven by a slowdown in Chinese solar installations, before recovering in 2027. The intersection of AI infrastructure pullback, energy price volatility, and the renewable deceleration presents a complex backdrop for global capital expenditure planning heading into 2027.
