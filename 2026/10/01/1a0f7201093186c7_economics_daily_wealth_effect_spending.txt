Wealth-Effect Spending

Americans have been contending with historically high inflation for more than 
half a decade now. As gasoline and diesel costs climb anew, it’s perhaps no 
surprise thatconsumer confidence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/us-consumer-confidence-plunges-to-lowest-level-since-2014?cmpid=BBD100126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9537>
 just hit its lowest level since 2014 — back when the US was stuck in a subpar 
recovery from the credit meltdown.

And yet, US consumer spending last month clocked the biggest gain in more than 
a year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/us-consumer-spending-rises-by-most-in-a-year-core-pce-up-0-2?cmpid=BBD100126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9537>
. Inflation-adjusted, the monthly increase was 0.6%, data showed on Wednesday.

In China, poor sentiment surveys match up with weak consumer spending — 
Chinese retail sales growth wasbarely above zero 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/us-consumer-confidence-plunges-to-lowest-level-since-2014?cmpid=BBD100126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9537>
 last month. But in the US, there are enough households with enough financial 
firepower to keep the spending juggernaut going.

It’s not strong incomes that are behind this. The 0.3% monthly rise in 
after-tax personal income recorded last month was entirely wiped out by a 0.3% 
increase in prices, Diane Swonk, chief economist at KPMG, highlighted in anote 
<https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/august-2026-pce.html> Wednesday.

“Savings kept the party going,” she wrote. The personal saving rate is now at 
its lowest in almost four years, at 4.1% last month. That cushion “is shrinking 
fast,” Swonk said.

Another factor is the wealth effect 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-household-net-worth-jumped-last-quarter-on-stock-market-rally?cmpid=BBD100126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9537>
. Data earlier this month showed US household net worth climbed by a record 
$12.8 trillion in the second quarter, propelled by a rising stock market. 
Evercore ISI economists including Francesca Ponziani estimate that around half 
of the second-quarter gain in consumer spending came from the wealth effect.

Ponziani and her colleagues reckon that this “works with a lag.” That means 
that stronger household balance sheets will be a tailwind for consumer spending 
through the rest of this year, they wrote in a note this week.

Still, it means financial markets need to keep bubbly. Any major tumble — 
triggered by surging bond yields or something else — could leave spending 
vulnerable.

Need-to-Know Research

Taking a look back at China’s August economic indicators, not only did they 
reinforce the “two-speed economy” narrative — solid manufacturing alongside 
weak consumption — but there’s a two-speed dynamic evenwithin manufacturing.

So observes Jeremy Stevens, an international economist at Standard Bank Group 
in Beijing. While industrial output posted a 5.2% year-on-year rise, only 286 
of the 626 reported categories saw positive gains, Stevens wrote in a recent 
note. The median product shrank by 1.8%. “Computers and handsets, solar and 
wind, power batteries and charging infrastructure have all slipped into 
outright contraction in volume terms.”

“This surviving core is capital-intensive and too narrow to generate  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-26/china-s-economic-regime-shift-may-sow-global-risk-new-economy?cmpid=BBD100126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261001&utm_content=9537>
the domestic demand the transition” toward advanced manufacturing was supposed 
to produce, Stevens wrote. China’s economy now has three distinct segments: “a 
star strategic core that is still accelerating, an export-electronics middle 
now exposed to trade restrictions and the global technology cycle, and a legacy 
economy that has stabilised only at a low base.”
