Jimmie Åkesson won’t become prime minister in coming elections in Sweden but 
in a way he’s already won 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-10/sweden-election-agenda-set-by-far-right-leader-jimmie-akesson?cmpid=091026_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8740>
. For more than a decade now, the 47-year-old far-right populist has been 
steering the national conversation in one of the most stubbornly progressive 
corners of Europe.

 * So when Swedes cast their votes this Sunday, it will be in a political 
landscape remade by the leader of the Sweden Democrats. With days left before 
ballots are cast, Åkesson is back in his hometown of Sölvesborg along the 
Baltic coast for one last push to take his party into government after 16 years 
on the outside. His look is smart but approachable: slicked-back hair, neatly 
trimmed beard, suit, no tie, unbuttoned collar.
 * The crowd roars as he comes on stage with a laugh and swig from a water 
bottle in the late summer sun to deliver a stump speech on the grounds of a 
medieval castle.
 * “We’ve pressed the other parties and now they all sound more or less like 
us,” Åkesson said in an interview at the Aug. 29 event. “That is, of course, 
already a victory in itself.”
 * Even so, his path to power is complicated. Polls suggest Åkesson will again 
outperform Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson’s center-right Moderate Party but 
that the opposition Social Democrats, who have stepped onto his turf with 
promises of tighter border controls, will win by a hair.
 * Long ostracized because of their roots in Swedish neo-Nazi groups active in 
the 1980s, the Sweden Democrats have methodically worked their way in from the 
cold at a time when their ideological brethren are on the march.
