**Monetary Policy**
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's silence on the policy outlook at an IMF event in Bangkok was interpreted by markets as confirmation of a hold at the October 29–30 FOMC meeting. Futures markets pared back rate-hike probabilities, and US equities extended modest gains. Warsh's restraint is notable given that 10-year Treasury yields remain near 5.10%, levels the Trump administration has publicly flagged as inconsistent with its lower-borrowing-cost agenda. IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva argued separately that US fiscal deficits are a structural driver of elevated global yields — a framing that implicitly challenges the administration's growth-centric fiscal strategy.

**Equity and Credit Markets**
CoreWeave remains the focal point of the week's technology selloff. Its bonds traded at distressed levels earlier in the week, with markets pricing a greater-than-60% default probability within five years — a direct reflection of how rising risk-free rates interact with heavily leveraged AI infrastructure business models. The stock partially recovered Thursday following Nvidia's public endorsement. S&P 500 futures pointed modestly higher heading into Friday's close, though the Nasdaq's 3% weekly decline signals that the AI valuation reset remains unresolved.
