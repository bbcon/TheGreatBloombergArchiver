Just before new US tariffs were set to hit Canadian goods at midnight, the two 
governments announced a three-day delay to give talks a little more time.

Negotiators will now have until the end of the day Friday to finish their work 
to avoid the duties, according to a White Houseproclamation 
<https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/temporary-suspension-of-additional-duties-to-offset-canadian-discrimination-against-the-commerce-of-the-united-states-with-respect-to-alcoholic-beverages-dairy-and-motor-vehicles/>
, which fixes the new effective date for the American import taxes on Aug. 22 
at 12:01 a.m. Washington time.

In a social media post 
<https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117119761850520698> less than 
two hours before 50% duties took effect on about $20 billion worth of Canadian 
merchandise, President Donald Trump said the two sides, “subject to the 
finalization of documents, have a DEAL!” and added that the Keystone XL 
pipeline “may be awoken from the grave!”

Back in April, Trump signed a permit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-30/trump-signs-permit-for-pipeline-from-canada-echoing-keystone-xl?cmpid=BBD081926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7849>
 authorizing a quasi-revival of the canceled Keystone XL project. But his line 
about the deal being “subject to the finalization of documents” is key because 
the fine print near the finish line is usually the most tedious and contentious 
part of trade negotiations.

The US Trade Representatives office elaborated, saying this in a post 
<https://x.com/USTradeRep/status/2089905312337658144> on X: “The deal will 
include comprehensive market access for all American goods, economic security 
commitments, digital trade alignment, and many important provisions that will 
continue to protect our market and American workers, along with our Canadian 
partners.”

Whether that’s wishful thinking remains unclear. Canadian Prime Minister Mark 
Carney stopped short of exclaiming about an agreement or mentioning details 
like the pipeline, saying in a statement that “substantial progress has been 
made, although there is important work still to be done.”

70% Export Drop

There’s a long list of sticking points that neither side mentioned, ranging 
fromauto tariff levels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/stellantis-union-says-automaker-may-shut-and-sell-canada-plant?cmpid=BBD081926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7849>
 to a Canadian ban on American-made booze.

Chris Swonger, head of the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States, 
said exports of US spirits to Canada have dropped 70% inprovincial boycotts 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/canada-must-remove-retaliation-to-avoid-new-tariffs-greer-says?cmpid=BBD081926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7849>
 since 2025.

“As discussions continue over the next few days, we encourage leaders on both 
sides of the border to reach a negotiated solution that gets American spirits 
back on retail shelves in all Canadian provinces and returns the spirits sector 
to a zero-for-zero tariff framework,” Swonger wrote in an emailed statement 
late Tuesday.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-19/trump-delays-50-tariffs-on-canadian-products-video?cmpid=BBD081926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7849>

After a marathon day of deliberations for stakeholders in US-Canada trade like 
the car industry, Flavio Volpe, president of Canada’s Automotive Parts 
Manufacturers’ Association, indicated that the waiting game for more clarity 
would resume again Wednesday. “My mother used to say, “ok it’s bedtime now”. 
See you all tomorrow,” hewrote 
<https://x.com/FlavioVolpe1/status/2089901669869928730> on social media late 
Tuesday.

Other observers, curious to see a US president’s first-ever use of Section 338 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-24/trump-s-tariff-options-after-supreme-court-defeat-sections-122-338-and-more?cmpid=BBD081926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7849>
 of the Tariff Act of 1930, expressed a temporary sense of letdown.

“Good news for North American trade, the US, and Canada,” Peter Harrell, 
visiting scholar at Georgetown Law School,wrote on X 
<https://x.com/petereharrell/status/2089907790496030866> about the 11th-hour 
deadline extension. “Less good news for those few of us who would have been 
fascinated by the legal arguments over Section 338 tariffs.”

Deborah Elms, head of trade policy at the Hinrich Foundation, said the latest 
delay in US tariffs “does highlight the continued uncertainty in shipping to 
the United States” and raises questions about “why companies aren’t simply 
pivoting away from the US market.”

“Let me say again that tariffs on most Canadian goods are still zero, have 
been at zero for qualifying goods for decades, and may stay at zero. A rational 
firm would look at the costs of pivoting and, likely, opt to delay,” she wrote 
Wednesday onLinkedIn 
<https://www.linkedin.com/posts/deborah-elms_tariffs-on-hold-as-trump-claims-deal-with-share-7495685627456163840-Czs1/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAQhrDgB0bYwZGt1gaWdmMK9EIR1HhRFMEU>
. “But continuing uncertainty and a looming 50% tariff may change that 
calculus.”
