**Political Economy**
Brazil's post-election period is defined by two overlapping tensions: domestic fiscal credibility and regional diplomacy. Lula's public commitment to fiscal discipline attempts to anchor market expectations after a campaign that raised concerns about spending trajectories. Simultaneously, the Argentina-Brazil relationship has deteriorated sharply, with Milei's refusal to recognize the electoral outcome and his role in Bolsonaro's exit prompting formal diplomatic protests from Brasília. Pope Leo XIV's intervention reflects the broader concern that South America's two largest economies are entering a period of sustained political estrangement with potential trade and investment implications that remain difficult to quantify.
