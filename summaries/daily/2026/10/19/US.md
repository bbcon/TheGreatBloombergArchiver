**Equity Markets**
US equity futures rebounded Monday, with Nasdaq 100 futures up 1.1% and S&P 500 futures up 0.8%, offering partial relief after a sharp two-session decline in which the index dropped more than 1% on both Wednesday and Friday of the prior week — ending a 53-session streak without a single 1% drawdown, the longest since January 2020. The semiconductor index SOX remains up approximately 60% year-to-date but is testing a resistance level reached twice in the prior three months. The rotation away from technology toward consumer staples ahead of earnings season suggests investors are reassessing AI-driven growth expectations rather than simply taking profits.

**Credit and Monetary Signals**
The more consequential development may be in credit markets. High-yield spreads have risen sharply in recent weeks without an identifiable external catalyst, with debt issued to finance AI data center construction seeing particularly acute repricing — CoreWeave bonds now yield approximately 15%, signaling genuine solvency concern. Separately, Treasuries appear to be reclaiming their haven function: the sharp rise in 10-year yields has partially reversed, and the correlation between Nasdaq weakness and Treasury strength has re-emerged. The near-term trajectory of the AI trade, and with it broader equity momentum, will likely depend on whether credit stress in AI-related issuance remains contained.
