Germany’s beleaguered coalition is facing an economic hit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/rhine-falls-to-lowest-since-1880-disrupting-european-trade?cmpid=BBD080526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7339>
 from reduced barge traffic on the Rhine, whilewater shortages on the Danube 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-05/danube-river-hits-record-lows-and-threatens-romania-hungary-nuclear-plants?cmpid=BBD080526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7339>
 prompted voluntary production cutbacks by carmakers and battery plants in 
Hungary as parliament in Budapest delayed sessions to save energy.

Downriver in Romania, the historic drought is adding to power-supply strains, 
double-digitinflation and a stagnant economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/romania-narrowly-averts-junk-rating-on-debt-amid-budget-scrutiny?cmpid=BBD080526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7339>
 that together leave Bucharest little room to maneuver.

The upshot is a string of overlapping shocks that are piling pressure on a 
political class that’s been deadlocked for months over the formation of a new 
government.
The Rhine River between Oberwesel and Loreley, Germany, on July 29.
Photographer: Alex Kraus/Bloomberg
In the firing line are Romania’s mainstream parties, which remain deeply 
divided after the largest of them pulled the plug on the previous coalition in 
May amid disagreements over fiscal austerity needed to rein in one of the 
European Union’s widest budget deficits.

Confronted by historically low levels on the Danube River, the caretaker 
government had to resort to unprecedented interventions this week. It deployed 
the militaryto blast rock  
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/romania-blasts-rock-to-redirect-danube-flows-to-nuclear-plant?cmpid=BBD080526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7339>
to divert flows to feed and cool the only reactor of its two still in operation 
at a nuclear plant that usually supplies 20% of the country’s electricity needs.

Lawmakers were meanwhile recalled in emergency sessions to pass key reforms 
needed to tap over €3 billion in EU funding.

A prolonged political vacuum has ramifications beyond just Romania. A NATO 
member, it hosts military assets including an air base used by the US for 
logistics support in the Iran conflict, and is a key route fortransporting goods
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/ukraine-railway-seeks-new-grain-routes-as-russia-strikes-ports?cmpid=BBD080526_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7339>
to and from neighboring Ukraine.

The stasis only helps the far right, which already leads in the polls. The 
so-called cordon sanitaire keeping them from power may buckle before the next 
election in 2028.
