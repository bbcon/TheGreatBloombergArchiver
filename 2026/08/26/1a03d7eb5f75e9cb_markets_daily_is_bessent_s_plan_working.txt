Bond bounce

A week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent jolted markets with his plan to 
bring down US borrowing costs, andthe impact is becoming clearer. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/short-squeeze-in-us-swaps-options-shows-bessent-put-at-work?cmpid=082626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8190>

Here’s what’s been happening:

 * Yields have inched lower. The 10-year US yield stands at 4.64% after 
touching 4.75% last week. Market moves suggest they would be higher absent the 
intervention.
 * The “Bessent put” effect. Traders say they know there is a big buyer in the 
market, and are reluctant to stand in the way.
 * Swap spreads have narrowed. Treasuries have outperformed 
equivalent-maturity swaps, narrowing the 30-year spread between the two to the 
smallest since February.
 * Lower oil prices help. With crude down about 5% in the past week, the bond 
rally looks like an oil-assisted move, rather than the durable shift Bessent 
wanted, says MLIV strategist Brendan Fagan.
 * Citadel flips to bullish. Strategist Frank Flight, who last month warned of 
a “cruel summer,” now sees conditionsripe for a bond rally. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/citadel-securities-flight-reverses-bearish-call-on-long-bonds?cmpid=082626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8190>
Bessent’s actions “all point to someone ready to do whatever it takes to 
achieve their goals,” says Jason Williams of Citi.

Of course, criticism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/druckenmiller-bessent-s-early-mentor-calls-bond-buys-a-mistake?cmpid=082626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8190>
 of the Treasury’s meddling and skepticism that it’ll work over the long-run 
haven’t gone away. And the moves have been fairly small. The 30-year yield is 
just 15 basis points from the highest since 2007.

But with midterm elections approaching, there’s more talk that Donald Trump 
and Bessent will attempt to bend markets to their will. In stocks, traders 
speculate that Trump will want to keep the market humming and they arebracing 
for surprises 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/vix-curve-bump-shows-stock-market-anxiety-growing-by-midterms?cmpid=082626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8190>
.

Any sign from Fed Chairman Warsh that he’s on board with Bessent’s plan would 
give investors a risk-on signal, says Brent Kochuba of SpotGamma.

“You can assume that Trump and Bessent are going to pump this thing,” he says.
