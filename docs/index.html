US

  • US private payrolls (ADP) beat consensus for October, with companies adding more jobs than expected — an early signal of continued labor market resilience heading into year-end.
  • The S&P 500 rose 1.6% Wednesday following the Democratic congressional sweep; bond yields and oil fell as markets interpreted gridlock favorably.
  • The debt ceiling — roughly $800 billion below the $41.1 trillion limit — emerges as the dominant near-term fiscal risk under a divided government.
  • AI sector rotation intensified: big tech hyperscalers rallied while semiconductor names (Qualcomm -5%, ARM -3.4%) and data-center developers sold off.

Election Aftermath and Market Reaction

Democrats' decisive sweep of Congress on Tuesday delivered a result markets initially feared but ultimately embraced. The S&P 500 gained 1.6% and the VIX posted its sharpest single-day decline since April 2025, as investors concluded that divided government eliminates the tail risk of further unfunded tax cuts and constrains executive overreach. The historical pattern — Republican president facing a Democratic Congress has tended to deliver positive returns for both equities and bonds — provided a useful analytical anchor. Bond yields fell and inflation swap markets reacted positively, suggesting participants view reduced fiscal expansion as disinflationary. The scale of the Democratic victory also precluded a contested outcome, removing the near-term political uncertainty that had weighed on positioning.

Federal Reserve and Monetary Policy

The Fed emerged as an institutional beneficiary of the election outcome. Senate Democratic control effectively neutralizes Trump's ability to pack the Fed board with loyalists beyond the single scheduled vacancy — Powell's seat in 2028. Chair Kevin Warsh, confirmed by the narrowest margin in Fed history, will now need to cultivate the incoming majority. The attempted removal of Governor Lisa Cook, who voted to hike rates last month, loses practical force under the new Senate arithmetic. Nomura economists characterized the reduction in risks to Fed credibility as meaningful.

Debt Ceiling and Year-End Outlook

Deutsche Bank's George Saravelos identified the debt ceiling as "the single biggest source of domestic policy uncertainty remaining." With the X-date projected in the first half of 2027, the Treasury bill curve remains orderly for now but prior episodes suggest that could change rapidly as the deadline approaches. Trading desks, led by Citadel Securities, are nonetheless constructive on a year-end equity rally, citing depressed institutional positioning and the anticipated Anthropic IPO as a potential catalyst for technology re-engagement. ---

Asia

  • Indonesia's Q3 GDP growth accelerated to 5.5%, beating consensus, as government stimulus supported household consumption and investment despite challenging global conditions.
  • Asian chipmakers sold off sharply in a post-election rotation: SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics tumbled on fears that a Democrat-controlled Congress would tighten AI and data-center regulation.
  • Toyota shares surged more than 16% — the largest single-day move since 1987 — after raising its annual profit forecast on strong hybrid demand and announcing an expanded buyback.
  • China blocked the sale of ABB's robotics unit to SoftBank, citing data and technology security concerns; Chinese equities remain in a bear market, with nearly $3 trillion in value erased year-to-date.

Macro Data: Indonesia Outperforms

Indonesia's Q3 GDP print of 5.5% represented a meaningful upside surprise, with domestic demand — particularly household consumption and government-led investment — serving as the primary growth engine. The result is notable given the absence of significant AI-linked export tailwinds that have benefited peers, and underscores the resilience of Southeast Asia's largest economy to external headwinds including elevated energy prices and global financial volatility.

Banking Sector Under Pressure

Asia-Pacific bank earnings are deteriorating. National Australia Bank reported a rise in credit impairment charges and announced cuts of up to 2,500 roles, while Singapore's DBS suffered a decline in markets trading income. UOB flagged narrowing interest margins amid intense deposit competition, and Westpac reported impairment charges more than doubling year-on-year. The common thread across Australian and Singaporean lenders is higher funding costs compressing net interest income ahead of any meaningful rate relief — a challenging setup for upcoming results from Commonwealth Bank and Japan's megabanks.

AI and Technology Dynamics

China's block of the ABB-SoftBank robotics deal reflects Beijing's sharpened focus on technology sovereignty in the AI era. Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs characterized the US Democratic sweep as having "major implications for AI and data center regulation," driving the rotation away from hardware manufacturers and toward Chinese internet platforms and other discounted assets. Hong Kong stocks staged a recovery as capital rotated into perceived beneficiaries of reduced US AI capital spending. ---

Europe

  • The Bank of England raised interest rates unanimously for the first time in more than three years, with consumer price inflation running close to 5%.
  • The Stoxx Europe 600 and FTSE 100 were on pace to close at all-time highs, carried by cyclical rotation following the US election outcome.
  • France faces a nationwide strike and ongoing budget deadlock, extending more than a month of protests over the cost of living with elections six months away.

Bank of England Policy

The BoE's unanimous decision to raise rates marks a material shift after an extended pause, responding to CPI that appears to be stabilizing near 5% — well above the 2% target. The unanimity of the vote signals that the committee assessed the inflation picture as sufficiently entrenched to warrant action, even as global growth signals remain mixed. The move places the BoE alongside a broader tightening trend, though the pace and terminal rate remain uncertain given the UK's particular exposure to energy cost pass-through and wage dynamics.

French Political and Fiscal Stress

France's political situation is deteriorating ahead of elections. Major unions from public and private sectors have called a nationwide mobilization, with the Yellow Vest movement and student protesters joining organized labor in a broadening coalition of discontent. Parliamentary budget negotiations remain deadlocked, and President Macron's critics are actively seeking to exploit public frustration over the cost of living. The political calendar — six months to elections — reduces incentives for compromise and raises the probability that fiscal uncertainty persists into 2027.

Markets

European equities rallied in sympathy with the US post-election move, with cyclicals outperforming and the rotation away from semiconductor-linked names benefiting more diversified continental benchmarks. UBS confirmed approximately 200 redundancies in London as Credit Suisse integration nears completion, spanning wealth management and investment banking divisions. Meta raised €9 billion in a euro bond debut that drew record demand, suggesting credit appetite for high-quality issuers remains robust despite elevated rates. ---

Brazil: Political Risk as Template

Brazil's experience from its own 2022 election cycle continues to serve as an instructive case study in how political uncertainty interacts with monetary policy. The post-Lula-victory selloff forced Banco do Brasil to abandon a rate reduction, illustrating the vulnerability of EM central banks to sudden shifts in risk sentiment. The contrast with the relatively sanguine US market reaction to the Democratic sweep — where yields fell and equities rose — highlights how domestic institutional credibility and fiscal trajectory shape the market's interpretation of political outcomes.

Chile and Regional Monetary Policy

Chile's rate expectations represent the most significant near-term policy development in the region. Investors are pulling forward their timeline for the first rate increase since 2022, suggesting that inflation dynamics have proven more persistent than the central bank's prior guidance implied. The shift in market pricing is occurring rapidly, indicating a meaningful reassessment of the disinflation trajectory. Elsewhere, Mexico's Banxico appears set to hold, consistent with its cautious posture amid still-elevated core inflation and peso dynamics tied to US policy uncertainty. ---

US-Iran Conflict: Shifting Calculus

The clearest immediate market signal from the US midterms was in energy. Brent crude fell more than 8% on Wednesday — its largest single-session decline in months — as traders reassessed the probability of further US-Iran military escalation. The conflict, now in its ninth month, has pushed oil prices more than 50% higher year-to-date, depleted US interceptor missile stockpiles, and contributed materially to the inflation that the election results suggest voters held against the administration. Vice President Vance explicitly cited the war as a primary reason for Republican losses, stating it "cost his party the Senate for at least six years." Whether the political pressure translates into a genuine diplomatic pivot remains uncertain — Iran retains leverage through Strait of Hormuz disruptions and Houthi operations against Saudi Arabia, and Trump retains executive authority — but the drumbeat of escalation has measurably softened.

COP31 and Energy Transition

COP31 convenes next week against an unusual backdrop: the energy shock from the Iran conflict is simultaneously straining fossil-fuel-dependent economies and accelerating their solar and electrification transitions, particularly in emerging markets. Bangladesh, where solar panel sales have risen 50% year-on-year, exemplifies a pattern visible across South and Southeast Asia. Australia's Chris Bowen, as COP31's president of negotiations, is seeking a binding commitment on the 35% electrification target — China's October endorsement of the goal represents meaningful momentum, though translating voluntary intent into enforceable commitment remains the structural challenge. Ireland's proposal to delay EU methane rules for energy importers illustrates the ongoing tension between climate ambition and near-term energy security.
