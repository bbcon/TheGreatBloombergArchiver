Warsh’s Choice

The 100-plus forecasters in the Bloomberg survey for Wednesday’s Federal 
Reserve interest-rate decision are almost entirely united: policymakers will 
stand pat. But that near-unanimity masks what for many — if not most — is one 
of the most difficult calls in years.

One thing is clear: at least some Fed voters likely want to hike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/fed-s-cook-says-she-s-prepared-to-act-if-inflation-doesn-t-cool?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
. So, if the Fed indeed leaves its benchmark unchanged today, many see at least 
a couple ofdissents 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/fed-s-logan-favors-modestly-higher-rates-to-lower-inflation?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
 in favor of the first rate increase in three years.

What’s also clear is that the new chairman, Kevin Warsh, has been talking tough
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-15/kevin-warsh-talks-the-talk-on-inflation-while-leaving-the-walk-in-question?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
 on inflation. In congressional testimony this month, Warsh played down the 
most recent, soft, USinflation report 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/us-cpi-falls-for-the-first-time-since-2020-core-gauge-unchanged?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
, saying that it’s not time for “mission accomplished,” and that “there’s 
plenty of work to do.” So it would appear odd if he cited the latest data as a 
reason to keep rates changed this week.

A hike would give Warsh instant inflation-fighting credibility 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/citadel-securities-sees-warsh-delivering-surprise-fed-rate-hike?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
, after opponents had painted him as being chosen to do President Donald 
Trump’s bidding and slash interest rates. (Trump argued once again on Monday 
that “we should have the lowest interest rate in the world.“)
Kevin WarshPhotographer: Daniel Heuer/Bloomberg
Some Fed watchers reckon that, should he choose to move, he’d be able to swing 
the entire Federal Open Market Committee behind him — a powerful signal of 
leadership. Raising now also has the advantage, advocates say, of tamping down 
price pressures well ahead of November congressional elections. Should 
inflation pick up again, lifting the benchmark on election eve, or failing to 
respond, would be all the more contentious.

The consensus, however, is for no change, given that underlying inflation is 
expected to trend lower — not least if the job marketcontinues to soften 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-02/us-hiring-slows-sharply-curbing-recent-momentum-in-job-growth?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
 as it did in June. The Fed’s preferred core price gauge, stripping out food 
and energy costs, is seen rising a tame 0.2% for June, though the year-on-year 
forecast of 3.3% is still well above the 2% target.

“Whichever way the FOMC decision turns out, we will have learned something 
about how to parse Warsh’s future comments,” Lou Crandall, senior economist at 
Wrightson ICAP, wrote in a note Monday. “We’ll find out this week whether his 
emphatic remarks to Congress earlier this month were just rhetorical flourishes 
or reflected actionable convictions about the current outlook.”

Need-to-Know Research

What determines 10-year Treasury yields 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/biggest-debate-at-state-street-is-where-bessent-bond-metric-goes?cmpid=BBD072926_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260729&utm_content=7032>
 — the financial benchmark that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent identified as 
the most important for the economy? JPMorgan Chase analysts recently took a 
look at that in a primer on the whole $31 trillion US government bond market.

Over the long term, the biggest factor is expectations for Fed policy, 
strategists led by Jay Barry wrote in theirHitchhiker’s Guide to US Treasuries. 
Over the past 30 years, they calculated that Fed rate expectations explained 
roughly 87% of 10-year yield variation. That compares with 95% for two-year 
note yields, and 71% for 30-year bond yields, they wrote.
Note: The 1YX1Y OIS rate is one gauge of investor expectations for the Fed’s 
benchmark
“Beyond their expectations for Fed policy, investors require extra 
compensation for the interest rate risk and funding lockup inherent in holding 
long-duration Treasury bonds,” the team said, defining the so-called term 
premium. That element is influenced by supply and demand in the Treasuries 
market, along with the volatility of expectations for Fed policy and inflation, 
they said.
