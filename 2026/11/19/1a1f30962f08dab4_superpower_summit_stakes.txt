Donald Trump put his personal relationships with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin 
front and center in Shenzhen on Thursday,calling both leaders friends 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/xi-says-he-s-committed-to-working-with-trump-as-two-leaders-meet?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11610>
 as he prepared for an unprecedented three-way summit that could have 
consequences far beyond China.

The US president met Putin 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/trump-meets-with-russia-s-putin-for-first-time-in-over-a-year?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11610>
 for the first time in more than a year, touting their “great relationship” as 
he pressed for an end to Russia’s war in Ukraine. Hours later, he sat down with 
Xi amid renewed tensions over technology and trade, while signalinghe wants 
China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/trump-pushes-for-reluctant-xi-to-curb-china-s-nuclear-arsenal?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11610>
 to curb its rapidly growing nuclear arsenal.

The warm words belie the difficult issues separating the three powers. Trump’s 
Ukraine diplomacy has unnerved European allies, China has shown little appetite 
for nuclear limits, and fresh US restrictions on advanced chips are straining a 
fragile trade truce with Beijing. The trilateral will put the leaders of the 
world’s three biggest military powers, and holders of the vast majority of its 
nuclear weapons, around the same table.

Japan Tobacco is putting an $8.9 billion price tag on its decision to stay in 
Russia after the invasion of Ukraine. The cigarette maker will takea ¥1.4 
trillion charge <> after Moscow seized control of its seven local subsidiaries, 
wiping out what had been a crucial profit engine. Russia accounted for about 
10% of sales but a quarter of adjusted operating profit. The hit will push JT 
to a full-year loss, halve its year-end dividend and cost its top executives 
pay and bonuses, in a stark reckoning for one of the major foreign companies 
that remained in Russia.
