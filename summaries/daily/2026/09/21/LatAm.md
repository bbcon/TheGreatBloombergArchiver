**Brazil Political Economy**
Brazil's ethanol program, long a source of bipartisan support and a competitive advantage in low-carbon fuels, is becoming a flashpoint in a tightening presidential race. The government's decision to raise the ethanol blend ratio in gasoline has fractured the traditional coalition behind the policy, with fuel-cost concerns feeding into broader inflation anxiety among voters. The development intersects with the global energy shock: higher oil prices driven by the US-Iran conflict are amplifying fuel-cost sensitivity across the region, and African central banks — facing similar dynamics — are signaling rates will stay elevated for longer to shield against energy pass-through.
