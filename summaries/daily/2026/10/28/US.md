**Monetary Policy**
The Federal Reserve's October decision is the immediate focal point, with consensus firmly in the hold camp despite September inflation printing above expectations. Chairman Kevin Warsh's post-decision press conference carries unusual weight: market participants remain in what analysts describe as a "discovery process" regarding his communications approach, having observed only three post-decision conferences since he took office in May. Vice Chair Jefferson and New York Fed President Williams have both signaled the Fed can afford patience, but a hawkish hold message — clarifying the committee's tolerance for above-target inflation — will be the key interpretive challenge. Bond traders who have built up bullish positions face the risk of a sharp reversal if Warsh's tone disappoints.

**Equity Markets**
Equity market internals have deteriorated notably. Roughly 40% of the 124,750 possible stock pairs within the S&P 500 are currently negatively correlated, an unusually high figure reflecting zero-sum competition driven by AI trade dynamics and elevated oil prices. The equal-weighted S&P 500 has meaningfully underperformed, and valuation multiples have compressed to three-year lows. Robinhood fell 8% after missing third-quarter revenue and earnings estimates, while NXP Semiconductors dropped 7% on a disappointing outlook. The AI hyperscaler earnings — Microsoft up 36% since July, Alphabet down 6.3% over the same period — represent the week's critical test of whether capital expenditure is translating into returns.
