**Economic Data**
The ISM Services prices-paid component for September delivered the week's most important domestic data surprise, reaching levels exceeded only briefly during the 2008 oil spike — a reading that reinforces the case for sustained inflation even as the goods disinflation narrative persists. SocGen's model, incorporating the lag from diesel prices above $6.50 per gallon and US refineries running at 97% capacity, suggests September CPI — due next week — could print at 4.0% year-on-year versus a 3.5–3.6% consensus. That discrepancy would represent a material upside shock. Consumer confidence (Conference Board) fell to a 12-year low midweek, while one-year inflation expectations in the New York Fed survey rose to 3.9%, a three-year high. Against that, retail sales data beat expectations, illustrating the bifurcated consumer picture that has characterized this cycle.

**Monetary Policy and Rates**
Wednesday's FOMC minutes showed all 19 officials backing September's hike unanimously, with "several participants" characterizing policy as not yet restrictive and "a number" viewing a higher rate path as warranted under their modal — not merely risk-management — outlooks. Markets have priced a further hike at approximately 96% probability. The 10-year yield's breach of its 2007 peak is analytically significant beyond the headline: Ray Dalio warned on Bloomberg Television that China and Japan may reduce Treasury demand given geopolitical friction and an adverse debtor-creditor dynamic, adding a structural dimension to what has thus far been framed primarily as an inflation story. Goldman Sachs continues to forecast a year-end decline to 4.75%; Bank of America projects 5.0% but acknowledges upside risk.

**Equity Markets and Corporate News**
Deutsche Bank's Bankim Chadha projects 34% S&P 500 EPS growth in Q3 versus a 27% consensus, setting up above-average beats as earnings season opens with the large banks. The S&P 500 fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq 100 posted its worst session in over seven weeks Thursday, after initial OpenAI revenue reports disappointed, before Friday futures stabilized on clarification that annualized run-rate revenue is tracking toward $70 billion or above by year-end. PepsiCo cut its earnings outlook mid-week, citing slower North American recovery and cost pressure — a rare negative data point signaling that consumer stress is not yet fully visible in aggregate spending data. The 30-year mortgage rate hit 7.38%, its highest since January, adding to affordability strain ahead of midterms. The Trump administration suspended Microsoft, Adobe, and six additional companies from the H-1B green card sponsorship program, escalating immigration enforcement targeting the technology sector's foreign-talent pipeline at a moment when AI competitiveness is a stated national priority — a tension the administration has not yet resolved publicly.
