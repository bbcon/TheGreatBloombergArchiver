Silver lining

For all the anxiety that’s been gripping the world’s biggest bond market, some 
investors see a compelling reason to buy:income 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/bond-rout-s-silver-lining-emerges-with-chance-to-grab-5-yields?cmpid=091826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9031>
.

In the latest milestone for US Treasuries, the benchmark 10-year yield this 
week exceeded 5% for the first time in almost three years, and then hit the 
highest since 2007 as oil gained on growing risks to global supplies.

While the ascent in yields has dealt plenty of pain to investors, they now 
have an opportunity seen only fleetingly since the global financial crisis — 
buy now and lock in a roughly 5% annualized return for the next decade or 
longer. More money managers are finding that tough to pass up.

A net $625 billion poured into US bond mutual funds and exchange-traded funds 
this year through August, the most for that stretch in Morningstar data going 
back to 2010.Asset managers including PIMCO and Vanguard expect the pace will 
accelerate as investors shift into fixed-income from stocks.

“Those are attractive rates for putting money to work, especially when you 
think about the investor that for the last 15 years or more has been all-in on 
equities,” said Kevin Nicholson at Riverfront Investment Group. —Michael 
MacKenzie and Silla Brush

‘All over the map’

For more than a year, concerns have been mounting about stresses in private 
credit. Good luck, though, trying to figure out how bad things have gotten.

That’s because there’s no agreed-upon way to measure the rate of default 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/private-credit-defaults-are-1-6-or-19-depending-who-you-ask?cmpid=091826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9031>
 on private loans, or even what constitutes default — a lack of transparency 
that’s all the more important because financial firms increasingly aremarketing 
private assets to the masses 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/private-credit-buyout-shops-face-uphill-fight-for-401-k-cash?cmpid=091826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9031>
.

Fitch Ratings, for instance, put the default rate at a record 6.3% this week. 
The latest reading from another credit rating firm, KBRA, also pointed to a new 
high. Yet weigh the market by loan size and defaults remain below 1% because 
the largest borrowers continue to perform, investment bank Houlihan Lokey found.

“Estimates are all over the map,” said Lotfi Karoui, a multi-asset credit 
strategist at Pimco. —Kat Hidalgo 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWUMVk267mc/kat-hidalgo?cmpid=091826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260918&utm_content=9031>
