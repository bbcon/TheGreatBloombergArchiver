What You Need to Know Today

Speaking of Nvidia, Taiwanese prosecutors detained an employee of the company 
as part of a probe into thealleged smuggling of AI chips into China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/taiwan-detains-nvidia-employee-in-china-chip-smuggling-probe?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7064>
, thrusting the US chipmaker into a high-profile case concerning the black 
market for its products. Local investigators searched the home of the staffer 
and his desk at Nvidia’s Taipei office on July 24, according to people familiar 
with the matter. The courts later granted prosecutors’ request to detain the 
person on allegations of forgery and breach of trust, the people said, asking 
not to be named as the employee’s identity isn’t public. The case is one of at 
least eight probes into Nvidia chip smuggling across four jurisdictions. 
Prosecutors haven’t accused Nvidia or any other company of wrongdoing.

DeepSeek’s billionaire founder Liang Wenfeng was one of the big winners in the 
blockbuster IPO of Chinese chipmaker CXMT after his investment firms were
allocated the most shares among local hedge funds 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/deepseek-founder-s-hedge-funds-are-among-big-winners-of-cxmt-ipo?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7064>
, according to data compiled by Shenzhen PaiPaiWang Investment & Management. 
Liang’s firms were awarded a combined 20.2 million shares in the initial public 
offering, coming top among the more than 100 Chinese private funds that 
participated. That meant CXMT’s strong debut Monday gave Liang’s funds about 
820 million yuan ($121 million) in paper gains, according to Bloomberg 
calculations.

German officials are working behind the scenes to identify Chinese economic 
vulnerabilities that they could exploit if the European Union finds itself in a 
trade war with the world’s second-largest economy. The German government is
informally mapping China’s weaknesses by analyzing trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/germany-maps-china-s-weaknesses-in-preparation-for-a-trade-war?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7064>
 flows, supply chains and company-level data, according to people familiar with 
the matter. The exercise is aimed at identifying where China remains dependent 
on German and European technology, specialized components and industrial 
know-how — and where Beijing could face pressure in a potential economic 
confrontation.

Bank Indonesia Chief’s Shock Exit Fuels Prabowo Policy Worries

As recently as Friday, Bank Indonesia Governor Perry Warjiyo was meeting 
investors in Singapore after a regional central bank forum. The discussions 
were business as usual and Warjiyo gave no indication he was leaving his post, 
people who attended said. Perhaps sensing concern, he even told them in jest he 
remained the governor. The next day, according to Bank Indonesia, Warjiyo 
abruptly resigned for “personal reasons.”

Myanmar’s military-backed parliament passed a law authorizing the death 
penalty forforced labor in cyber-scam operations 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/myanmar-passes-law-allowing-the-death-penalty-for-scam-operators?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7064>

death penalty shall be imposed,” according to the legislation passed by the 
Pyidaungsu Hluttaw, or Union Parliament.
