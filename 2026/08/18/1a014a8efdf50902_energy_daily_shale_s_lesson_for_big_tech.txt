Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, oil 
reporter Kevin Crowley looks at what big-spending technology companies can 
learn from the shifting fortunes of US shale.

Diamondback Energy Inc. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/FANG:US?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
, one of the largest drillers in America’s vast Permian Basin, languishes 
mid-table in theS&P 500 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SPX:IND?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
 in terms of market value.

Yet when it comes to cash generation, the company punches above its weight. 
Diamondback had more free cash flow in the last quarter thantech behemoths 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-03/big-tech-earnings-show-split-between-ai-trade-winners-and-losers?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
 Amazon.com Inc., Meta Platforms Inc. and Google ownerAlphabet Inc. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/google-boosts-2026-spending-estimate-to-as-much-as-205-billion?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
combined.

The energy price surge driven by the war in Iran has inflated oil companies’ 
profits this year. But the evolution of US shale from cash incinerator tocash 
generator 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-04/us-driller-diamondback-raising-output-immediately-on-oil-rally?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
 has been far longer in the making.

The industry burned through about $350 billion in the decade through 2019 as 
companies plowed borrowed money into drilling and fracking that increased 
output, but not reliable profits.

Shale learned its lesson the hard way. The more production shot up, the more 
indebted the companies became 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-08-10/occidental-books-total-impairment-charges-of-6-6-billion?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
 and the further stock market valuations fell.

Big Tech is at risk of falling into the same trap due to gargantuan spending 
on data centers for artificial intelligence, according to Doug Terreson, a 
former analyst at Evercore ISI.

In his memoir <https://dougterreson.com/>, Terreson recounts how he urged 
energy CEOs to pursue greater capital discipline in the late 2010s.

“When companies are spending tons of money in relation to what they’re 
distributing to shareholders, returns on capital valuation and share prices 
almost always suffer,” he said. “It doesn’t matter what sector we’re talking 
about.”

Since its pandemic-era shakeout — which brought a wave of bankruptcies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2020-07-21/u-s-oil-shale-industry-faces-extinction-amid-shutdowns?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
 — shale has mostly heeded Terreson’s warning, drilling only wells that can 
generate strong returns, resulting in dependable profits that are less 
sensitive to oil price swings.

It’s a lesson that Big Tech will learn sooner rather than later as it pursues 
anepic capital spending binge 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/big-tech-earnings-slam-into-a-market-in-revolt-over-ai-spending?cmpid=BBBXT081826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260818&utm_content=7801>
, according to Terreson.

“Not only does profitability have to improve for some of these models, but it 
has to improve a lot and quickly,” he said. “Otherwise, valuations are going to 
come down more than they already have.”

—Kevin Crowley, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

A weather shift brewing in the Indian Ocean could bring a wetter end to 
India’s monsoon season, replenishing hydropower reservoirs and easing pressure 
on the country’s coal-heavy energy mix, according toBloombergNEF 
<https://about.bnef.com/>. The exceptionally dry summer has had a stark impact 
on the power system. As high temperatures boosted cooling demand and strained 
the grid, coal — which supplies about three-quarters of India’s electricity — 
picked up the slack.
