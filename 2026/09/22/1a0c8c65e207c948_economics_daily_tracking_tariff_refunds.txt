Where the Refunds Went

“The ultimate corporate welfare 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-20/bessent-says-tariff-revenue-to-be-virtually-unchanged-in-2026?cmpid=BBD092226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9166>
.” That’s what Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said that tariff refunds 
stemming from the Supreme Court’s invalidation of the bulk of President Donald 
Trump’s levies would amount to.

The February ruling by the high court set in motion steps towards repaying an 
estimated$166 billion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-30/trump-tariff-rebates-the-web-portal-refunding-166-billion?cmpid=BBD092226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9166>
 in revenue collected via duties Trump imposed after taking office in January 
last year.

The bulk of those payments has now been distributed, US Treasury data 
indicate. After three straight months of net declines in customs duties, flows 
turned positive again inAugust 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/us-2026-budget-gap-hits-1-96-trillion-with-a-month-left-to-go?cmpid=BBD092226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9166>
. A court filing shows that, as of Sept. 11, about $134.7 billion in refunds, 
including interest, had been paid out or approved for processing.

So, was it all corporate welfare? Accounts of where the money went are now 
starting to emerge. Evidence suggests it will be deployed in myriad ways.

A survey released Monday by the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta suggested 
companies are holding on to at least part of the cash. The 220 executives who 
shared their plans — in a broader survey of more than 1,100 taken last month — 
ticked multiple boxes in identifying their intentions.

Respondents were told to select all of the uses that applied. Roughly 
three-quarters said they expected tokeep refunds in cash 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/most-firms-keep-cash-from-tariff-refunds-fed-study-finds?cmpid=BBD092226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9166>
, while slightly more than half said they planned to invest in research and 
capital projects. As for customer rebates, 17% said they expected to give 
those. And nearly 15% said they’d use the funds to lower prices.

“These results also suggest that a nontrivial portion of tariff refunds 
directly benefit customers and employees,” the researchers wrote in ablog post 
<https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/policy-hub-macroblog/2026/09/21/how-are-firms-using-tariff-refunds>
.

Bloomberg News analysis published last week showed that companies in the 
Russell 3000 Index of stocks mentioned tariff refundsalmost 1,000 times 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/us-companies-get-creative-as-tariff-refunds-start-flowing-in?cmpid=BBD092226_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9166>
 in earnings calls and filings in July, August and September. It showed firms 
are using the windfall in a range of creative ways. Among them:

 * Stepped up contributions to employee retirement-funds.
 * Payouts to vendors that gave retailers discounts on inventory purchases.
 * Reducing debt.
 * Lowering prices, including via temporary discounts and deals.
 * Offsets against expected costs tied to new, forthcoming tariffs.
The US Treasury declined to respond to a request for comment.

Need-to-Know Research

In the US housing market, existing properties make up well over 80% of the 
market by sales. Japan, by contrast, has long been dominated bynew construction 
<https://japanbi.substack.com/p/tsukuruba-building-the-redfin-and>, which has 
made up over two-thirds of the market. China, before its property bust, was 
much like Japan. But a structural shift may be underway.

Existing home sales accounted for 57% of the online total in August, well 
above the roughly 50% share in 2024-25, Bank of America economists Benson Wu 
and Helen Qiao wrote in a recent note. This was the first time the National 
Bureau of Statistics had disclosed this ratio, the duo said.

“Going forward, we expect this structural shift to continue, with the 
‘K-shaped’ divergence between new- and existing-home sales growth likely to 
widen further,” Wu and Qiao wrote. This is particularly true following the 
reform of the presale housing system, they said.
