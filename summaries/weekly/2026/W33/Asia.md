• China's July exports climbed approximately 24% YoY and imports approximately 28%, both well ahead of consensus, with AI-related electronics a key driver.

• The Bank of Japan's July meeting summary flagged rising upside inflation risks, with one board member raising the possibility of an accelerated tightening pace; the yen hovered near ¥159 per dollar, close to a 40-year low.
• South Korea's Kospi surged 22% over 10 sessions, re-entering bull market territory after a 39% plunge from its June peak, though it remains roughly 25% below that high.

**China: Deflation Risk and Capital Markets**
China's inflation data present a deteriorating domestic demand picture even as the trade account remains robust, underscoring the economy's two-speed character. The combination of weak CPI and easing PPI raises the risk that factory-gate disinflation re-accelerates, particularly if global demand softens. In response to an AI-driven margin debt frenzy that pushed the balance above 3 trillion yuan in late June, brokerages including Citic Securities tightened compliance requirements; the margin balance has since retreated to 2.6 trillion yuan. Beijing appears to be threading a narrow path — supporting AI-related equities for strategic reasons while containing the speculative leverage that contributed to July's market turbulence — while simultaneously intensifying a tax crackdown on offshore trusts and preferential "high-tech enterprise" status, with over 6,000 firms stripped of preferential rates since 2025.

**BOJ and Yen Dynamics**
The Bank of Japan's July meeting summary, released Monday, firmed expectations for a September or October rate hike. Japan's government subsequently signaled explicit support for a near-term move, aligning with the BOJ's own concerns that yen weakness is feeding into domestic prices. A notable complication is the Bessent-Takaichi rift: Treasury Secretary Bessent views BOJ tightening as essential to stabilizing the yen, while Prime Minister Takaichi remains wary of raising rates too quickly. The prior week's coordinated intervention — which cost Japan roughly $53 billion — appears only temporary, constrained by the US Exchange Stabilization Fund's sub-$220 billion capacity. Household spending fell for a seventh consecutive month in June, leaving policymakers balancing currency support against fragile consumption.

**Regional Divergence**
Malaysia's GDP beat and Taiwan's projected double-digit expansion contrast with the Philippines, where Q2 growth slumped to 2.3% — well below expectations — even as inflation at 6.2% runs more than double the government's target, forcing Governor Remolona to signal continued tightening despite slowing output. Australia's Reserve Bank held its cash rate at 4.35% for a second straight meeting but maintained a hawkish bias, with Governor Bullock calling further tightening "quite possible." Indonesia's newly nominated Bank Indonesia governor, Destry Damayanti, faces an immediate test balancing growth support against policy independence. Singapore upgraded its 2026 growth forecast to as high as 5.5% on AI-driven trade momentum. South Korea plans to inject over 1 trillion won into a new sovereign wealth fund targeting AI, robotics, and infrastructure in 2027.

**Corporate Developments**
Lenovo shares surged as much as 22% in Hong Kong on Tuesday after quarterly revenue rose 43%, beating estimates, while Tencent declined after doubling AI spending. TSMC's 45% monthly sales gain confirmed sustained AI hardware demand. Shein plans to open investor orders for its Hong Kong IPO around August 20, targeting a valuation near $30 billion — a fraction of its prior peak. Tata Sons Chairman Chandrasekaran announced he will step down in February 2027 following a reappointment deadlock, introducing uncertainty into one of India's largest conglomerates ahead of its August 18 annual meeting.
