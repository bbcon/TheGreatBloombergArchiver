**Fixed Income and Monetary Policy**

The dominant macro signal in US markets is the bond selloff. The 10-year Treasury yield reached 4.78%, its highest since January, extending a five-day climb that reflects markets pricing a higher probability of further Federal Reserve tightening. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks — emphasizing that supply-side inflation driven by energy and food prices requires a policy response — have shifted the consensus. Barclays and Société Générale now incorporate additional hikes into their forecasts, though no Wall Street consensus has emerged; some analysts argue rate hikes cannot effectively address supply-driven inflation. The Friday payrolls report will be pivotal in clarifying the Fed's path.

**Fiscal Dynamics and Markets**

Treasury Secretary Bessent's bond-buyback program has failed to durably compress long-end yields, with the 30-year rate nearly retracing its pre-announcement levels. US public debt now exceeds $40 trillion, having risen by a third in under five years, and the government faces increasing competition from AI-driven corporate bond issuance for investor capital. Equity markets are adjusting accordingly, with rate-sensitive sectors under pressure ahead of what promises to be a consequential September for policy.
