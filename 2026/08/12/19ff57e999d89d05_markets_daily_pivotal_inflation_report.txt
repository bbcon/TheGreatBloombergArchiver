Pivotal inflation reading

Wall Street could hardly be more divided over whether the Fed will raise 
interest rates next month. One thing isn’t in dispute, however: Today’s 
inflation report will go a long way towardshaping the Fed’s next move 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/traders-brace-for-pivotal-cpi-as-fed-hike-hangs-in-balance?cmpid=081226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7610>
.

Traders are pricing in a roughly 50% chance of a quarter-point increase, 
swaps-market activity shows. A hotter-than-expected print could sharply boost 
the chances of a hike. A soft reading gives the Fed room to keep waiting.

“The market is telling the Fed that it has an inflation issue, but the Fed’s 
view at the moment is that the recent data is soft enough for them to not have 
to hike,” said Bilal Hafeez, head of market strategy at Macro Hive.

What economists expect: Core CPI in July probably climbed 0.1% 
month-over-month. Bloomberg Economics sees the core year-over-year rate falling 
to 2.4%, the lowest since March 2021.

One reason that the stakes are high going into the report is that 
trend-chasing investors have built upa record level of bets against global bonds
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/trend-chasing-funds-big-short-on-bonds-raises-stakes-for-cpi?cmpid=081226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7610>
, according to UBS. That lopsided positioning leaves the those traders 
vulnerable to a sharp reversal if bonds start to rally. —Cameron Fozi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXbWf6PO3bw/cameron-fozi?cmpid=081226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7610>

The Katsu Curry Index

Just how undervalued is the yen? A Bank of New York Mellon strategist says the 
best gauge is price of a Japanese fast-food staple.

Geoff Yu created the Katsu Curry Index 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/curry-dish-not-big-mac-captures-just-how-weak-yen-is-bny-says?cmpid=081226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7610>
 as an alternative toThe Economist’s Big Mac Index, arguing that an 
international price comparison for the pork-and-rice dish — rather than burgers 
— gives a better sense of how the yen’s retreat feels in Japan.

One dollar buys about 159.23 yen at the moment. Yet by Yu’s calculations, 
which account for the purchasing power of the Japanese currency as reflected in 
the price of curry dishes, one dollar should buy just 62.18 yen. —Naomi Tajitsu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVtlmblMDyc/naomi-tajitsu?cmpid=081226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260812&utm_content=7610>
