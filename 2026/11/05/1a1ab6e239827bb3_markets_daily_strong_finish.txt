Finishing with a bang

Wall Street’s biggest trading desks are betting on a rally 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/wall-street-trading-desks-bet-on-us-stock-rally-after-midterms?cmpid=110526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11042>
 to close out 2026.

While US stocks are poised for an underwhelming open on Thursday, the basic 
idea is that reduced policy uncertainty and lackluster investor positioning 
ahead of the midterm elections will combine to power a move higher into 
year-end.

“With the election behind us and volatility potentially normalizing, there is 
considerable capacity for investors to rebuild risk,” said Citadel Securities’ 
Scott Rubner. “The path of least resistance for US equities is higher into 
year-end.”

Among the factors pointing to a potential rally:

 * A bruising October triggered a sharp reduction in risk-taking among 
institutional investors. Hedge funds cut exposure to US equities. That’s now 
providing a favorable backdrop for stocks.
 * Wall Street’s fear gauge, the VIX Index, fell by the most since April 2025 
on Wednesday, suggesting investors feel less of a need for protection.
 * Congress could slow spending on AI. Big tech shares led the market higher 
Wednesday as investorsembraced the theory 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/big-tech-stocks-soar-as-traders-bet-democrats-will-slow-ai-spend?cmpid=110526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11042>
 that a Democrat-controlled Congress could save big AI spenders from 
themselves. (Of course, that’s bad for makers of memory chips and all the other 
gear that goes into data centers, which is whyKorean stocks slumped today 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/blue-wave-stock-rotation-sinks-asian-chipmakers-boosts-autos?cmpid=110526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11042>
.)
 * The election results could spur President Trump to wind down a war in Iran 
that has jacked up oil prices, fueled inflation and stoked uncertainty. 
Investors are also looking toward the highly anticipated Anthropic IPO. The 
mega deal could clear the way for a more decisive return to technology shares, 
which slumped last month as investors sought havens in consumer staples, 
utilities and healthcare.

“For me the real clearing event is the Anthropic IPO,” said Max Kettner of 
HSBC. “Once we get the Anthropic IPO announced and then over the line, then I 
think we’re full-on long tech and actually short all of the stuff that’s ripped 
in October.” —Natalia Kniazhevich

Debt ceiling looms

A divided US government means the debt ceiling is back in play 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/wall-street-girding-for-us-debt-ceiling-showdown-in-2027?cmpid=110526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11042>
.

The US is about $800 billion shy of its $41.1 trillion borrowing limit, and 
strategists expect the government to run out of road in the first half of next 
year.

“The single biggest source of domestic policy uncertainty remaining in the US 
is the debt ceiling,” said George Saravelos of Deutsche Bank.

A standoff could disrupt the bond market. During past confrontations, 
investors have sold Treasury bills maturing around the date the government was 
projected to run out of money, while buying securities maturing before or after 
the so-called X-date, creating a kink in the curve.

The curve shows little sign of concern right now, but that could change. — 
Michael MacKenzie
