**Brazil Election and Energy Policy**
The October 25 presidential runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro carries meaningful implications for Brazil's energy sector. A Bolsonaro administration would likely revive the asset-divestiture agenda pursued under his father, with Petrobras's Bahia cluster, the Urucu field, and its Braskem stake among probable candidates for sale. Braskem is already in creditor negotiations over deep indebtedness, and the question of whether Petrobras injects capital or exits the position entirely may be resolved by the election outcome. Independent producers PRIO and PetroReconcavo are viewed as natural buyers of divested oil assets.

**Amazon Infrastructure and Climate**
The ongoing paving of BR-319, a 550-mile highway through the Amazon connecting Amazonas state to southern Brazil, illustrates the tension at the center of Lula's reelection campaign: balancing development in one of Brazil's poorest regions against deforestation commitments that have been a signature achievement of his administration. Native forest cover in the Brazilian Amazon has shrunk by 208,000 square miles over 40 years, virtually all along major road corridors. Bolsonaro has signaled support for additional Amazon highway construction, while Lula's backing of BR-319 despite environmental objections reflects the political constraints of governing a deeply unequal country.
