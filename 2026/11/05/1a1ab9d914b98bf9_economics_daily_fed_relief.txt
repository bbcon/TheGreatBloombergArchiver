Fed Relief

It wasn’t on the ballot on Tuesday, but the Fed is among the big winners from 
the midterm UScongressional elections 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/2026-midterm-elections-what-democrats-wins-in-congress-mean-for-trump?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 — or, at least, the central bank’s autonomy is.

At one point this year, a lone Republican 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-20/can-trump-remove-powell-a-guide-to-the-fed-chair-succession-battle?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
, Senator Thom Tillis, appeared to be the bulwark against moves by President 
Donald Trump against then-Fed Chair Jerome Powell. Tillis had refused to let 
Trump’s nomination for Warsh as Powell’s successor progress through the Senate 
unless the administration dropped its judicial probe into the Fed chief.

Now, the Fed gets a whole Democratic majority in the Senate as effective 
protection 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/fed-wins-protection-with-democrats-taking-control-of-the-senate?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 against extreme Trump moves, economists said. As with the Tillis maneuver, 
that comes especially via the appointment process.
Kevin Warsh and Donald TrumpPhotographer: Al Drago/Bloomberg
Trump was open in seeking to get a majority 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-08-28/trump-attacks-on-lisa-cook-risk-wrecking-fed-s-credibility?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 of his picks on the Fed’s seven-person board (he has three currently.) Now 
it’s a question whether he’ll get any more. There’s only one scheduled opening 
— Powell’s seat — in 2028. And Senates in the past havehad a record <> of 
sitting on nominations asclocks wind down 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-11-18/shelton-running-out-of-time-to-get-confirmed-to-federal-reserve?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 to the next presidential handover of power.

This new reality, which begins with the Congress taking office Jan. 3, also 
undermines the rationale for Trump’s attempts toremove Lisa Cook 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/fed-s-lisa-cook-puts-off-white-house-hearing-over-trump-s-allegations?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 from the board. The president’s limited ability to install loyalists at the 
Fed from now on will reduce risks to Fed credibility, Nomura economists wrote 
in a note.

As for Warsh, who was confirmed with just one vote 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-13/senate-confirms-warsh-to-lead-fed-as-trump-tests-its-autonomy?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 from the Democratic side of the aisle in the narrowest-ever vote for a Fed 
chief, he may now need to cultivate ties with the incoming majority — a 
skillset Powell was known for.

The current chairman likes to say he looks out at the window to assess 
economic conditions (he used the phraseseven times 
<https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20261028.pdf> at 
his latest press briefing after the Fed held rates steady last month).

“Unless he wants to be a one-term chair, Warsh will need to spend less time 
looking out the window and more time walking the halls of Congress,” Michael 
Feroli, JPMorgan Chase’s chief US economist, wrote in a note.

Need-to-Know Research

The US trade deficit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/us-trade-deficit-narrows-slightly-on-pickup-in-goods-exports?cmpid=BBD110526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11017>
 is on course for another outsized figure this year, tracking the 
second-biggest total on record through September at almost $920 billion. But 
that masks a notable change in composition.

Well over half of the gap nowadays is due to technology products, in which the 
US was a net exporter just three years ago, James Knightley, an economist at 
ING, wrote in a note Wednesday. For the month of September some $64 billion of 
the $105 billion deficit was due to computers, peripherals and semiconductors, 
he tallies.

One implication of this is that the giant sums that are going into artificial 
intelligence investment isn’t necessarily boosting the output of value added in 
the US itself. “Money is flowing out of the country to the likes of Korea and 
Taiwan,” Knightley wrote. Those two nations’ shipments to the US are up 35% and 
61% respectively so far this year.

“The US tech roll-out has been a huge boost to tech-orientated exporting 
nations,” and that’s offsetting the capital-spending boost within US GDP, he 
wrote.
