Tech euphoria is back

In the span of four days, trading in tech stocks has gone from misery to 
euphoria.

A scorecard of the rebound: 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/tech-melt-up-drives-3-5-trillion-nasdaq-100-rally-in-four-days?cmpid=080526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7343>

 * The Nasdaq 100 has soared 9.3% in four days, the biggest rally we’ve seen 
since April 2025. In market value terms, that’s a $3.5 trillion gain.
 * Microsoft is up 26% on results that proved its cloud services are winning 
over customers.
 * It’s a similar story at Amazon, which is reaping billions from its 
cloud-computing revenue. The stock rallied 18%.
 * Alphabet and Nvidia both rose 11%, Sandisk surged 40% and Palantir climbed 
32%.
Almost everything — semiconductors, software and hyperscalers — is going up 
again. Blowout second-quarter results have triggered a major turnaround and 
reassured investors that AI spending is paying off.

That’s wiping away a lot of the skepticism that’s been hanging over 
hyperscalers for spending trillions of dollars to build data centers. The 
popular trade had been to embrace the companies that make chips for the centers 
— the recipients of all that spending — until that trade too faltered last 
month.

Of course, that momentum can shift directions in the blink of an eye. It was 
just last month, remember, that the Nasdaq 100 dropped 11% peak to trough. —
Michael Msika

No summer lull

Investors in the northern hemisphere are again ruing their inability to truly 
take August off.

While the month should be prime vacation time, it’s kicking off with market 
players contending with the Middle East conflict, questions about the Fed’s 
inflation-fighting resolve, the US-Japan campaign tostrengthen the yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/treasury-secretary-scott-bessent-draws-on-hedge-fund-tactics-to-help-rescue-yen?cmpid=080526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7343>
 and a busy earnings season.

“Investors hoping August will bring a summer lull may be disappointed,” wrote 
strategists at BlackRock.

August has a track record for being volatile. Last year, markets were roiled 
by US tariffs taking effect. And in 2024, it was the unwind of the yen carry 
trade strategy. For those with longer memory, China began devaluing the yuan in 
August 2015 and Russia’s debt default happened the same month in 1998.

For now, no markets have tipped over, but we’re only two and a half trading 
days into the month. —Simon Kennedy
