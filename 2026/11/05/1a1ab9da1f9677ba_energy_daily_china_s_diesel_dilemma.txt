Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today we 
explore what’s at stake for China as it decides whether to ramp up diesel 
exports to a fuel-starved world market.

When it comes to diesel exports, China has an important choice to make.

A fresh round <bbg://news/stories/TNLF8IKIP3J4> of export quotas gives Beijing 
the option of easing a global supply shortage as the Northern Hemisphere heads 
into winter. But policymakers might just as easily balk at shipping the fuel 
while the domestic market remains tight.

A top-up allocation last week brought China’s total fuel-export quotas for the 
year to about 42 million tons. That’s the most since 2021, and comes despite a 
recentdrawdown <bbg://news/stories/TNJL00KGIFPY> of diesel stockpiles as the 
Middle East war stretches into its ninth month.

The product breakdown hasn’t yet been announced. Refiners are currently 
drawing up new export plans for gasoline, jet fuel and diesel, with monthly 
shipments — as always — subject to approval from Beijing.

China has good reason to ship more diesel.

For one, the margins look attractive. Crude’s sharp pullback 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-nov-5?cmpid=BBBXT110526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11040>
 after the US midterm elections is an incentive for the world’s biggest 
importer to buy more, while diesel remains neareye-watering highs 
<bbg://news/stories/TNZLULKK3NYA> in America and other big markets.

There’s a corporate motivation, too. With the end of the year approaching, 
quick sales to markets such as Singapore and Australia would be a 
straightforward way for state-owned refiners to round out astrong 12 months 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/sinopec-s-third-quarter-profits-soar-on-higher-oil-prices?cmpid=BBBXT110526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11040>
.
A Sinopec gas station in Shanghai. China’s biggest oil refiner has posted 
higher profits this year as refining margins have improved.Photographer: Qilai 
Shen/Bloomberg
Thirdly, there’s diplomacy. China has some breathing room to respond to US 
President Donald Trump’scall to produce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-26/us-says-china-to-buy-10-million-tons-of-coal-in-2027-and-2028?cmpid=BBBXT110526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11040>
 more oil products to help stabilize global supply. More exports could be a 
useful goodwill gesture ahead of the APEC summit in Shenzhen this month.

Beijing’s next move, however, will ultimately be determined by its comfort 
level with letting diesel leave its borders. China views the fuel as 
strategically important, prioritizing domestic needs when supply tightens — a 
caution that isrooted in experience 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-01-03/china-s-wintry-cold-lifts-diesel-use-as-factories-buy-generators?cmpid=BBBXT110526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11040>
.

And there’s already cause for concern: A key gauge 
<bbg://securities/CHPZDSOE%20Index> of diesel inventories has dropped to its 
lowest since 2022.

Perhaps China will draw confidence from the rapid electrification 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-29/eighteen-wheeler-e-trucks-are-china-s-answer-to-soaring-global-diesel-prices?cmpid=BBBXT110526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261105&utm_content=11040>
 of its transport fleet, which is gradually eating into diesel consumption. The 
country’s peak demand period is also set to fade going into December.

Whatever it decides, Beijing’s approach to diesel exports for the remainder of 
the year will be felt beyond its borders.

BNEF today

Turkey’s industrial policies have built more solar manufacturing capacity 
<bbg://news/stories/TO0WVDKK3NY8> than the domestic market can absorb, meaning 
exports will emerge as an increasingly important outlet for factories. Since 
2022, Turkey’s module manufacturing capacity has risen to nearly 30 gigawatts 
from 11 gigawatts, while cell capacity jumped to 5.3 gigawatts from almost 3.3 
gigawatts and wafer capacity to about 5 gigawatts from 500 megawatts. This 
makes Turkey the fifth-largest market for module manufacturing capacity 
globally.
