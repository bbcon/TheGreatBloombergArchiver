The US and Iran are shooting at each other again. American forces struck 
Iranian rocket launchers, andTehran retaliated 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/us-strikes-iranian-rocket-launchers-in-first-attack-in-weeks?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 with missiles and drones aimed at US bases in Jordan, the first exchange of 
fire in about a month. Jordan intercepted eight missiles, while Brent crude 
jumped more than 2%above $90 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-aug-31?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 as the flare-up revived fears of a broader conflict. A sustained return to 
fighting would push up energy costs, add to inflation and hand Donald Trump 
another political problem just two months before the midterms.

For Narendra Modi, the Iran war is also making an old balancing act 
considerably harder 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/modi-putin-meeting-how-trump-tariffs-test-india-s-russia-ties?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
. India has spent decades keeping Moscow close while building a strategic 
partnership with Washington, but Trump’s tariffs and pressure campaign over 
Russian oil are testing how long New Delhi can comfortably do both. India’s 
purchases of Russian crudehit a record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/indian-refiners-widen-oil-search-as-attacks-hurt-russian-flows?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 2.8 million barrels a day in July as the Iran conflict squeezed Middle Eastern 
supplies. Now Washington is threateningfresh penalties 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/us-senate-passes-russia-sanctions-bill-championed-by-graham?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 (tariffs of up to 100% on goods from Moscow’s biggest oil customers). With 
Modi and Putin due to meet at today’s Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit, 
the question is how long India can preserve its prized strategic autonomy 
without having to pick a side.

And that summit presents a different test 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-30/trump-s-iran-war-sets-test-for-xi-and-putin-s-security-club?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 for Xi Jinping and Vladmir Putin: whether the club they spent years building 
as a counterweight to US power can actually do much when one of its members is 
under attack. Iran joined the SCO in 2023, yet neither China nor Russia has 
shown much appetite for coming to Tehran’s aid beyond rhetoric and limited 
economic support. The bloc now represents more than 40% of the global 
population and roughly a quarter of GDP; what it conspicuously lacks is a 
mechanism for turning that heft into collective action. Expect plenty of 
solidarity in Bishkek, and a harder question hanging over it: what, exactly, is 
this increasingly ambitious security club for?

The scale of Nepal’s catastrophe is still coming into focus, as is how little 
warning people had. At least 919 people are dead and roughly4,800 remain missing
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/nepal-china-glacier-flood-kills-919-with-4-800-still-missing?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 in Nepal and China after a glacial collapse sent ice, mud and water tearing 
through the Himalayan border region, with rescuers still struggling to reach 
isolated communities and people trapped in hydropower tunnels. Some victims had 
less than five minutes to flee. Nepal says vast stretches of its high mountains 
lack weather stations and thatlimited data-sharing with China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/nepal-floods-expose-gaps-in-warning-systems-china-data-sharing?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 leaves a dangerous blind spot as warming destabilizes glaciers and slopes. 
Improving early-warning systems is now a priority, both to give communities 
more time to prepare and to protect critical infrastructure and a tourism 
industry vital to Nepal’s economy.

The yen is back in uncomfortable territory. Its slide through 160 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/yen-s-breach-of-160-to-dollar-puts-traders-on-intervention-watch?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 to the dollar has traders watching for another round of intervention, barely a 
month after Japanspent a record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/japan-s-yen-intervention-hits-record-96-billion-in-past-month?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
 $96.4 billion (with US help) trying to prop it up. Strategists see 161 and 
then 162-163 as levels where the risk of action rises, though Tokyo insists it 
cares more about the speed of the move than any magic number. The deeper 
problem is that intervention has proved an expensive way to buy time: the yen 
has already surrendered more than half its gains from July, as a stronger 
dollar and expectations for higher US rates keep pushing in the other 
direction. Swaps are now pricing a 90% chance of a BOJ rate hike in September.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/japan-s-biggest-curry-chain-coco-ichibanya-headed-for-sale?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8353>
