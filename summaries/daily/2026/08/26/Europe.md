• ECB Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel said interest rates must rise further, warning that the prolonged Middle East conflict and a surprisingly resilient euro-zone economy pose upside risks to inflation, which she said is likely to exceed 2% for an "extended period."
• European natural gas has overtaken oil as the top inflation concern for regional bond traders; prices are near five-month highs and winter contracts cost more than double year-ago levels, pushing 10-year German and UK yields to levels not seen in decades.
• France enters a new season of budget brinkmanship widely seen as President Macron's riskiest yet, with a post-holiday cabinet meeting and the first of several credit-rating reviews due Friday.
• German exporter sentiment rose to a 4½-year high in August, the latest evidence of a recovery taking hold in Europe's largest economy.

**ECB and Inflation Risk**
Schnabel's comments reinforce a hawkish ECB tilt, cautioning that waiting for energy costs to feed into wages before acting would leave policymakers "behind the curve." Gas is playing an outsized role: it accounts for 21% of the EU's energy mix and 25%-35% of the UK's, and the Netherlands' network operator has warned the country will miss its winter storage target, leaving the region ill-prepared entering the heating season.

**Political Risk**
France's fiscal standoff, with parliament gridlocked, adds to a backdrop in which Russia is expanding hybrid attacks — cyberattacks, drones, arson — against countries supplying Ukraine. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz warned perpetrators "will pay the price," though the EU has yet to shift its defensive posture.
