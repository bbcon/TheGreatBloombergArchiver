**Energy Shock and Growth Implications**
The protracted closure of the Strait of Hormuz is inflicting measurable economic damage across energy-importing Asia. Bloomberg Economics analysis of NASA satellite imagery shows nighttime radiance has declined across nearly 60% of the region's land area since the conflict began — an indicator corroborated by sector-level data showing curtailed manufacturing, transport, and tourism activity. Bangladesh, Pakistan, and Cambodia face the most acute stress. The ADB warns that scarring effects — household debt accumulation, job cuts — may persist well into 2027 even after oil prices ease.

**Currency and Corporate Developments**
The coordinated US-Japan yen intervention represents a meaningful shift in currency diplomacy. Treasury Secretary Bessent framed it as a gesture of alliance solidarity, though analysts note a range of US economic interests — including Japan's $550 billion investment commitment and ongoing trade negotiations — likely motivated Washington's participation. Toyota lifted its full-year profit outlook and announced a $6.3 billion buyback, supported by hybrid demand and yen weakness. Indonesia's central bank independence appears at risk as President Prabowo moves to install a more aligned governor, a development worth monitoring for regional monetary policy credibility.
