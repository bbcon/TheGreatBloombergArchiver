Four decades ago, late King Hassan II formally requested that Morocco become a 
member of the European Union’s predecessor.

The bid sought to ease tensions over the Spanish enclaves of Ceuta and Melilla 
just as Madrid’s admission to the bloc allowed Spain and the UK to manage their 
disagreements over Gibraltar on the other side of the narrow strait.

Morocco’s application was rejected on geographical grounds.

Fast forward to 2026, and the status of the EU’s only African outposts is 
center stage after a massive influx of migrants into Ceuta laid bare the 
combustible nature ofEurope’s border politics 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/eu-tries-to-tamp-down-divisions-after-ceuta-migration-surge?cmpid=BBD082126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8004>
.

For Morocco, the timing and location of the surge was politically charged. 

It coincided with nearby celebrations marking King Mohammed VI’s enthronement 
anniversary, making a crackdown by Moroccan forces untenable in the monarch’s 
presence.

Two weeks later, Moroccan authorities pulled out the stops to thwart a second 
attempt 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/second-ceuta-incursion-attempt-fails-as-morocco-spain-boost-security?cmpid=BBD082126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8004>
.

What’s clear is that, barring the unlikely event that Spain agrees to discuss 
a new status for the enclaves, the EU needs Rabat’s full cooperation tosecure 
its borders 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/spain-says-vast-majority-of-ceuta-migrants-have-returned?cmpid=BBD082126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8004>
 — giving Morocco the upper hand.
Spanish soldiers escort migrants back to Morocco from Ceuta on Aug. 2.
Photographer: Henry Nicholls/AFP/Getty Images
It may choose to pursue a broader deal with Brussels — modeled on a 2024 EU 
agreement with Egypt — that would include more investment, easier market 
access, and less scrutiny of China ties.

Rabat also faces pressing fiscal challenges, making alternative budget 
financing critical to sustaining infrastructure spending andWorld Cup 
preparations 
<bbg://screens/bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/morocco-football-boss-enters-politics-with-premier-bid-possible?srnd=undefined>
.

There is wariness, too, of Spain’s commitment to disputed Moroccan sovereignty 
over the Western Sahara 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-05-07/morocco-western-sahara-becomes-el-dorado-thanks-to-trump?cmpid=BBD082126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8004>
.

Suspicions were stoked during Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’s July visit to 
Algeria, and Madrid’s decision to grant citizenship to over 100,000 Sahrawi 
expatriates.

High youth unemployment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-04/next-africa-ceuta-tragedy-puts-spotlight-on-morocco-spanish-enclaves?cmpid=BBD082126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8004>
 means that millions of Moroccans and Algerians see Europe as their greatest 
chance of better opportunities.

So the migratory pressure may not abate in the near future, neither from 
Morocco nor the rest of Africa.— Souhail Karam 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATiT2jmuFOk/souhail-karam?cmpid=BBD082126_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8004>
