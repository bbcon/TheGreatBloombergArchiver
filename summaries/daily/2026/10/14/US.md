**Macro and Monetary Policy**

September CPI data — the most consequential near-term release — arrives today, with Bloomberg consensus at 3.6% YoY. Headline inflation is expected to have accelerated driven by energy costs, while core is seen moderating. The outcome carries direct implications for the Fed's path: NY Fed President John Williams stated Wednesday that one additional hike remains appropriate this year but signaled no further tightening is warranted in 2027. That guidance modestly softened rate expectations, with futures markets repricing accordingly. The IMF, meeting in Bangkok, reiterated that US growth alone is insufficient to stabilize its debt trajectory — projecting federal interest payments reaching 5.7% of GDP by 2031.

**Banking and Equity Markets**

Q3 bank earnings delivered a nuanced picture. JPMorgan, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo combined for $56.5 billion in net interest income — a fifth consecutive quarterly record — yet share prices were largely unrewarding. Fixed-income trading revenue disappointed broadly, with JPMorgan posting its first year-on-year FICC decline in over two years. Equities desks outperformed strikingly: Goldman's equities revenue rose 72% quarter-on-quarter. Bank of America was separately fined over $1.2 billion for money laundering and sanctions violations. S&P 500 futures traded modestly higher ahead of the CPI print, with semiconductor earnings expectations continuing to underpin the broader bull market.
