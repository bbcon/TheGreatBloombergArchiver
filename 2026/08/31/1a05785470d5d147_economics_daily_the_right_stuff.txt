The Right Stuff

In his keynote speech 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/warsh-says-inflation-isn-t-slowing-vows-to-hit-to-2-target?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
 at the annual Jackson Hole conference Friday, Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin 
Warsh borrowed a line from one of the most legendary American aerospace 
pioneers, the experimental test pilot Chuck Yeager: “At the moment of truth, 
there are either reasons or results.”

“The only true test of our credibility 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/warsh-s-inflation-warning-sets-up-september-showdown-for-the-fed?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
” is delivering on the Fed’s mandate, Warsh said in setting up that quote. The 
potential implication, then, is that the Fed’s next policy meeting on Sept. 
16-17 now looms as a “moment of truth” for US monetary policymakers.

Some economists not already predicting a September rate rise have joined that 
camp <bbg://news/stories/TKHT3JT9NJLS>, including those at Barclays and Societe 
Generale. Stephen Stanley at Santander US Capital Markets set a high hurdle for 
a hold: “barring a stunning turn in the data over the next few weeks, the Fed 
is poised to hike.”

At Evercore ISI, the view now is that the Fed needs “additional good news on 
the inflation outlook” to stand pat. “This is at odds with our prior assessment 
that summer inflation data was good enough to create a default to hold in 
September absent bad news,” Krishna Guha and his colleagues wrote.

Warsh made clear that his reading of the summer data 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/key-us-inflation-gauge-posts-muted-advance-spending-stalls?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
 was that it wasn’t good enough to get inflation back to 2%. The recent reports 
“do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved,” he said. 
And unless the Fed is confident that underlying inflation is moving to target 
“clearly and at sufficient speed,” then “we have work to do,” he said.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-29/warsh-sets-the-tone-for-fed-s-road-ahead-video?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
The Fed chief on Friday also directly addressed one of the perceived mistakes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-28/fed-latest-speech-offers-warsh-chance-to-explain-why-he-didn-t-hike-rate?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
 from a July 29 press conference, by stating that it’s the PCE price gauge that 
the Fed will be using for its inflation target. In other words, no potential to 
shift to a metric that’s rising more slowly, as a way to claim success on 2%.

Some Fed watchers noted that he could have made these points last month. But 
it’s worth keeping in mind a couple of Chuck Yeager’s other quotes. (Warsh 
provided a link to atop 20 
<https://www.newsweek.com/top-20-quotes-chuck-yeager-first-man-break-sound-barrier-1553038>
 list.) “There’s no such thing as a natural-born pilot,” and “the best pilots 
fly more than the others; that’s why they’re the best.”

In other words, you learn by doing. If Warsh suffered a crash on July 29, his 
flight on Friday proved much smoother. Certainly, the financialmarket reaction 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/warsh-is-driving-treasuries-market-whether-he-wants-to-or-not?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
 was a mirror image to July 29.

Last month, the Treasury yield curve steepened — longer-dated yields jumped 
relative to shorter-dated ones, reflecting concerns about the Fed’s willingness 
to use policy-rate hikes to rein in inflation. And on Friday itflattened 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/wall-street-piles-on-rate-hike-bets-as-warsh-renews-hawkish-tone?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
, with the 2-year, 10-year spread shrinking to the narrowest since, yes, July 
28.

Yeager was depicted among the golden generation of US aerospace pioneers in a 
1980s film classic,The Right Stuff. That might also be what Warsh delivered 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-29/key-takeaways-from-the-fed-s-annual-jackson-hole-conference?cmpid=BBD083126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260831&utm_content=8298>
 at Jackson Hole.

The Week Ahead

US payrolls growth likely got back on track in August, with general steadiness 
in the labor market helping the Fed focus more intently on its battle with 
inflation.

Economists estimate the monthly jobs report from the Bureau of Labor

this year.

The unemployment rate, based on a survey of households rather than

Need-to-Know Research

Trump administration officials regularly argue that the Strait of Hormuz is 
“open” and that oil is flowing through, despite continuing tensions with Iran. 
Goldman Sachs analysis suggests flowhas indeed picked up 
<bbg://news/stories/TKHBRJKIJH8T> compared with earlier this year, though 
measuring the amount has been getting harder.

Among the challenges: tankers are turning off their transponders to reduce 
detectability — so-called dark crossings. “Using two independent methods, we 
estimate recent total Gulf exports at roughly 15-16 million barrels per day,” 
Goldman analysts led by Daan Struyven wrote in a note late last week. That’s 
still down 7-8 million barrels compared with pre-war levels, but 5-6 million 
above a March low, they said.

“The rise in dark crossings by specialized shippers and in ship-to-ship 
transfers shows that producers and shippers are adapting to the Mideast 
conflict,” the Goldman team wrote. That said, looking at the market for future 
freight rates, they suggest an increase in the “perceived probability of 
disruptions persisting well into 2027.”
