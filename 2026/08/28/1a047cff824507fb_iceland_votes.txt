In a world where big powers increasingly flex their muscles, Iceland is 
debating whether now is a good time tocleave closer to the rest of Europe 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/referendum-in-iceland-tests-lure-of-eu-membership?cmpid=BBD082826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8261>
.

Icelanders head to the polls tomorrow to decide if the government should 
receive a mandate to reopen membership talks with the European Union.

The last time it applied, in 2009, Iceland’s economy was in tatters after the 
global financial crisis. Negotiations were abandoned four years later after a 
change of government.

Now, as one of the world’s wealthiest nations per capita and with the 
geopolitical winds having shifted, some see an opportunityto land a better deal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-06/iceland-debates-benefits-of-euro-entry-as-vote-on-eu-talks-nears?cmpid=BBD082826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8261>
.
The Harpa Concert Hall and Conference Centre in downtown Reykjavík on May 29.
Photographer: Betty Laura Zapata/Bloomberg
Iceland’s population may be small at 400,000 souls, but the island plays an 
outsize role due to its strategic location in the North Atlantic.

That importance has been heightened by US threats to quit NATO and grab 
Greenland,Russia’s renewed submarine presence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-russian-arctic-submarines-nato-response/?cmpid=BBD082826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8261>
, and competitionfor the Arctic 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2025-arctic-russia-high-north-threat/?cmpid=BBD082826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8261>
’s resources and emerging status as a shipping route from east to west.

Though a founding member of NATO, Iceland has never had an army of its own and 
has been under the protective wing of Washington since 1951. Authorities want 
to add to its defenses through European cooperation.

Moving closer into Europe’s orbit has long been a dividing issue for Iceland, 
which gained independence from Denmark in 1944.

Fisheries and agriculture are key stumbling blocks. In the end, most people 
are likely to vote with their wallet.
A “NEI” sign, meaning “no.”Photographer: Heiða Helgadóttir/Bloomberg
Polls suggest it’s too close to call. EU membership would require another 
referendum further down the line.

Both sides have run campaigns during the summer vacation months 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/spain-and-iceland-see-tourism-boom-with-total-solar-eclipse?cmpid=BBD082826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8261>
, leaving many weary of the topic as the long days draw shorter.

One slogan translates as “yes to see,” meaning vote in favor and see what the 
EU offers.

Others post simply: “Nei til að fá frið” — No, for some peace and quiet. —

Ratko Mladic, the former Serb military commander known as the “Butcher of 
Bosnia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/ratko-mladic-bosnia-s-convicted-war-criminal-has-died?cmpid=BBD082826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8261>

against humanity and genocide during the 1990s war in Bosnia-Herzegovina that 
left more than 100,000 dead and over 2 million people displaced. The fighting

and was Europe’s deadliest conflict since World War II, until Russia’s 2022 
all-out invasion of Ukraine.
