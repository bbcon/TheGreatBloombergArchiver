Wrong-footed

Wall Street trading desks predicted the stocks rally would resume once the 
election was over.The market had other ideas 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/wall-street-traders-wrong-footed-after-bullish-equity-calls?cmpid=110626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11101>
.

The logic was that investors had been keeping cash on the sidelines ahead of 
US midterms, and with the election in the rearview mirror, they would jump back 
into a US stock market that’s been languishing for months.

Instead, soon after the opening bell on the New York Stock Exchange, they 
rushed for the exits, pulling down everything from high-flying chipmakers like 
Nvidia to defensive fallbacks like Walmart.

While it’s hard to make the case that the US economic outlook changed 
dramatically overnight, investors are clearly worried that sweeping Democratic 
victories and popular pushback against data centerswill slow the AI rollout 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/wall-street-peers-into-abyss-of-ai-trade-as-midterms-expose-risk?cmpid=110626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11101>
.

Other factors that might explain the speed and breadth of the selloff include 
positioning, selling by“fast-money” systematic investors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/fast-money-systematic-funds-stoked-2-3-trillion-tech-stock-rout?cmpid=110626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11101>
 andcrowded hedge funds bets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/crowded-hedge-fund-trades-are-unwinding-fast-after-ugly-october?cmpid=110626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261106&utm_content=11101>
 that were unwound at warp speed.

Hedge funds, which had a rough October, are coming under scrutiny because 
strategies that are designed to behave independently are losing money in 
lockstep, exposing crowded positions and heavy leverage.

Of course, one session doesn’t make a trend in the stock market and the 
bullish views that we highlighted in yesterday’s newsletter may be borne out 
over the coming months. But that job is now harder, with new risks coming into 
view.

“I suspect the bigger risk is still ahead: As losses mount, risk managers may 
force funds to cut exposure, potentially triggering another wave of selling 
regardless of fundamentals,” said Bruno Schneller of Erlen Capital Management. —
