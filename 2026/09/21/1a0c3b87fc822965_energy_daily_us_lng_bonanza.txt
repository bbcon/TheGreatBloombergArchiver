Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Asia Energy Team Leader Stephen Stapczynski looks at a possible windfall for US 
gas exporters if China lifts import duties.

The US liquefied natural gas industry will be watching this week’s meeting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/bessent-hails-very-successful-china-talks-on-ai-threats-trade?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 between Donald Trump and Xi Jinping for any sign Beijing will remove tariffs 
on American LNG. Such a move could open the floodgates for billions of dollars’ 
worth of new supply deals.

China imposed a 15% levy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-04/china-probes-google-moments-after-trump-s-tariffs-enter-effect?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 on imports of the super-chilled fuel from the US last year as trade tensions 
escalated. Thatall but halted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-04/chinese-tariffs-on-us-lng-set-to-reroute-global-trade-flows?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 flows between the world’s biggest buyer and seller.

If China resumed importing all of its contracted US supply for a full year, 
those shipments would be valued atroughly $6 billion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/trump-xi-talks-could-mean-6-billion-of-us-natural-gas-for-china?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 at current prices, Bloomberg calculations show.

That would give Trump something tangible to point to as he seeks to narrow the 
trade deficit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/us-trade-gap-widens-to-largest-since-early-last-year-on-imports?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
. Yet for the LNG producers, the bigger opportunity would be reopening the door 
to fresh deals.

Chinese companies have largely steered clear of signing new US contracts since 
the trade dispute erupted. That became a hurdle for several proposed American 
LNG export projects, since such deals are vital for developers to secure 
financing and start large-scale construction.
US LNG exporters are hoping that Chinese President Xi Jinping agrees to relax 
tariffs at a meeting with President Donald Trump this week.Photographer: Dado 
Galdieri/Bloomberg
It isn’t just LNG. China slapped 10% retaliatory duties on American liquefied 
petroleum gas, and that industry may also get a boost if Beijing decides to 
waive them.

Removing the tariffs, as well as some of the political baggage around buying 
US energy, could entice Chinese importers back to the table. In fact, there are 
signs that’s already happening.

China Gas Holdings Ltd. last week agreed to purchase 
<https://news.bloomberglaw.com/international-trade/china-gas-signs-rare-deal-to-buy-us-lng-from-venture-global>
 US LNG for 20 years starting in the 2030s, while other Chinese firms were 
discussingsimilar deals 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/lng-buyers-look-to-us-to-hedge-future-risk-of-hormuz-disruption?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 on the sidelines of the Gastech conference in Bangkok.

The timing makes sense. Chinese importers have more reason to diversify 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-16/lng-buyers-turn-to-us-as-hormuz-crisis-forces-supply-rethink?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 after the US-Iran war disrupted supplies through the Strait of Hormuz. Qatar’s 
LNG expansion plans — which Chinese buyers have bought into — aresuffering 
delays 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-20/qatar-energy-chief-al-kaabi-sees-lng-expansion-startup-in-2027?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 as the near-closure of the waterway continues.

These contracts can also deliver hefty trade numbers. A single, 1 
million-ton-a-year LNG deal lasting 20 years would be worth roughly $8 billion. 
That would be the bigger trade prize for the industry, and for Trump.

—Stephen Stapczynski, Bloomberg News

The soaring cost of shipping oil is making some long-distance crude trades 
uneconomical, threatening to disrupt flows at a time when fuel markets have 
never been tighter. Moving a cargo from Houston to Asia now adds about$26 a 
barrel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-19/world-running-short-of-supertankers-threatens-long-haul-oil-flow?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>
 to the cost of supplying the world’s largest importing region. And while high 
freight costs aren’t a new normal, they mayhang around for a while 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-09-21/oil-market-the-1-million-a-day-oil-tanker-will-drive-inflation?cmpid=BBBXT092126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9128>

BNEF today

Emerging Asia-Pacific markets flipped from net clean-tech exports to net 
imports last year as a $2.1 billion surplus in 2024 became a $9.6 billion 
deficit, according toBloombergNEF <https://about.bnef.com/>. Outbound shipments 
fell sharply as US tariffs hit solar products. Imports rose by $6.6 billion 
across all seven markets, led by lithium-ion batteries, wind nacelles and 
passenger battery-electric vehicles.
