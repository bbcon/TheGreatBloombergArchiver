Deep Dive: Russia’s Struggling War Economy
A billboard promoting contract army service, reading “5,200,000 rubles for the 
first year of contract,” in Moscow.Photographer: Alexander Nemenov/AFP/Getty 
Images
As President Vladimir Putin’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine extends for a 
fifth year, Russian businessescan no longer afford 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/russia-s-war-economy-is-fueling-pay-gains-companies-can-t-afford?cmpid=080626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7430>
 to keep pace with the Kremlin’s war machine in the wage race.

 * Years of acute labor shortages have pushed salaries well ahead of 
productivity growth, forcing companies to pay more to generate the same amount 
of output. In prior years, surging corporate revenues absorbed the pressure.
 * With the wartime economic boom now at an end, businesses are shifting into 
survival mode, slashing costs in ways that may push many out of the race for 
workers.
 * Wages have outpaced productivity by about 5 percentage points since the war 
began in 2022, according to Bloomberg Economics estimates. That’s close to the 
gap recorded during Russia’s oil-fueled boom in the 2000s and well above the 
average of about 1 percentage point between 2009 and 2021.
