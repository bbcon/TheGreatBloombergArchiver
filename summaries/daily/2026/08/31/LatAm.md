• Trump's plan to seize direct control of Venezuela's oil reserves is drawing warnings the country risks becoming a "modern-day resource colony," with economists flagging contract-repudiation risk for investors under any future government.
• The EU-Mercosur trade deal, creating a free-trade zone of 780 million consumers, is stoking concern among some Mercosur producers about fiercer competition from European goods in their home markets.

**Political Economy**
President Abelardo de la Espriella's Trump-aligned administration issued a series of resolutions formally terminating negotiations with Colombia's rebel groups, a sharp reversal from the Petro-era peace process and one that follows a more confrontational posture toward armed groups, including recent bombing operations. In Venezuela, the prospect of direct US control over oil reserves has revived comparisons to early-20th-century resource extraction arrangements; University of Denver economist Francisco Rodríguez noted that investors must now weigh whether a future government would repudiate contracts signed under such an arrangement, a meaningful overhang for capital committed to the sector.

**Trade**
The EU-Mercosur agreement's implications remain two-sided for the region: it opens European market access for regional exporters but simultaneously exposes domestic producers to increased competition from European goods, a tension likely to shape the deal's ratification politics across member economies in the months ahead.
