Deep Dive: AI Peace Dividend

During the Cold War, the Soviet Union’s smallest republic 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/nvidia-chips-donald-trump-turn-armenia-into-ai-hotspot?cmpid=092226_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260922&utm_content=9174>
 was an electronics and tech center for the Communist superpower. Now Armenia 
is on track to become one of the world’s top hubs for American AI 
infrastructure.

 * In August, a San Francisco-based startup called Firebird began operations 
of a data center in Armenia that’s set to reach 300 megawatts and more than 
70,000 cutting-edge Nvidia chips by the end of next year.
 * A fifth of its computing power is reserved for domestic use — to power 
local research, government and startup ecosystems — with the remainder 
allocated to foreign firms, including US ones like Perplexity AI.
 * The data center played a role in securing a peace treaty between Armenia 
and neighboring Azerbaijan after decades of conflict over a disputed territory. 
President Donald Trump’s team promised permission for Nvidia exports in order 
to advance conversations with Armenia, according to people involved in the 
talks.
 * The role of Nvidia chips in that deal, first reported by the Wall Street 
Journal, is one of the more extraordinary examples of Trump’s unorthodox 
approach to AI diplomacy, showing how Trump’s team utilizes certain chip export 
regulations to achieve goals that extend far beyond technology.
