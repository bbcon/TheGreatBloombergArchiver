**Commodity Dynamics and Trade Flows**
Chile's copper output deterioration — the worst monthly performance in over 15 years — is providing fundamental support to global copper prices at a moment when AI infrastructure investment is generating structural demand. The supply shortfall is unlikely to reverse quickly given the capital intensity and lead times involved in expanding Chilean mine capacity. Separately, Brazil's beef exporters face an abrupt disruption: Chinese tariffs triggered by quota exhaustion will make Brazilian shipments materially less competitive in Beijing, potentially redirecting supply toward alternative markets and affecting global protein price dynamics. The extent of trade diversion will depend on how quickly alternative buyers can absorb Brazilian volumes.

**Mexico Trade Optimism**
Mexico's increasing confidence around a US trade deal — covering steel, aluminum, and light vehicles — would, if realized, represent a meaningful de-escalation in bilateral trade tensions and support near-term investment sentiment in the country's manufacturing sector. Ford's CEO publicly echoed the urgency of reaching agreements with both Canada and Mexico, reflecting the degree to which automotive supply chain integration makes prolonged tariff uncertainty costly for US industry as well. The outcome remains subject to US domestic political dynamics, with midterm elections sharpening the administration's focus on delivering tangible economic wins.
