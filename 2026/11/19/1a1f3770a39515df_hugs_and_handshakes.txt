It’s seven days since Jensen Huang stood down as head of US chip giant Nvidia, 
and already his legacy as a key advocate for China looks to be in trouble.

The Commerce Department this week announced sweeping new curbs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/us-moves-to-limit-remote-access-to-advanced-ai-chips?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
 on Beijing’s access to the kind of high-end AI chips Nvidia excels at 
producing, signaling a resurgence of the China hawks in Washington.

But then came China’s APEC summit, and Xi Jinping’s hug for Donald Trump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/china-s-xi-greets-trump-at-airport-in-shenzhen-in-rare-move?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
 as he touched down in Shenzhen yesterday.

China made sure to say it was returning the favor after Trump met Xi at the 
airport in September. But it got the visit off to a friendly start.

Contrast their embrace 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/china-airport-greetings-show-how-xi-views-trump-putin-takaichi?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
 with poor Sanae Takaichi’s treatment. The Japanese premier has displeased 
China over Taiwan, and was received by aformer official. Playing the gracious 
host, Xi later gave her cordial handshakes.

Xi and Trump were joined in an unprecedented three-way meeting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/xi-says-he-s-committed-to-working-with-trump-as-two-leaders-meet?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
 today by Russian President Vladimir Putin, emboldened by his elevation to a 
trilateral despite waging war on Ukraine.

For all the optics, this show was very much Xi’s. And for him, China’s US 
relationship is a more pressing concern.

Tensions over chips, trade, Taiwan and rare earths are all still present.

The AI race remains inescapable with Anthropic’s monster IPO 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/anthropic-is-said-to-price-ipo-at-83-per-share?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
 valuing the frontier lab at some $1.5 trillion — more than half the size of 
Russia’s economy.

The new US restrictions will inevitably slow China’s AI progress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/tencent-says-us-chip-curbs-to-slow-chinese-ai-in-the-short-run?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
. Beijing has warned of retaliation, and Xi has yet to choose his response.

Above all, he needs stability to help China’s economy out of the doldrums.

Trump has his own domestic considerations with serial outages 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/tech-paranoia-intensifies-after-outages-roil-public-trust?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
 scaring Americans into stockpiling.

The hugs and handshakes are over, but the US and China are still very much 
grappling with how to live with each other.— Alan Crawford

Chart of the Day

China’s anemic labor market has contributed to weak consumption in recent 
years, inflaming global trade tensions as Xi’s government relied on exports to 
buoy growth. Now AI threatens to worsen one of the top vulnerabilities facing 
the world’s second-biggest economy — and demoralize a generation of younger 
workers in the process. While other nations have seen aslowdown in hiring 
<bbg://screens/NSN%20TOPX43KIJH8H> for young professionals in AI-exposed 
occupations, the sheer scale of the challenge in China is unique.

Norway has more than half a century of expertise in extracting oil and gas 
from the North Sea, one of the harshest environments in which companies 
operate. Now the energy industry is moving into the even more inhospitable 
territory of the Arctic and the small town of Hammerfest, 1,000 miles closer to 
the North Pole than Norway’s oil capital of Stavanger, is lookingto reap rewards
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-19/norway-expands-arctic-oil-and-gas-exploration-defense-near-russia-border?cmpid=BBD111926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11611>
