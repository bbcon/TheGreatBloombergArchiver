**Bond Markets and the $100 Oil Nexus**
The defining macro theme of the quarter is the reinforcing loop between elevated energy prices and rising sovereign yields. Brent averaging approximately $102 in September — sustained by Strait of Hormuz disruption — has rekindled inflation expectations globally, reducing the likelihood of near-term central bank easing and simultaneously weakening the case for duration in fixed income. The result has been a synchronized global bond selloff: US 10-year yields at their highest since 2002, UK 30-year gilt yields breaching 6%, and French spreads at 2012 levels. BloombergNEF's base case — a November Hormuz reopening — implies Brent averaging $92 for full-year 2026; a closure extending into April 2027 could push prices toward $147–$150, a scenario that would render the current bond pressure look modest by comparison.

**Aviation Security and Geopolitical Contagion**
The FlyDubai incident — in which a co-pilot attacked the pilot on a Tel Aviv-bound flight carrying predominantly Israeli passengers — has crystallized concerns about the vulnerability of civilian aviation to geopolitical contagion from the US-Iran conflict. Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu stopped short of confirming Iranian links, while UK Prime Minister Burnham stated his belief in Iranian involvement in a separate airbase security incident. Whether the FlyDubai attack was terrorism or an isolated act of violence remains unresolved, but the episode is accelerating debate about cockpit security standards globally. Agricultural commodity prices adding their largest quarterly gain since Russia's Ukraine invasion compounds the inflationary pressure facing policymakers across multiple regions simultaneously.
