**Monetary Policy — Australia**
The RBA's nine-member board voted unanimously to raise the cash rate from 4.35% to 4.60%, citing persistent cost pressures on firms and evidence of pass-through to consumer prices. The statement retained an explicit tightening bias, noting that "upside risks to inflation are materialising." Swap markets subsequently priced a 56% probability of a further hike in November, suggesting the tightening cycle is not yet perceived as complete. The decision will exert additional pressure on Australia's housing market, which is already in a downturn, creating a familiar tension between inflation control and credit-sensitive sectors of the domestic economy.

**Geopolitics and Corporate News**
Sino-Japanese relations deteriorated to what Beijing described as a low point unseen in decades, with Foreign Minister Wang Yi pressing a Japanese delegation on Prime Minister Takaichi's Taiwan remarks. The diplomatic friction adds complexity for regional capital flows and trade. On the corporate side, Shein's first earnings release as a public company disappointed materially — a 53% decline in first-half operating income reflecting rising costs and weakening demand — underscoring that the fast-fashion model faces structural margin pressure. Indonesia's wildfire crisis worsened, with hotspot counts at decade highs and disaster-response budgets cut to roughly one-tenth of 2024 levels, raising spillover risks for regional air quality and diplomatic relations with neighbors.
