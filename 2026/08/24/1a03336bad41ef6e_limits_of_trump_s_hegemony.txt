 over the hemisphere.

So why has Noboa just concluded a weeklong trip to China, meeting with 
President Xi Jinping and securing Chinese support to help prepare for the El 
Niño climate event, including an AI-powered meteorological system?

The fact is that for Noboa and many of his regional counterparts, political 
closeness with the Trump administration doesn’t translate intochoosing the US 
over China 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-02-10/us-china-trump-s-focus-on-west-hands-xi-economic-edge-in-asia?cmpid=BBD082426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8110>
 on the economic front.

China has overtaken the US as Latin America’s top trading partner, while goods 
trade with the region has grown from just over $14 billion in 2000 to more than 
$500 billion in 2024.
Noboa and Xi after a signing ceremony in Beijing on Aug. 18.Photographer: 
Andres Martinez Casares/Pool/Getty Images
The rightward political turn under Trump’s so-called Donroe Doctrine has done 
little so far to unwind those strongeconomic ties with Beijing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-fails-to-curb-china-s-economic-influence-in-latin-america?cmpid=BBD082426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8110>
.

China continues to deepen its position in sectors it’s cultivated for decades 
— including critical minerals, power and infrastructure — while expanding into 
areas like financial services, consumer businesses and manufacturing.

It’s also moved up the value chain into electric vehicles 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2023-08-09/ford-shuts-brazil-electric-car-factory-as-lula-turns-to-china-s-byd?cmpid=BBD082426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8110>
, batteries and other clean technologies that the Trump administration largely 
shuns.

That’s not to say that Beijing is ignoring Trump’s push for hemispheric 
hegemony. It’s adapting to US scrutiny, shifting some of its push away from 
exposed government-to-government projects toward quieter private-sector 
channels.

China’s reach increasingly extends below the national level, with Chinese 
firms cultivating governors, mayors and local business groups even when 
governments tilt closer to Washington on issues like security.

The result is an economic relationship that even some of Trump’s staunchest 
regional allies are unwilling to unwind.

To much of Latin America, political alignment with Washington is one thing; 
walking away from what China has to offer is another. — Mie Dahl 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYIc6C8LnOs/mie-dahl?cmpid=BBD082426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8110>

Chart of the Day

Londoners are facing historic levels of risk in subsidence — a phenomenon 
associated with bouts of hot, dry weather that shrink the soil and destabilize 
the foundations on which buildings stand — after five successive heat waves 
dried out the clay soil on which much of the city is built. Insurance claims 
tied to the issuehit a record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/londoners-find-horrendous-cracks-in-homes-after-heat-waves?cmpid=BBD082426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260824&utm_content=8110>
 last quarter, with households claiming £20,000 on average for the risk, more 
than in any previous quarter and a 15% jump from the same period in 2025.
