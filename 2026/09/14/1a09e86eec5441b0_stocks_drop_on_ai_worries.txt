For Swiss billionaire Christoph Blocher, there’s always one more fight 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/switzerland-debates-longstanding-neutrality-ahead-of-vote-this-month?cmpid=091426_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8868>
.

 * The 85-year-old built his business reputation at manufacturer EMS-Chemie, 
then turned his political party into the country’s biggest by pushing a 
nationalist and anti-immigration message. He’s stepped back somewhat in recent 
years, but never sits on the sidelines. With the country about to vote on its 
longstanding neutrality this month, he’s in full campaign mode.
 * The proposal on the ballot wants to change the constitution, eliminating 
any flexibility in how neutrality is applied. At the root of the campaign is 
Switzerland’sshock 2022 decision 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-02-28/swiss-break-with-historic-neutrality-to-enforce-russia-sanctions?cmpid=091426_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8868>
 to adopt EU sanctions against Russia, which Blocher and the Swiss People’s 
Party have opposed.
 * The debate reflects bigger questions about where the country sees itself in 
a world of increasingly fractured international relations. For many on the 
right, it’s lost much of what made it special, and can’t afford to let 
neutrality be weakened.
 * It’s a head-to-head between two competing visions. One points to 
sovereignty, neutrality and distance from larger powers — particularly the EU — 
as the foundations of Switzerland’s success. The second sees international 
cooperation asessential for a small economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-05/switzerland-s-us-tariff-blow-threatens-its-go-it-alone-approach?cmpid=091426_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260914&utm_content=8868>
, which can steer its own fate only so far.
 * “Swiss neutrality — not just any neutrality, but Swiss neutrality — is part 
of the Swiss identity,” Blocher told Bloomberg. He’s put 2 million francs of 
his own money into a media blitz ahead of the vote on Sept. 27.
