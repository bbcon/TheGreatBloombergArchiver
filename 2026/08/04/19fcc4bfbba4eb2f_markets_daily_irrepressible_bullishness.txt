The bull market is intact

It may be hard to believe, given all the recent drama in stock markets, but 
the S&P 500 ison the verge of a fresh record high 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=080426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7323>
 for the first time since June 2.

After the selloff in chip stocks and other AI winners over the past couple of 
months, a lot of froth in the equity market has been aggressively removed and 
valuations have been reset. Data from prime brokers — the firms that finance 
hedge funds — shows thatleverage has largely been cleaned up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/dip-buyers-need-to-heed-the-market-s-warning-signs-taking-stock?cmpid=080426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7323>
.

Individual investors, too, have dialed back their speculation: Last week they 
posted theirlargest week of stock selling since 2022 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/citadel-securities-says-bull-market-drivers-are-firmly-intact?cmpid=080426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7323>
, and it was largely concentrated in tech stocks, according to Citadel 
Securities.

Over the course of the year, investors have been broadening their search for 
winners, snapping up shares of smaller companies, industrials and real estate 
stocks.The Russell 2000 small-cap index is on pace to beat the S&P 500 for the 
first time since 2020.

Forget the war in Iran and rising interest rates: The forces that have 
propelled US stocks to record highs this year remain “firmly intact,” according 
to Citadel.

“Markets are transitioning from a flow-driven environment back to one 
increasingly dictated by earnings, corporate demand, and the macroeconomic 
backdrop,” said Citadel’s Scott Rubner. —Phil Serafino 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ABoP3GGeTtg/phil-serafino?cmpid=080426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7323>

SpaceX faces a test

SpaceX’s first earnings report following its blockbuster IPO is imminent. It 
may not give investorsa reason to buy the sinking stock 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/spacex-s-first-earnings-offer-a-chance-to-reverse-stock-s-plunge?cmpid=080426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7323>
.

The problem is SpaceX isn’t profitable and has a very speculative business at 
this point, so the results may end up raising more questions than they answer. 
With the shares still trading at a sky-high valuation despite the selloff, it 
will be difficult to entice new buyers.

To make matters even more challenging, a flood of fresh SpaceX shares also is 
about to hit the market, as the first of many lockups that ban early investors 
from selling expires in two days. As many as 911.5 million shares worth more 
than $100 billion will be released Thursday. —Carmen Reinicke 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AT8Yc28TTKE/carmen-reinicke?cmpid=080426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7323>
