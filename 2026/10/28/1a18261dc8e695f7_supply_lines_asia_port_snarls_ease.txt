Port congestion in Asia is showing signs of easing after a seasonal rush to 
restock inventories.

That’s among the takeaways in a research note Wednesday from Denise Wong, a 
Bloomberg Intelligence industry analyst. She says front-loaded orders aimed at 
buffering against shipping delays pulled China’s export peak into August. Now 
activity is cooling after a holiday in early October.

“Calls at terminals run by Cosco Shipping Ports and China Merchants Port have 
since dropped nearly 30% following Golden Week shutdowns, yet remain 8% above 
year-ago levels, suggesting a seasonal pullback rather than broader trade 
contraction,” Wong wrote in thenote published <> on the Bloomberg Terminal.

Related: Asian Ports Snarled as El Niño Fuels Busy Typhoon Season 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/asian-ports-snarled-as-el-nino-fuels-busy-typhoon-season?cmpid=BBD102826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10563>

According to Wong, cargo dwell times at Asian hubs have returned to normal in 
recent weeks, with PSA’s Singapore terminals “steady near one day and Colombo 
easing from spring highs.” Meantime, US West Coast congestion was edging up, 
with the average call duration up 16% in Los Angeles to 2.4 days and 11% higher 
in neighboring Long Beach to 2 days compared with a year earlier, she wrote.

Asian port snarls, caused in part by a typhoon and other harsh weather events, 
were among the main reasons container schedule reliability plunged in August 
from a month earlier, reaching the lowest level since September 2022, according 
todata 
<https://www.sea-intelligence.com/press-room/410-global-schedule-reliability-continues-to-plummet-in-august>
 from Sea-Intelligence. Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd, partners in the Gemini 
alliance, were ranked as the most reliable, followed by MSC and CMA CGM. Cosco 
ranked sixth.

Given the port delays through the summer months and solid demand for imports 
into the US,spot container rates 
<https://www.drewry.co.uk/supply-chain-advisors/supply-chain-expertise/world-container-index-assessed-by-drewry>
 have remained elevated since May. That’s helping boosting profits for the 
carriers, according to shipping industry analyst John McCown, who expects US 
inbound volumes will be strong in October, too, “heavily influenced by 
front-loading.”

McCown tracks US container volumes and carrier profitability, and his estimate 
for second-quarter net income for the industry was $6 billion, a seven-fold 
increase from the first quarter. “The latest pricing trends point to another 
significant increase in net income” during for the third quarter, according to 
the latest McCown Container Volume Observer.
