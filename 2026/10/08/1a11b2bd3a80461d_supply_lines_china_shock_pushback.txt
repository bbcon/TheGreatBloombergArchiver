The global pushback against the threat of China’s growing export dominance is 
reaching a critical juncture.

European Union officials are striking a more assertive approach to China as it 
heads into two days of negotiations in Beijing aimed at avoiding an 
economically damaging trade war.

The EU’s trade chief, Maros Sefcovic, will meet with Chinese Commerce Minister 
Wang Wentao to address imbalances and discuss measures including limits on 
imports of Chinese hybrid vehicles, Bloomberg News isreporting Thursday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/eu-is-taking-harder-line-on-china-s-trade-gap-as-talks-kick-off?cmpid=BBD100826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9711>
.

“It represents a significant change of approach,” said Alicia García Herrero, 
a senior fellow at Bruegel, a think tank in Brussels, referring to so-called 
safeguard measures aimed at protecting domestic industry.

Sefcovic’s kicked off talks today speaking with European businesses. “The 
message around the table was clear: The need to improve access to the Chinese 
market, while boosting our economic security,” he wrote in apost on X 
<https://x.com/MarosSefcovic/status/2108104655767048327>.

Beijing has repeatedly threatened to retaliate if the EU introduces new trade 
tools or discriminatory restrictions targeting Chinese companies or products.

Still, Germany on Wednesday said it’s blocking 
<bbg://news/stories/TMJFXEVTTD09> China’s Cosco Shipping from acquiring an 80% 
stake in Hamburg-based freight forwarder Konrad Zippel, citing security risks 
and a fear that the deal would expand strategic dependencies and weaken supply 
chain resilience.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s cabinet nixed the transaction under 
foreign-investment screening rules, the Finance Ministry said. “The acquisition 
would have deepened dependencies and jeopardized the resilience of Germany’s 
and the EU’s supply chains,” it said.

Coordinated Response

Also Wednesday, the US joined 14 other economies in pledging to confront 
excess manufacturing capacity and production in certain sectors — a show of 
unity aimed at China even if didn’t specify that the world’s biggest exporter 
is the target.

Economies signing the joint statement 
<https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/Joint%20Ministerial%20Statement%20Addressing%20Structural%20Excess%20Capacity%20and%20Production%20(10.2026).pdf>
 with the Trump administration included Argentina, Australia, Canada, the 
European Union, France, Germany, India, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, 
Poland, Turkey and the UK.

G20 countries that opted not to sign include China, Brazil, Russia and South 
Africa.

The US is carrying out an investigation into excess capacity targeting more 
than a dozen major trading partners. It is not clear when that probe will 
conclude but it is expected to result in higher tariffs on the targeted 
economies.

Brazil’s Auto Surge

Alarm bells are also ringing in South America’s biggest economy. Brazil’s 
rising imports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/brazil-auto-jobs-imperiled-by-chinese-imports-stellantis-says?cmpid=BBD100826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9711>
 of vehicles partially assembled in China could eventually put thousands of 
auto-industry jobs at risk in the South American country, the head of 
Stellantis in the region said.

Chinese auto brands accounted for about 17% of new-car sales in Brazil through 
the first nine months of 2026, including a 25% share in September alone, 
according to Herlander Zola, Stellantis’ president for South America. That’s a 
rapid gain from roughly 9% for all of last year.

 | Global goods trade is withstanding the drags of tariffs, wars and 
geopolitical rifts, fueled by the boom in demand for artificial intelligence 
gear, a new report showed. Growth in merchandise trade this year is forecast at 
4.6%, stronger than the 3.6% gain predicted in January, according to the DHL 
Globalization Tracker, which is co-produced by the Bonn, Germany-based parcel

of 2.7%.
