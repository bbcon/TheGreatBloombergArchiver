‘Not going to miss it’

After the S&P 500 fell into a correction, the talk on the street this morning 
is whether this is the end of the selloff or not.

Morgan Stanley strategist Michael Wilson arguably has the most bullish view, 
and says the capitulation hasserved up a rare opportunity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/morgan-stanley-s-wilson-says-buying-opportunity-too-good-to-miss?cmpid=112326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11724>
, particularly in hyperscalers and industrials.

“This is the fat pitch we have patiently been waiting for, and we’re not going 
to miss it,” Wilson wrote on Monday. “Markets bottom on bad news and 
deleveraging. We have had a lot of both.”

Another prominent strategist, Ed Yardeni, cut his S&P 500 target, but is still 
holding onto a belief that a year-end rally is coming. He sees the index ending 
2026 at 7,500 points, down from the 7,900 he projected in September. Still, 
that represents a recovery of more than 8% from Friday.

“We got more of a buying opportunity than we bargained for,” said Yardeni. “We 
expected a pullback. It has turned into a correction.”

There are reasons for caution. While Goldman Sachs’ trading desk said the 
selloffcreated a buying opportunity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-22/goldman-traders-see-entry-point-in-us-stocks-but-warn-of-risks?cmpid=112326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11724>
, they also warned that overall positioning is elevated and the broader 
capitulation needed for a meaningful rally could be missing.

“There will necessarily be a technical rebound given the violence of the drop 
last week, but I don’t think it will last,” said Pierre-Yves Gauthier at 
AlphaValue.

One positive: The last time stocks slumped as much in a week, in 2020, the US 
was about to enter a brief recession. Now, the one-year-ahead recession 
indicator shows a one-in-five chance of the economy shrinking.

That suggests the latest selloff is about Wall Street, not the economy. —

The week ahead

Key economic data on Wednesday will offer fresh insight into how US consumers 
are holding up in the face of rising prices.

Economists surveyed by Bloomberg estimate inflation-adjusted personal spending 
rose by 0.1% in October from a month earlier, a modest increase that builds on 
two robust quarters of consumer spending growth.

Yet price pressures are mounting. The personal consumption expenditures price 
index — the Fed’s preferred inflation metric — is projected to rise 3.8%. That 
would match May as the biggest annual increase in three years. The core measure 
is also seen quickening, making interest-rate hikes more likely.

 * Monday: US PMI data from S&P Global
 * Tuesday: Germany’s Ifo business sentiment survey
 * Wednesday: US personal income and spending, PCE, durable goods, new home 
sales and the Fed’s Beige Book; France consumer confidence
 * Thursday: Thanksgiving holiday; South Korea interest-rate decision
 * Friday: Taiwan GDP; France inflation, payrolls and GDP, S&P Global Ratings

Metals mayhem

The metals world is reeling from a series of massive squeezes in copper, 
aluminum and tin that were followed last week by price crashes. Many traders 
saw it coming, but were stillcaught off guard 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-22/from-squeezes-to-meltdown-a-week-of-mayhem-rocks-metals-markets?cmpid=112326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261123&utm_content=11724>
 by the magnitude of selling.

Where does that leave investors?

The spot supply crunches that sent spreads soaring remain in place, creating 
the prospect of more volatility to come. Across several key metals, traders are 
grappling with a confusing reality: the outlook for prices is weakening 
rapidly, even as some parts of the market are facing extreme supply squeezes.

“This is not just about the physical world, this is about the financial world 
as well, and what’s happening with rates and the dollar and the stock market,” 
said Guy Wolf of Marex Group.
