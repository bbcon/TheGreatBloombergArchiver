Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Kevin Crowley looks at how Trump’s rhetoric about the high cost of 
fuel is starting to echo that of his predecessor.

The recent surge in US gasoline prices is creating an awkward political moment 
for President Donald Trump’s relationship with Big Oil.

Trump, after all, pledged to reduce the price at the pump to below $2 a gallon 
during his campaign. Instead, the average cost isnow above $4 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/rising-fuel-prices-hit-americans-now-without-tax-refunds-to-tap?cmpid=BBBXT080526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7376>
 — a 30% rise since he took office. Most of that run-up has come since his 
decision to go to war with Iran in February.

The average nationwide gasoline price during the 18 months of Trump’s second 
term is just 3% lower than Joe Biden’s four years in the White House. That’s 
ramping up political pressure on Republicans as the mid-term elections loom in 
November.

While Trump’s pro-oil agenda is the polar opposite of Biden’s, his solution to 
dealing with high gasoline prices has a familiar ring: blame Big Oil.

ExxonMobil Holdings Corp. and Chevron Corp. are “making too much money 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/trump-hits-exxon-chevron-for-profits-amid-iran-war-he-launched?cmpid=BBBXT080526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7376>
,” Trump said at the White House after the companies reported $29 billion of 
combined profit for the second quarter — or $318 million a day.

“I don’t like it,” Trump said, urging them to “give some of that back to the 
public” by reducing the price at the pump.

His comments were reminiscent of Biden, who memorably claimed that ExxonMobil 
“made more money than God” in 2022 after Russia invaded Ukraine.

Oil executives tend to take such accusations in their stride, given they are 
price takers rather than price makers in the global marketplace.

But they must be careful. Trump could call for a windfall profits tax, invoke 
judicial action or even push for an export ban on petroleum products, according 
to Bob McNally, president of Rapidan Energy Group LLC. While such steps are 
unlikely, he said, the mere specter of them is worrisome.

“It’s unusual to have a Republican president saying this,” McNally said. 
“That’s got to be concerning.”

Trump last month ordered the Department of Justice to investigate why gas 
prices have not fallen quick enough. Democratspledged yesterday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/big-oil-s-soaring-profits-in-democrats-sights-if-house-flips?cmpid=BBBXT080526_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7376>
 to also scrutinize the industry’s profits if they retake the House of 
Representatives in November.

It’s perhaps the one thing both sides can agree on.

—Kevin Crowley, Bloomberg News
