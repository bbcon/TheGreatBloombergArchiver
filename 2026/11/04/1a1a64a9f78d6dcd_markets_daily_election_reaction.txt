Oil and bonds

It was a safe bet for much of the year: higher oil prices are bad for 
government bonds.Now the link has been broken 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/oil-loses-its-grip-on-bonds-as-growth-fears-displace-inflation?cmpid=110426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10949>
 as money managers worry more about growth and less about inflation.

Brent crude prices and yields on 10-year Treasuries shot up in tandem when the 
US and Israel attacked Iran and the correlation stayed tight before peaking on 
Sept. 15. Since then it has reversed. Similar trends have been seen in German 
and UK government bonds.

The break is increasing the appeal of bonds, despite the risk of a flare-up in 
violence that could lead to another oil price spike. Traders say interest rates 
can’t rise much further given the economic damage already being caused by the 
energy shock and higher borrowing costs.

“The higher oil goes, the lower yields go now in the current environment, 
which seems counterintuitive but it really is the case. That’s because where 
yields are now is growth dampening,” said Megum Muhic at Royal Bank of Canada. 
“The market’s giving a signal that central banks shouldn’t be as aggressive in 
tightening.” —Georgia Hall 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXPC76oMnbY/georgia-hall?cmpid=110426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10949>
