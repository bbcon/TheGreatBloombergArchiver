The anti-incumbent sentiment against President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva was 
strong going into Brazil’s runoff election.

Resistance to what a Flávio Bolsonaro presidency might mean for Latin 
America’s biggest economy proved stronger in the end.

After a first-round defeat that put the leftist Lula’s political future in 
doubt,a sprawling mobilization 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/brazil-election-lula-rallies-supporters-for-one-last-comeback?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
 took shape around one goal: stopping Bolsonaro, and so preventing his 
right-wing movement from building on the gains it had already made in 
congressional elections.

Elderly Brazilians made their way to polling stations in wheelchairs. Voters 
abroad boarded planes to cast ballots back home. Gen Z protesters took to the 
streets from Brasília to São Paulo.

Even Madonna and Bad Bunny threw their support behind Lula, joining a parade 
of Brazilian and international celebrities urging voters to turn out.

The mission was simple: find the votes Lula had thus far failed to rally.

And they did.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-26/brazil-s-lula-wins-record-fourth-term-video?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
WATCH: Lula came from behind to win Brazil’s runoff election.
In a stunning plot twist, Lula stormed to victory 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/lula-wins-a-record-fourth-term-in-brazil-in-a-surprise-comeback?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
 — breaking a pattern that had held through every presidential runoff under the 
current system.

Flávio Bolsonaro, echoing his father Jair Bolsonaro’s defeat to Lula in 2022, 
refused to recognize the result andcalled for its annulment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/bolsonaro-calls-for-annulment-of-election-after-loss-to-lula?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
.

That spells yet more polarization ahead 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/brazil-court-extends-voting-by-two-hours-on-candidate-requests?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
, adding to Lula’s challenges.

Regardless, this was another improbable chapter for an 80-year-old leader 
whose career has included three presidential terms punctuated by a prison 
stretch.

But this comeback was different.

Lula entered the runoff wounded, facing an energized right and a clear 
appetite for change.

But after a frantic image makeover — one that even saw Lula finally get a 
mobile phone — the left realized that it didn’t need Brazil to fall back in 
love with him.

It just needed enough Brazilians to decide they wanted Bolsonaro even less 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-17/lula-boosts-social-welfare-payments-ahead-of-tight-election?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
.

That proved enough for Lula to win four more years. How long the vindication 
lasts is another matter. — Beatriz Reis

Chart of the Day

Under President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Turkey has grown its military and 
geopolitical footprint to an extent not seen since the Ottoman Empire, with 
troops and bases from Libya to northern Iraq, Syria, Qatar and Azerbaijan. In 
Somalia, Istanbul has built roads, hospitals, and schools and trains soldiers 
at a military academy. Turkey is one of a number of powers looking toexpand 
their influence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/erdogan-s-somalia-bet-pulls-turkey-into-a-wider-red-sea-contest?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>
 there, with the Horn of Africa nation growing in importance since the US and 
Israel’s war on Iran upended global shipping.

And Finally

Russia and Ukraine have stepped up assaults on each other’s export industries 
over the past year. The Kremlin is targeting ports, warehouses, ships and other 
infrastructure critical to Ukrainian agriculture. Kyiv is striking refineries, 
oil terminals and tankers as well as Russian grain hubs on the Black Sea and 
ships. Asthis story 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ukraine-russia-strikes-exports/?cmpid=BBD102626_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261026&utm_content=10571>

regional conflict can become a global one, with the impacts reverberating 
through markets.
