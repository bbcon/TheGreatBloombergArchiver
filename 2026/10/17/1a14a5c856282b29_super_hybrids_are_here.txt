Nearly electric

By Kyle Stock 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARKz8x17rNs/kyle-stock?cmpid=BBD101726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261017&utm_content=10216>

Sara Gray buys gas once a month.

Her 40-mile commute to work is entirely electric in her Volvo V60 plug-in 
hybrid. Gray, director of arts at California’s Monterey Museum of Art, only 
relies on the car’s combustion engine for longer weekend road trips up the 
coast or to see her parents in Reno.

“This was kind of a happy medium” between an EV and a fully gasoline-powered 
car, she said. Gray’s Volvo, a 2025 model, goes farther on battery power alone 
than all but a few other hybrids on the market.

A new generation of plug-in hybrid vehicles will soon be able to cover even 
the longer trips on batteries alone. The standard30 miles or so 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-03/mercedes-toyota-among-automakers-building-super-hybrids?cmpid=BBD101726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261017&utm_content=10216>
 of electric range will be pushed close to 50, analysts expect, and the top 
performers, including two models just unveiled by Volvo Car AB, will travel 
about 50% farther than that before the gas engine kicks on.
Volvo XC60, Black Edition Volvo Car CorporationSource: Volvo Car Corporation
“I wouldn’t call it an arms race, but there’s definitely a push,” said Peter 
Nagle, associate director of Americas demand forecasting at Mobility Global, an 
industry research group. “They’re for the consumer who wants to have that 
improved fuel economy and does plan to take these vehicles on long trips.”

Next year, Volvo’s XC60 will go almost twice as far on electrons. The company 
is fitting the machine and its larger SUV sibling, the XC90, with batteries 
that have roughly twice as much capacity as the current models, extending their 
electric-only range toalmost 80 miles 
<https://www.volvocars.com/intl/media/press-releases/AB3EBECCF06FA9C1/>. The 
goal, according to Luis Rezende, president of Volvo Car Americas, is to let the 
spark plugs and cylinders sleep on pretty much everything but road trips.

Mercedes-Benz Group AG is also long on long-range hybrids. It currently sells 
five plug-in models in the US, including the GLC 350e, which can travel 54 
miles on battery power. In part because of the new plug-ins, one in four 
Mercedes sold in the recent quarter was electrified, according to Greg Gates, 
vice president of sales at Mercedes-Benz USA.

To some extent, carmakers are riding a technological tailwind. As scientists 
develop cheaper,more efficient batteries 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-09/bnef-why-global-battery-prices-are-expected-to-fall-in-2026?cmpid=BBD101726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261017&utm_content=10216>
, auto executives can push their hybrids farther for the same cost.

That said, combining a gas engine with a large battery and motor is an 
expensive exercise. A plug-in hybrid generally costs $5,000 to $15,000 more 
than a conventional hybrid in the same class.

Heavy discount

By Angelina Rascouet 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQPSS6PssLE/angelina-rascouet?cmpid=BBD101726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261017&utm_content=10216>
,Jacob Reid 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AW6xtbTD-gc/jacob-reid?cmpid=BBD101726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261017&utm_content=10216>
, andJillian Deutsch 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVcbFaoiSa4/jillian-deutsch?cmpid=BBD101726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261017&utm_content=10216>

Earlier this month, shoppers on Yoox could snap up suede Brunello Cucinelli 
sneakers for €617 ($693), around half the price on the brand’s official website.

If they felt like pairing them with a beige Gucci mini-skirt, that was 
available for €800, close to 50% off as well. A Burberry t-shirt? The 
e-commerce website sold one for less than €200, or 60% off its original price.

Overall, some 2,500 products from those three labels were available, heavily 
discounted.

High-end fashion and leather goods makers try to avoid slashing prices or 
selling off excess stock because it damages the aura of exclusivity that they 
try to build around their products. That’s why at times, they’ve preferred to 
burn or shred their unsold goods: a controversial policy that is now banned in 
the European Union, home to the industry’s largest brands.
Chanel Spring/Summer 2027 collection during Paris Fashion Week on Oct. 5.
Photographer: JULIEN DE ROSA/AFP
Experts warn that there are many loopholes that may allow brands to continue 
to destroy goods. Nevertheless, the EU’s drive for greater sustainability in 
the fashion industry is about to create a serious challenge for luxury 
companies. From next March, the bloc will require them to report how many 
unsold products they’ve destroyed, and why.

That not only risks a backlash from customers and civil society, but could 
also give competitors’ commercially sensitive information about how particular 
ranges are selling.

The new rules risk a “double penalty,” Julie El Ghouzzi, a luxury goods 
consultant, said. “When you do something that could be understood as 
reprehensible, then you have a huge risk of reputation, and reputation in 
luxury means desirability.”
The Prada factory in Valvigna, Italy in 2023.Photographer: Alessia 
Pierdomenico/Bloomberg
In 2020, between 264,000 and 594,000 tons of textiles were destroyed before 
use in Europe annually, according tofigures 
<https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/the-destruction-of-returned-and-unsold-textiles-in-europes-circular-economy>
 from the European Environment Agency — equivalent to between 4% and 9% of all 
products on the market, and accounting for as much as 5.6 million tons of CO2 
equivalent, roughly the annual emissions of a million petrol cars.

“The transparency requirements could actually be more impactful than the ban 
on destroying clothes,” said Viola Wohlgemuth, a senior expert on textiles and 
the circular economy at Deutsche Umwelthilfe, a German environmental NGO.

Photo finish

Duke’s Malibu <https://www.dukesmalibu.com/> has endured one disaster after 
another. The famed restaurant perched over the Pacific narrowly survived last 
year’s Palisades Fire. Five weeks later, a mudslide forced it to close for 13 
months.

Last weekend, the powerful El Niño weather system sent damaging waves crashing 
through its windows.
Lead server Matthew Ammons watches as a wave breaks through a window at Duke’s 
in Malibu, flooding the interior.Photographer: Anna Sophia Moltke/Bloomberg
The compounding shocks are testing the enduring appeal — and extraordinary 
prices — of living at the edge of the Pacific. In Malibu, where 
multimillion-dollar homes have long commanded a premium precisely because they 
sit steps from the ocean, that proximity is increasingly becoming part of the 
risk residents are pricing in.
