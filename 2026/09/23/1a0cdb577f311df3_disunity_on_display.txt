It was jarring when US President Donald Trump appeared before the United 
Nations andthreatened to annihilate Iran 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/trump-set-to-defend-war-in-iran-in-latest-appearance-at-the-un?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>
. Even more dissonant was that hardly anyone batted an eye.

“Will a deal be made with Iran that lets them rebuild?” Trump mused from the 
green-marble rostrum, laying out his alternative scenario of obliteration.

“Do I drive them into hell with no chance of survival and no hope of future 
greatness?” he asked.

As ominous portents go, it was an all-timer.

Critics have long accused the UN of irrelevance, but those arguments feel more 
acute at this year’s session. Rarely have the world’s most powerful nations 
seemed so unwilling to seek forgiveness — to say nothing of permission — for 
their actions.
Trump after addressing the UN General Assembly yesterday.Photographer: 
Angelina Katsanis/Bloomberg
The world body has been relegated to the sidelines more than ever this year, 
helpless to stop the US-Israeli war on Iran, China’s amped-up stance in the 
South China Sea, orRussia’s war on Ukraine 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/ukrainians-are-bracing-for-worst-wartime-winter-of-airstrikes?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>
.

Iranian diplomats walked out when Trump threatened to destroy their country, 
but others in attendance didn’t seem to care.

Underscoring the disconnect between the UN chamber and real life, mediated 
talks between US and Iranian officials took place later — onesTrump described 
as productive 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/trump-says-us-team-met-with-iranians-earlier-tuesday-in-new-york?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>
.

Secretary-General António Guterres lamented the power plays on show while 
sounding the alarm 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/un-chief-calls-for-ai-pact-to-prevent-the-rise-of-killer-robots?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>
 about the next global threat: artificial intelligence.

AI titans Sam Altman and Dario Amodei are due to address the Security Council 
today to advocate slowing the breakneck AI advances they helped push forward.

Those pleas are falling on the same deaf ears that greeted Trump’s Iran 
threat. China says it has no plans to let up in the AI race. Trumpbrushed off 
the notion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/trump-rejects-push-for-international-pact-to-manage-risks-of-ai?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>
 as a “globalist scheme.”

Guterres talked of a better future, when superpowers recognize that “their 
power isn’t so super.”

The US and the world’s other strongest nations aren’t getting the message. — 
Nick Wadhams 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQdGGM5CjA8/nick-wadhams?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/machado-tries-fails-to-enter-venezuela-as-rodriguez-meets-trump?cmpid=BBD092326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9220>

for UNGA, where she met with Trump.
