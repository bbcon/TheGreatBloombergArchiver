Midterm optimists

Tomorrow’s midterm elections look set to upend the balance of power 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-01/trump-says-he-may-become-mr-veto-if-democrats-take-majority?cmpid=110226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261102&utm_content=10811>
 in the US Congress, if the polls are right. Bulls on stocks and the dollar, 
though, are betting that any market disruption is likely to be short lived.

First up, stocks: The conventional wisdom is that a big win for Democrats will 
cause a selloff. The view is that the party is likely to put roadblocks in the 
way of the AI industry that’s been driving gains for the better part of four 
years. (The S&P 500 has returned 12% this year.)

But Mike Wilson from Morgan Stanley points out that history going back to 1950 
is on the side of equity investors — six to 12 months after the midterms, the 
market is always higher.

“The track record is pretty good,” he said. “Now we may need to go down first 
and then up, but yeah, I’m pretty convinced that we’re going to have a pretty 
good at least six month period, maybe 12.”

The week ahead

It’s jobs week for the US, which means a heavy slate of data capped off with 
the monthly employment report on Friday.

Forecasters expect monthly job growth to more than double in October, to 
65,000, while the unemployment rate should remain steady at 4.2%. That would 
leave the Fed on track for additional interest-rate increases.

It’s another busy week of earnings, with Apple after the close today
