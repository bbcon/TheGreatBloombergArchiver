Falling oil prices are boosting under-fire government bonds by easing 
inflation concerns. But such a reprieve is likely to be short-lived, given the 
forces pushing central banks toward higher rates haven’t gone away.

Brent traded below 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-aug-26?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 $87 a barrel, taking its decline this week to about 8%, as Iran and Oman 
discussed an “interim framework 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/iran-oman-push-talks-for-interim-reopening-of-hormuz-strait?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
” aimed at resuming shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Sovereign bonds in 
Japan and New Zealand advanced, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s plan to 
tamp down borrowing costs by expanding bond buybacksaiding longer-dated debt 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/short-squeeze-in-us-swaps-options-shows-bessent-put-at-work?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
.

Yet the reality is that hopes have been raised and dashed repeatedly about a 
reopening of the strait. Inflationary pressures are broad-based, with potential 
US tariffslikely to 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/fed-s-barkin-says-rising-us-debt-will-lead-to-eventual-reckoning?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 exacerbate matters.

On Wednesday alone, stronger-than-expected inflation in Australia prompted 
tradersto boost bets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/australia-core-inflation-comes-in-stronger-than-expected-in-july?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 on the central bank lifting borrowing costs for a fourth time this year — 
after reducing rates in 2025. A European Central Bank board member said 
interest ratesmust rise further 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/ecb-s-schnabel-says-rates-must-rise-more-on-resilient-economy?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 as the Mideast conflict and strong euro-zone economy pose upside risks to 
inflation. Markets have all but priced in a Bank of Japanrate hike 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/groundwork-is-set-for-boj-rate-hike-next-month-ex-official-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 next month.

Investors may get clearer insight about the US’ monetary policy path on 
Friday, when Federal Reserve chair Kevin Warsh speaks at the central bank’s 
annual gathering in Jackson Hole, Wyoming. Focus on his comments will be
particularly intense 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/jackson-hole-offers-warsh-high-profile-slot-to-rebut-his-critics?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
, given his press conference after the Fed’s July policy meeting triggered the 
bond market’s worst selloff in years and spurred criticism over his 
communication style.

For now, the pressure on central banks shows little sign of easing.

Natural gas has overtaken oil to become the biggest concern for European bond 
traders as depleted supplies of the key fuelthreaten a resurgence 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/natural-gas-eclipses-oil-as-key-inflation-risk-for-european-debt?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 in inflation.

European gas prices are near five-month highs and winter contracts cost more 
than twice as much as they did a year ago. Yields on 10-year German and UK debt 
have touched levels not seen in decades. Gas plays a central role in the 
region’s economy, accounting for 21% of the EU’s energy mix, and between 25% 
and 35% for the UK.

A flood of bond issuance by tech firms has also weighed on government debt. 
SoftBank is said to be talking with investment banks about a potential $10 
billion to $20 billionbond offering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/softbank-mulls-up-to-20-billion-bond-sale-for-openai-financing?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8192>
 to help refinance a loan for its investment in US tech giant OpenAI.

The offering could be denominated in dollars and euros and could come as early 
as next month, according to people familiar with the matter. Proceeds from the 
sale would partly be used to repay a $40 billion bridge loan secured earlier 
this year for its OpenAI investment, and also for other AI investments, they 
said.

forced a rare retreat by the government.
