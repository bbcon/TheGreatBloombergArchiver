Oil and bond yields

While investors have been riveted by AI stocks and the Nvidia spy drama 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-09/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-s-affair-with-alleged-spy-raises-succession-concerns?cmpid=111026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11185>
, a couple of momentous market moves have been taking place almost below the 
radar.

Brent crude oil, which stood near $118 as recently as Oct. 30, has dropped 
below $90 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-nov-10?cmpid=111026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11185>
. That, in turn, has helped drive US Treasury bond yields lower. The 10-year 
has fallen to 4.89% from a 24-year high around 5.36% a month ago.

Today’s consumer price report 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/inflation-data-to-test-limits-of-treasury-rally-after-haven-bid?cmpid=111026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11185>
 in the US could add to the optimism: Economists expect it to show that the 
pace of inflation moderated last month. A surprisingly weak reading, coupled 
with the pullback in oil, might encourage traders to pare their predictions for 
Fed tightening.

Cooling inflation, falling oil prices and declining bond yields: That would be 
a pretty great backdrop for equities, almost irrespective of what happens with 
the AI boom.European stocks are on the verge of setting a record for the first 
time since August. (See the Word from Wall Street below for a contrary view on 
the inflation outlook.)

The declines in oil and bond yields are further proof that elections have 
consequences. The drubbing Donald Trump suffered in the midterms last week sent 
the message that voters want prices to come down, and the US and Iran are 
pursuing diplomatic efforts to end hostilities.

“The political window for escalation has closed,” Eurasia Group analysts 
including Gregory Brew and Clayton Allen said. That’s “based on evidence of 
increasing political constraints on Trump following last week’s midterm 
elections and the mounting opposition from inside the administration and the 
Republicans” to another round of all-out hostilities. —Phil Serafino 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ABoP3GGeTtg/phil-serafino?cmpid=111026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11185>

The anti-AI trade

Stock pickers in emerging markets have faced a daunting challenge the past 
couple of years — the MSCI index many of them are judged against is dominated 
by a handful of big AI stocks.

Avoid shares of Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung and SK Hynix, and you’re 
guaranteed to underperform the benchmark.

But now that those stocks are slumping, fund managers are sifting through the 
rest of the market, andthey like what they see 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/ai-slump-sends-investors-scouring-emerging-markets-for-bargains?cmpid=111026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11185>
: Stocks selling at rock-bottom valuations with high dividend yields, in 
markets that by one metric are the cheapest since the global financial crisis 
18 years ago.

Investors highlight South American utilities, Chinese and Taiwanese 
consumer-goods manufacturers, and eastern and central European financial firms 
as compelling alternatives to AI-exposed stocks.

“Just hogging three or four names and being really concentrated actually 
doesn’t work over the long term,” said Sat Duhra at Janus Henderson Investors. 
“There’s a whole bunch of other areas outside of AI that have worked really 
well, and that’s where we positioned.” —Mpho Hlakudi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWMdM8ErNWg/mpho-hlakudi?cmpid=111026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261110&utm_content=11185>
