Jet fuel from tropical fruit

By Peter Millard 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/APwKWDVQ9k8/peter-millard?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>

In Brazil’s Minas Gerais state north of Sao Paulo, more than 200 researchers 
are racing to commercialize an oil-rich fruit so that it can one day power 
jetliners.

Agronomists, biotechnicians and automation experts are the brains behind a $3 
billion project to plant the little-known macauba palm tree across as many as 
144,000 hectares (356,000 acres) of land — an area slightly larger than the 
city of Los Angeles — and then harvest it forsustainable aviation fuel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-21/airlines-cleaner-fuel-is-no-match-for-rising-emissions-as-people-fly-more?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>
.
A worker at the Agripark opens up a macauba fruit.Photographer: Maira 
Erlich/Bloomberg
Once the first trees planted start bearing fruit, likely in 2030, energy and 
biofuels companyAcelen Renováveis <https://www.acelenrenovaveis.com.br/> plans 
to start processing macauba oil at a biorefinery it’s building in another part 
of Brazil.

Acelen Renováveis, which is fully owned by Abu Dhabi-based Mubadala Capital, 
hopes to solidify Brazil’s role as one of the world’s top producers ofbiofuels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/how-controversial-biofuels-are-booming-on-energy-security-push-in-india-brazil?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>
 and a leading supplier of clean jet fuel by industrializing the exotic fruit.

The company says its biorefinery under construction in Bahia state will 
eventually produce 20,000 barrels of SAF a day. That would translate to a big 
jump in worldwide output, which in 2025 was 41,000 barrels a day. Most SAF is 
derived from used cooking oil.

Macauba (pronounced mah-kah-OO-bah) is indigenous to South America and is 
naturally adapted to the semi-arid regions where Acelen Renováveis is planting 
its trees. The individual fruit is roughly the size of a small plum and has a 
fleshy pulp, which is what will be processed. According to the company, macauba 
can deliver seven to 10 times more oil per hectare than soybeans.
Victor BarraPhotographer: Maira Erlich/Bloomberg
“We call it the fruit of the future,” Victor Barra, director of agribusiness, 
said in a greenhouse full of macauba clones and seedlings. “It’s literally a 
power plant, an energy plant.”

The trees in Minas Gerais will grow on degraded pasture so they don’t compete 
with food production. Brazil has roughly 40 million hectares of degraded 
pasture. With that potential at hand, Mubadala Capital — the alternative asset 
management arm of wealth fundMubadala Investment Co. 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1448162D:UH?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>
 — has longer-term plans for up to five biorefineries in Brazil.

Macauba farming doesn’t come without challenges. The palm trees only have one 
harvest a year, and their thorn-ridden trunks and leaves hamper collection. 
Harvesting the fruit will be more difficult to mechanize than reaping soybeans 
or sugarcane, Brazil’s two main sources of biofuels, and will rely more on 
human labor.

Acelen Renováveis has developed fruit separation and oil extraction equipment 
at the Minas Gerais Agripark research site. The company plans to replicate 
these at its macauba plantations, so it can ship oil instead of fruit and cut 
logistics costs.

North Sea alarm

By Alastair Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQvVwWuhxcQ/alastair-marsh?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>

Institutional investors are raising concerns over the prospect of UK Prime 
Minister Andy Burnham supporting more oil and gas drilling in the North Sea.

“If what you’re trying to achieve is the trifecta that most governments appear 
to be looking for in energy policy now, which is affordability, energy 
independence and decarbonization,” then increasing North Sea drilling “doesn’t 
achieve any of those,” said Laura Hillis, managing director for responsible 
investment at the £3.4 billion ($4.6 billion) Church of England Pensions Board.
An oil rig on the Cromarty Firth on the northeast coast of Scotland.
Photographer: Andy Buchanan/AFP/Getty Images
The comments come as the Burnham government signals it’s prepared to boost oil 
and gas drilling off the coast of Scotland. The prime minister, who has been in 
office for less than a month,told 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/burnham-says-won-t-ignore-north-sea-in-hint-at-more-uk-drilling?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>
 US President Donald Trump last week that the UK would take a “pragmatic” 
approach to its North Sea resources, suggesting it would be unwise to “ignore” 
the country’s untapped oil and gas potential.

Burnham’s Labour government has yet to announce a formal white paper outlining 
its complete energy policy. His appointment of Miatta Fahnbulleh to replace Ed 
Miliband as energy secretary has added to speculation that Burnham will back 
pro-net zero policies. The government hasn’t decided whether to issue new North 
Sea drilling licenses.

Spokane under threat

By Emma Haidar 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXbWgRzUKcU/emma-haidar?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>

<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AF4NRCMRVn8/brian-k-sullivan?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>

The fires surrounding Spokane, Washington, are threatening tens of thousands 
of structures and have forced the evacuation of 67,000 people, officials said 
Monday, as dry conditions fuel blazes across the Northwest.

In Spokane, a metro area of about 550,000 people near the border with Idaho, 
local officials said roughly 700 structures have burned in the past 48 hours. 
No injuries or fatalities have been reported.
A damaged truck sits in the driveway of a burned-out residential home in the

 on InciWeb, a federal fire tracking website. There are 17 total fires 
currently active across Washington, Public Lands Commissioner Dave Upthegrove 
told reporters Monday, burning more than 262,000 acres in the state.

Fires fuel cat bond sales

By Gautam Naik 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVyoWbluWhQ/gautam-naik?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>
 andAlastair Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQvVwWuhxcQ/alastair-marsh?cmpid=BBD080426_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260804&utm_content=7316>

Insurers are turning to catastrophe bonds to pass on historic levels of 
wildfire risk to the capital markets.

Issuance of cat bonds that include exposure to wildfires has already soared to 
more than $5 billion so far this year, according todata 
<https://www.artemis.bm/> published on Monday by industry specialist Artemis. 
That’s approaching the record amount sold during all of 2025, which was roughly 
double the level issued the previous year.

Though the market remains dominated by California, insurers and investors 
interviewed by Bloomberg say Europe will inevitably need to explore ways to 
transfer wildfire risks to private investors. That’s as the fastest-warming 
continent struggles with increasingly frequent and devastating blazes.
