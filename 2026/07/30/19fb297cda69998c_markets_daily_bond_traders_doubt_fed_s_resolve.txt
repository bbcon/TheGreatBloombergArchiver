Bond market backlash

Markets are delivering a harsh verdict 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/bond-rout-sends-warning-to-warsh-that-tough-talk-is-not-enough?cmpid=073026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7162>
 on Kevin Warsh’s performance yesterday.

As the Fed chairman spoke, 30-year Treasury yields spiked, inflation 
expectations rose, the dollar slid, and even stocks tumbled. Taken together, 
they point to concern that policymakers won’t manage to curtail inflation that 
has run above the Fed’s target for five straight years.

That’s causing several investors and strategists on Wall Street to call into 
question Warsh’s resolve 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/fed-s-warsh-rebuked-by-investors-craving-a-real-inflation-fight?cmpid=073026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7162>
 and credibility, especially after his hawkish comments in the run-up to 
yesterday’s meeting.

“He once again failed to specify how he intended to achieve his stridently 
asserted inflation resolve,” wrote JPMorgan’s Michael Feroli. “We believe this 
will add some urgency for the rest of the committee to act on its mandate.”

While markets largely took the decision to hold rates in stride, the backlash 
came through during thepress conference 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-07-29/warsh-says-fed-won-t-be-constrained-by-market-prices-video?cmpid=073026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7162>
 as Warsh didn’t offer a clear explanation for the move or say he would support 
raising rates should inflation fail to slow.

At stake is Warsh’s ability to lead a committee that is gradually losing 
patience with high inflation. Households and businesses could also start to 
doubt their long-standing belief that the Fed will do what it takes to contain 
price pressures.

Bondholders both pushed down yields on the most short-term Treasuries — a 
reflection of how they rapidly scaled back bets on immediate increases — and 
demanded higher payouts on longer-term bonds to compensate for inflation risks 
in the years ahead.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-07-29/apollo-s-slok-says-warsh-silence-fuels-treasury-selloff-video?cmpid=073026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7162>
The drop in two-year yields coupled with the rise in 30-year rates made for 
one of the biggest such steepenings of the yield curve after a Fed meeting 
since at least the mid-1990s.

As Apollo Global Management’s Torsten Slok put it, bond markets have decided 
to do the tightening themselves: “If you are not hiking rates, we are.” —

Credit risks building in big tech

A key part of the credit market is flashing a warning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/wall-street-picks-ai-winners-and-losers-as-credit-swaps-surge?cmpid=073026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7162>
 on the trillions of AI spending.

The price of protecting tech firms’ debt against default is jumping, 
especially for companies like Oracle and SpaceX. As credit default swap levels 
climb, bond yields have been rising too. Oracle has seen yields on notes due in 
2054 surge to 7.8%, up almost a full percentage point this year.

“The market is picking winners and losers in the AI race, and the number of 
companies on the negative side is growing,” said Matt Maley, chief market 
strategist at Miller Tabak.—Caleb Mutua 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATsVYoCVS_g/caleb-mutua?cmpid=073026_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260730&utm_content=7162>
