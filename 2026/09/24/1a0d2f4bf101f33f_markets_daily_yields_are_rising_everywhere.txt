Yields are rising everywhere

Strong growth, sticky inflation and a hawkish Fed are a near-perfect storm for 
higher Treasury yields.

Business activity is rising at the fastest pace in more than five years 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/us-business-activity-expands-at-the-fastest-pace-since-2021?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>
, S&P Global’s purchasing managers index showed yesterday. Fed Governor Michael 
Barr and the New York Fed’s John Williamschipped in 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/fed-s-williams-says-more-work-needed-to-bring-down-us-inflation?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>
 that they’re still concerned about inflation, pointing to further interest 
rate hikes.

And just in case the message was too subtle, the auction of five-year Treasury 
notes landed with a thud, with the weakest demand in years by some measures. 
The results pushedfive-year yields above 5% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/us-treasury-five-year-yields-breach-5-for-first-time-since-2007?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>
 for the first time since 2007, leaving the two- and three-year maturities the 
only coupon-bearing tenors below that level.

Turns out running an economy hot when oil is stuck near $100 a barrel will 
cause yields to rise. Another milestone: The US 30-year yield hit 5.44% this 
morning, the highest since 2004. (It’sa worldwide phenomenon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/tumbling-global-government-bonds-put-yields-on-brink-of-4?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>
: The average yield on global government debt is now within a whisker of 4%, a 
level not seen since 2007.)

The White House has enough trouble with energy into November’s midterm 
elections, but losing the Treasury market at the same time would be a much 
bigger problem. —Brendan Fagan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXYymCcs0t0/brendan-fagan?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>

Priced for perfection

Cybersecurity stocks are so hot that even bulls are questioning whether 
they’ve gone too far 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/cybersecurity-stocks-are-so-hot-investors-question-staying-power?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>
.

A basket of cybersecurity stocks has more than doubled since April on concern 
that powerful AI models could lead to a rise in cyberattacks. Crowdstrike, Palo 
Alto Networks and Fortinet have all gained more than 130%.

The stocks are now some of the most expensive in the market, meaning the 
prices may not reflect risks such as the possibility that AI development slows, 
or a reduction in sophisticated AI attacks.

“If you’re looking to get in now, you have to recognize that what you’re 
paying reflects the expectation that everything will be perfect in the future,” 
said Brad Long at Wealthspire. —Ryan Vlastelica 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AT3hUmpnSRM/ryan-vlastelica?cmpid=092426_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9236>
