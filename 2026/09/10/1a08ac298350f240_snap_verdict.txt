Serbia’s long-serving strongman president, Aleksandar Vučić, usually appears 
unfazed in the face of recurring crises at home or pressure from abroad.

But staying cool is far more difficult when European Union chief Ursula von 
der Leyen — whom he considers a friend — firesblistering criticism 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/eu-urges-serbia-to-not-give-honors-burial-to-war-criminal-mladic?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
 his way just as he faces a snap election.

The reason was a glimpse of Serbia’s past during the 1990s collapse of the 
former Yugoslavia that may furtherhinder its future progress 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/serbian-funeral-for-war-criminal-mladic-casts-doubt-over-eu-path?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
 toward joining the EU.

Von der Leyen said Serbia’s EU bid might come into question after perceived 
state support for Monday’s funeral in Belgrade of Ratko Mladić, Bosnia’s 
wartime commander who died last week while serving a life sentence for crimes 
against humanity.
People gather for Mladić’s funeral procession in Belgrade.Photographer: Srdjan 
Stevanovic/Getty Images
Vučić insisted the authorities had only provided security and logistics for 
the sendoff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/masses-attend-funeral-of-convicted-war-criminal-mladic-in-serbia?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
. The president skipped it but his son and two government ministers showed up.

It was the latest example of his trademark east-west geopolitical balancing act
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-05-15/serbia-president-vucic-looks-to-leverage-trump-family-ties?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
 that has long defied predictions it will crash to Earth.

Still, his ability to juggle the proclaimed goal of joining the bloc while 
catering to a range of Serb voters, from EU enthusiasts to hardline 
nationalists, is about to be put to a different test.

The Oct. 25 parliamentary election, officially called yesterday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/serbia-snap-elections-vote-ballot-oct-25-aleksandar-vucic-premiership?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
, will be a referendum on his policies and tendency to micromanage matters 
beyond his prerogative, which has led to accusations of authoritarian rule.
Vučić (center) arrives at the EU-Western Balkans Summit in Tivat, Montenegro, 
on June 5.Photographer: Oliver Bunic/Bloomberg
The top challenger this time around isn’t Serbia’s mainstream opposition 
parties, which have been repeatedly trounced since 2012, but anascent, 
student-led movement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/serb-president-faces-biggest-threat-to-his-rule-after-student-led-protests?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
 that has spearheaded almost two years of anti-government protests.

Polls point to a tight race.

He’s expected to resign as president and pivot to the premiership, emulating 
Vladimir Putin’s ruse to retain power in Russia almost two decades ago.

Vučić says Serbia can exit crisis mode after the vote. That’s an aspiration 
even he may prove unable to meet.— Misha Savic 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQHO_MsXKlg/misha-savic?cmpid=BBD091026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260910&utm_content=8694>
