**China: Stimulus and Capital Flows**
Beijing approved provinces to draw on billions in unspent bond quotas, adding incremental fiscal support aimed at meeting the full-year growth target. Separately, the PBOC's formal rebuttal of yuan undervaluation allegations — issued as EU-China trade negotiations continue — underscores Beijing's sensitivity to exchange-rate framing as a driver of bilateral imbalances. China's bond market is also emerging as an unexpected source of low-cost cross-border funding: regulators approved offshore use of proceeds from over 40% of panda bond issuances year-to-date, the highest share on record, as subdued domestic inflation compresses onshore borrowing costs relative to global alternatives.

**Japan and Technology**
Prime Minister Takaichi's parliamentary defense of her fiscal approach — framing competitiveness-oriented tax cuts as ultimately yen-supportive — signals that domestic political pressure on Bank of Japan normalization will remain an overhang. Meanwhile, Nvidia-backed Firmus shelved its planned Australian IPO after failing to attract sufficient global investor demand, and is now exploring a $2–3 billion private round. The failed listing highlights investor wariness toward capital-intensive AI infrastructure companies at a moment when borrowing costs are elevated globally.
