COP31’s host has a plastic dilemma

By Selcan Hacaoglu 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQ6hs25pQys/selcan-hacaoglu?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
 andJohn Ainger 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>

A couple of hundred miles along the Mediterranean coast from Antalya, where 
Turkey is hosting next month’s COP31 climate summit, sits Adanalioglu Beach. 
Along its sands, fingernail-sized pieces of shredded plastic in bright, 
artificial colors regularly wash ashore.

“I once found a small, turquoise-colored piece of plastic inside a sea bass,” 
said Ahmet Takar, 46, an amateur fisherman on the beach. “There have been fewer 
fish in recent years.”
A shepherd herds sheep past a pile of waste at a recycling facility near the 
southern Turkish city of Adana in September.Photographer: Selcan 
Hacaoglu/Bloomberg
Experts blame recycling facilities in nearby Adana, which process waste from 
the UK and Europe as well as domestic plastic, for allowing the particles to 
leak into the environment. They litter the banks of irrigation canals feeding 
the Seyhan River, which empties into the Mediterranean Sea about a dozen miles 
from the beach. Sea currents can then carry pollution along the coast.

Much of the world is struggling with a boom in discarded plastic and Turkey is 
the top recipient of plastic waste from the UK and European Union. It imports 
hundreds of thousands of tons a year, which can poison marine life, contaminate 
farmland and enter the human food chain. And the country’s waste intake is 
expected to grow.

Ahead of COP31, Turkish officials and environmental groups are pressing for 
tougher action on plastic pollution and recycling. Last year,global negotiations
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-15/global-plastic-talks-fail-as-hardline-positions-block-deal?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
 to curb plastic waste failed as some countries wanted to focus on collection 
and recycling, rather than limiting production. Now, Turkey is seeking a 
commitment to halve the growth in global waste by 2035. The total amount 
generated already sits at more than2.5 billion 
<https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/1f8e0136-c279-46e7-877c-657b7f8ab5f6/content>
 metric tons a year.

“We aim to bring zero waste and methane reduction to the center of climate 
action,” Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurumsaid 
<https://unfccc.int/news/cop31-presidency-announces-new-targets-on-global-electrification-cutting-waste-resilient-cities>
 in June.

Environmental groups in Turkey are also pushing for cuts in plastic production 
and single-use packaging, tighter waste-export rules and greater producer 
responsibility.

The EU has agreed to cut packaging waste 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/terminal/S5UYMMDWX2PS?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
, introduced tighterexport rules 
<https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-waste-shipment-regulation-and-diwass-platform-go-live-2026-05-21_en>
 in May and is due to come out with a Circular Economy Act later this year, 
aiming to boost demand for plastic recycled within the bloc. Yet it’s also been
criticized 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-17/exxon-halts-100-million-recycling-investments-due-to-eu-rules?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
 by industry, like energy giant ExxonMobil Holdings Corp., for not doing enough 
to support new chemical recycling technologies.

Insurance inflection point

By Eric Roston 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQdgGkZhizQ/eric-roston?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>

Home insurance rates have risen so dramatically over the last decade that the 
average premium now exceeds the average property-tax bill in two-thirds of US 
counties, according toan analysis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/terminal/TN5IFFKGZAIX?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
 published Monday by Bloomberg Intelligence.

It points to the role of insurance in the larger affordability crunch 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-15/healthcare-costs-health-insurance-premiums-are-key-issues-for-midterm-voters?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
 that is putting pressure on households and shaping battle lines ahead of the 
midterm elections next month. Rates are being pushed up, in part, byextreme 
weather linked to climate change 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-18/us-home-insurance-prices-set-to-keep-rising-with-severe-weather?cmpid=BBD102026_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261020&utm_content=10249>
.

US homeowner policies, averaged at the county level, have gone up by an 
inflation-adjusted 45% in a decade. But some states are seeing much higher 
jumps — such as Nebraska, with a rise of 79%. The analysts also estimated the 
portion of auto premiums related to extreme weather. When that is added to home 
insurance premiums, the combination is now more expensive than property taxes 
in 75% of the nation’s counties.

Hail and winter weather are driving rates up in the Midwest and Mountain West, 
led by Colorado, Utah and Minnesota. Southeastern states have some of the 
country’s lowest property taxes, and there, insurance premiums can now run 2.5 
to 4 times the tax levy. Northeastern and Western states and big metropolitan 
areas pay high taxes, which makes rising insurance premiums equal a smaller 
percentage of the tax bill.
