Narendra Modi’s cockroach problem 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-18/modi-has-no-easy-fix-to-end-resurgent-india-cockroach-protests?cmpid=BBD102226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10416>
 is proving difficult for India’s prime minister to eradicate.

Two of the top leaders of the so-called Cockroach Janta Party walked free 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/india-s-cockroach-leaders-released-from-police-custody?cmpid=BBD102226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10416>
 after more than a week in one of New Delhi’s most notorious prison complexes, 
having been accused of violating peace bonds and trespassing at a site where 
the youth-led street movement planned another round of massive demonstrations.

Hours later, the pair appeared in a video with fists raised behind an Indian 
flag, reiterating one of the movement’s latest demands: the resignation of 
India’s chief election commissioner, Gyanesh Kumar, over allegations that he 
oversaw theillegitimate removal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-09/india-s-voter-roll-revision-what-is-sir-and-why-is-it-controversial?cmpid=BBD102226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10416>
 of millions of eligible voters from India’s voter rolls.
A CJP protest in New Delhi on Oct. 10.Photographer: Prakash Singh/Bloomberg
The CJP hasn’t yet called for new protests 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/india-education-minister-pradhan-resigns-after-student-protests?cmpid=BBD102226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10416>
, but it seems likely that more will come. The movement’s energy hasn’t 
dissipated, despite efforts by Modi’s government to take a hard line by 
restricting demonstrations and closing down parts of the capital.

Modi isn’t likely to give in to the CJP easily. The group’s last big political 
win was the resignation in summer of the country’s education minister over a 
series of nationwide testing scandals. In that case, the impropriety was clear 
for all to see — the question was only how the government would respond.

This time, the government maintains that the voter-roll overhaul is a routine 
and legal exercise. The polling commission denies wrongdoing.

Modi’s long summer of discontent is stretching into the fall. A coalition of 
farmers’ groups, has called for nationwide protests tomorrow, while the 
opposition Congress party has permission to hold a rally in New Delhi on Nov. 
1, according to the Press Trust of India.

The CJP began as a social-media meme and evolved into a headache for Modi. 
It’s increasingly starting toact like his true opposition
