The Nvidia affair

The last thing tech-stock bulls needed was another obstacle to reviving the AI 
trade. That’s what they’re getting with Nvidia’s spy scandal.

There’s no immediate, direct impact on Nvidia’s sales and earnings. Still, the 
saga calls into question whether Jensen Huang will remain at the head of the 
company he co-founded and built into the world’s largest enterprise.

More importantly, his relentless appearances, featuring science lectures, dad 
jokes and his signature leather jackets,turned him into AI’s main cheerleader 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-08/nvidia-board-to-probe-spying-case-after-ceo-jensen-huang-admits-infidelity?cmpid=110926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261109&utm_content=11119>
.

Nvidia was already under pressure, along with the rest of the AI trade. 
Investors worry that the company is involved in circular financing deals with 
customers, that the AI boom has turned into a bubble and, as of last week, that 
the newly empowered US Democratic Party will put the brakes on further 
development.

Those concerns are affecting the whole market: Last week, hedge funds sold 
global technology stocks atone of the fastest paces on record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/hedge-funds-dump-tech-stocks-at-one-of-fastest-paces-on-record?cmpid=110926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261109&utm_content=11119>
.

But Nvidia occupies a special place by virtue of its size. At its peak about a 
month ago, the company was the world’s largest by value, at $5.8 trillion. It’s 
since fallen to $4.5 trillion, second only to Apple.

The stock is down about 4.5% in premarket trading. That means the spy scandal 
is going to take a $200 billion whack out of the company at the open.

More broadly, the episode shines a light on the Cold War-like maneuverings 
between the US and China, addinganother point of tension 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-newspaper-dismisses-jensen-huang-spy-claim-warns-on-ties?cmpid=110926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261109&utm_content=11119>
 in an already fraught relationship.

Nvidia for more than a year described the CEO’s relationship with Huang Le as 
professional in nature 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/nvidia-ceo-s-affair-admission-counters-past-statements-on-ties?cmpid=110926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261109&utm_content=11119>
 and minimized his ties with Megaspeed, where she worked. The US government had 
concerns that China could be using the Singapore-based firm to gain access to 
advanced chips. (Readthe weekend statements from Nvidia’s board and CEO 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-08/read-the-memos-from-nvidia-s-board-and-ceo-jensen-huang?cmpid=110926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261109&utm_content=11119>
 andan explainer on the spying controversy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/what-to-know-about-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-s-affair-with-suspected-chinese-spy?cmpid=110926_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261109&utm_content=11119>
