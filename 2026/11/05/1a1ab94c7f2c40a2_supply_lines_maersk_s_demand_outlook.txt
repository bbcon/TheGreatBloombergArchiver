Globalization never died despite political attempts to smother it. Lately, 
it’s more the bottlenecks at ports and other junctions on land that are 
inhibiting merchandise trade.

That’s among the messages from Maersk CEO Vincent Clerc, who spoke with 
Bloomberg TV on Thursday to explain the short- and longer-term outlooks for the 
Copenhagen-based bellwether for global commerce.

“There has been an expectation for about a decade that globalization had 
reached its peak and that we would see either deglobalization or a slowdown of 
it,” he said. “Actually what has happened on the ground pretty consistently for 
a decade is a continued increase in globalization and integration of the global 
economy.”

At the same time, Clerc said, “investment in infrastructure on the land side 
to keep up with the demands of these higher trade flows have not followed to 
the extent that was necessary.”

The container industry is “starting to see that we’re are pushing towards the 
limits of what the current infrastructure can cope with,” he said. “Hence, 
these bottlenecks will be with us on and off — and maybe more and more on than 
off in the years to come.”

Those dynamics are boosting Maersk’s business over the shorter run. Clerc said 
the world’s No. 2 container carrier saw “a lot of stability” in demand and 
freight rates through the third quarter. Bottlenecks are creating constraints 
that “absorb some capacity and put the rate environment in a very, very 
favorable light,” he added.

“This has continued in the fourth quarter,” he said. “Demand continues 
unabated, still fueled by a lot of investment in electrification and AI coming 
out of Asia and going all over the world.”

‘Another Busy Month’

Port of Long Beach CEO Noel Hacegaba shared a similarly optimistic perspective 
in an interview in London this week. The US’s second-busiest port had its best 
month on record in September and year-to-date was nearly 4% ahead of last 
year’s volumes, driven largely by uncertainty and frontloading.

“We expect another round of frontloading ahead of Jan. 9,” Hacegaba said, 
referring to the expiration date for the currentUS-China

For October, “we are looking at another 900,000 TEU mark, which for us is 
another busy month” and “we expect to be busy at least through the first month 
of the new year and possibly through the first quarter,” he said, adding that 
cargo needed to supply the AI boom is among the growth drivers.
