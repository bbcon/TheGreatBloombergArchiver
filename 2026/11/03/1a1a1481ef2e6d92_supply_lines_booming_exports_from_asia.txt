Some of Asia’s biggest export engines look to finish an already solid year for 
global merchandise trade on a upbeat note.

Vietnam’s trade surplus unexpectedly surged <> in October to more than five 
times the September level, underscoring government efforts to spur exports and 
reach double-digit economic growth this year.

In the first 10 months of this year, the US remained the biggest export market 
for Vietnam, whose trade surplus with the world’s largest economy widened by 
26% from a year earlier. With China, Vietnam imported more than it exported, 
swelling a deficit by 43% from a year earlier.

Vietnam’s latest trade data echo numbers released this week in South Korea, 
where exports maintainedstrong growth 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-01/south-korea-exports-extend-robust-growth-as-chip-boom-continues?cmpid=BBD110326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10815>
 last month and highlighted the strength of an artificial intelligence-driven 
boom.

Last week Taiwan reported its fourth straight quarter 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-30/taiwan-posts-longest-run-of-double-digit-gdp-growth-since-1987?cmpid=BBD110326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10815>
 of double-digit economic growth, the longest such stretch in nearly 39 years 
driven largely by the boom in demand for AI chips, components and other 
equipment.

Home to key suppliers of tech giants like Nvidia and Apple, Taiwan has been 
one of the biggest beneficiaries of the spread of AI. The island’s exports 
surged almost 61% to a record of over $87 billion in September, beating the 
forecasts of all economists surveyed by Bloomberg.

‘Force Multiplier’

Given how intertwined Taiwan’s exports have become with the US economy, the 
island’s officials argue it’s an increasingly crucial partner in Washington’s 
campaign to beat China in the race for AI dominance.

“Taiwan is a force multiplier for the United States in artificial 
intelligence: it has the capability, reliability, and values necessary to 
support the United States’ technological ambitions,” Taiwan Foreign Minister 
Lin Chia-lung writes inan essay 
<https://www.foreignaffairs.com/taiwan/americas-ai-ambitions-depend-taiwan> for 
Foreign Affairs magazine published online Tuesday. “If Washington wants to 
compete effectively in AI, Taiwan is indispensable.”

Fresh data from Thailand showed exports remained exceptionally strong, rising 
24% in September. Thailand’s Port Authority is exploring afurther expansion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/thailand-eyes-expanding-its-largest-port-to-meet-trade-boom?cmpid=BBD110326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10815>
 of Laem Chabang port, the nation’s biggest deep-sea gateway, to handle 
increasing trade of products from automobiles to electronics and petrochemicals.

With such resilient international commerce fueling growth in Asian nations 
other than China, container shipping lines are following the money.

“Cosco, Maersk, Ocean Network Express, and other major transpacific container 
liners are reallocating capacity to intra-Asia trade lanes as US-China trade 
tensions and tariffs continue to reshape global supply chains,” according to a
research note <> Tuesday from Bloomberg Intelligence. “High-frequency container 
shipment tracking and capacity utilization metrics indicate China’s exports to 
Southeast Asia are poised for solid growth beyond 2026.”

According to an Oct. 23 reading 
<https://www.cpb.nl/en/world-trade-monitor/cpb-world-trade-monitor-august-2026> 
from CPB Netherlands Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis, an index of global 
merchandise trade volumes reached a record high in August — led by emerging 
Asian economies.

Tracking Prices

As Bloomberg Economics’ Chief US Economist Anna Wong describes it, a project 
that started 18 months ago to track how President Donald Trump’s “liberation 
day” tariffs were feeding through to inflation “has since grown into something 
much bigger,”she writes on X 
<https://x.com/AnnaEconomist/status/2117469697784401972>. Wong and her 
colleagues are introducing The Bloomberg Price Project, an attempt to build the 
US Consumer Price Index from the ground up using alternative data. Explore its 
findings in today’sBig Take 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-inflation-price-index/?cmpid=BBD110326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10815>
, with our paywall coming down briefly for those without aBloomberg.com 
<https://t.co/XYp4l1nBLn> subscription.

Factory buzz 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/us-factory-activity-picks-up-production-grows-most-since-2021?cmpid=BBD110326_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261103&utm_content=10815>
 | US manufacturing activity picked up in October, supported by the strongest 
production growth in nearly five years. The Institute for Supply Management’s 
manufacturing index rose one point to 55.5 in October. (Figures above 50 
indicate expansion.) The production gauge notched its best reading since late 
2021, the data showed. A measure of new orders was the highest since July and 
remained well within expansionary territory.
