Politics has always been about telling voters what they want to hear. 
Artificial intelligence can now make sure they hear it from someone who looks 
just like them.

In Brazil — one of the world’s most connected nations — AI-generated personas 
are taking on theguise of influencers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/brazil-presidential-election-ai-influencers-extend-reach-into-politics?cmpid=BBD082726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8246>
 in a combination that’s proving powerful and difficult to regulate.
A screenshot of the AI-generated Brazilian avatar Dona Maria.Source: Daniel 
Santos/Instagram
Latin America’s largest economy, which was at the forefront of the debate on 
the use ofsocial media in politics 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/brazil-slaps-tiktok-owner-with-30-million-fine-over-data-use?cmpid=BBD082726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8246>
, offers an early glimpse of what that mix could mean for elections.

It’s a phenomenon on show in the run-up to October’s presidential ballot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-20/brazil-election-lula-seen-as-frontrunner-over-flavio-bolsonaro?cmpid=BBD082726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260827&utm_content=8246>
 between leftist incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and his Trump-supporting 
rival, Flávio Bolsonaro.

AI-generated personalities are weighing in on grocery prices, crime and Lula’s 
government, adopting the language, appearance and everyday concerns of the 
audiences they seek to reach.

So far, there’s no evidence these AI influencers are collectively backing one 
side or formally working for campaigns. Some of the most visible, however, lean 
right.

They’re already attracting political scrutiny, with Lula’s party challenging 
AI-generated content and the electoral court tightening rules requiring 
disclosure of synthetic political material.

The appeal of AI personalities lies in the same qualities that made 
influencers powerful in the first place: familiarity, repetition and the sense 
that the “person” understands your life.

An AI grandmother griping about supermarket prices lands differently than a 
politician making the same point from a podium.

That grandmother can also post around the clock, be replicated across accounts 
and cost little to reproduce, while easily shifting persona to appeal to a 
different slice of the electorate.

Brazil seems especially receptive given how online — and polarized — its 
voters are.
