Scott Bessent is bringing a hedge fund playbook to the Treasury, and Japan is 
the latest test case. The former Soros trader is taking an unusually hands-on 
role in theUS effort to prop up the yen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/treasury-secretary-scott-bessent-draws-on-hedge-fund-tactics-to-help-rescue-yen?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7371>
, from deliberatelyflashing a notepad 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/bessent-and-the-fed-help-japan-reverse-months-of-yen-losses?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7371>
 hinting at a $10 billion yen purchase to tapping tools rarely associated with 
Treasury currency policy. The intervention, coordinated with Tokyo, marks the 
first joint US-Japan effort to support the yen in nearly three decades, 
illustrating just how seriously Washington views the currency’s slide.

The bigger story is what it says about Bessent’s style. Rather than acting 
like a traditional Treasury secretary, he’s leaning into the instincts that 
made him one of Wall Street’s best-known macro investors: moving markets with 
signals, deploying the Treasury’s toolkit creatively, and framing currency 
policy as geopolitical strategy. With the yen still under pressure, Bessent is 
signalingmore action 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/bessent-says-yen-level-problematic-for-japan-other-currencies?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7371>
 could be ahead, in what could become the clearest example yet of how the Trump 
administration is reshaping US economic statecraft.
