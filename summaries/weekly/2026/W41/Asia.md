**India: Monetary Policy and External Pressures**
The RBI's decision to hike reflects broadening domestic inflation — amplified by record diesel prices and a weak monsoon that forced Maharashtra to cut water supplies by at least 10% — but the external constraint is equally consequential. With US 10-year Treasuries yielding 5.3% against approximately 7.2% on equivalent Indian paper, the India-US spread has compressed to near multi-decade lows, reducing the risk premium investors receive for rupee-denominated assets. The rupee approached a record low near 96.97 per dollar, with RBI intervention drawing down reserves by $51 billion in the four weeks through October 2 — even as total reserves stand at approximately $786 billion following a diaspora deposit mobilization. The episode is a textbook expression of the impossible trinity: the RBI's room to diverge from global monetary conditions is visibly narrowing.

**China: AI Capital Markets and Stimulus**
China's AI funding cycle accelerated this week: DeepSeek closed in on a $12 billion round anchored by Tencent and CATL, and Moonshot AI reached a $50 billion private valuation ahead of a planned Q1 2027 Hong Kong IPO — together signaling a concentrated IPO pipeline that will test market absorption capacity. Beijing's pre-Golden Week stimulus package drew skeptical assessments from major banks. JPMorgan characterized it as "state-guided and supply heavy"; Morgan Stanley warned it could only "backstop the growth floor." Property sales remain down over 13% year-to-date through August, and the package contained minimal consumption support. Separately, the PBOC formally rejected yuan undervaluation claims as EU-China trade talks opened in Beijing — underscoring Beijing's sensitivity to exchange-rate framing as bilateral tensions persist.

**Japan and Technology Earnings**
Bank of Japan Governor Ueda offered no signal of a back-to-back October hike early in the week, and Prime Minister Takaichi's parliamentary defense of planned tax cuts — framed as ultimately yen-supportive — reinforces the view that domestic political pressure on BOJ normalization will remain an overhang. On the corporate side, Samsung's near-ninefold profit surge still disappointed sky-high expectations, illustrating the execution risk embedded in AI-driven valuations. TSMC's 51% revenue gain offered a more constructive read on infrastructure demand durability. Singapore's Straits Times Index tumbled 3.5% Thursday — its worst session since April 2025 — after JPMorgan warned that surging long-bond yields would hurt Southeast Asian bank earnings, demonstrating how the global rates shock is propagating into regional financial systems.
