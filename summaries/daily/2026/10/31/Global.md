**Biodiversity Finance**
The conclusion of COP17 in Yerevan produced a renewed political commitment to accelerate biodiversity targets, but the gap between pledge and implementation remains wide. A UN global review released at the summit's outset found concrete action insufficient against the goals of the 2022 Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, which was signed by nearly 200 nations. Institutional investors present at the summit identified the absence of clear policy signals and investable opportunities as the binding constraint on private capital mobilization — a structural problem that political declarations alone do not resolve. The Church of England Pensions Board, managing £3.5 billion in assets, framed nature loss explicitly as a long-term portfolio risk, a framing increasingly common among large asset owners but not yet reflected in mainstream allocation practices. Cautious optimism among observers appears warranted given the gap between stated ambition and the financing architecture required to deliver it.
