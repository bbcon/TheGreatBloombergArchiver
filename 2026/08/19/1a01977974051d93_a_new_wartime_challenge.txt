The late-night call by Ukraine’s former defense minister for wartime elections 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/zelenskyy-s-popular-ex-minister-calls-for-ukrainian-election?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 represents a serious challenge to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the man who 
fired him.

“We have a systemic crisis of governance,” Mykhailo Fedorov says in a video 
address on YouTube. “Ukraine can simultaneously wage war and remain a 
democracy.”

Fedorov is widely popular as a key architect of Ukraine’s innovative drone 
warfare against Russia. Thousands protested in Kyiv and other cities when 
Zelenskyyousted him 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/ukraine-tech-savvy-defense-minister-fedorov-leaves-in-reshuffle?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 in July after only six months in the post, during which he’d overseensweeping 
changes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/ukraine-s-former-defense-minister-links-his-dismissal-to-procurement-reforms?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 to boost efficiency.

Zelenskyy sought to appease the protesters by offering another top job 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/protests-flare-in-kyiv-over-ouster-of-popular-defense-minister?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 to Fedorov, who insisted he’donly return 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/ukraine-s-fedorov-snubs-zelenskyy-job-offers-seeking-comeback?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 as defense minister. The president has nominated a high-ranking military 
officer to parliament for that role.
People react to damage from a Russian missile strike in Lviv, Ukraine, on July 
30.Photographer: Yuriy Dyachyshyn/Getty Images
The timing of Fedorov’s statement is unlikely to have been a coincidence. Like 
Zelenskyy, the parliament last faced elections in 2019.

Either way, his move shows that he’s unafraid to take on his former boss and 
strike at perhaps his chief domestic weakness.

US President Donald Trump also pushed for 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-13/trump-pushes-a-fading-zelenskiy-toward-a-risky-ukraine-election?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 a ballot last yearamid tensions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-19/trump-calls-zelenskiy-a-dictator-broadens-attacks-on-ukraine?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 in relations with Zelenskyy. He may now see an opportunity to renew that call.

Ukraine’s constitution forbids elections under martial law, which was 
introduced when Russia began the February 2022 full-scale invasion. Fedorov 
acknowledged there’d be huge obstacles to enabling frontline troops to vote as 
well as millions of Ukrainians displaced inside and outside the country.

It’s hard to imagine campaigning under constant Russian air attacks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-19/zelenskyy-says-one-dead-in-russian-missile-barrage-on-capital?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
. And President Vladimir Putin would seize on the chance to stirsocial divisions
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-28/russia-delights-in-the-dressing-down-of-zelenskiy-in-oval-office?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
 and election chaos.

Social-media responses illustrated the challenges. Ukrainians were split 
between those applauding Fedorov and others who accused him of benefiting Putin 
during a critical period in the war.

Ukraine marks 35 years of its independence from the Soviet Union on Monday. 
Fedorov is the same age as his country.

The question is whether he’s positioning himself as the person best placed to 
lead it. — Anthony Halpin 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASAcLGhGNAk/anthony-halpin?cmpid=BBD081926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260819&utm_content=7891>
