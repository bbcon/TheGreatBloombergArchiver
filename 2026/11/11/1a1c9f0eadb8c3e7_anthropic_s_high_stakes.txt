The excitement around Anthropic’s initial public offering only seems to be 
gaining traction. Bankers are weighing plans to increase the company’s 
valuation in its listing to$1.5 trillion or more 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/anthropic-targets-64-4-billion-ipo-nearing-spacex-s-record?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11272>
, the Financial Times reported, a sign of robust demand from investors.

Bankers indicated that they expect to raise the share price above the $70 to 
$78 range announced in a filing on Tuesday, the newspaper said, citing people 
it didn’t identify. The IPO was fully covered with orders from prospective 
investors for all the available shares when the deal launched.

While the news helped boost sentiment over the AI trade — with stocks in Asia 
up for athird straight day 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11272>
 — some skeptics remain. Jack Selby, who runs the venture capital firm Copper 
Sky, warned that the IPO risks causing a “pretty massive 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/anthropic-ipo-lockup-risks-triggering-ai-correction-selby-says?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261111&utm_content=11272>
” market correction. If it falls very short of its projected valuation, “just 
imagine where it’s going to go after the lockups expire,” he said in an 
interview with Bloomberg Television.

The IPO is set to price next Wednesday and start trading the following day. 
With so much enthusiasm riding on it, the stakes are high for the first 
frontier lab to enter public markets.

Korea Fines Bond Dealers Record $1.2 Billion Over Collusion

South Korea’s antitrust regulator imposed a record $1.2 billion fine on more 
than a dozen domestic banks and brokerages for colluding in sovereign bond 
auctions, exposing a yearslong practice that the watchdog said undermined 
competition and raised the government’s borrowing costs. The investigation 
found dealers coordinated so-called “goalpost yields” — bids designed to help 
meet their underwriting obligations while avoiding actually buying the bonds.
