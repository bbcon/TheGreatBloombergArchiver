Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Mia Gindis discusses the oil market outlook, with supply projections 
coming full-circle following a dramatic year.

In a year when the Iran war created the biggest energy supply disruption in 
history, the word “glut” — whichdominated oil traders’ discussions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-13/iea-boosts-forecast-of-record-global-oil-glut-for-a-sixth-month?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
 a year ago — almost disappeared from market chatter.

But over the past few weeks, it has come back.

The International Energy Agency set the tone earlier this month, projecting a 
2 million-barrel-a-day surplus 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/global-oil-market-swings-to-surplus-on-gulf-exports-iea-says?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
 for this quarter, the first since the conflict began.

Goldman Sachs Group Inc., JPMorgan Chase & Co. and other Wall Street firms 
followed suit 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/goldman-sachs-predicts-sizeable-surplus-for-oil-market-in-2027?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
. Bernstein analysts now see an oversupply of as much as 2.5 million barrels a 
day next year.

All this talk of excess oil attests to the remarkable resilience of the 
industry.

The near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz had been widely regarded as a 
worst-case scenario 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-01/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-march-2?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
 for the global economy, and initially the war triggered market chaos and 
soaring energy prices.

But almost nine months later — and with attacks on shipping continuing — 
Persian Gulf producers are nonetheless exporting crudenear prewar levels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/gulf-arab-oil-giants-are-exporting-crude-at-pre-war-rates-again?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
, not just via tankers but increasinglyby pipeline 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/uae-mulls-third-crude-oil-pipeline-to-avoid-strait-of-hormuz?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
, too.
Vessels transit the Strait of Hormuz on Sept. 5, 2026.Photographer: ATTA 
KENARE/AFP via Getty Images
In the US, a sweeping Democratic victory at the midterm polls has raised 
expectations of aquicker end to the war 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/trump-plays-down-prospect-of-strikes-on-iran-after-midterm-rout?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
, and President Donald Trump has recently signaled a reluctance to escalate 
hostilities.

Production from the country’s shale fields, as well as from Canada and Latin 
America, has also grown this year to help fill the supply gap.

Meanwhile, global oil demand has taken a hit because of the war-driven price 
spike and is set to shrink more than 2% this year, according to the IEA.

All those factors have pushed benchmark crude futures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-nov-24?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
 down by more than a fifth this month.

Of course, there’s no guarantee that Trump’s recent restraint over Iran will 
last. And while flows have increased, inventories remainseverely depleted 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/global-oil-supply-buffer-running-scarily-thin-aramco-ceo-says?cmpid=BBBXT112426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261124&utm_content=11732>
, meaning the system will be less forgiving if there’s another supply shock.

Yet with the unwinding of the war risk premium as Middle East exports recover, 
traders and analysts are focused on a supply-demand outlook that points to a 
markedly looser market.

—Mia Gindis, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

The global ethanol industry has long been dominated by the US and Brazil, but 
India is now making inroads. After eight years of rapid expansion, the country 
made 9% of the world’s ethanol in 2025, positioning it as the third-largest 
producer, according toBloombergNEF <https://about.bnef.com/>. India even 
reached its target for a 20% ethanol blend in road fuel five years ahead of the 
2030 deadline.
