Unofficial US leadership

By Jennifer A Dlouhy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASlQFjfALa0/jennifer-a-dlouhy?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>

Leaders of cities and states representing more than 60% of the US economy on 
Thursday pledged to collaborate with United Nations officials in tracking the 
country’s greenhouse gas emissions, counteracting President Donald Trump’s 
retreat from the process.

The commitment was outlined as US governors and mayors, including California 
Governor Gavin Newsom, arrived at the COP31 climate negotiations in Antalya, 
Turkey. It was a show of force designed to illustrate the work that cities and 
states are still doing to decarbonize, following the Trump administration’s 
rollback of emission-limiting regulations and withdrawal from the Paris 
Agreement.

US Senator Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island, Minnesota Governor Tim Walz and 
New Mexico Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham are among the other US officials 
participating in the climate summit Thursday, along with a number of mayors. A 
coalition of some 19 US House representatives also are set to participate later 
this week. There’s no official US delegation at COP31.

Thursday’s pledge came in a joint statement from America Is All In, the US 
Climate Alliance and Climate Mayors initiatives. Collectively, they represent 
24 governors with constituents making up 55% of the US population as well as 
350 mayors across 47 states.
Gavin Newsom, governor of California, speaks at an event hosted by the 
unofficial US delegation of Democrats at COP31.Photographer: Kerem 
Uzel/Bloomberg
The announcement came on the fourth day of the two-week COP31 summit meant to 
galvanize a worldwide shift away from fossil fuels, an embrace of more 
emission-free renewable alternatives and additional conservation efforts. The 
alliances of state and local leaders committed to working with the United 
Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, the treaty and institution 
undergirding the 2015 Paris accord, without support from the US federal 
government. Under a formal process kicked off this year, the US is set to 
officially exit the UNFCCC next February.

The new effort by cities, states and other leaders will involve elevating data 
on US progress toward decarbonization by midcentury and ensuring engagement in 
a “global stocktake” exercise meant to assess how the world is doing in meeting 
its climate goals. A first global stocktake culminated in 2023 with a 
recognition the world was badly off track its key temperature target, prompting 
a pledge at COP29 in Dubai to transition away from fossil fuels. Initial 
discussions are underway in Turkey on the second stocktake set to conclude in 
2028.

Britain is back

By John Ainger 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AOj_Ky2g/john-ainger?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham promised to show renewed leadership on tackling 
global warming as volatile fuel prices and concerns over energy security test 
international resolve over climate action.

“Britain is back and ready to play its full part,” Burnham said Thursday at 
the COP31 leaders’ summit. “The message I carry is this: we’re committed to 
meeting our 2050 net zero target.”

Burnham’s remarks come after a series of UK policy decisions put into question 
the premier’s resolve on reducing emissions. Ministers are consideringwatering 
down 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/uk-starts-process-to-water-down-ev-mandate-in-boon-for-carmakers?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
 a zero-emission vehicle sales mandate, and in the budget last month, 
Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healeycut £1 billion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/healey-to-scrap-uk-s-standing-charges-on-gas-bills?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
 ($1.3 billion) from programs aimed at making homes cleaner and cheaper to heat.

His government’s support for fossil fuel extraction in the North Sea has also 
drawn criticism from environmentalists. After the UK approved the Jackdaw gas 
field in the North Sea, climate campaigners are urging his administration to 
block the even larger Rosebank oil project.

Windfall tax

By Laura Millan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASzdyi2b6bE/laura-millan?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
 andJennifer Zabasajja 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATajhYi1xIs/jennifer-zabasajja?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>

Energy sector profits that swelled alongside global conflicts are “morally 
unjustifiable” and should face additional taxes to fund climate measures, 
according to United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres.

“There’s something fundamentally wrong,” Guterres said Thursday in an 
interview with Bloomberg Television at the annual UN climate summit in Antalya, 
Turkey. “They are making these profits with an activity that is causing severe 
damage to the global environment and severe damage in the acceleration of 
climate change.”
 <https://youtu.be/yxIjIQ8GKz0?si=8O4DWmEXYR8l90sB>
Guterres has held discussions with governments about an additional levy on oil 
and gas companies, and on Wednesday told global leaders at theCOP31 talks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/latest/cop-climate-summit?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
 that a group of nine major producers had reported collective profits of $95 
billion in the last quarter — many multiples higher than funding available to 
respond to climate disasters.

“Those taxes should be used to alleviate the situation of those that are 
suffering,” Guterres said in the interview. Nations have existing mechanisms 
“able to tax windfall profits – it’s a political decision, it’s not a technical 
problem,” he said.

Permitting bill division

By Julia Press 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWhYcuCCgEw/julia-press?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
,Akshat Rathi 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOobfNzkQ/akshat-rathi?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
 andAaron Rutkoff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARM9-qdW8iw/aaron-rutkoff?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>

The permitting reform bill that has advanced in this US Senate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/permitting-bill-before-us-senate-would-unlock-clean-tech?cmpid=BBD111226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261112&utm_content=11334>
 with bipartisan support has divided environmentalists and clean-energy 
supporters. It has also split two of the leading American climate hawks: former 
Vice President Al Gore and former Secretary of State John Kerry.

Gore urged lawmakers to stop the legislation. “We do need to speed things up 
in these approval processes,” Gore said Wednesday in an interview onstage at 
Bloomberg Green’s event on the sidelines of COP31. “But I think this gives too 
many tools to a president who has proven that he will abuse whatever tools he’s 
given.”

Kerry, who negotiated the Paris Agreement and served as a US climate envoy, 
offered qualified support for the permitting reforms. “We have 2,600 gigawatts 
of electricity waiting to be released to the marketplace,” he said at the 
Bloomberg Green event. “Politics gets in the way.”

The current bill, called the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, 
or BAAJA, is intended to expedite approval processes for all sorts of energy 
projects. That means making it easier for new power projects to connect to the 
grid, facilitating the expansion of wind and solar power. It also restricts the 
government’s ability to discriminate against power projects based on energy 
type.
Al Gore speaking at a Bloomberg Green event at the COP31 conference on Nov. 11.
Photographer: Jason Alden/Bloomberg
That could also ease construction of fossil-fuel infrastructure and data 
centers. Expressing his support for the legislation, President Donald Trumptold 
reporters 
<https://thehill.com/policy/energy-environment/6209267-trump-wind-turbines-permitting-reform-bill/>
 Monday, “I will not approve wind turbines because wind turbines are a major 
loser.”

Gore pointed to Trump’s hostility to clean energy: “I just think that it is 
unwise in the extreme to give him the power to fast-track all of these new 
pipelines they’re trying to get approved.”

Kerry stated that he’s “dramatically in favor of permitting reform” and would 
support making a deal now if certain environmental protections were in place 
after a flurry of amendments. “Get it done. That’s what’s really important at 
this point in time.”

With the Democrats’ midterm sweep of the House and Senate, Gore said there 
could be more ambitious legislation next year. “The balance of power in 
Washington will be very different come January,” he said.
