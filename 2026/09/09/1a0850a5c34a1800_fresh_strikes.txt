Deep Dive: Sugar Mills Battle for Survival
Sugar-cane fields in Maphumulo, KwaZulu Natal.Photographer: CEBISILE 
MBONANI/Bloomberg
More than a million South Africans depend on sugar, a more than century-old 
industry that’s nowstruggling to compete 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-09/south-african-sugar-industry-in-crisis-as-imports-surge-ethanol-plans-stalled?cmpid=090926_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8683>
 with imports from global giants like Brazil and low labor costs in neighboring 
Eswatini. It is also dealing with the aftermath of the country’s bungled 
industrial policy that’s failed to create local demand after two decades of 
indecision.

 * The sugar industry is of limited economic importance to the country, 
generating about $1.5 billion annually, but it sustains more than a million 
people and provides 270,000 direct and indirect jobs in a country where one in 
three is unemployed.
 * Those pressures are now coming to a head. Tongaat Hulett, which accounts 
for more than 40% of South Africa’s refined sugar production, narrowly avoided 
liquidation after winning a state-led lifeline in June.
 * While welcoming the intervention, the cane growers that supply Tongaat’s 
mills see the measure as a band-aid that doesn’t address the challenges faced 
by the industry.
 * “What’s the point in spending so much money to keep mills open while 
failing to resolve the matter of cheap imports?” says Kiki Mzoneli, a 
fifth-generation sugar farmer.
