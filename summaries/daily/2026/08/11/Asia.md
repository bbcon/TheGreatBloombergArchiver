• Australia's central bank held its cash rate unchanged at 4.35% for a second straight meeting, wagering that rising unemployment and a weaker property market will cool inflation; Governor Michele Bullock still called further tightening "quite possible" after a roughly five-year miss on the RBA's inflation target.
• South Korea's Bank of Korea deputy governor signaled more rate hikes are possible.
• Asia-Pacific government bonds fell in sympathy with US Treasuries as Monday's oil rally revived inflation concerns ahead of US CPI data; China's CSI 300 fell 0.8%.
• Indonesia nominated Destry Damayanti as sole candidate for Bank Indonesia governor, seen as balancing growth ambitions against central bank autonomy.

**Monetary Policy Diverges on Inflation Risk**
Australia's hold came with a hawkish cast, Bullock warning further tightening remains possible despite the RBA's prolonged miss on its inflation target. South Korea's central bank struck a similar note. The region's bond selloff reflects the same oil-driven inflation anxiety weighing on US markets ahead of Wednesday's CPI.

**Currencies and Corporate Pressures**
The yen extended its slide toward 160 per dollar, a week after a coordinated Bank of Japan-US Treasury intervention that now appears only temporary; Bessent's "whatever it takes" pledge is constrained by an Exchange Stabilization Fund holding under $220 billion, versus the roughly $53 billion Japan spent alone on July 30. German automakers' China struggles underscored regional pressure: Mercedes sold just 1,153 China units in H1 versus Xiaomi's 80,000-plus SU7 deliveries. Singapore upgraded its 2026 growth forecast to as high as 5.5% on AI-driven trade.
