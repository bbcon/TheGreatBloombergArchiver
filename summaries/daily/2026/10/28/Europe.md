**Fiscal Policy**
Chancellor Healey's inaugural budget was calibrated explicitly to avoid the adverse market reactions that destabilized predecessor administrations. The government has limited financial firepower — business rates reform and reversal of payroll tax increases are effectively off the table — constraining the scale of high-street regeneration measures that Prime Minister Burnham has made a political priority. The restrained approach appears designed to preserve fiscal credibility while delivering tangible near-term household relief, though analysts will assess whether the combination is sufficient to maintain the administration's early poll momentum.

**Corporate Earnings**
European bank results were broadly constructive. UBS's wealth inflows of $55.1 billion signal continued resilience in private client demand even as CEO Ermotti escalated rhetoric around Swiss capital requirement proposals, issuing an unusually explicit relocation warning. Deutsche Bank's fixed-income trading met estimates. The contrast with GSK and Adyen — where earnings disappointments triggered severe single-day drawdowns — illustrates the bifurcated environment facing European equities, with a weak euro providing partial support for export-oriented sectors.
