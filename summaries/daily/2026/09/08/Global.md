**Oil, Hormuz, and Energy Markets**
The Hormuz situation remains the central node of global macro uncertainty. The partial resumption of Qatari LNG flows provides modest relief, but European gas storage is tracking 67% full heading into winter — 14 percentage points below last year — with BNEF's base case assuming Hormuz reopens in early November. If that timeline slips, European inventories could fall to 21% by winter's end. Saudi facility attacks attributed to Houthi forces represent a further escalation, with meaningful tail risk to supply. Vitol's CEO noted that 9 of the 10 million daily barrels transiting Hormuz are crude, underscoring the chokepoint's systemic importance.

**Global Bond Markets and Policy Divergence**
The simultaneous rise of Treasury, Bund, gilt, and JGB yields reflects a structural repricing of the post-GFC era's suppressed term premiums, amplified by large fiscal deficits across major economies. Unusually, emerging market currencies have strengthened for 11 consecutive weeks — the longest streak since 2007 — even as the dollar fails to benefit from widening rate differentials in the normal fashion. High real rates, stronger EM fiscal positions, and commodity windfalls appear to be driving idiosyncratic resilience. Canada's escalation of tariffs on US goods to 15–50% on hundreds of products introduces a fresh vector of trade fragmentation, while the approaching Xi-Trump summit carries significant implications for whether the existing tariff truce extends or fractures.
