Financial toll

By Olivia Rudgard 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWBnLoYN_tg/olivia-rudgard?cmpid=BBD110226_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261102&utm_content=10875>

Extreme weather is having a measurable impact on Britain’s public finances as 
areas including railways, prisons and healthcare are increasingly affected, the 
UK’s National Audit Office said.

The full scale of the damage remains difficult to gauge, however, as many 
government departments are failing to provide the disclosures needed for a 
proper assessment, the NAO said in a report published on Monday.

“Parliament needs clear information on the resilience of infrastructure, 
service provision and finances to climate change, and on how these risks are 
being managed,” said Gareth Davies, head of the NAO, in a statement. “We expect 
this reporting to become more useful over time.”

This is the first year in which larger UK government departments have been 
required to publish the full details of the risks they face due to global 
warming. In many cases, efforts to understand such exposures remain at an early 
stage, the NAO said. Disclosures are necessary to help lawmakers keep track of 
how prepared the UK’s public services are, it said.

Britons are facing increasingly severe — and in some cases deadly — periods of 
extreme heat followed by flash floods. Intense heat has already resulted in 
damage to roads and railways, and left schools and hospitals dangerously hot 
this summer. At the same time, floods are becoming more frequent and more 
destructive to key infrastructure such as railways.
A flooded house in the hamlet of Weycroft, England, after Storm Chandra in 
January 2026.Photographer: Finnbarr Webster/Getty Images via Getty Images Europe
Flooding was the most common hazard identified in the disclosures monitored by 
the NAO. Some 89% of prisons face a high risk of flooding, while sites managed 
by the National Health Service saw 391 flooding incidents in 2024-25. Among the 
many impacts of overheating is the tendency toward more violence in prisons, 
the NAO report found.
