Deep Dive: Deep Divisions in Ceuta
Migrants queue at the temporary reception center in Ceuta on August 24.
Photographer: Marcos Moreno/Europa Press/Getty Images
Julián Domínguez, the 65-year-old head of preventive medicine at Ceuta’s only 
hospital, is used to seeing people searching for a better lifecrossing into the 
Spanish territory 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/mass-border-breach-exposes-deep-divisions-in-spain-s-african-exclave-ceuta?cmpid=082626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8186>
 on the northern tip of North Africa.

 * But when he rushed to El Tarajal beach on July 30, after reports that 
thousands of people were arriving, he wasn’t prepared for what he found. 
Domínguez watched as Spain’s Civil Guard pulled 15 lifeless bodies from the 
choppy waters, and helped care for those who emerged from them alive.
 * By the end of the day, some 80,000 people had breached the border. “There 
is no precedent, either in scale or speed,” said Domínguez. “At times, on the 
beach, we were counting between 60 and 100 arrivals a minute.”
 * Images broadcast around the world were seized on by far-right parties and 
fueled debate over Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’srelatively permissive
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/spain-set-to-hand-papers-to-over-1-million-undocumented-migrants?cmpid=082626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260826&utm_content=8186>
 policies on migration. As politicians trade barbs in cities far away, Ceuta is 
still grappling with the real-life consequences of that day.
 * Most people returned quickly, but others remain: estimates vary from around 
5,000, according to the interior ministry — which says that among them are 
genuine asylum seekers — to as many as 10,000, according to local officials.
