**Energy Supply**
Europe's gas supply outlook into winter is the region's most pressing near-term macro risk. Storage at 73% of capacity — well below the 90% seasonal norm — leaves limited buffer against a cold weather scenario. The Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis estimates that covering the shortfall via LNG imports would cost €3 billion at current prices, themselves elevated by over 100% relative to pre-Iran-conflict levels. Alternatively, demand would need to fall approximately 7% year-on-year to protect inventories. An EU ban on residual Russian gas flows takes effect early next year, further constraining optionality. October's mild weather provides a temporary reprieve, but ENTSOG warns that an unusually cold season could reduce storage to just 11% of capacity by end-March — a level that would substantially complicate summer refilling.

The ECB's implicit ally in Brussels — Dombrovskis's push for fiscal discipline — faces political resistance from Rome and Athens, reflecting an unresolved tension between the bloc's fiscal framework and member states' growth ambitions. In the UK, Labour held Keir Starmer's former London constituency in a by-election, defeating the Greens, providing a modest political stabilizer for the government.
