**Oil Markets and Geopolitics**
Crude's $25 decline from last Friday's near-$120 high represents a sharp but incomplete reversal of the Iran-war risk premium. Middle East producers — Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Iraq, the UAE — have maintained combined exports near prewar levels, with flows through the Strait of Hormuz and bypass routes holding relatively stable. Executives at a major UAE energy conference this week described supply as robust, with Persian Gulf export volumes surging even as demand in China remains well below prewar levels. The critical uncertainty is Trump's next move: lacking a clear diplomatic off-ramp from the Iran conflict, and with his domestic political standing materially weakened, the range of foreign policy outcomes has widened. Traders who priced in escalation last week were wrong; those pricing in durable peace today may be equally mistaken.

**AI Infrastructure and Global Capital Allocation**
The midterm results have simultaneously triggered an equity rout and introduced a structural question about the pace of AI capital deployment globally. Goldman Sachs estimates place 45% of planned US data center capacity in states with newly elected governors opposed to current construction timelines. The IEA separately reported that global renewable power capacity additions are set to fall 11% in 2026 — the first decline in over two decades — driven by a slowdown in Chinese solar installations, before recovering in 2027. The intersection of AI infrastructure pullback, energy price volatility, and the renewable deceleration presents a complex backdrop for global capital expenditure planning heading into 2027.
