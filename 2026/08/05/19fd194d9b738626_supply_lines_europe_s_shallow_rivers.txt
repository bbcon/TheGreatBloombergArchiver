Europe’s Shallow Rivers Pose New Risks to Beleaguered Factories
Europe’s factories have a new supply risk to reckon with — vital rivers too

Europe’s factories have been pummeled by wave after wave of supply shocks over 
the past decade — US tariffs on steel and other exports, the war in Ukraine, 
the Covid pandemic, an ongoingenergy crisis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/yemen-s-houthis-say-they-ll-target-tankers-in-northern-red-sea?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
 in the Mideast and an influx ofChinese imports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/germany-maps-china-s-weaknesses-in-preparation-for-a-trade-war?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
.

Now the weather is having a turn, and the timing could hardly be worse.

From the North to the Black seas, droughts have choked inland waterways vital 
for trade, leaving the Rhine and Danube at historically low levels. It’s not 
just crimping the flow of cargo with payload restrictions — it’s threatening 
power plants, local water supplies and tourism.

The Danube remains navigable, though many cargo vessels are taking reduced 
loads, Bloomberg Newsreports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-05/danube-river-hits-record-lows-and-threatens-romania-hungary-nuclear-plants?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
 Wednesday. ARhine chokepoint for vessels heading to southern Germany and 
Switzerland is theshallowest 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/rhine-set-for-more-disruption-after-hitting-lowest-on-record?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
 since records began in 1880, ahead of the peak summer dry season, with 
conditions likely to worsen in the weeks ahead.
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-05/danube-river-hits-record-lows-and-threatens-romania-hungary-nuclear-plants?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
The situation on the Rhine may shave 0.3 percentage point from Germany’s GDP 
growth this year, Carsten Brzeski, ING’s global head of macro, wrote in a 
research note Tuesday.

For Europe’s biggest economy, “the Rhine is not simply one transport option 
among many. It is a piece of industrial infrastructure around which much of 
German industry was physically built,” he wrote.

Related: Germany’s Elusive Economic Revival Is Within Touching Distance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-01/germany-s-elusive-economic-revival-is-within-touching-distance?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>

Brzeski noted that about 285 million metric tons of freight move along the 
river annually. The corridor connects the Port of Rotterdam — Europe’s busiest 
for seaborne trade — with three important German industrial hubs: the steel 
center of Rhine-Ruhr, Rhine-Main encompassing the Frankfurt area and the 
Ludwigshafen chemical complex.

Higher Freight Costs

The restrictions will boost freight costs and threaten to delay deliveries of 
manufacturing inputs, according toMartin Ademmer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVzOvZ0QL9k/martin-ademmer?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
 andSimona Delle Chiaie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWiNbQNQyeE/simona-delle-chiaie?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
 of Bloomberg Economics.

In a research note 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/german-industry-is-better-prepared-for-low-rhine-levels-be-says?cmpid=BBD080526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260805&utm_content=7243>
 published Wednesday, they estimated that a month of severely restricted 
navigation reduces German industrial production by up to 1% and “higher 
transport costs can also add to inflationary pressures.”

But previous droughts and river restrictions have forced companies to 
diversify their transport options and build resilience in other ways.

Read More: Germany’s Troubled Chemical Makers Risk Disorderly Decline <>

For example, BASF, the world’s largest chemicals maker, “has commissioned a 
low-draft barge that can operate even under exceptionally low water 
conditions,” Ademmer and Delle Chiaie wrote.

“We expect the economic impact of low water on inland waterways to be somewhat 
smaller now than in previous droughts,” according to Ademmer and Delle Chiaie. 
“Firms have learned from past disruptions, strengthening inventories and 
contingency plans to make supply chains more resilient. But if water levels 
stay low for long, the economic damage could still be significant.”
