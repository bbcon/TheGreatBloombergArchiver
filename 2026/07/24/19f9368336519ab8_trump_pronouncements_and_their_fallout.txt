Japanese shoppers are driving up luxury jewelry sales to a record as consumers 
seek a safe haven from inflation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/japanese-shoppers-are-driving-up-jewelry-sales-to-a-record?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6935>
 and the weakening yen. Sales of gems, precious metals and artwork at Japan’s 
department stores climbed 19% in the first half of 2026 from a year earlier to 
¥330 billion ($2 billion), the highest sales for the period since records began 
in 2008, according to data from the Japan Department Stores Association. The 
yen has weakened to nearly ¥164 per dollar, its lowest level since the 1980s, 
while core consumer prices, excluding fresh food, rose 1.6% in June. The 
economic backdrop is prompting consumers to shift spending toward assets they 
see as better stores of value.

age-appropriate content.

proposed merger between Iyogin and Ehime Bank adds to a series of recentM&A

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/japan-banks-with-shipping-finance-expertise-are-latest-to-merge?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260724&utm_content=6935>

based on the island of Shikoku agreed to integrate next April, with Ehime to 
become a wholly owned unit of Iyogin. Combined assets of the companies exceed 
¥12 trillion ($73 billion). The deal will bring together two of Japan’s most 
prominent providers of shipping finance. Shikoku, the smallest of the country’s 
four main islands, is home to shipbuilders and other marine-related companies.
