• China Q3 GDP grew 4.5% year-over-year, slightly above the Q2 pace of 4.3% and above consensus, keeping full-year growth on track to meet Beijing's 4.5%–5.0% target.
• September retail sales beat forecasts by a wide margin, rising at more than twice the expected pace — the strongest reading since March — though the absolute level of 1.1% year-over-year growth remains modest.
• Indonesia's equity market extended a severe selloff, deepening what authorities describe as the worst rout in nearly 30 years, following MSCI's warning of a potential two-step index downgrade.
• China's coal output fell year-over-year for a sixth consecutive month in September following the May mine explosion in Shanxi, raising energy supply concerns ahead of winter.

**China Growth and Stimulus Outlook**
The Q3 data dump provided Beijing with a degree of relief. Retail sales, industrial production, and export momentum collectively kept the economy within striking distance of the official growth target. However, the composition of growth remains skewed toward exports and manufacturing rather than domestic consumption, reinforcing the structural imbalance that trading partners raised at last week's IMF meetings in Bangkok. The IMF's Kristalina Georgieva explicitly called on China to spend more domestically. Macquarie economist Larry Hu characterizes Beijing's approach as a "do-just-enough rule," suggesting further near-term stimulus is unlikely given that targets appear achievable. Attention is already shifting to 2027 policy plans.

**Indonesia and Regional Markets**
Indonesian equities and the rupiah extended losses after MSCI warned of a potential two-step downgrade — an unusually severe signal that has amplified selling pressure across domestic institutions and foreign funds. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi denied responsibility for yen weakness and elevated interest rates, underscoring continued political sensitivity around Bank of Japan normalization. Nidec shares fell to decade lows after an independent committee found civil liability for accounting irregularities involving founder Shigenobu Nagamori.
