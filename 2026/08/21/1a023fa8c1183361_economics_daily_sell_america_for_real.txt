Sell America, Maybe for Real

The theme ‘Sell America 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-04-21/trump-s-trade-war-puts-us-dollar-bonds-safe-haven-status-at-risk?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
’ has waxed and waned through President Donald Trump’s second term, as market 
participants warned volatile policymaking — everything from historic tariff 
hikes and new wars to the corrosion of long-term alliances — was sending 
foreign investors fleeing from US stocks and bonds.

While it made for a good headline, there was little in the data 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-18/foreigners-rebuff-sell-america-and-pour-in-a-net-1-5-trillion?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 to back it up as foreign holdings of Treasury securities hit a record this 
year, and as US stocks soared to new highs.

This week’s sudden announcement of a bond market intervention 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent risks nudging that narrative from 
clickbait to something closer to reality.

Bessent’s move to bring down bond yields through purchases of longer-dated 
securities hasrattled investors 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/dollar-risks-becoming-biggest-loser-from-bessent-s-bond-buying?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 who say it will weaken the dollar, deter foreign investors and ultimately does 
nothing to resolve the underlying issue of surging federal borrowing. Total US 
public debt this week surpassed$40 trillion 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 for the first time.

Robin Brooks, a Brookings Institution senior fellow, wrote in a Substack post 
titledDollar Debasement Has Begun that Bessent’s move “is the clearest sign yet 
that the US is following Japan on its path towards currency debasement.” (The 
Japanese yen hit a four-decade low last month amid concern over fiscal policy 
in a nation where the debt-to-GDP ratio exceeds 200%.)
Scott BessentPhotographer: Yuri Gripas/Abaca/Bloomberg
Brooks, a former Goldman Sachs chief FX strategist, is far from alone.

Investors and analysts across Wall Street warned the Treasury’s buyback 
maneuver stokes unpredictability in what’s meant to be an open and transparent 
market — and may ultimately result in higher, not lower, borrowing costs.

That’s because it appeared to violate the long-held “regular and predictable” 
US principle in issuing debt. Investors now could demand anadditional risk 
premium 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/investors-warn-us-over-shifting-from-predictable-debt-management?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 to hold Treasuries, especially longer-dated ones, going forward if they are 
uncertain about the Treasury’s intentions.

Bessent rebuffed such views on Thursday, and talked up the traditional “strong 
dollar policy.” Hedescribed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/bessent-says-buybacks-could-be-more-than-4-billion-per-issue?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 his measures as merely an effort to focus investors minds amid thinly traded 
summer markets.

He has his work cut out for him swinging opinion around. Kit Juckes, chief 
currency strategist at Societe Generale, opened his daily note Thursday with: 
“Scott Bessent is the strong dollar’s nemesis.”

Need-to-Know Research

The Arctic Ocean is rapidly emerging as a new Asia-to-Europe trade route 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-11/arctic-shipping-routes-and-container-carriers?cmpid=BBD082126_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260821&utm_content=8015>
 — one that bypasses the increasingly risky and expensive itinerary via the 
Middle East. The biggest takeaway may be that it marks a win for China’s 
geo-economic strategy-making, says Alicia Garcia-Herrero.

China’s new scheduled Arctic routes cut the journey from China to northern 
Europe to about 20 days, the Natixis chief economist for Asia Pacific and the 
Middle East wrote in a recent note. Russian controls the route, giving China an 
advantage — as Moscow’s geopolitical partner, it gets a pass, while rival 
nations are left out. “China’s access was not granted; it was engineered.”
The Istanbul Bridge container ship sailed from Russia to China over the 
Northern Sea Route
Garcia-Herrero also flagged that the new route is a product of industrial 
policy. Russia operates around 45 icebreakers, while China is expanding its 
capabilities. The US, however, has had to turn to Finland to help build new 
vessels. The lesson for America: industrial capacity is becoming central to 
strategic competition, and once lost, “it cannot be regained quickly,” she 
wrote.
