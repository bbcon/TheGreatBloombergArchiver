• MSCI Asia Pacific Index rebounded 2.4% — its largest single-day recovery in over 18 months — tracking Friday's US equity stabilization.
• China extended its suspension of the additional 24% tariff on US goods for two further months through January, formalizing the trade truce; the 10% baseline tariff remains in effect.
• China's producer price inflation accelerated to its fastest pace since June 2022, though deflation risks in the consumer economy persist.

**Trade and Macro**
China's decision to extend the tariff pause formalizes an agreement reached between Presidents Trump and Xi, providing a constructive backdrop ahead of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18–19. Beijing also suspended special fees on American ships docking at Chinese ports through January 10 and extended exemptions on 27 US companies from trade and export restrictions. The gesture signals continued interest in stabilizing bilateral relations, though US Treasury Secretary Bessent and USTR Greer separately raised concerns about implementation of commitments from the Busan accord. Separately, Chinese domestic gas prices are weakening, potentially freeing seaborne LNG cargoes for European and other Asian markets.

**Corporate and Geopolitical**
The Nvidia scandal carries particular resonance in Asia. The board's investigation centers on whether CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence through a Singapore-based customer. Beijing has dismissed the allegations. The episode risks complicating the Trump-Xi APEC encounter and may harden Democratic congressional scrutiny of Nvidia's China chip sales. Taiwan equities recovered as authorities moved to stabilize markets following last week's rout. Citadel Securities is expanding its Asia headcount — adding nearly 100 people this year to bring regional staff to approximately 400 — and is increasing Hong Kong office space by 40%.
