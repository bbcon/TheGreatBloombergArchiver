Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Will Kubzansky looks at the dynamics of the American motor-fuel market 
as drivers again contend with $4-a-gallon gas.

The vast majority of US motorists don’t buy diesel. But whether they realize 
it or not, each time they fill up with gasoline they’re also paying the price 
for a global squeeze on diesel supplies.

The cost of the workhorse fuel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/us-drivers-are-again-paying-more-than-5-a-gallon-for-diesel?cmpid=BBBXT072826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7036>
 has surged in recent months because of the disruption to shipments through the 
Strait of Hormuz, as well as Ukrainian attacks on Russian energy infrastructure.

Russia, previously accounting for more than a 10th of global supply, has 
suspended exports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/russia-bans-diesel-exports-after-ukraine-s-refinery-attacks?cmpid=BBBXT072826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7036>
 amid the bombardment.

Enter the hidden hand of the refining industry 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-19/us-oil-refiners-are-running-so-hard-they-are-risking-breakdowns?cmpid=BBBXT072826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7036>
, which breaks down each barrel of oil into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel. That 
mix is dynamic, responding to price incentives. It’s also zero-sum: More 
gasoline means a reduced volume of other products.

The typical US refinery makes almost twice as much 
<https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/oil-and-petroleum-products/refining-crude-oil-inputs-and-outputs.php>
 gasoline as it does diesel. Plants often eke out even more when demand peaks 
in the summer.

But the margin for producing diesel in the US right now is hovering near an 
all-time high, prompting refiners to join a global scramble to make more of the 
fuel.

American refiners have produced an average of 5.3 million barrels of 
distillate fuel oil 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/us-goes-all-in-on-diesel-production-amid-growing-global-shortage?cmpid=BBBXT072826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7036>
 — a category that includes diesel as well as other products such as heating 
oil — every day this month, on course for a seasonal record.

Crucially, much of that distillate is going overseas. The US is on track for 
one of the most prolific months of diesel exports ever, with regions from South 
America to Europedesperate for supplies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/diesel-squeeze-in-europe-is-set-to-deepen-morgan-stanley-says?cmpid=BBBXT072826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7036>
.

Since jet fuel production is also at a record, gasoline appears to be the 
loser, with monthly output set to fall below the five-year average. Stockpiles 
are at their lowest for this time of year since 2012, and average pump prices 
are once again above$4 a gallon 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/us-average-gasoline-prices-rise-above-4-a-gallon-again?cmpid=BBBXT072826_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7036>
.

To be sure, the oil price remains the biggest single determinant of the cost 
of gasoline or diesel. But understanding the refining sector helps explain why 
the fuels don’t always move in lockstep with crude. For now, the economics 
still favor a diesel ramp-up.

—Will Kubzansky, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Southeast Asia’s LNG demand is set to expand much faster than long-term 
contracting, increasing the region’s dependence on a volatile spot market, 
according to BloombergNEF. Falling domestic gas output, new gas-fired power 
capacity and lower piped-gas imports are expected to drive up LNG purchases. 
But buyers are delaying signing extended contracts given uncertain demand, 
geopolitical risks and projections for lower prices in the future, BNEF says.
