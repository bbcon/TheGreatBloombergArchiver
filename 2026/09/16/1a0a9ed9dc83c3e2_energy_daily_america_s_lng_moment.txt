Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Asia Energy Team Leader Stephen Stapczynski looks at how the gas industry plans 
to fill a widening supply gap.

One word — diversification — was on the lips of most executives at the world’s 
biggest natural gas conference in Bangkok this week.

That’s the industry’s euphemism for cutting exposure to the Middle East.

After all, more than six months into the US-Iran war, there’s still no sign of 
liquefied natural gas flowing again through the Strait of Hormuz. A fifth of 
global supply remains effectively trapped inside the Persian Gulf.

Publicly, speakers at the Gastech event stressed that Qatar and the United 
Arab Emirates will continue to be important suppliers to the world. On the 
sidelines, however, another thought was whispered: What if LNG traffic through 
Hormuz never fully returns to normal?

Buyers and traders can no longer afford to wait, and their search for 
alternative supplies has put the US — already the world’s biggest exporter —
front and center 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/lng-buyers-look-to-us-to-hedge-future-risk-of-hormuz-disruption?cmpid=BBBXT091626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8959>
 in the conversation.

With America set to roughly double LNG capacity by 2030, those countries most 
exposed to the Middle East disruption likePakistan 
<bbg://news/stories/TLC41PT96OSM> and Bangladesh 
<bbg://news/stories/TLCBNFKIP3IM> are exploring how to secure some of this 
supply.

Companies in Thailand 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/thailand-s-ptt-seeks-lng-deals-in-us-gulf-to-widen-supply-base?cmpid=BBBXT091626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8959>
 are doing the same, while Japan’s Inpex Corp. is consideringinvesting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/lng-buyers-look-to-us-to-hedge-future-risk-of-hormuz-disruption?cmpid=BBBXT091626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8959>
 in an export project in the US.
The Golden Pass LNG facility on the Texas coast is a joint venture between 
QatarEnergy and Exxon Mobil Corp.Photographer: Brett Coomer/Houston 
Chronicle/Getty Images via Hearst Newspapers
For buyers, American LNG offers a hedge. Cargoes can be shipped to Asia or 
Europe depending where they’re needed more, or where prices are higher.

The economics are also attractive. Long-term contracts linked to Henry Hub, 
the US benchmark, are delivering LNG to Asia at roughly $8 per million British 
thermal units today. The currentspot price <bbg://news/stories/TLCEK3T96OSG> is 
close to $30.

But there’s a catch.

Rising construction costs, supply-chain bottlenecks and competition for fuel 
needed to power data centers are making US Gulf Coast export plants more 
expensive, a trend that’s being reflected in the price of new contracts. That 
matters to cash-strapped economies.

If the US and other suppliers like Canada and South America don’t seize this 
opportunity, buyers may simply opt toburn more coal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/vietnam-eyes-building-more-coal-power-as-war-squeezes-lng-supply?cmpid=BBBXT091626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8959>
 oraccelerate their shift 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-energy-transition-iran-war?cmpid=BBBXT091626_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8959>
 toward renewables.

And if that happens, diversification takes on a wider meaning — not just a 
shift away from the Middle East, but from LNG itself.

—Stephen Stapczynski, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

Gas turbine makers are concentrating their expansion on the biggest machines, 
betting that utilities and data centers will need scale and efficiency in 
generating electricity. Four-fifths of the industry’s additional manufacturing 
capacity by 2030 will go for heavy-frame turbines that are typically rated over 
100 megawatts, according to analysis by BloombergNEF.
