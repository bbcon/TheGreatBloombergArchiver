AI Outlier

When global stocks have AI down-days, India has been a rare bright spot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-25/indian-stocks-extend-world-beating-run-as-ai-selloff-resumes?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
. If Asia’s third-biggest economy has emerged as a hedge, that’s because it has 
a different relationship with the technology than many peers.

Lacking a frontier AI model of its own, India also plays little role in 
supplying the hardware that powers them. That sets it apart fromKorea 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/south-korea-s-early-exports-gain-after-1-trillion-milestone?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
, Malaysia and other economies that have seen booming exports of data-center 
equipment — and are vulnerable if the AI bandwagon slows down.

India has also been relatively slow out of the gates in building its own data 
centers. With only 153 of them, it ranks 14th globally.

Tighter US restrictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-22/trump-s-ai-chip-crackdown-on-china-after-nvidia-crisis-squeezes-southeast-asia?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
 on China’s AI ambitions, like the recent curbs on sales of advanced chips, 
couldhelp India catch up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/nvidia-scandal-fallout-india-ai-yotta-ambani-adani-am-intelligence-e2e-networks?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
. They’ll bring closer scrutiny for other Asian hubs like Malaysia, Singapore 
and Thailand.
A Nvidia HGX H100 server at the Yotta Data Services Pvt. data center in Navi 
Mumbai.Photographer: Dhiraj Singh/Bloomberg
Meantime India is ramping up its bet on data centers, hoping to become a 
reliable alternative for US tech firms. BillionairesMukesh Ambani 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-19/ambani-s-reliance-to-invest-110-billion-in-ai-infrastructure?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
 andGautam Adani 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-03/adani-group-said-to-seek-1-5-billion-for-data-center-financing?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
 have each pledged at least $100 billion toward AI and digital infrastructure, 
while investors including Blackstone Inc. are also expanding. The government 
has offered significant tax incentives.

The economic payoff could extend beyond the facilities themselves. Data 
centers may not create large numbers of jobs directly and their environmental 
impact remains contentious, but the investments could boost demand in the power 
and construction industries, and accelerate AI adoption more broadly.

India also has a large pool of skilled IT workers and developed digital 
infrastructure, which should allow it to adopt AI faster than economies in a 
similar position, such as the Philippines, according to Leif Eskesen, chief 
economist at CLSA.

One catch is the new threat to a key economic strength. The software industry 
accounts for about 40% of Indian services exports, and is a major white-collar 
employer. But AI is damping demand. It’s telling that IT firms have been 
largely left out of this month’s stock rally.

Still, new opportunities 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/ai-is-creating-more-jobs-than-it-cuts-in-india-nomura-says?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
 could also open up for Indian IT providers, since businesses will need help 
integrating and maintaining AI tools.

And even without the AI boost that others enjoyed, the economy has been posting
growth rates 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/india-s-economy-grows-faster-than-expected-in-april-june-quarter?cmpid=BBD112726_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11932>
 approaching 8%, driven largely by domestic consumption and a young workforce. 
It’s expected to maintain that pace in third-quarter data due Monday.

India may ultimately occupy a middle ground in the AI revolution: not among 
its biggest winners, but less exposed if the boom unwinds. In an uncertain 
market, investors are concluding, that neutrality itself has value.
