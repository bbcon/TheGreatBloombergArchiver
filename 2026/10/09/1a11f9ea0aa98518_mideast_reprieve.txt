Kazim/Bloomberg
Simon Wood launched his first venture in August: a four-court padel center 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-09/will-padel-s-300-million-uk-bubble-burst-in-race-to-build-courts?cmpid=100926_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261009&utm_content=9912>
 under Victorian railway arches in Birmingham. Armed with £700,000 ($945,000), 
it is Neighbourhood Padel’s debut venue, with expansion plans already in 
motion. Across the UK, hundreds of padel courts are popping up as entrepreneurs 
race to cash in on the frenzy that the sport has become.

 * Money has been flowing in with English rapper Stormzy and former tennis 
star Andy Murray both investing in different clubs. In total, investors have 
poured at least $300 million into UK padel companies in recent years, according 
to Bloomberg calculations
 * While the sport swells in popularity which has made booking a court 
difficult, the industry is on alert with the stampede to build courts fueling 
concerns about a wave of bankruptcies
 * The cracks are beginning to appear. Social Sports Society, one of the 
largest padel operators in Britain with five sites, is going through an 
administration process, a form of insolvency. Founder Jesper Konstantinov said 
its financing partner Mindspring Padel prevented it from raising more funds.
