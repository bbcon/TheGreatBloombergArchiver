**Climate and Infrastructure Stress**
Europe's heat waves are producing measurable economic damage beyond the immediate human cost. Dangerously low river levels are disrupting barge transport of chemicals, oil products, and other industrial goods, while curtailing power generation at riverside reactors in France and Hungary. European natural gas storage is at its lowest level for this time of year in almost two decades, a consequence of the Middle East conflict's impact on energy prices and winter stockpiling. The combination of energy price pressure, transport disruption, and power supply constraints represents a compound supply-side shock for continental industry as the heating season approaches.

**Politics and Corporate**
UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham's devolution agenda — devolving economic power to regions modeled on Manchester — faces structural questions. Scotland, the UK's most devolved economy, has grown more slowly than England since 2008 despite higher tax rates and spending, complicating the evidence base for the policy. In the UK labor market, AI is producing a bifurcation: IT and engineering vacancies have held up while accounting, retail, and manufacturing roles have fallen by double digits, casting doubt on the premise that broad technical qualifications will future-proof young workers.
