• S&P 500 opens the week at an all-time high, up 13.3% year-to-date, even as AI-sector credit spreads widen materially.

• JPMorgan raised its S&P 500 forecast for a second time in two months, citing strong earnings and AI spending payoff.

**Labor Market**
July payrolls fell 23,000 against a median economist expectation of +80,000 gain, a significant miss that marks the third consecutive summer of employment underperformance. A 60,000 slide in local government employment — partly reflecting the exhaustion of post-Covid federal teaching assistance — was a key driver. Critically, initial jobless claims remain spectacularly low, suggesting minimal firing accompanies the lack of hiring. JPMorgan's Michael Feroli acknowledges the miss may partly reflect a seasonal pattern that corrected in prior autumns. Santander's Stephen Stanley argues the Fed, having been "fooled" into cutting rates in the past two Septembers, will look through the weakness this time given core inflation remains well above the 2% target.

**AI Credit and Markets**
The S&P 500's all-time high masks growing stress in AI-sector credit. Barclays data show the top six tech groups — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, and SpaceX — account for 8.4% of the US investment-grade market's duration-times-spread risk, exceeding the 7.3% share of the six largest banks. Alphabet reported negative free cash flow for the first time since its IPO; Meta's second-quarter free cash flow plunged 91% year-over-year. JPMorgan estimates $4.1 trillion of $5.5 trillion in projected AI capex will be debt-financed, making the buildout acutely sensitive to the Fed's next move. Berkshire Hathaway spent $4.5 billion on share buybacks in Q2, its largest since 2021.
