An unlikely new battleground has emerged <> as Brazil enters the final week 
before the presidential election: a religious dispute sparked by fake news.

It’s the latest twist in a campaign already hijacked by the biggest banking 
fraud 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-banco-master-brazil-supreme-court-presidential-election/?cmpid=BBD092926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9460>
 in the country’s history and an unprecedentedcrisis at the Supreme Court 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/alexandre-de-moraes-says-probe-against-him-driven-by-politics?cmpid=BBD092926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9460>
.

The new flashpoint between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and his main 
rival, Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, centers on a fabricated claim involving 
Brazil’s patron saint, Our Lady of Aparecida.

It began with a false social-media post claiming Bolsonaro would support a 
bill shelved in Congress two decades ago that sought to strip the saint of the 
title she has held since 1930.

The claim was bound to be explosive in a country with more than 100 million 
Catholics — nearly three in five Brazilians. Devotion to Our Lady of Aparecida 
dates back to 1717, when three fishermen found a small statue of the Virgin 
Mary in a river.
Lula at a rally in São Paulo on Saturday.Photographer: Victor 
Moriyama/Bloomberg
The false post appeared to target Bolsonaro as an evangelical Christian, since 
some evangelicals reject images of saints as idolatry.

Left-leaning social-media accounts amplified the claim, which blew up on X. 
Lula and his allies rushed to the saint’s defense. Bolsonaro responded by 
holding up an image of Our Lady of Aparecida.

With the Oct. 4 first-round ballot days away, the dispute even reached the 
Supreme Court 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/powerful-brazil-judge-faces-reckoning-over-ties-to-10-billion-bank-scandal?cmpid=BBD092926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9460>
, pitting a justice appointed by Lula against another named by former President 
Jair Bolsonaro, Flávio’s father. All of this in a constitutionally secular 
state.

Disinformation has become a recurring feature 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/famous-brazil-judge-yanked-from-fake-news-probe-that-made-him?cmpid=BBD092926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9460>
 of Brazilian elections. Four years ago, electoral authorities ordered the 
removal of scores of false or misleading posts.

The dispute may even be affecting the race, with a poll yesterday suggesting 
Lula’s support among Catholics had risen.

Violence, corruption and the economy are Brazilians’ biggest concerns, however.
