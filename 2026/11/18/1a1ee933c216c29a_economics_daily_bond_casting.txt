Bond Casting

No-one can be sure about when the next crisis will hit France, which is 
struggling to fixbloated public finances 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/france-s-le-pen-proposes-individual-investments-in-pensions?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 and enduring unrest on its streets.

What we do now have a fair idea of however is the cast of characters that 
could be called upon to firefight turmoil if bond vigilantes test Paris to the 
brink.

A flurry of diplomacy in recent days has largely resolved the suspense around 
the European Central Bank’s leadership, namely over who will run the 
institution after President Christine Lagarde leaves her post early in February.

The choice is one for euro-region leaders to make. As usual however, a deal 
between Paris and Berlin has probably settled the matter on behalf of everyone 
else. And the outcome there is thatPablo Hernandez de Cos 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/spain-hopes-for-quick-deal-on-de-cos-as-next-ecb-president?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
, currently chief of the Bank for International Settlements, is firmly on track 
for the ECB’s top job.

He is seen as the more dovish of the two candidates — former Dutch central 
banker Klaas Knot is also in the running. With Germany normally favoring hawks, 
the result looks like a win for French President Emmanuel Macron.

He and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz had more to hammer out 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/germany-to-make-proposal-for-top-ecb-jobs-next-week-merz-says?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 though. Each of them has a vacancy to fill on the ECB’s Executive Board in 
advance of a deadline for nominations later on Wednesday.

That matter seems also to be resolved, with France set to clinch the markets 
portfolio currently held by the German member of the panel, Isabel Schnabel.

Berlin has secured the chief economist role that will become available when 
Ireland’s Philip Lane leaves in May. Germany’s coalitionhas just agreed 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/german-government-nominates-brunnermeier-for-ecb-board-seat?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 on Princeton academicMarkus Brunnermeier 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/de-cos-on-path-to-ecb-as-berlin-backs-brunnermeier-for-lane-job?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 for that influential job that involves actually proposing any changes in 
policy.

Paris’s pick has yet to be revealed, but it’s increasingly expected to be 
former OECD Chief Economist Laurence Boone, once one of Macron’s ministers.

She would be the ECB’s only female policymaker. And should a full-blown bond 
crisis erupt, Boone would probably be in operational control of implementing 
whateverturmoil-calming action 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/what-s-the-tpi-how-ecb-can-respond-to-french-bond-turmoil?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 the central bank might concoct.

Another win for Macron then? Possibly. Then again, given France’s troubles 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/france-to-miss-deficit-goals-as-economic-growth-falters-eu-says?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
, such victories may have limited value. Stuck at a fiscal impasse, and with
presidential elections next year 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-18/french-election-jean-luc-melenchon-is-wild-card-for-voters?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 likely to highlight just how polarized and fractured its political system is, 
the bond vigilantes will beready and waiting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/ecb-warns-hedge-funds-boost-volatility-risks-for-sovereign-bonds?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>

Need-to-Know Research

Artificial intelligence is set to become the biggest investment 
<https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/4c_Van-Nieuwerburgh.pdf> 
in US history, already surpassing the railroad buildout of the 19th century. 
Economists at the Federal Reserve of St. Louis alsofound 
<https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2026/nov/hyperscaler-cash-investment-ai-boom?utm_source=Federal+Reserve+Bank+of+St.+Louis+Publications&utm_campaign=517c286615-BlogAlert&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_c572dedae2-517c286615-297502063>
 that capital spending is driving the most recent boom, more so than research 
and development.

Capital expenditures at the so-called hyperscalers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-13/jpmorgan-sees-ai-spree-driving-record-2027-us-high-grade-supply?cmpid=BBD111826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261118&utm_content=11533>
 jumped to almost 13% of assets in early 2026 from about 5% at the start of 
2028, the St. Louis Fed researchers said. But just looking at R&D spending to 
total assets, that ratio for the hyperscalers has dropped to 9.3% from a peak 
of about 11% at the start of 2023, which was a few months after the public 
release of ChatGPT.

Meanwhile, cash holdings at hyperscalers have plummeted as a share of their 
assets, to 16% this year from about 34% in 2018. That’s only about 5 percentage 
points above the average of other companies, St. Louis Fed researchers Julian 
Kozlowski, Juan M. Sánchez and Nicholas Sullivan wrote in a blog post.
