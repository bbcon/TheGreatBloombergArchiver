Deep Dive: Hungary’s Road to Adopting the Euro
Peter Magyar, Hungary’s prime minister, and members of Tisza party arrive at 
Parliament in Budapest, Hungary, on May 9, 2026.Photographer: Akos 
Stiller/Bloomberg
Prime Minister Peter Magyar galvanized Hungary with his vision of a national 
makeover 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-13/can-peter-magyar-deliver-euro-membership-for-hungary?cmpid=101326_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10012>
 following the Viktor Orban era. Now comes the tough task of delivering the 
economic centerpiece of that plan: a credible road map for the adoption of the 
euro.

 * Magyar pledged to meet criteria for membership in the European currency by 
2030, sparking a rally in the Hungarian forint and double-digit returns for 
bond investors. That now hinges on the reality of reducing a bloated budget 
deficit by more than half in the next three years.
 * Hungary has undergone a significant political transformation since Magyar’s 
victory in April, restoring access to €16.4 billion in EU funds and overhauling 
the state broadcaster. Magyar has acknowledged that fixing the economy is a 
wholly different prospect.
 * The chief executive officer of Hungary’s largest lender, OTP Bank, 
cautioned at a Bloomberg event late last month that diverging from the 
budget-cutting euro path could trigger a rush for the exits by investors.
