Donald Trump may have been unable to meaningfully reduce China’s global 
influence or to secure victory in his war with Iran, but this UN week has 
highlighted some notable successes in the US president’s bid to dominate the 
Western Hemisphere.

On Tuesday, he signed an agreement 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-19/us-strikes-deal-over-greenland-to-end-row-that-threatened-nato?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 with Denmark and Greenland expanding US military access to the Arctic island 
he so publicly covets.

Praised by Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen as good for all sides, it 
fails to give US ownership but it doesbroaden a 1951 NATO-era defense pact 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/trump-to-sign-deal-tuesday-that-leaves-greenland-in-danish-hands?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 to explicitly include defense of the American continent.

US aircraft can fly over and land anywhere on Greenland, and its forces gain 
broad rights to move through its territory and waters. It also restricts 
potentially security-sensitive investments from countries outside the European 
Union, NATO and allies.

Also on Tuesday, Trump met with Venezuela’s acting President 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/trump-and-rodriguez-talked-venezuelan-debt-return-to-democracy?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 Delcy Rodríguez in New York as her government deepens cooperation with 
Washington over the country’s oil industry.

She then addressed the UN General Assembly 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/venezuelan-leader-rodriguez-vows-orderly-return-to-democracy?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 in a striking illustration of her diplomatic rehabilitation, while her 
predecessor Nicolás Maduro sits in federal custody just miles away in Brooklyn 
after US forces captured him in Caracas in January.
Rodríguez addresses the UN General Assembly in New York yesterday.
Photographer: Angelina Katsanis/Bloomberg
At the same time, opposition leader María Corina Machado repeatedly tried and 
failed to return 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-23/machado-tries-fails-to-enter-venezuela-as-rodriguez-meets-trump?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 to Venezuela, complicating her efforts to shape any democratic transition.

Rodríguez has used the UN gathering to show she can secure engagement from 
governments once critical of Caracas. She met UN Secretary-General António 
Guterres, briefly spoke with Spain’s Pedro Sánchez and was due to meet Chile’s 
José Antonio Kast.

Equally, Trump’s declaration in his own UN speech 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/trump-set-to-defend-war-in-iran-in-latest-appearance-at-the-un?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 that the US controls Venezuela’s oil is a reminder of the pressure Rodríguez, 
and other administrations negotiating with the US, are under.

Cuba, for example, remains under intense US pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/fresh-us-sanctions-hit-cuba-s-fuel-sector-and-a-castro-grandson?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
. Its delegation walked out during Trump’s address. — Mie Dahl 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYIc6C8LnOs/mie-dahl?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>

And Finally

Among the contents of a shipping container from a commercial vessel in the 
southern Yemeni port of Aden, weapons experts found rear fins and other parts 
resembling components used to assemble an Iranian ballistic missile. The cargo 
had been en-route to Hodeida, a Red Sea city controlled by the Houthis. The 
find provided new evidence that the group, once heavily reliant on Iran for 
finished weapons, is now able to manufacture arms inside Yemen thanks to 
equipment from China, providing the kind ofautonomous military power 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-23/houthi-s-war-efforts-iran-support-sustained-with-chinese-imports?cmpid=BBD092426_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260924&utm_content=9269>
 it’s never previously enjoyed.
