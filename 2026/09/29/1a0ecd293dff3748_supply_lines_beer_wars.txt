US, Canada Trade Ties Suffer Setback With Trump’s Import Ban

US-Canada trade relations took another step backward overnight as President 
Donald Trump’s ban on about $1 billion worth of Canadian imports took effect.

Washington’s latest restrictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-28/us-import-ban-on-canadian-alcohol-motorcycles-set-to-begin?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>
 on the country’s No. 2 US trading partner are even blunter than high tariffs — 
they simply block shipments of motorcycles, alcoholic beverages and whey 
products from Canada.

The US Customs and Border Protection agency, in an advisory 
<https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/42ce49a?reqfrom=share>
 on Monday, said “covered products will be unconditionally rejected and denied 
entry.”

The move, announced 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/trump-plans-to-cut-canadian-products-out-of-government-contracts?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>
 earlier this month, is a delayed response to retaliatory tariffs imposed by 
Prime Minister Mark Carney’s government on some American-made goods, as well as
provincial boycotts 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/us-wants-its-booze-back-in-canada-s-stores-but-some-say-don-t-buy-it?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>
 of US booze.

Trump cited Section 338 of the Trade Act of 1930, the same authority used to 
“offset discrimination” with tariffs and the first time it’s been used to put 
levies on a trading partner, according to areport this month 
<https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12595> from the Congressional Research 
Service.

It’s not a huge economic blow in a relationship that totals about $900 billion 
in bilateral commerce — one of the largest in the world — but it stings plenty 
for those directly effected.

Explainer: Why the US-Canada Trade War Is Escalating 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/why-the-us-canada-trade-war-is-escalating?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>

And it’s not just Canadian producers that need to be aware of Tuesday’s new 
curbs. US firms that make targeted goods north of the border are also subject 
to the ban, according to industry observers.

In the case of beer, “the restrictions are based on where the beer is 
produced, not where the brand is headquartered or who owns it,” according to an
online post 
<https://www.brewersassociation.org/government-affairs-updates/canadian-beer-import-ban-set-to-take-effect-september-29/>
 last week by the Brewers Association, a nonprofit trade group representing 
more than 9,500 American craft breweries. “In other words, beer brewed and 
packaged in Canada can be subject to the ban even when the brand itself is 
American.”
Cases of Canadian-made beer on a shelf at a store in Toronto.Photographer: 
Christopher Katsarov Luna/Bloomberg
The prohibition went into effect just after the White House trumpeted plans 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-28/trump-to-tout-15-billion-iowa-steel-plant-ahead-of-midterms?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>
 for a new steel plant in Iowa fed by iron ore mined in Minnesota, which shares 
a 547-mile border with Canada and is linked to Canadian mills by Great Lakes 
shipping routes.

Steel Reshoring

According to Bloomberg’s Canada Daily 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-28/us-steel-tariffs-push-cleveland-cliffs-stelco-to-idle-some-canada-operations?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>
 newsletter, Stelco, a Canadian steelmaker that is a unit of Ohio-based 
Cleveland-Cliffs, plans to suspend some operations in Hamilton, Ontario, 
blaming the impact of tariffs and trade disruptions on the sector.

Read More: US Tariffs Threaten Great Lakes Shipping Tied to Canada Trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-19/great-lakes-bind-us-canada-trade-imperiled-by-trump-tariffs?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>

Trump said on Monday he expects Canadian officials to cut their tariffs on US 
goods and apologize within the next three to four weeks. US Trade 
Representative Jamieson Greer recently characterized the dispute with Ottawa as 
a larger ideological battle over the future of globalization.

“We are reshoring steel, we are reshoring cars. We have new announcements of 
Stellantis, we have new announcements for steel — and a lot of this is because 
of our global trade policy which of course includes Canada,” Greer said inan 
interview 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-21/ustr-greer-on-china-trade-diesel-exports-canada-video?cmpid=BBD092926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9355>
 on Bloomberg TV last week. “The difference is, Prime Minister Carney and his 
team want to double-down on globalization, you see it now. We don’t — for us, 
globalization has been a problem.”

Such views will likely be among the topics discussed at this week’s meeting of 
G-20 trade ministers in Milwaukee, which Greer is hosting.Here’s the agenda 
<https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/september/ambassador-greer-travel-milwaukee-wisconsin-host-g20-trade-ministerial>
.
