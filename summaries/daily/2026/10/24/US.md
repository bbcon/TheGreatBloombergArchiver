**Consumer and Energy Dynamics**
The removal of federal EV tax credits has materially altered the economics of electric vehicle adoption. With new EVs carrying a roughly $4,800 premium over traditional alternatives and no subsidy offset, the financial calculus has become highly individualized — dependent on local electricity costs, annual mileage, and driving patterns. Survey data from Cox Automotive indicate that nearly half of American drivers report elevated gasoline prices have increased their likelihood of purchasing an EV, suggesting demand impulses remain intact even as the policy tailwind has disappeared.

**Fiscal and Policy Context**
The expiration of federal EV incentives reflects a broader shift in US fiscal and industrial policy. The downstream effect — shifting EV adoption economics from a broadly favorable to a more case-specific calculus — may temper the pace of fleet electrification in the near term, with implications for automakers carrying elevated EV inventory and capex commitments. Whether gasoline price pressure proves sufficient to sustain adoption momentum absent policy support remains an open question.
