**Oil Markets and the Strait of Hormuz**
The US-Iran conflict, now seven months old, has moved through a partial normalization — crude briefly retraced toward the $60s by July as backup logistics emerged — and is now re-escalating sharply. Houthi advances toward the Bab el-Mandeb, fresh tanker attacks in the Gulf, and US military strikes on five IRGC tankers Wednesday have compressed the range of benign near-term scenarios. The IEA's latest monthly report cut both supply and demand forecasts, warning the supply shortfall will be larger and more prolonged than previously estimated and acknowledging that closing the gap may require demand destruction — a stagflationary dynamic. Saudi Arabia has been forced to cut production to its lowest level since the 1990 Gulf War. Global tanker freight rates hit records, with supertanker earnings reaching $800,000 per day on the Middle East-to-China route. European gas storage is tracking 67% full heading into winter — 14 percentage points below last year — with BNEF's base case assuming Hormuz reopens in early November; a slippage in that timeline raises meaningful tail risk for European inventories.

**Global Monetary Policy and Capital Flows**
The oil shock creates a genuinely difficult policy environment for central banks globally. The ECB hiked Thursday into an energy-driven inflation surge. The Fed faces a binary decision next week contingent on Friday's CPI print. The Bank of Japan is widely expected to hike September 18, with potential GPIF reallocation toward domestic bonds representing a structural shift in global capital flows — a withdrawal of what has been a reliable source of low-cost external financing for the rest of the world. Unusually, emerging-market currencies strengthened for 11 consecutive weeks through this period, the longest streak since 2007, even as the dollar failed to benefit from widening rate differentials in the normal fashion. The tension between resilient risk appetite — sustained by AI-driven earnings growth above 20% year-on-year — and rising risk-free rates across every major market is the central macro dynamic of the moment, and its resolution will determine the path for global financial conditions through year-end.
