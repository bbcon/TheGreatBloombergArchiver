The AI agent trade

The Nasdaq 100 Index’s jump to a record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/us-stock-futures-drift-after-ai-driven-rally-oil-prices-slip?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>
 yesterday came with a twist on the AI trade: Personal assistants driven by the 
technology are getting so good that they’re going totake a chunk out of 
businesses 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/meta-s-muse-drags-down-stocks-that-depend-on-consumer-inertia?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>
 ranging from banking to travel booking.

Meta’s Muse AI agent has rocketed to the top of the app download charts, 
reigniting fresh optimism for all things tech. The Nasdaq 100 has gained for 
five straight sessions and is up 22% this year.

But as Muse and rivals improve at price comparison, trip booking and dealing 
with customer service, industries that rely on recurring bills, negotiable 
pricing and add-ons could come under pressure, Goldman traders said.In other 
words, AI is a fix for consumer inertia, the tendency to keep buying something 
out of habit even when a better alternative exists.

Financial shares fell yesterday, with JPMorgan and Wells Fargo dropping more 
than 3%. Allstate and Charles Schwab finished around 6% lower. Travel company 
Booking Holdings slipped 2.6%. Planet Fitness sank 9.5%.

Of course, we’ve seen this reaction before, notably back in February when 
Citrini Research published a report onAI’s disruptive possibilities 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-24/citrini-report-author-calls-for-ai-tax-after-scare-trade-selloff?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>
 and startups rolled out apps forservices such as tax planning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-10/wealth-manager-stocks-sink-as-new-ai-tool-sparks-disruption-fear?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>
. But this time it feels more real to many investors because the Meta app, 
aimed at the broad consumer market, has been a hit.

Muse is “no doubt a negative for those kinds of companies,” said Rhys Williams 
at Wayve Capital Management. “Right now it’s more of a curiosity, but I think 
two years from now we’re all going to have agents.” —Henry Ren 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AU9jKkMZcro/henry-ren?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>

Value at 5% yields

Pimco, one of the biggest investors in the bond market, says long-term US 
Treasury yields above 5% 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/pimco-trims-underweight-on-long-us-bonds-as-yields-top-5?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>
 are leading the firm to trim back its underweight position on the debt.

A global selloff has sent longer-dated bond yields to near their highest in 
two decades. The firm, which manages about $2.3 trillion, has been bearish on 
long-term US government debt in recent years on fiscal concerns.

“It’s not that we have great comfort in the fiscal situation in a narrow 
sense, but we think at these kind of yield levels, and inflation-adjusted 
yields, there’s now better value,” said Daniel Ivascyn, Pimco’s chief 
investment officer. —Michael MacKenzie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVgVy7_JF34/michael-mackenzie?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>

Word from Wall Street

“Forcing rates down by buying bonds is like a doctor applying an ice pack to a 
patient with a fever. The ice pack may lower the patient’s temperature, but the 
patient isn’t likely to get healthy until the underlying cause of the fever has 
been dealt with.”

Howard Marks

Co-founder, Oaktree Capital Management

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s efforts to push down long-term borrowing 
costs areunlikely to work 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/oaktree-s-marks-says-us-displays-total-lack-of-fiscal-discipline?cmpid=092326_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260923&utm_content=9189>
 unless the government addresses the mounting deficit, Marks says.
