Crowded bond markets

The “crowding out” theory in bond markets suggests a torrent of government 
borrowing leaves scant space for companies to raise funds, driving their 
interest rates to punishing levels.

But now that the tech hyperscalers are on a borrowing binge for artificial 
intelligence, hoovering up hundreds of billions of dollars, some investors are 
wondering ifthe opposite is starting to happen 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything?cmpid=081726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7772>
.

Already this year, investment-grade companies have sold nearly $1.5 trillion 
of bonds, a 36% jump from a year earlier.The accelerating AI borrowing spree is 
taxing the market’s ability to absorb so much debt and adding to the pressures 
that have pushed the Treasury’s long-term borrowing costs to a 25-year-high.

“Whoever’s issuing, be it a government or a hyperscaler or a non-hyperscaler 
credit, is now competing with more borrowers,” said Tony Rodriguez of Nuveen 
Asset Management. “And therefore yields have to be higher.” —Davide Barbuscia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AYDn3Y9NjKY/davide-barbuscia?cmpid=081726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7772>

Protecting against perfection

In a market priced for perfection, it may be the ideal time to put on hedges 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/chasing-this-rally-boosts-the-need-for-protection-taking-stock?cmpid=081726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7772>
.

Global stock indexes are chalking up fresh records after the earnings season 
confirmed just how much companies are prospering, with positive economic news 
giving further support. In the US, the latest inflation data showed easing 
price pressures, removing expectations of an imminent Fed rate hike.

And yet, the bond market hasn’t given the all-clear.

“The Goldilocks-ish shift in macro data should be supportive, especially for 
fixed income, as pressure on the Fed to tighten further continues to abate,” 
said Barclays strategists led by Emmanuel Cau. “However, bond yields have so 
far remained stubbornly close to recent highs, which may be due to volatile oil 
prices and rates investors staying in wait-and-see mode.”

The VIX, which measures how much volatility investors expect in the S&P 500, 
closed below 15 on a weekly basis in August for the first time this year. That 
suggests an opportune moment to put protection in place. In fact, many traders 
aredoing the opposite 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/-fomo-insurance-drives-options-buying-spree-as-stocks-rally-on?cmpid=081726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7772>
, buying less downside protection and piling into contracts that pay off if the 
market continues to rally.

“With volatility near cycle lows and seasonal risks rising in the 
August-October months, current levels may offer opportunity to add protection,” 
said Bank of America technical analyst Paul Ciana. —Michael Msika 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AP7hp2DanV8/michael-msika?cmpid=081726_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260817&utm_content=7772>
