Daily Brief

Tuesday, October 06, 2026

US

  • ISM Services prices-paid index surged to its highest level since the post-pandemic surge, well above the series' historical average, signaling persistent services inflation ahead of next week's September CPI print.
  • US 10-year Treasury yield breached its 2007 high to reach 5.31%, a 24-year high, driven by intractable services inflation and fiscal concerns.
  • Nvidia approached a $6 trillion market capitalization, up nearly 30% year-to-date, as the Nasdaq-100 set a new record above 31,000 — even as 40% of S&P 500 constituents have declined since the March 30 low.
  • Wall Street trading and investment-banking firms are on track for record earnings above $90 billion this year, with bonuses expected to reach all-time highs.

Economic Data

The ISM Services report for September delivered an uncomfortable upside surprise: the prices-paid subcomponent climbed to its highest reading since the post-pandemic inflationary surge, matching levels last seen during the 2005 Hurricane Katrina period and exceeded only briefly during the 2008 oil spike. Services inflation is proving stubborn, and SocGen's model — incorporating the diesel price transmission lag — suggests September CPI could print at 4.0% year-on-year versus the current consensus of 3.6%. That figure will be released next week and warrants close attention given record diesel prices above $6.50 per gallon and US refineries running at 97% capacity.

Monetary Policy and Rates

The 10-year Treasury yield's breach of its 2007 peak to 5.31% is a meaningful threshold, not merely a headline. Ray Dalio warned on Bloomberg Television that China and Japan — two of the largest foreign creditors — may reduce Treasury demand, citing an adverse debtor-creditor dynamic overlaid with geopolitical tension. That structural concern, combined with a fiscal trajectory where corporate tax cuts have widened deficits, may keep upward pressure on long yields independent of Fed policy.

Equity Markets

The equity market's apparent immunity to rising yields rests on an increasingly narrow foundation. The Nasdaq-100 has doubled the return of its equal-weighted counterpart since the ChatGPT launch in November 2022, driven overwhelmingly by a handful of hyperscalers whose earnings and revenue forecasts continue to rise. Nvidia's robust revenue guidance and record buyback announcement illustrate the dynamic: strong growth at compressed multiples makes the stock appear defensible even in a high-rate environment. The political risk to this configuration — potential redistribution or regulation targeting AI-era profit concentration — may be underpriced as midterm election sentiment sours. ---

Asia

  • DeepSeek closed in on a $12 billion funding round backed by Tencent and CATL; Moonshot AI reached a $50 billion private valuation ahead of a planned Q1 2027 Hong Kong IPO.
  • India's central bank is expected to deliver its first rate hike in nearly four years at Wednesday's meeting, a significant policy inflection for the world's most populous economy.
  • Jio Platforms is seeking a roughly $114 billion IPO valuation, below earlier expectations, in what would still rank as India's largest-ever listing.

Monetary Policy

Governor Ueda's comments at a public appearance Tuesday offered little to disturb prevailing market consensus that the BOJ will hold rates at its October meeting. With inflation dynamics still uncertain and the yen having weakened materially, the central bank appears content to proceed gradually. The contrast with India is sharp: the Reserve Bank of India is expected Wednesday to raise rates for the first time since 2022, a decision that reflects the inflationary pass-through from record diesel prices and a weak monsoon — Maharashtra, India's wealthiest state, has cut water supplies by at least 10% after the worst monsoon in over a decade.

China AI Capital Markets

China's AI funding cycle is accelerating ahead of anticipated public listings. DeepSeek's $12 billion round — anchored by Tencent and CATL — and Moonshot AI's $50 billion valuation reflect sustained investor appetite for exposure to China's AI champions at the private stage. Both companies are targeting early 2027 listings, suggesting a concentrated IPO pipeline in Hong Kong that will test market absorption capacity. Meanwhile, Germany and France are jointly pushing the EU to adopt new tools capable of severing Chinese firms' access to the bloc's $8 trillion goods-and-services market, adding a trade-policy overhang to Chinese technology's international ambitions.

Corporate and Capital Flows

In Japan, a bidding contest for TDK's magnetic-heads business between Toshiba and Seagate — valued at potentially several billion dollars — highlights the strategic importance of HDD supply chain control in an AI infrastructure buildout that is driving data storage demand. TDK is the sole independent maker of HDD magnetic-recording heads, making this acquisition consequential for both suitors and their downstream customers, including Western Digital. ---

Europe

  • German factory orders fell by the most since January, the latest indication that the manufacturing sector's attempted turnaround remains fragile.
  • French student protests expanded to include teachers and unions, with strikes planned at TotalEnergies refineries on October 8 and nationwide industrial action called for November 5, amplifying fiscal and political risk.
  • The euro fell to its weakest level since May 2025, reflecting compounding concerns about French and German fiscal trajectories and political instability.
  • France and Germany jointly proposed new EU trade tools capable of blocking Chinese firms' access to the single market, ahead of EU-China talks in Beijing this week.

Economic Data

German factory orders delivered a sharper-than-expected decline in August, falling by the most since January. The data is difficult to dismiss as noise: it arrives as the Merz coalition struggles to agree on budget consolidation measures, with internal disputes over tax hikes threatening coalition stability. The combination of weak industrial momentum and fiscal paralysis is weighing on the euro area's largest economy. In France, the picture is similarly troubled — a selloff in French sovereign debt has accelerated beyond what markets anticipated, with spread widening reflecting concern over missed deficit targets, political gridlock, and now student-led social unrest that is broadening into a wider labor movement.

Politics and Fiscal Policy

Marine Le Pen's National Rally published a counter-budget Tuesday, targeting a fiscal deficit below 3% of GDP by 2032. The credibility of the savings identified remains contested, but the document forces a market assessment of an increasingly plausible 2027 electoral scenario. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Sánchez of Spain called a snap election for November 29 after parliament rejected a housing plan, adding a third major European political uncertainty to an already crowded calendar. The ECB faces a difficult backdrop: Le Pen called publicly for rate cuts to ease sovereign financing costs, while France's finance minister pushed back, and euro-area September inflation came in above consensus on Friday.

Markets

The Stoxx Europe 600 gained 1.0%, helped by falling oil prices and some stabilization in risk sentiment, even as French assets underperformed. Alstom rose 5.6% after securing a deal to supply 200 train sets to Hanoi's planned urban rail network. ---

LatAm

  • Flávio Bolsonaro won 47.06% of first-round votes versus Lula's 45.16%, a result that materially exceeded consensus expectations and makes Bolsonaro the heavy favorite ahead of the October 25 run-off.
  • Brazil's Bovespa surged to its highest level in dollar terms since 2011 on the election result; the real posted its largest single-day gain against the dollar in four years.
  • The right secured an outright majority in Brazil's upper house and a substantially strengthened position in the lower house, improving prospects for fiscal consolidation.

Election Outcome and Market Reaction

Sunday's Brazilian first-round result constitutes a genuine political surprise. Bolsonaro's margin over Lula — while not commanding in percentage terms — carries historical significance: every first-round leader since Brazil adopted this format has proceeded to win the run-off. Prediction markets had assigned Bolsonaro only modest chances of victory as recently as several weeks ago. The market reaction was unambiguous: the Bovespa's dollar-terms rally to a 15-year high and the real's outsized appreciation reflect investor relief at the prospect of more credible fiscal consolidation. Lula's government had persistently struggled to convince bond markets of its commitment to deficit reduction; Bolsonaro now inherits that challenge along with a legislature more amenable to his stated target of cutting government spending by 1.5% of GDP.

Fiscal Outlook

The legislative sweep matters as much as the presidential trajectory. With the right holding an outright Senate majority, the path to structural fiscal reform — long blocked by fragmented coalitions — appears more navigable, though execution risk remains. TS Lombard notes that fiscal reform prospects, rather than the presidential race per se, have driven the asset-price reaction. Lula's campaign is reassessing strategy for the run-off, with aides acknowledging the need to broaden appeal beyond the left-wing base. At 81, Lula remains a formidable political survivor, but the structural arithmetic is challenging. ---

Energy and Inflation Transmission

The inflationary architecture of the current shock is more complex than 2022's energy crisis in one critical respect: refining capacity has been destroyed, not just crude supply interrupted. Middle Eastern and Russian refinery outages — collateral damage from active conflicts — have helped push diesel above $6.50 per gallon in the US, with American refineries already running at 97% capacity, leaving virtually no buffer against maintenance shutdowns. SocGen's analysis is explicit: if underlying supply does not normalize by year-end, the year-on-year inflationary impulse from diesel may not dissipate until Q3 2027.
