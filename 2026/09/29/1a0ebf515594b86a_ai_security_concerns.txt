Deep Dive: Broken Gas Economics
A liquified natural gas tanker docked at the port of Barcelona..Photographer: 
Angel Garcia/Bloomberg
For decades, Europe relied on a tried-and-tested way to ensure there’s enough 
gas to get through winter: Traders bought when the fuel was cheaper in the 
summer, stored it and then sold at a higher price when needed. But for the 
second year in a row, the economics of that tradehave unraveled 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-29/war-and-lng-shift-breaks-economics-of-european-gas-storage?cmpid=092926_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260929&utm_content=9463>
.

 * The loss of steady year-round Russian pipeline gas following the war in 
Ukraine means Europe has become more reliant on global liquefied natural gas 
supplies — and fears about a tight LNG market in 2025 made European summer 
prices more expensive at times.
 * It has been a similar story this year, made even worse by the Iran conflict 
that has choked off crucial supplies from the Middle East.
 * The flattening of the summer-winter price spread has made it less feasible 
for commercial traders to inject gas into Europe’s vast storage sites, which 
encompass depleted fields, aquifers and even old salt caverns.
 * As a result, the continent is heading into the heating season with 
inventories at their lowest ever for this time of year, raising the risk of 
bigger price spikes this winter. There’s no sign of the problem going away any 
time soon.
