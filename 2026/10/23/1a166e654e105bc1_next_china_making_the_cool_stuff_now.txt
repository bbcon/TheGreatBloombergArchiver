Hi, this is Allen Wan in Shanghai.

Made in China was once synonymous with cheap, sometimes low-quality goods.

No longer.

Many people are familiar with China’s skill in making and exporting electric 
vehicles, solar equipment and an expanding list of other high-tech gear,
production that helps backstop its economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-s-growth-rebounds-to-4-5-puts-annual-target-within-reach?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
.

What’s less well known is that the nation’s household appliances and consumer 
goods are now making their way around the world.

I’ve experienced this transformation firsthand. Two decades ago, seemingly 
everything I owned was American and Japanese. Now? Chinese companies like 
Xiaomi, Midea and many others havewon over consumers like me with their 
combination 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/viltrox-lenses-dyna-shoes-prove-made-in-china-can-mean-qualityhttps://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/viltrox-lenses-dyna-shoes-prove-made-in-china-can-mean-quality?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
 of price, quality, style and functionality.
The Midea booth at a consumer electronics fair in Berlin in September.
Photographer: Krisztian Bocsi/Bloomberg
That helps explain why Made in China is now increasingly seen as a status 
symbol by many shoppers globally. It is challenging established Western brands 
not just in household products but even in stuff like camera lenses, fountain 
pens and running shoes — areas where buyers can be very discerning.

The downside, of course, is that the rapid expansion of China’s exports has 
rankled nations around the world because now they’re worried about their 
manufacturing bases. That led to a trade war with the US, witha truce reached 
last year tested frequently 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/latest/us-china-trade-war?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
.

And tensions with Europe are simmering. This week German Chancellor Friedrich 
Merz, a conservative defender of his nation’s export economy, said the European 
Union will ideally not need to usea tough new set of trade powers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/merz-says-protectionism-is-throwing-a-wrench-at-global-trade?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
 to counter China. He added that the bloc would defend itself as needed.

Despite those frictions, it’s full speed ahead for Chinese companies seeking 
to win profits by selling better products than in the past.

Besides luring buyers through generally lower prices and good quality, Chinese 
brands are trying to be responsive to the latest needs of customers and, in the 
process,attracting plenty of Gen-Z buzz 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-24/-chinamaxxing-boosts-inbound-tourism-in-china-video?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
.

For instance, it’s not unheard of for photography enthusiasts to drop $1,000 
or more on a single Nikon, Sony or Fujifilm lens. What’s new is that 
once-skeptical buyers are now opting for Viltrox, which has gone from the 
budget Chinese alternative to a purveyor of lenses that aren’t just good for 
the money — they’re good, period.

The change can be traced back to the firm stepping up investment in materials 
and manufacturing.

Viltrox, owned by Shenzhen Jueying Technology, also maintains a perpetual 
presence on social media platforms where people talk about camera gear, asking 
how it can improve its wares.

Like many Chinese companies, Viltrox recognizes it has to work hard to earn 
the trust of choosy international customers.
A pair of Dyna Super shoes.Source: Dynafish US via Source: Dynafish U.S.
Social media also played a big role in the success of Dynafish Xiaonian — 
known in the US as Dyna Super — though in a different way.

The Chinese athletic footwear brand became an overnight sensation through word 
of mouth. Despite having no US presence and not much of a website, its $130 
training shoe became a hotly coveted item after chatter on Reddit, where 
glowing reviews from runners appeared. Internet users shared tips on how to 
find the kicks.

Chinese firms are also tapping into a generational divide.

According to Glyn Atwal, an associate professor at the Burgundy School of 
Business in Dijon, France, many younger consumers in the US and Europe lack the 
same kind of attachment to Western brands that older generations have.

Young shoppers are more likely to “see China as being cool, in terms of 
innovation and technology,” he said.

Readying Reform

China’s central bank may be nearing its final step in a three-decade effort to 
reform the interest rate system toward a modern framework seen in advanced 
economies.

The People’s Bank of China will study the possibility of switching 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-signals-plan-for-overnight-rate-to-play-bigger-policy-role?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
 the main tenor of its open market operations to the overnight variety, its 
governor, Pan Gongsheng, said this week — in a shift away from the current 
seven-day benchmark.

The overnight reverse repo rate would then become the monetary authority’s 
primary policy tool, he added.

The move would bring the PBOC more in line with global practices, including 
those used at the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank. In theory, it 
would allow the central bank to be a more effective steward of the economy, 
partly because the overnight market is very big, amounting to trillions of yuan 
per day.
The People’s Bank of China in Beijing.Source: Bloomberg
It could also help push down borrowing costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/pboc-rate-shift-plan-would-lower-borrowing-costs-analysts?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
. Some economists will see the change as basically easing considering the 
overnight rate is lower than the current seven-day benchmark.

This could provide a shot in the arm for the economy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-19/china-s-growth-rebounds-to-4-5-puts-annual-target-within-reach?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
, which is weighed down by lackluster consumer sentiment, a property slump and 
deflationary pressures.

A newspaper backed by the central bank cautioned against interpreting the 
switchover as a rate cut, an apparent effort to tame expectations that this 
will play out as easing. Still, there are signs that’s exactly how the market 
sees it, partly evidenced by 10-year government bond yields this week hitting 
their lowest level in more than a year.

The PBOC’s move is hardly surprising. Earlier this year, it made some tweaks 
to key reports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-13/pboc-s-rising-focus-on-overnight-rate-spurs-talk-of-policy-shift?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
 that put the overnight rate in greater focus.

And the central bank has been fairly deliberate about actually making the 
change 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-20/pboc-faces-multi-month-road-to-new-rate-anchor-analysts-say?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
. The PBOC seems to be rolling out the policy piecemeal, an indication it 
values patience in the matter.

Throughout the reform process, the institution has used a communication style 
that could be described as ambiguous — and that includes this week’s 
developments. For example, while Pan said what the goal of the transition is, 
he avoided giving a timetable.

The central bank is also refraining from revealing the cost of the overnight 
liquidity tool, another sign it may be curbing the market’s enthusiasm.

The government may hope that the reform not only results in a unified, 
efficient policy framework but also makes money cheaper to get, greasing the 
gears of the economy.

The thing is, for that to happen it’ll have to be more transparent with market 
participants than it has been and accept more volatility — not exactly what it 
is accustomed to doing.

Behind the Great Firewall

A weekly look at the big water cooler news in China.

People in China were, like a lot of folks around the world, appalled and 
fascinated by thesometimes-violent protests last weekend in France 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/paris-luxury-boutiques-battered-by-riots-brace-for-more-unrest?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
.

France, it should be noted, holds a special place in the imagination of modern 
China and is a popular tourist destination. Part of that is because Zhou Enlai, 
Deng Xiaoping and other revolutionaries spent time there before rising to 
prominence back home. Deng himself developed a lifelong taste for croissants 
out of the experience.

About 1.5 million Chinese people visited France last year, the most of any 
nation in Europe.

Much of the condemnation of the unrest on Chinese social media was related to 
the plight of a woman named Aline Li, whose cafe/shop in Paris was torched by 
rioters.

A handful of people took to online platforms to criticize the protesters for 
their attack on the immigrant, who could be seen in viral videos with injuries 
to her face.
A demonstrator amid tear gas that police in Paris fired on Saturday.
Photographer: Wayan Barre/Bloomberg
The turmoil in France 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/france-protests-how-fuel-prices-student-anger-are-driving-riots-and-unrest?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
 “is the inevitable outcome of decades of misguided policies, warped values and 
chaotic governance,” said a Weibo influencer with the handle Former HR 
Practitioner, who has some 187,000 followers.

“Amidst this chaos, hardworking Chinese residents in France have emerged as 
some of the most innocent victims and as a poignant symbol of societal 
dysfunction.”

Not everyone was unsympathetic toward the protesters, possibly because young 
people in China are themselves struggling with unemployment, stagnant wages and 
inequality.

“The economic crisis is actually uneven distribution” of wealth and 
opportunity, another influencer told his 3.3 million followers.

It doesn’t appear that Chinese tourists are canceling trips to France in large 
numbers just yet, though some travel agents have reported an uptick in tour 
groups asking that the nation be skipped as part of a European itinerary or 
that sightseeing in Paris be cut back.

That could change, though, considering large demonstrations are set for 
Saturday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/paris-police-ban-protests-in-some-high-profile-areas-on-thursday?cmpid=BBD102326_CN&utm_campaign=china&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10403>
. Unions in France are also planning to take action early next month.
