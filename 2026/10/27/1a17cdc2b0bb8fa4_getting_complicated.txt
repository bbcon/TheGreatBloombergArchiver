Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado’s return home complicates a 
political arrangement that had been working surprisingly well for Donald Trump.

Since the US capture of Nicolás Maduro in January, Washington has dealt with 
acting President Delcy Rodríguez while overseeing the opening of Venezuela’s 
oil industry.

Machado has largely been outside that process.

Now she’s back from exile 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/venezuela-maria-corina-machado-s-return-fuels-hope-for-shift-to-democracy?cmpid=BBD102726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10614>
, welcomed by thousands of supporters and pressing for elections.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio says the conditions that allowed Machado to 
return safely were the result of US-backed reconciliation talks.

But her presence also creates a problem for the US. Unlike Rodríguez, Machado 
has political legitimacy that doesn’t come from Trump.

Under Rodríguez, the administration has been able to strike a sweeping oil 
deal on favorable terms 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/pentagon-is-in-talks-with-venezuelan-mogul-for-massive-oil-deal?cmpid=BBD102726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10614>
. Machado has accused the businessman at the center of that accord, Alejandro 
Betancourt, of “obscene corruption,” suggesting she wouldn’t work with him.

Her return adds pressure both for a faster electoral timetable and for greater 
scrutiny of economic deals the US has struck while the transition remains 
unresolved.
Machado greets supporters after her arrival in Venezuela on Sunday.
Photographer: Carlos Becerra/Bloomberg
Washington yesterday laid out its clearest election timetable yet 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/us-maps-path-for-venezuela-to-hold-fresh-presidential-election?cmpid=BBD102726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10614>
, although no date has been set.

Demonstrating progress toward democracy might help Trump at the US midterms in 
places like Florida, where Venezuelan politics matter.

But there’s little incentive to rock the boat on an arrangement that has 
delivered growing US influence over Caracas and the Venezuelan oil sector.

Machado has had to navigate that tension carefully, courting Trump while 
increasingly distancing herself from the US’s embrace of an authoritarian 
government she has spent her career opposing.

Now one important source of leverage over her — access to her Venezuelan 
homeland — has disappeared.

That’s bad news for Rodríguez, too.

Small wonder that she used rare remarks to reporters 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-26/venezuela-s-regime-says-machado-must-repent-and-avoid-spectacle?cmpid=BBD102726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261027&utm_content=10614>
 yesterday to advise Machado to show “regret, remorse — and a change of course.”
