• Commercial cargo crossings through the Strait of Hormuz remain severely depressed; supertanker daily earnings on the Middle East-to-China route have risen more than sixfold since the Iran conflict began, to approximately $1.37 million.

**Hormuz Disruption and Inflation Pass-Through**
The Strait of Hormuz conflict continues to generate material supply chain stress beyond crude oil. Commercial cargo crossings have collapsed, and freight costs — while below pandemic peaks — remain elevated, with Bank of America data showing small-business freight payments running approximately 12.7% above year-ago levels, nearly four times the rate for mid-sized firms. ISM services prices-paid reached a four-year high in September. Import-sensitive US inflation categories including apparel and consumer appliances have posted sharp increases following two years of deflation. Bloomberg Intelligence expects elevated freight rates to persist through year-end, with downward pressure possible only if new vessel deliveries and a Suez Canal normalization materialize in 2026.

**Geopolitics and Monetary Policy**
Trump's decision to waive Russia sanctions, framed publicly as supporting Ukraine peace talks, appears primarily driven by midterm electoral concerns: diesel prices have put Republican Senate seats at risk. Putin reportedly proposed a three-way summit with Trump and Xi to discuss Ukraine. Separately, China disclosed its most advanced nuclear submarines conducted an ICBM test-fire in the Pacific — the first public acknowledgment of such a deployment. On monetary policy, ECB Governing Council member Sleijpen indicated colleagues are preparing for a December rate hike, while separately warning that the 1.5°C Paris Agreement warming limit is "almost out of reach."
