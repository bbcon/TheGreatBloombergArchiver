**AI Sector and Credit Stress**
Nvidia's earnings, the first since CEO Jensen Huang's resignation amid a scandal involving alleged export control violations, represent the week's pivotal corporate event. With revenue potentially 20% linked to China and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly demanding a "dramatic" leadership overhaul, the company enters results under acute institutional and regulatory scrutiny. The broader AI trade has weakened materially: six major technology firms now account for 9.7% of duration-times-spread risk in the US investment-grade corporate bond market — exceeding the combined contribution of the six largest banks at 7% — and credit default swap costs for Oracle and SpaceX have more than doubled in recent months, suggesting investors are increasingly questioning the return profile of the AI capital expenditure cycle.

**Monetary Policy and Rates**
October macro data, the specifics of which prompted a renewed rise in Treasury yields last week, appear to have cemented expectations for a December Fed rate hike. The 10-year yield stood near 5.13%. Rising financing costs raise the hurdle rate for successive rounds of data center investment and may slow the pace of corporate bond issuance that has been reshaping the IG credit market. The Fed's own research finds AI's productivity effects remain uncertain, while its inflationary impact via capital spending is already measurable in GDP and price data.
