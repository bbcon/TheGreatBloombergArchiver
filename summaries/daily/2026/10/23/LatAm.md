**Brazil: Election in the Balance**
The first-round result, in which Lula finished behind Flávio Bolsonaro, appears to have caught the incumbent off-guard and prompted a visible shift in campaign tactics, including Lula's adoption of a smartphone — symbolically freighted given the role of social media in Brazilian electoral politics. With polling at exactly 50-50, the outcome of Sunday's runoff is genuinely uncertain. The result carries significant implications for Brazilian fiscal policy, commodity sector regulation, and the country's posture on trade and foreign investment. Markets will likely see elevated volatility around the result.

**Argentina and Regional Dynamics**
The Buenos Aires water utility deal, valued at $340 million, represents the clearest evidence yet that Milei's privatization agenda is moving beyond rhetoric. Bloomberg Economics notes that Argentina's macroeconomic performance since Milei took office has surprised to the upside relative to most forecasts, though vulnerabilities remain. Chile faces a near-term headwind from El Niño-driven storms disrupting copper output — a development with potential global commodity market implications given Chile's dominant share of world copper supply. Turkey's rate cut, while geographically outside the region, reflects a broader emerging-market theme of central banks beginning cautious easing cycles as inflation recedes from its post-war peaks.
