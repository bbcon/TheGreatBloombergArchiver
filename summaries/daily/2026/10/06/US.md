**Economic Data**
The ISM Services report for September delivered an uncomfortable upside surprise: the prices-paid subcomponent climbed to its highest reading since the post-pandemic inflationary surge, matching levels last seen during the 2005 Hurricane Katrina period and exceeded only briefly during the 2008 oil spike. Services inflation is proving stubborn, and SocGen's model — incorporating the diesel price transmission lag — suggests September CPI could print at 4.0% year-on-year versus the current consensus of 3.6%. That figure will be released next week and warrants close attention given record diesel prices above $6.50 per gallon and US refineries running at 97% capacity.

**Monetary Policy and Rates**
The 10-year Treasury yield's breach of its 2007 peak to 5.31% is a meaningful threshold, not merely a headline. Ray Dalio warned on Bloomberg Television that China and Japan — two of the largest foreign creditors — may reduce Treasury demand, citing an adverse debtor-creditor dynamic overlaid with geopolitical tension. That structural concern, combined with a fiscal trajectory where corporate tax cuts have widened deficits, may keep upward pressure on long yields independent of Fed policy.

**Equity Markets**
The equity market's apparent immunity to rising yields rests on an increasingly narrow foundation. The Nasdaq-100 has doubled the return of its equal-weighted counterpart since the ChatGPT launch in November 2022, driven overwhelmingly by a handful of hyperscalers whose earnings and revenue forecasts continue to rise. Nvidia's robust revenue guidance and record buyback announcement illustrate the dynamic: strong growth at compressed multiples makes the stock appear defensible even in a high-rate environment. The political risk to this configuration — potential redistribution or regulation targeting AI-era profit concentration — may be underpriced as midterm election sentiment sours.
