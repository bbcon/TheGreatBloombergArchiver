The big takeaway from last week’s Washington summit between Donald Trump and 
Xi Jinping was that it was heavy on pageantry and light on substance.

That’s invited all sorts of comparisons and analysis.

Stephen Roach, the former Morgan Stanley Asia chairman, dubbed it 
<https://stephenroach.substack.com/p/trumps-vanity-summit?utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web>
 “America’s first vanity summit.” Hoover Institution visiting fellow Matt 
Turpin turned to Victor Hugo and theawkward significance 
<https://chinaarticles.substack.com/p/victor-hugo-makes-an-appearance-the?r=zlx1&utm_medium=ios>
 of the Les Misérables songs performed at the state dinner. Author and analyst 
James Crabtreeinvoked 
<https://jamescrabtree.substack.com/p/a-mafia-boss-theory-of-trump-xi-meetings?utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web>
 Goodfellas and Wolf Hall. Richard Fontaine of the Center for a New American 
Securityobserved <https://lnkd.in/p/g8cUi3HH> there were “more military 
flyovers than substantive agreements.”

Wendy Cutler with the Asia Society Policy Institute had a more blunt 
<https://www.linkedin.com/posts/wendy-cutler-8584202a4_finally-a-fact-sheet-on-outcomes-from-trump-share-7509590389536784385-ySOe/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAQhrDgB0bYwZGt1gaWdmMK9EIR1HhRFMEU>
 assessment: “The totality of outcomes on trade and investment are 
underwhelming to say the least.”

What really matters though is what comes next and the developing context. The 
two leaders are expected to meet twice more before year’s end. So here are four 
things to consider as we enter the final quarter of 2026 this week.

The first is the US political calendar. It and the politics of the economy 
hang over everything including these trade wars.

Consumer Products

The Trump administration has promised more tariffs as part of its Section 301 
investigation in industrial overcapacity in China, the EU and other major 
economies. But with affordability on the agenda and Democrats actively trying 
to exploit the unpopularity in polls of Trump’s tariffs, is now really the time 
to roll more out taxes?

A list <> of recommended products for post-summit tariff relief released late 
Sunday focused heavily on Chinese-made consumer products from fireworks to 
microwave ovens, billiard balls, fish hooks and high chairs. China, in turn, 
would lower duties on US goods including agricultural items and coal. (To see 
both lists,click here 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-28/here-s-the-list-of-goods-the-us-china-agreed-on-for-tariff-cuts?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
.)

Read More: Trump-Xi Summit Yields Coal Pledge as Tariff Talks Extend 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-26/us-says-china-to-buy-10-million-tons-of-coal-in-2027-and-2028?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>

And if Trump’s Republicans suffer the losses now being predicted by pundits, 
what might that do to the economic and trade agenda? Or the way other countries 
including China respond to Trump?

The second — and it could really be the first — is the bond market.

As Bloomberg’s John Authers wrote on Friday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-09-25/the-big-one-is-rumbling-in-the-bond-market?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
 under the headline “The Big One is Rumbling in the Bond Market,” the surge in 
yields on long government bonds hitting levels not seen in decades offers an 
ominous signal. And one that feels bigger in consequences than the moment after 
“Liberation Day” last year when Trumppaused his tariffs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-09/trump-pauses-higher-duties-on-most-nations-hikes-china-tariffs?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
 because markets “were getting a little bit yippy.”

Pressure Points

 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-09/trump-pauses-higher-duties-on-most-nations-hikes-china-tariffs?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
Thethird is all the below-radar actions the US and China are taking against 
each other. Or what Ryan Hass of the Brookings Institutiondescribed 
<https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/21/business/economy/trump-xi-china-trade.html> 
to the New York Times as the “shin-kicking under the table.”

Think of the placing of prominent Chinese companies like Alibaba on Pentagon 
blacklists. Or thetight control 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/china-rare-earth-magnet-exports-to-us-drop-before-xi-trump-meet?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
 Chinese authorities are still applying to exports of rare earths. There are 
dozens of ways in which the two sides can apply pressure on each other or 
miscalculate. And plenty of people in either camp would like to see more action.

The fourth goes back to politics and the midterms. It’s the escalation that 
could follow.

Trump already has reportedly flagged 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-26/trump-rejects-iran-s-seven-day-ceasefire-proposal-wsj-reports?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
 a return to bombing Iran after the midterm election.

It seems unlikely that he would escalate the fight with China at the same 
time. Or do so going into a scheduled trip to the Nov. 18-19 APEC summit China 
is hosting or the G20 leaders gathering he is hosting a little under a month 
later in Florida.

Read More: US Policy on Taiwan Is Unchanged After Xi’s Visit, Perdue Says 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-27/us-policy-stance-on-taiwan-unchanged-after-xi-visit-perdue-says?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>

But Trump, as we all know, is unpredictable and he just might. Or, he could 
wait until the trade truce with China extended last week expires in January 
next year.

For all the warmth and courtesy on display in Washington last week the reality 
is that the pageantry masked tensions rather than solved any. The trade wars 
are still simmering. And over the next few months there’s plenty out there that 
could again raise the temperature.

Shallow river 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/rhine-sinks-to-record-low-in-further-blow-for-river-transport?cmpid=BBD092826_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9324>
 | Water levels in the Rhine River dropped to the lowest on record, further 
hindering transport on a critical shipping route for coal, fuel and industrial 
commodities across central Europe. The water level at Kaub, a critical 
bottleneck for vessels en route to Germany and Switzerland, fell to 4 
centimeters on Thursday. That’s the lowest in records going back to 1880. The 
all-time low marks eclipses the level reached in August, when Western Europe’s 
hottest ever summer depleted waterways across the region.
