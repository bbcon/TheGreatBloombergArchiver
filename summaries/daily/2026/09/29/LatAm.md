**Brazil: Electoral and Market Dynamics**
With the October 4 first-round ballot days away, Brazil's election has been roiled by disinformation — including a fabricated claim that Bolsonaro would strip the country's patron saint of her title, a politically charged allegation in a nation with over 100 million Catholics. The episode reached the Supreme Court, itself already under strain from an unprecedented institutional crisis. Market implications are significant: Capital Economics estimates that Brazilian equities outperformed peers by roughly 20% in the 100 working days following Bolsonaro's father's 2018 victory, and underperformed by 15% after Lula's 2022 win. A Flávio Bolsonaro victory would also raise the probability of US tariff relief — the current 25% US tariff on Brazilian goods would more likely be lifted under a right-leaning government — while a Lula win could weigh on the real despite its year-to-date resilience.

**Chile**
President Kast unveiled a short-term stimulus package equivalent to $1.34 billion, targeting approximately 100,000 jobs through investment, public works, and employment subsidies, as Chile's labor market reached its weakest point in five years. The measures reflect the political difficulty of sustaining market-oriented reform agendas when economic conditions deteriorate for households.
