Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Simon Casey and Brian Eckhouse explore the implications of the Democrats’ 
landslide election win for US energy.

Many Americans went to the polls 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/inflation-backlash-trump-fatigue-powers-huge-wins-for-democrats?cmpid=BBBXT110426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10955>
 on Tuesday angry about energy prices — not just for gasoline, which has surged 
during the Iran war, but also for power, pushed higher in part by the data 
center boom.

Now the Democrats’ dramatic gains in the midterm elections will have important 
ramifications for the country’s energy sector in the months ahead.

Green energy stocks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/global-solar-stocks-jump-as-democrats-hold-early-us-midterm-lead?cmpid=BBBXT110426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10955>
 rallied around the world today on expectations the Democrats’ control of the 
House and Senate will act as a brake on the Trump administration and its 
assault on renewables.

The next thing to watch will be a Senate vote on permitting reform 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-10-12/congress-just-say-yes-to-common-sense-permitting-reform?cmpid=BBBXT110426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10955>
, which could be one of the most consequential pieces of energy legislation in 
years as the country seeks to speed up federal approval of major projects 
ranging from pipelines to power lines.

A first procedural vote is scheduled for Monday, before the bill can advance.
Power lines in Stone Ridge, Virginia. Washington is confronting the fact that 
the US needs enormous amounts of new generation and transmission infrastructure.
Photographer: BRENDAN SMIALOWSKI/AFP via Getty Images
Emboldened by last night’s results, Democrats may seek to renegotiate the 
legislation, which could further delay a bill long sought by both fossil fuel 
and renewable lobbies.

But it’s perhaps at the state level where the election results are garnering 
the most interest from an energy standpoint.

In Georgia, a hub for the data center buildout, Democrats are set to take 
control of thestate’s utility regulator 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/democrats-ride-anger-over-power-bills-to-take-over-georgia-board?cmpid=BBBXT110426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10955>
 for the first time in decades, propelled by voter frustration over rising 
electricity bills.

The same dynamics played out in Texas, which has successfully courted 
hyperscalers to build vast AI facilities.

Gina Hinojosa <https://house.texas.gov/members/3210/biography>’s ouster of 
longtime Texas Governor Greg Abbott was arguably the biggest shock of the 
night. Sheaccused developers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/ai-data-center-boom-is-set-for-upheaval-after-us-election-rout?cmpid=BBBXT110426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10955>
 of “raising our utility rates and depleting our water,” seeking to outflank 
Abbott on the issue even after he paused approvals of new data centers in 
response to public concerns.

Hinojosa’s Texas administration 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/texas-turns-blue-in-stunning-rebuke-of-trump-musk-republicans?cmpid=BBBXT110426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10955>
 now has a mandate to put in place strong protections, something that’s likely 
to slow approvals and become a key focus in the state’s legislative session 
that starts in January, Citigroup Inc. analysts said.

For developers seeking to build dozens of sprawling AI campuses across the 
Lone Star State, Tuesday’s election outcome is their bleakest scenario.

—Simon Casey and Brian Eckhouse, Bloomberg News
