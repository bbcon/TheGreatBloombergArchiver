Deep Dive: Preparing for Doomsday
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/can-andy-burnham-finally-build-uk-civil-defense-with-be-ready?cmpid=112726_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11929>
Tins of soup in a supermarket in Northwich.Photographer: Christopher 
Furlong/Getty Images
Britain is telling households to prepare for the moment everyday life stops 
working. The government’s new “Be Ready” campaign urges people tostock 
emergency supplies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/can-andy-burnham-finally-build-uk-civil-defense-with-be-ready?cmpid=112726_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11929>
 as storms, power outages, cyberattacks and geopolitical tensions expose 
vulnerabilities in the country’s infrastructure.

 * Be ready: Britons are being advised to keep three days of bottled water and 
non-perishable food, plus a torch, batteries, radio and cash. The three-year 
campaign is intended to bring the UK closer to European countries that have 
spent years strengthening civil defense.
 * Ill prepared: Just 13% of households say they’re largely or totally 
prepared for an emergency, while a quarter say they could survive no more than 
a day if a power cut also knocked out gas and water. A parliamentary committee 
has warned the country remains underprepared for major crises.
 * Preppers go mainstream: Demand for emergency gear is already rising. One 
specialist retailer sold 1,400 wind-up radios after the guidance was published, 
compared with just eight to 10 previously, as preparedness attracts more 
mainstream customers.
