Daily Brief

Tuesday, September 22, 2026

US

  • No major US macro data releases crossed the tape today; market focus centered on geopolitical diplomacy, commercial real estate stress, and corporate developments.
  • US CMBS delinquency rate reached 12% in August, near a record and above post-2008 crisis levels, as lenders lose patience with the office recovery.
  • S&P 500 futures were little changed near 7,837, with 10-year Treasury yields holding at 4.92%, reflecting a market in a holding pattern ahead of the Xi-Trump summit.
  • AMD crossed $1 trillion in market cap for the first time; Meta surged 11% on strong reception for its Muse AI agent.

Commercial Real Estate

The US commercial real estate sector continues to deteriorate. The CMBS delinquency rate reached 12% in August — near a record and above levels seen in the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis — as more than six years of post-pandemic office vacancy erode lender patience. The Atlanta Fed's Survey of Business Uncertainty, drawn from over 1,100 C-suite executives, found that roughly three-quarters of firms receiving tariff refunds plan to hold proceeds as cash, with slightly more than half earmarking funds for R&D and capital investment. Fewer than 15% indicated plans to lower prices, suggesting limited near-term pass-through to consumers.

Equity Markets

US equity futures pointed to a muted open. Nvidia's forward price-earnings multiple has compressed to below 17 times expected earnings, roughly half its 2025 level, as investors weigh whether current earnings power is sustainable amid rising competition and chip costs. Analysts still expect Nvidia earnings to double this year, leaving bulls to argue the valuation offers an attractive entry point. Separately, digital assets surpassed $3 trillion in aggregate market value for the first time since January, though Bitcoin remains well below its October record, with the rally appearing driven more by risk-appetite momentum than structural demand. ---

Asia

  • Alibaba unveiled the Zhenwu V900 AI accelerator, which it claims triples the performance of its predecessor and is designed to compete directly with Nvidia; Alibaba and Tencent shares rose in Hong Kong.
  • Asian equity markets held modest gains — MSCI Asia-Pacific up 0.5% — though technology names including Samsung and SK Hynix gave back early advances of 3.5%, reflecting continued caution after recent volatility.
  • Chinese and US officials concluded a second day of preparatory talks in New York, seeking to advance negotiations on AI, investment, and trade ahead of President Xi Jinping's visit to the US.
  • Vietnam's President To Lam said his country is "very close" to a trade deal with the US, pledging to purchase more high-tech goods to narrow the bilateral trade gap.

Xi-Trump Summit Preparations

Markets adopted a holding pattern ahead of the Xi-Trump summit, with traders reluctant to build new positions before the outcome is known. Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng's delegation wrapped up two days of talks at JPMorgan headquarters in New York, covering AI governance, investment flows, and trade. A persistent irritant remains Washington's Pentagon blacklist targeting major Chinese firms, which the US has shown no indication of softening. How this issue is handled may prove consequential for broader technology and investment flows between the two economies.

Corporate and Capital Markets

Alibaba's chip announcement underscores China's accelerating effort to reduce dependence on foreign semiconductor suppliers. SoftBank drew over $20 billion of preliminary demand for what appears to be one of the largest junk bond deals on record, with proceeds earmarked to fund its OpenAI investment. Deutsche Bank noted that Chinese wealth inflows into Hong Kong are driving the city's private banking outperformance relative to other emerging market hubs, reinforcing Hong Kong's position as the world's largest offshore wealth center after recently surpassing Switzerland. ---

Europe

  • UK public borrowing overshot forecasts in the first five months of the fiscal year, complicating Chancellor John Healey's budget planning.
  • Italy's 2025 fiscal deficit was confirmed at 3.1% of GDP, a setback for Prime Minister Meloni's government, which had anticipated a downward revision.
  • The Stoxx Europe 600 gained 1.0%, lifted by the broader AI-driven equity rally, though momentum in technology names faded through the session.

UK Fiscal Strain

Britain's borrowing overshoot in the April-to-August period represents a meaningful constraint for Chancellor Healey ahead of the autumn budget. The Burnham government is simultaneously evaluating whether to exclude capital spending from fiscal rules — a response to sluggishly advancing housebuilding despite Labour's pledge to deliver 1.5 million homes. The tension between fiscal discipline and growth-oriented capital expenditure is becoming a defining political economy question. Separately, the UK's decision to support Saudi Arabia's military in countering the Houthis marks one of Prime Minister Burnham's first significant defense commitments since taking office in July.

ECB and Energy Inflation

ECB Chief Economist Philip Lane warned that a new energy shock would extend the inflation timeline, a view consistent with rising shipping costs, fuel surcharges, and the broader supply disruption stemming from the Iran conflict. The ECB has previously acknowledged the inflationary impact of weather shocks — a concern reinforced by a summer of extreme European heat that disrupted river transport and agricultural output. France's Macron separately called on the European Commission to relax diesel and kerosene product rules as the Iran war threatens supply shortages, adding political pressure to an already complex energy policy environment. ---

LatAm

  • With Brazil's presidential election two weeks away, candidates are using Javier Milei's Argentina as a proxy for contrasting economic visions, according to Bloomberg Economics analysis.
  • Brazil-US bilateral dynamics remain a subtext at UNGA, with both Lula and Trump facing elections within weeks of each other.

Brazilian Political Economy

Brazil's election contest is crystallizing around competing economic models, with Argentina's Milei-led experiment serving as a touchstone for both sides. The proximity of elections in both Brazil and the US adds a layer of diplomatic sensitivity to bilateral interactions at UNGA. A precedent from last year — when an unscripted behind-the-scenes exchange between Trump and Lula defused a tariff escalation — suggests that informal summit-margin diplomacy could matter here as well, though no equivalent breakthrough has been reported this week. No additional significant macro data releases, central bank decisions, or major market developments were reported for the broader Latin American region during this period. ---

Global

  • The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index rose for an eighth consecutive week to 3,688 — its highest since July 2024 — as Iran war fuel costs, port congestion, and rerouting constraints compress global shipping capacity.
  • Brent crude briefly rose above $101 per barrel before easing, as Saudi Arabia moved to resume flows on its east-west pipeline and diplomatic signals around the Strait of Hormuz improved at the margins.
  • The UK Met Office confirmed 2026's El Niño is the strongest ever recorded, already exceeding prior peaks before reaching its expected maximum.
  • Iran reportedly proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the US blockade is lifted, according to Japan's Kyodo News Agency; Trump indicated openness to meeting Iran's president at UNGA.

Energy and Shipping

The most consequential cross-regional development remains the cascading economic effects of the US-Iran conflict on global commodity and freight markets. Very large crude carriers are now earning over $1.2 million per day on Persian Gulf-to-Asia routes — a level without historical precedent — with earnings trickling down through Suezmax and Aframax classes as cargoes are split across smaller vessels. The Drewry spot rate for a 40-foot container from Shanghai to New York reached $10,394, more than triple mid-February levels. Analysts warn rates could approach pandemic-era records. Saudi Arabia's move to test its east-west pipeline for a potential restart this week, combined with Iran's Hormuz proposal, offers tentative relief, though the trajectory of diplomacy at UNGA remains uncertain.

Climate

The record El Niño confirmation adds a structural dimension to the inflation and supply chain disruptions already underway. European summer heat disrupted river logistics and crop output; Pacific cyclones have caused over 230 deaths and $8.5 billion in direct economic losses in China in July alone. Carbon dioxide removal purchases through August totaled 23 million credits, down sharply from 70 million for all of 2025, underscoring the financing gap between stated climate ambitions and market-driven action. The UN Secretary General framed wars, AI, and climate change as interconnected rather than separate crises.
