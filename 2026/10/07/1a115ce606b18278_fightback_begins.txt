Saudi Arabia and the coalition of forces it backs in Yemen have begun a 
counter-offensive 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/saudi-led-forces-claim-ground-advances-in-yemen-against-houthis?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
 against the Houthis.

The kingdom and its partners say they’ve regained territory near the crucial 
Bab el-Mandeb strait, including the Red Sea port of Mokha. The Yemeni ground 
troops are being backed by dozens of Saudi fighter jets.

Their goal is ambitious — not only to retake land they lost to the Houthis 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/mbs-and-saudi-arabia-face-crisis-with-key-oil-pipeline-shut-for-weeks?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
 since early September, but to “eliminate” the Iran-backed group.

That will be a tough task, especially with US President Donald Trump reluctant 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/trump-resists-deeper-us-role-in-saudi-arabia-houthi-conflict?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
 to support the offensive beyond providing intelligence.

The Houthis and their reclusive leader 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/reclusive-houthi-leader-wreaks-havoc-from-his-yemen-stronghold?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
, Abdul Malik al-Houthi, have withstood more than adecade of bombing campaigns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/why-saudi-arabia-s-aim-of-defeating-the-houthis-is-a-tall-order?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
 from the Saudis, United Arab Emirates, US, UK and Israel.

As the fightback continues, Saudi Arabia is coming under intense fire from the 
militants, who are launching drones and missiles at the kingdom’s cities and 
energy sites. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman will be eager to end that as 
soon as possible, not least to keep his multi-trillion-dollar economic 
diversification plan on track.

Trump is keen for the Houthi conflict to be contained, especially with the US 
military alreadystretched 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-us-iran-war-blockade/?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
 dealing with Iran in the nearby Persian Gulf, including through a blockade of 
the Islamic Republic’s ports.

That naval operation is ensuring Iran can barely export oil 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-03/pressure-builds-on-iran-as-trump-expands-us-military-presence?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
, thus choking its economy. The White House hopes that will end a deadlock in 
US-Iran diplomacy and force Tehran to restart peace talks.

There’s no obvious sign of that yet. Iran has even ramped up shipping attacks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/iran-ramps-up-ship-attacks-in-hormuz-as-oil-and-gas-flows-climb?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
 in the Strait of Hormuz in recent days, perhaps trying to put pressure on 
Washington through higher energy prices.

Between its disruption of Hormuz and its proxies in Yemen, Iran can still 
cause the US, and ally Saudi Arabia, plenty of pain. — Paul Wallace 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASAuUIp54ZQ/paul-wallace?cmpid=BBD100726_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261007&utm_content=9821>
