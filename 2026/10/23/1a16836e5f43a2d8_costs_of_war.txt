The two main architects of the war on Iran are preparing to face voters for 
the first time since the start of the conflict in February.

Neither would have expected to still be engaged in a confrontation that 
continues to roil energy markets and may well be about to escalate.

Israelis go to the polls on Tuesday, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu 
and his far-right allies facing theirstrongest challenge 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/ex-general-who-lost-son-in-gaza-war-emerges-as-netanyahu-s-main-rival?cmpid=BBD102326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10465>
 from a bloc led by former general Gadi Eisenkot.

Polls suggest none of the groups will get a majority in parliament, which 
could mean weeks ofhorse-trading 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/israel-election-2026-will-trump-endorsed-netanyahu-be-reelected?cmpid=BBD102326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10465>
, or even another election, before someone emerges strong enough to form a 
government.

In Israel, unlike in the US, the war against Iran was broadly popular. What has
hurt Netanyahu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-19/netanyahu-risks-israel-election-loss-but-his-iran-gaza-policies-will-continue?cmpid=BBD102326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10465>
 is that it’s turned into a months-long battle with no end in sight and the 
fact the Islamic Republic, the theocratic government in Tehran, has survived.

For Donald Trump, it’s been a political disaster 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/us-pulls-diplomats-from-saudi-as-houthi-iran-tensions-worsen?cmpid=BBD102326_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10465>
. Fuel prices are soaring and his popularity among American voters has dived.

The US president won’t be on the ballot for the Nov. 3 midterm elections, but 
if his Republican Party loses the House of Representatives and, especially, the 
Senate, his decision to start the Iran war will be one of the main reasons.

Whatever voters decide, fears of a new US-Israeli bombing campaign are 
growing. Trump has threatened more strikes, perhaps soon after the midterms, 
and Netanyahu has, in recent days, vowed to achieve regime change in Iran.

And Finally

Prime Minister Narendra Modi wants more freight shipped on India’s new 
US-style freeways as part of his campaign to build a developed economy by 2047. 
Better, wider roads would boost economic activity by decreasing fuel use and 
vehicle wear, while enabling faster delivery. But truckers’ preference for the 
older, slower and busier routes, partly for the facilities they offer, shows 
how the new facilities aren’t always aligned with the realities on the ground.
