Deep Dive: Poland’s Economic Fragility
Poland’s Prime Minister Donald Tusk and Poland’s President Karol Nawrocki
Photographer: Wojtek Radwanski/AFP
Marek Piechocki’s rise mirrors Poland’s own transformation. He started out 
selling imported sweaters as communist rule ended in 1989, before going on to 
build one of the nation’s biggest retailers. Over the same time period, Poland 
has gone from empty grocery-store shelves to a thriving $1 trillion economy. 
But as Poland prepares to rub shoulders with the Group of 20 elite in Florida 
next month, its vulnerabilities arebecoming clearer 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-26/poland-s-1-trillion-economy-shows-weak-spots-as-tusk-nawrocki-feud-play-out?cmpid=112626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261126&utm_content=11894>
.

 * Looming in the background are deteriorating public finances and a political 
crisis that has embroiled the central bank and Constitutional Court as Prime 
Minister Donald Tusk feuds with President Karol Nawrocki.
 * Corporate Poland warned that decades of trust built with investors could be 
diminished “in a matter of weeks” because of the power struggles, with S&P 
Global Ratings signaling it may follow Moody’s and lower Poland’s credit rating.
 * Polish companies are now increasingly looking to invest abroad after 
outgrowing what is now a shrinking economy. The country’s position as Europe’s 
leader in outsourced IT, finance and human resources jobs is now under threat 
as those positions are vulnerable to the AI boom.
