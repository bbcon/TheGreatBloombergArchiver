No patience

Investors aren’t loving the latest round of tech earnings. Heading into the 
earnings season, the big question was whether AI spending was producing 
results. The answer today might be no.

Here’s a look at the latest:

 * Tesla said it was pulling back its spending plans 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/tesla-s-third-quarter-earnings-beat-wall-street-estimates?cmpid=102226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10413>
, which could slow efforts to transform the carmaker into an AI and robotics 
company.
 * Weaker-than-expected guidance from chip-equipment maker Lam Research sent 
its shares more than 9% lower.
 * Meta is an exception. CEO Mark Zuckerberg said that AI investments are 
“paying off.” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/meta-boosts-bet-on-ai-with-new-spending-outlook-stock-offering?cmpid=102226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10413>
 The company boosted its spending plans.
In recent quarters, much of the attention has been on capital expenditure, 
which is one of the few ways that investors have to measure progress on AI. But 
increasingly, the focus is on monetization, according to Wei Li of BlackRock.

“The bar has risen with yields repricing higher,” she told Bloomberg 
Television. “Markets can get impatient. They want to see signs of revenue, new 
revenues being created, signs of monetization that can take time.”

With Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon not reporting results until next week, 
patience seems to be in short supply.

“The market is still willing to reward heavy AI investment, but it is becoming 
much more demanding about the returns on that spending,” said Santiago Mateo of 
CaixaBank AM. “Capex alone is no longer enough.”

With sentiment souring, investors may turn to Apple for relief 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-22/apple-s-cash-flow-machine-makes-it-investors-favorite-ai-hedge?cmpid=102226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10413>
. That’s because the iPhone maker is largely sitting out the AI race. Apple’s 
stock is up 27% in 2026, making it by far the best performer among the 
Magnificent 7. —Charles Riley

Nowhere to hide

Money managers are scrambling to prepare their portfolios 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/money-managers-say-there-s-nowhere-to-hide-as-super-el-nino-forms?cmpid=102226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10413>
 for what threatens to be the strongest El Niño weather cycle in history.

The climate phenomenon is already fanning crop-damaging droughts and floods. 
Food commodity prices are rising, and economists are revising estimates for 
inflation and deficits.

“It’s become almost a full-on certainty that this is going to be the most 
extreme El Niño we’ve had in history,” and figuring out how to navigate the 
disruptions ahead is now a priority, said Katie Self of Pictet Asset Management.

Man Group has warned that everything from grid infrastructure to fertilizer 
could be in El Niño’s crosshairs. T. Rowe Price has turned to custom-made 
models to protect its $165 billion portfolio of emerging-market debt and 
equities. Hedge funds are building entire investment cases around the weather 
cycle.

“There is not a single place in the world that will not feel some kind of a 
knock-on effect from El Niño,” said Self.—Ishika Mookerjee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJMYcOXODs/ishika-mookerjee?cmpid=102226_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261022&utm_content=10413>
