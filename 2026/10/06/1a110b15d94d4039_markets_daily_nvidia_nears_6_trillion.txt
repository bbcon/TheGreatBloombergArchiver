Nvidia is back

The AI frenzy is alive and well, putting Nvidia on the verge of becoming the 
first company with a $6 trillion market value 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/nvidia-heads-for-6-trillion-value-with-chipmaker-back-at-record?cmpid=100626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9713>
.

The stock is once more at a record high after the company gave a robust 
revenue outlook and announced the biggest buyback in history, which takes 
advantage of a valuation that’s near multi-year lows. Those twin pillars — 
strong growth and a cheap multiple — stand out, especially as investors grapple 
with high interest rates and tepid economic data.

Nvidia is now up close to 30% so far in 2026, compared with a 14% rally in the 
S&P 500.

The move is particularly striking considering the stock was down 11% for the 
year on March 30 as investors questioned the hundreds of billions of dollars 
being spent on AI infrastructure.Since then, sentiment around the AI landscape 
has flipped, with more existential questions about the potential threats it 
poses to humanity now leading the conversation.

“Nvidia is attractive on both a growth basis and a value basis, and it looks 
like a haven from any damage higher rates could do to the economy,” said Jim 
Awad at Clearstead Advisors, which owns the stock. —Ryan Vlastelica 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AT3hUmpnSRM/ryan-vlastelica?cmpid=100626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9713>

‘A very difficult dynamic’

As if investors needed more gloom on Treasuries, here’s Ray Dalio’s take: 
Bonds are vulnerable toa pullback of demand from China and Japan 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/ray-dalio-warns-china-japan-may-pull-back-from-us-treasuries?cmpid=100626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9713>
, two of the US’s largest foreign creditors.

“The Chinese don’t want to continue to accumulate — there are geopolitical 
issues as well as economic issues,” said Dalio, the founder of hedge fund 
company Bridgewater Associates. “When you have a debtor-creditor relationship 
and you have an adversary relationship, that’s a very difficult dynamic.”
 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-06/dalio-sees-possibility-of-us-debt-crisis-within-3-years-video?cmpid=100626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261006&utm_content=9713>
