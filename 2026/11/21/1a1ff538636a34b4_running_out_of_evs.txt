A hunt for EVs

By Kyle Stock 
<https://origin.www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARKz8x17rNs/kyle-stock?cmpid=BBD112126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261121&utm_content=11706>

A driver keen on a midsize SUV that costs less than the average car and 
doesn’t need gas might land on a Chevrolet Blazer EV. That driver might, 
however, have trouble finding one.

Early this week in Salt Lake City, Utah, for example, there were just five of 
the electric Chevys for sale. Kansas City dealers only had three. And Nashville 
drivers were out of luck entirely, according to the searchable inventory on 
General Motors Co.’s site.
A General Motors Co. Chevrolet Blazer electric vehicle at a dealership in 
Colma, California.Photographer: David Paul Morris/Bloomberg
The American electric vehicle market is regaining momentum after federal 
purchase incentives expired in September of last year. US consumersbought 83,300
 <https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/ev-market-monitor-october-2026/> new 
electric cars and trucks in October, an 11% increase over October 2025 and the 
first such uptick of the year.

However, as interest in electric vehicles climbed in step 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-14/iran-war-reignites-ev-interest-in-us-markets?cmpid=BBD112126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261121&utm_content=11706>
 with gas prices, EV inventory faltered. At a peak last year, American dealers 
had three times as many electric cars and trucks as they have on offer now. 
Without any new supply, US dealers would run out of EVs in a little less than 
three months — sooner than gas cars.

Gil Tal, director of The Electric Vehicle Research Center at the University of 
California at Davis, said American EV appetite is going unmet and remains far 
higher than sales suggest. “You cannot measure demand for a new product if 
you’re not stocking the shelf,” Tal said. “In our case, the shelf is empty.”

When federal subsidies disappeared in September of last year, automakers 
steered away from EVs in the US, sending a crowd of modelsto the scrap heap 
<https://www.caranddriver.com/news/g68920984/evs-discontinued-canceled/>. For a 
few months, they had a glut of unsold cars. After the Iran War began and gas 
prices spiked, they kept production down. Last month, as EV interest continued 
to climb, there were about 65,000 electric vehicles sitting on dealer lots, 38% 
fewer than there were a year ago, according to data from S&P Global.

General Motors, for example, has lowered EV production this year to stem 
losses. As a result, in October, there were roughly half as many EVs for sale 
at GM dealers as there were 12 months earlier. On an earnings call in late 
October, Chief Financial Officer Paul Jacobson called its EV pullback 
“rightsizing.”

Meanwhile, Ford Motor Co. stores no longer have electric pickup trucks, since 
the companyaxed its F-150 Lightning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-10-12/ford-s-axed-f-150-lightning-is-a-surprise-hit-in-the-used-market?cmpid=BBD112126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261121&utm_content=11706>
 in December. Over the past 12 months, about 70% of the company’s EV inventory 
has disappeared.

Meta reviews net zero target

By Olivia Raimonde 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUdOpjWq8Dk/olivia-raimonde?cmpid=BBD112126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261121&utm_content=11706>

Meta is “re-evaluating” its 2030 net zero target amid a rapid infrastructure 
build-out to support artificial intelligence, the company said in its annual 
sustainability report.

The tech giant reported that its net greenhouse gas emissions surged 45% last 
year, to 11.8 million metric tons of carbon dioxide equivalent.

The company’s current goal to reach net zero emissions by the end of the 
decade came about in 2020, before the rapid advance of AI. “When we set our net 
zero emissions goal, we anticipated steady growth, falling clean energy costs, 
and changes in the broader system that would enable progress at the pace we 
envisioned,” two Meta executives, Rachel Peterson and Blair Swedeen, wrote in 
the report. “The reality has been far more complex.”

Demand for AI data centers has grown faster than projected and meeting it 
requires building at a pace and scale the industry has never attempted, they 
wrote. The 45% jump was driven primarily by supply-chain or scope 3 emissions, 
especially purchased goods and services and capital goods, the company said.
