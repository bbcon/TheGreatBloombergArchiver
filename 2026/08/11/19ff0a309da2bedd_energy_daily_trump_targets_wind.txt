Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
reporter Ruth Liao looks at the Trump administration’s efforts to divert 
investments in renewables toward expansions of fossil fuels.

US President Donald Trump has long shown contempt 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-20/trump-says-he-will-end-leasing-for-wind-power?cmpid=BBBXT081126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7592>
 for wind power, dismissing the turbines as expensive eyesores and claiming — 
without evidence — that wind farms cause cancer.

In his second term, he’s followed through on that rhetoric by canceling 
offshore leases, withrefunds to developers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-refunds-4-billion-to-cancel-us-offshore-wind-projects?cmpid=BBBXT081126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7592>
 now totaling $4 billion.

What may be less appreciated is that the cash isn’t simply disappearing into 
investors’ pockets. Much of it is being redirected into fossil-fuel projects.

The latest example came just last week, when German utility RWE AG reached a 
settlement 
<https://news.bloomberglaw.com/environment-and-energy/rwe-settles-with-us-on-offshore-wind-leases-for-1-22-billion>
 with the US government to relinquish leases off the coasts of New York, 
California and Louisiana.

As part of that agreement, RWE pledged to spend $900 million to take a stake 
in aliquefied natural gas 
<https://www.woodside.com/what-we-do/growth-projects/woodside-louisiana-lng> 
export facility being built by Woodside Energy Group Ltd. in Louisiana.
A tanker off Louisiana. Woodside Energy is building a large LNG production and 
export terminal near Lake Charles.Photographer: Bloomberg
That followed a similar deal earlier this year between the government and 
TotalEnergies SE, with the French company getting backalmost $1 billion 
<https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/interior-and-totalenergies-agree-end-offshore-wind-projects-lowering-costs-american>
 and committing to invest in natural gas and LNG projects in the US.

One month after that, Bluepoint Wind, a venture part-owned by BlackRock Inc., 
agreed to a payout forgiving up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-27/trump-cancels-more-wind-leases-to-spur-oil-and-gas-investment?cmpid=BBBXT081126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7592>
 a New York wind development. BlackRock’s Global Infrastructure Partnerssaid it 
<https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/interior-announces-two-historic-agreements-promote-affordable-reliable-energy>
 would direct as much as $765 million into US LNG infrastructure.

Such investments together comprise only a small fraction of the money going 
into US gas. But the settlements are a stark illustration of how the Trump 
administration is diverting funds away from renewables and into fossil fuels.

The agreements add to the momentum behind domestic gas production that’s 
already at record levels, just as Big Techturns to the fuel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-29/meta-s-need-for-gas-power-boosts-entergy-spending-by-14-billion?cmpid=BBBXT081126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7592>
 to meet surging power demand from new data centers.

And LNG gets a boost too, at a time when the Iran war is throttling flows from 
the Persian Gulf and pushing up global prices. The disruption makes US supply 
look increasingly attractive for international customers — a fact that won’t be 
lost on those in the White House.

—Ruth Liao, Bloomberg News

Chart of the day

Oil inventories in Shandong — home to most of China’s “teapot” refiners — 
dropped to an eight-month low in July, according to data compiled by Energy 
Aspects. The refiners are by far the biggest buyers of Iranian crude, and 
shrinking stockpiles mayspur more purchases 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/china-s-teapots-may-boost-iran-oil-buying-as-stockpiles-dwindle?cmpid=BBBXT081126_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260811&utm_content=7592>
, offering Tehran a much-needed boost after sales slowed.

BNEF today

India is lifting South Asia’s LNG imports even as traffic remains stalled in 
the Strait of Hormuz. As of Aug. 9, its cumulative August imports were 131% 
above the same period in 2025, according to BloombergNEF. That’s likely 
supported by war-related disruptions tightening propane supply and pushing 
industrial users to natural gas.
