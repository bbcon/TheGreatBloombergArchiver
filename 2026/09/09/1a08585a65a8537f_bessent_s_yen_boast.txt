US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent appears to be enjoying taking on the 
market — whether in currencies or government bonds.

The former hedge fund executive said he possessed “asymmetric information” when
challenging traders 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-dares-traders-to-bet-against-yen-i-am-the-house-now?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
 to test his resolve on boosting Japan’s currency.

“I am the house now, so when we intervene with the Japanese yen, I have pretty 
good insight into what the Japanese, what the Bank of Japan is going to do, 
what Japanese policymakers are going to do,” Bessent said at a Southern 
Methodist University event in Texas on Tuesday. “And you can bet against me if 
you want.”

So far his efforts to shore up the Japanese currency, which included the first 
purchases of yen by US authorities in three decades, have paid off. Although 
initial gains from theJuly 31 move 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/bessent-s-whatever-it-takes-yen-pledge-masks-limited-firepower?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
 faded, the yen is up 4% this month to around 153.6, thebest performance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-09-09/rising-yen-finally-gets-some-respect?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
 against the dollar among 31 currencies.

Hedge funds are betting 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-09/hedge-funds-bet-yen-will-strengthen-beyond-150-by-year-end?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
 the yen will strengthen beyond 150 per dollar by year-end, with some 
longer-dated options trades targeting a move to 140. There are risks to 
Bessent’s self-confidence: support for the yen partly rests on interest-rate 
hikes by the Bank of Japan, and that pathremains uncertain 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/boj-is-said-to-favor-quarter-point-hike-flexible-on-future-path?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
.

Bessent separately said his move last month to expand a buyback program for 
older US government securities was aimed at quelling a “fever” in the bond 
market. This came after Stanley Druckenmiller, the billionaire investor who 
mentored Bessent in his early career as a hedge fund trader, suggested his 
former pupil ismaking a mistake 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/druckenmiller-bessent-s-early-mentor-calls-bond-buys-a-mistake?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
 by wading into the bond market.

Whether Bessent truly can bend markets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/bessent-s-fever-quelling-debt-buybacks-put-wall-street-on-edge?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8679>
 to his will remains to be seen, but it seems clear he’s prepared to take 
unorthodox measures as needed.
