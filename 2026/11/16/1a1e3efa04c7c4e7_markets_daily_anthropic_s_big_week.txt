Anthropic takes center stage

The moment of truth arrives this week for Anthropic’s IPO, which could be the 
biggest first-time share sale ever. Bankers expect to set a price for the stock 
on Wednesday, with trading to begin Thursday.

To investors, the offering is about much more than one company — it’s 
reassurance that the entire AI trade will endure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-15/anthropic-s-ipo-makes-dario-amodei-the-new-face-of-the-ai-boom?cmpid=111626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11449>
, albeit with a new leader in the form of Dario Amodei, now that Jensen Huang is
out at Nvidia 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-16/the-final-days-of-the-jensen-huang-hype-train-at-nvidia?cmpid=111626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11449>
.

“It’s extremely important because they might be the best indicator for the 
health of the AI ecosystem,” said Angelo Zino at research firm CFRA. “What they 
have to say about monetizing AI, I think that becomes now equally, if not more 
important, than Nvidia with Jensen no longer there.”

Enthusiasm for the deal is palpable: The IPO has drawn orders for close to 
seven times the shares available, according to people familiar with the matter.

Anthropic is trying to raise $90.4 billion (including a separate private 
placement) when tech stocks are in turmoil: Asia’s two chip-heavy stock markets
came under renewed pressure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/nvidia-fallout-anthropic-listing-squeeze-asian-chip-stocks-anew?cmpid=111626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11449>
 today, following weakness in the US triggered by Nvidia’s turmoil. Some of the 
selling in Taiwan and South Korea probably reflects investors making room in 
their portfolios for Anthropic shares.

A few of Wall Street’s biggest bulls are undeterred: Morgan Stanley 
strategists are recommendinga “max overweight” position in global stocks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-16/morgan-stanley-max-overweight-global-stocks-on-intact-ai-cycle?cmpid=111626_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261116&utm_content=11449>
, saying the AI spending boom will make 2027 “the year to embrace risk.” —Phil

The week ahead

Investors will get a read on how the US consumer is holding up in the face of 
persistent inflation, with October retail sales data on Tuesday and earnings 
from a slew of retailers.

In the Tuesday data, sales outside of car dealers and gas stations are 
projected to post another modest advance, indicating a resilient yet cautious 
consumer. Meanwhile, reports Wednesday on housing starts and sales of 
previously owned homes are expected to underscore a property market hamstrung 
by high mortgage rates.

In a relatively quiet week for earnings, the big highlight aside from the 
retailers is Nvidia’s first results since the ouster of its CEO. The lineup:

 * Monday: Tyson Foods
 * Tuesday: Nvidia, Home Depot, Walt Disney
 * Wednesday: Target, Lowe’s, TJX, Palo Alto Networks
 * Thursday: Walmart, Williams-Sonoma, The Gap
 * Friday: BJ’s Wholesale
