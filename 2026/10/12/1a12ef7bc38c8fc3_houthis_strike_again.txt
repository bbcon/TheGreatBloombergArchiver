Six years and billions of dollars later, a global toolkit to help poor nations

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-11/senegal-puts-focus-on-global-bid-to-help-poor-countries-tackle-debt?cmpid=101226_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9960>
. As policymakers gather in Bangkok for theannual meetings 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-10/rich-countries-are-now-on-front-lines-as-world-s-risk-map-shifts?cmpid=101226_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261012&utm_content=9960>
 of the World Bank and International Monetary Fund, the West African nation’s 
framework decision to rework its debt is under scrutiny. Surging global bond 
yields have upped the stakes to make it work better, in case other 
cash-strapped borrowers follow suit.

 * Senegal didn’t want to go this route after watching the process endure by 
Ghana and Zambia following the aftermath of Covid-19. But last month Dakar 
requested a debt treatment under the Common Framework which has been used to 
restructure $45 billion in sovereign debt.
 * “The wall was coming toward us,” Prime Minister Ahmadou Al Aminou Lo told 
lawmakers in the capital, Dakar. “We had to stop.”
 * Senegal says it will use an improved version of the framework to secure a 
fair distribution of relief across creditors that span bond investors, banks 
and official creditors.
