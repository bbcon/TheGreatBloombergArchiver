• Developed-market government bond yields have reached their highest levels in decades, with 10-year Treasuries above 4.75%, driven by persistent inflation, expanding fiscal deficits, and energy price pressures from the US-Iran conflict.
• Global food prices rose in August to the highest since late 2022, compounding existing inflationary pressures across both developed and emerging economies.
• Emerging-market local-currency bonds have outperformed developed-market peers by approximately 5.2 percentage points year-to-date, in a structural break from historical patterns.

**Energy and Geopolitics**
The US-Iran conflict, which began with joint US-Israeli strikes in late February, continues to exert upward pressure on energy prices. US gasoline has hit a record high for September at $4.15 per gallon, and the conflict shows no sign of near-term resolution. Treasury Secretary Bessent has argued that Persian Gulf producers will eventually bypass the Strait of Hormuz, relieving price pressure, but markets appear skeptical: 10-year Treasury yields have risen more than 75 basis points since the conflict began. The energy shock is functioning as a simultaneous supply-side inflation impulse and fiscal cost amplifier across major economies.

**Structural Shift in Sovereign Risk Perception**
The deeper macro story is a potential reordering of the global risk hierarchy. Developed-market fiscal trajectories — the US adding $2 trillion annually, Japan expanding spending, France under pressure — are eroding the traditional safety premium embedded in their sovereign debt. The IMF has formally noted the compression of the Treasury safety premium. Against this backdrop, emerging markets with comparatively disciplined fiscal and monetary frameworks are attracting sustained capital inflows, a dynamic that analysts at Gavekal Research describe as "EMs are the new DMs." Whether this framing proves durable, or reverses sharply if global risk appetite deteriorates, remains the central uncertainty in cross-asset positioning.
