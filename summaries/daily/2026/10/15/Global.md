**Strait of Hormuz and Energy Markets**
The sinking of a vessel in the Strait of Hormuz following a strike by unidentified projectiles marks a further escalation in the disruption to one of the world's most important energy transit corridors. The BIS released research Thursday warning that chokepoint disruptions transmit more broadly through production networks than ordinary supply shocks, and specifically cited the Hormuz situation: oil inventories have been drawn down substantially during the disruption, leaving the market increasingly vulnerable to sharper price increases should transit remain constrained. Brent's retreat on the day reflects the dominant influence of the broader risk-off tone rather than a reassessment of supply fundamentals.

**Global Monetary Policy Divergence**
The IMF meetings in Bangkok crystallized the divergence in global monetary conditions. The Fund's Georgieva called for a "prudently hawkish bias" from central banks and praised Warsh's willingness to act on price stability. Yet the ECB faces a different calculus, with market tightening potentially substituting for further rate increases, while Australia's rising unemployment has already prompted traders to scale back hike expectations. The BIS research adds analytical weight to the view that supply-side inflation driven by geopolitical chokepoints complicates standard policy prescriptions, requiring central banks to assess the persistence and substitutability of specific disruptions before responding.
