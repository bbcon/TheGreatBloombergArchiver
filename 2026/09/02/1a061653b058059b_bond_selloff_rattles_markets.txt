The forces that have buffeted financial markets in recent weeks only seem to 
be intensifying 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8424>
. A bond selloff is deepening as fighting escalates between the US and Iran, 
driving up the price of oil and increasing concern that global central banks 
will need to keep hiking interest rates.

Brent crude advanced for a third day to about $95 a barrel, briefly hitting 
the highest level since July. Bonds fell in Europe and Asia, while 30-year 
Treasury yields hovered around 5.28%, near the19-year high 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/bessent-s-bond-gains-wiped-out-as-30-year-yields-jump-once-again?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8424>
 hit before Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expanded a buyback program to 
containlong-term borrowing costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-18/the-bond-market-is-sending-a-warning-with-rising-long-term-debt-costs?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8424>
.

The relative calm 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/bond-market-selloff-are-surging-yields-a-threat-to-the-global-stock-rally?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8424>
 in stocks is showing signs of wavering: MSCI’s Asian Pacific gauge fell as 
much as 2.1%, its biggest decline in two weeks. Tech shares led declines, 
dragging equity benchmarks in Japan and Korea down at least 2.4%. S&P 500 
futures were down 0.2% after three sessions of losses.

Traders now put the odds of a rate hike this month at more than 50% for three 
major central banks, including around 70% for the Federal Reserve. “The new 
baseline seems to be that the Fed will, after all, hike rates in September,” 
wrote Chris Turner, global head of markets at ING.

The US military carried out its second round of attacks 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/us-launches-new-round-of-iran-strikes-as-strait-tensions-grow?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8424>
 in three days, which US Central Command said targeted radar systems and 
mine-laying capabilities along Iran’s southern coast. This prompted the Islamic 
Republic to retaliate with drone and missile volleys on US bases across the 
Middle East, in line with tactics used throughout the six-month war.

Tehran accused the US of hitting a wedding ceremony during the overnight 
attacks, killing five people and injuring 68. Most of the injured were women 
and children, according to the Iranian Red Crescent Society. Centcom said it’s 
aware of reports but that “the U.S. military never targets civilians,” US Navy 
Captain Tim Hawkins told the BBC.

The European Central Bank will raise borrowing costs

wary on what comes after that. Policymakers have widely telegraphed another 
rate hike next week following a quarter-point move in June.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/boj-s-ueda-to-decide-on-policy-with-upside-price-risks-in-mind?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8424>
