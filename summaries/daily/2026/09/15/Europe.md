**Monetary Policy**
The divergence between market pricing and central bank communication in Europe has widened materially. Swaps now imply four additional ECB quarter-point hikes and five from the Bank of England over the next year — a pace well ahead of current official guidance. The Bank of England faces a particularly acute dilemma: labor market data this week is expected to show continued softness, yet the forecast 25% jump in household energy bills in January will mechanically push headline CPI higher. The ECB's own Vice President Boris Vujcic has separately warned that AI-driven equity valuations leave markets vulnerable to a correction — a concern with implications for the wealth channel and financial conditions more broadly.

**UK Fiscal and Energy**
The Burnham government is navigating intersecting pressures. Rising household energy costs, directly traceable to Strait of Hormuz disruptions, threaten both the inflation trajectory and the government's political positioning on living standards. Separately, an industry lobby group estimates that accelerating the new North Sea fiscal regime could generate an additional £15 billion in tax revenue over the next decade — a potentially significant revenue source, though the timing and design of any reform remain uncertain. UK Prime Minister Burnham is also reported to be weighing whether to provide military support to Saudi Arabia against Houthi attacks, adding a geopolitical dimension to an already complex policy environment.
