It was a day to remember, not just for India’s Ambani family. Jio Platforms 
surged in its debut Wednesday, defying a poor track record among India’s 
biggest share sales and givinginvestors a rare bright spot 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/ambani-looks-to-defy-gravity-in-debut-after-india-record-jio-ipo?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10668>
 in a market that’s experienced a bruising equity selloff in recent months. 
Shares of billionaire Mukesh Ambani’s telecom and digital services giant jumped 
as much as 23% in Mumbai, after it raised a record $3.1 billion.

The blockbuster start also helped Ambani’s empire topple the more 
internationally known Tata Group asIndia’s largest conglomerate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/jio-s-ipo-surge-ends-tata-s-reign-as-most-valuable-india-group?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10668>
 by market value. The combined market value of Reliance Group, which Jio is a 
key part of, climbed to 30.78 trillion rupees ($323.49 billion), surpassing 
Tata Group at 24.86 trillion rupees, according to data compiled by Bloomberg 
News. Tata has claimed the top spot for much of the past decade, the data shows.

Jio’s debut was an emotional moment 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/ambani-sheds-tears-as-india-s-richest-family-hails-jio-debut-pop?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10668>
 for Ambani, 69, as four generations of one of India’s wealthiest business 
dynasties came together to ring the opening bell. The patriarch repeatedly 
fought back tears as his three children took the stage and talked about the 
family’s journey. Ambani built Jio from a telco offering cheap phone Internet 
access to the flagship for his tech ambitions, which includes AI data centers.
Realizing that vision 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-10-28/jio-s-unlikely-making-the-ambani-feud-steve-jobs-and-a-bureaucrat?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261028&utm_content=10668>
 will fall largely to the next generation, and likely test the exuberance the 
stock has seen in its debut.
