As a regional power, Turkey had intended to use its role as host of the UN 
climate summit to raise its international profile. But after COP31 ended 
without an agreement, the government is under fire from opponents over the 
event’s cost, as the country grapples with runaway inflation and a funds crisis 
at home.

COP31 flop cost

By Firat Kozok 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASlXCn5_l-o/firat-kozok?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 andSelcan Hacaoglu 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQ6hs25pQys/selcan-hacaoglu?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>

Turkey spent nearly $700 million to host the COP31 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/latest/cop-climate-summit?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 climate summit, according to people familiar with the expenditure, only for 
the United Nations gathering to end without an overarching agreement after a 
fractious closing session.

About 23.8 billion liras ($470 million) was spent on the summit venue in 
Antalya this month, and on organizing the conference itself, said the people, 
asking not to be identified discussing non-public figures. When spending on 
roads, landscaping and other public agencies is included, the total reached 
about 35 billion liras, or $690 million, they said.



Turkey separately agreed to cover <https://unfccc.int/documents/660706?utm> 
about $7.7 million in additional UN costs associated with hosting COP31 in 
Antalya. A spokesperson for Turkey’s environment ministry declined to comment 
on the figures.
Attendees at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey.Photographer: 
Chris Ratcliffe/Bloomberg
The expense of the summit came under scrutiny in the country’s parliament this 
week as households grapple with a prolonged cost-of-living squeeze, while an 
investment fund crisis is eroding support for President Recep Tayyip Erdogan’s 
ruling AK Party, according to pollsters. Annual inflation remains near 30%, 
while housing and utility costs have risen about 40% from a year ago.

“We are calling for the conference’s total cost, the procedures used for the 
tenders, the companies awarded the contracts and the breakdown of spending to 
be opened to independent audit,” New Party lawmaker Evrim Rizvanoglu told COP31 
President and Environment Minister Murat Kurum at a parliamentary committee 
meeting on Thursday in Ankara.

Kurum defended the scale of the preparations, saying Turkey built a conference 
venue equivalent to 155 football fields and hosted more than 1 million 
visitors, 102 leaders and over 300 ministers. He described COP31 as the largest 
international event in Turkey’s history. Turkey also built new roads and 
upgraded transport links around Belek, a resort area known for its luxury 
hotels, Kurum said.

Read more: COP31 Collapse Shows Widening Fault Lines in Climate Diplomacy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/failure-of-cop31-talks-shows-hard-limits-of-climate-cooperation?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>

“We believe the $2 billion in direct economic activity generated by the summit 
in Antalya will double through the multiplier effect,” said Erdogan, speaking 
after a cabinet meeting on Monday. “In doing so, we have both attracted new 
investment to our country and created lasting infrastructure by strengthening 
Antalya’s capacity to host international events.”

UN climate conferences have grown in size over the last few years, with 
country hosts spending growing sums. The Brazilian government estimated it 
spent 6.12 billion reais ($1.1 billion) on COP30 in Belem, while the UK 
government put its spending at about £436 million ($573 million) on COP26 in 
Glasgow.

Hunt for space

By Srinidhi Ragavendran 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXaNe4_XBAk/srinidhi-ragavendran?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 andIshika Mookerjee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJMYcOXODs/ishika-mookerjee?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>

Since independence, Singapore has continuously expanded its land mass to 
deliver growth and housing in a tiny country. That strategy is now coming up 
against public pushback as a new generation questions the environmental toll.

In recent months, the government announced ambitious plans for more 
reclamation along Singapore’s southern coast for industrial use, as well as 
cutting down more forest for housing. The reasoning for creating more space was 
given by Prime Minister Lawrence Wong at the national day rally in August: “We 
cannot wait until we run out of space. We must create new options for future 
generations.”

The plans sparked an unusually vocal backlash. Some 2,000 people attended a 
rally in August — similar to the turnout for a climate-related gathering in 
2019 that had the highest attendance ever for such an event, according to 
organizers — while apetition 
<https://www.change.org/p/save-maju-forest-build-the-homes-without-erasing-the-wild>
 calling for greater transparency over housing plans and environmental impact 
studies garnered more than 50,000 signatures. A public consultation on plans to 
develop two forested areas drew about 9,500 submissions, with one site 
receiving four times as many responses as a similar proposal five years earlier.
Lawmakers 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/singapore-lawmakers-question-plan-to-bulldoze-forests-for-homes?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 also debated the plans in parliament.
A public housing block with a view of Maju Forest.Photographer: Amrita 
Chandradas/Bloomberg
The opposition comes as Singapore enters a new demographic phase that is 
leaving some citizens more skeptical about re-engineering nature in the name of 
growth. The country of 6.2 million recently became asuper-aged society 
<https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/singapore-now-a-super-aged-society-with-21-4-of-citizen-population-aged-65-and-older>
, and itsbirth rate 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-09/singapore-pivots-from-baby-incentives-as-fertility-rate-hits-low?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 remains one of the world’s lowest. Younger citizens in particular are 
concerned that their fears of a future marred by environmental destruction and
AI-driven job insecurity 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/one-paycheck-no-longer-enough-for-young-singaporeans-in-ai-age?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 may not be fully addressed. The concern is compounded by the recent toxichaze 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/singapore-proposes-tougher-haze-law-to-combat-indonesia-wildfire-pollution?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 that spread over Southeast Asia.

“There’s a growing disillusionment with the notion of endless growth and how 
it often takes priority over environmental concerns,” said Saw Yun Ya, an 
environmental studies student who lives near Maju Forest in western Singapore, 
one of the areas slated for housing construction. “There’s perhaps a sense of 
frustration that there doesn’t seem to be a ‘stop line’ to development.”
People gather in Hong Lim Park on Aug. 16 for a rally against the planned 
deforestation.Photographer: Ann Seojin via Photographer: Ann Seojin @annopano
The plan could wipe out more than half of Maju Forest, home to some critically 
endangered animals, and about 40% of the secondary forest around the Gillman 
Barracks area, at a time when the city-state isheating up 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2020-12-01/singapore-climate-change-reducing-heat-takes-trees-and-technology?cmpid=BBD112726_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261127&utm_content=11948>
 rapidly. The government has designated 2026 as the Year of Climate Adaptation, 
with extreme heat among the biggest challenges it faces.
