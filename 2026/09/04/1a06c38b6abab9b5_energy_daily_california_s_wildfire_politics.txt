Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Power and Natural Disasters Americas Team Leader Brian Eckhouse explores the 
uncertain policy environment keeping California’s utilities in limbo.

A fight over how to apportion the costs of fires sparked by utilities in 
California is intensifying just as the state approaches peak wildfire season.

Despite the backing of Governor Gavin Newsom, power suppliers PG&E Corp. and 
Edison International failed to convince legislators to approvemajor reforms 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/pg-e-edison-international-stocks-plunge-on-california-wildfire-bill?cmpid=BBBXT090426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8492>
 that would have shielded them from insurance claims.

A key reason: An unlikely coalition of fire survivors, insurers and ratepayer 
advocates lobbied against a shift of liabilities that they deemed a utility 
bailout.

Now the state assembly has adjourned 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/california-lawmakers-to-adjourn-without-voting-on-wildfire-bill?cmpid=BBBXT090426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8492>
, prolonging the uncertainty.
Workers from PG&E work to repair and restore power lines in January 2025 in 
Altadena, California, where the devastating Eaton Fire caused widespread damage.
Photographer: FREDERIC J. BROWN/AFP via Getty Images
For California’s biggest utilities, reform is critical: Without it, they say, 
there’s a risk of credit-rating downgrades and thus higher borrowing costs.

Shares in PG&E and Edison plunged Monday after it became clear that 
significant changes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-29/california-lawmakers-file-wildfire-plan-without-big-newsom-asks?cmpid=BBBXT090426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8492>
 weren’t in the offing. PG&E fell again Wednesday after saying it plans to 
defer about$2 billion of investments 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/pg-e-to-defer-2-billion-of-work-as-california-shelves-fire-bill?cmpid=BBBXT090426_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260904&utm_content=8492>
 next year as part of a strategic review.

“We have to take action to protect our customers,” Chief Executive Officer 
Patti Poppe 
<https://www.pgecorp.com/about/officers/corporation-officers/patricia-poppe.html>
 said in an interview. The company can’t be “hampered by the 
sub-investment-grade credit ratings that we currently have because of our 
exposure to this wildfire framework.”

Investors aren’t used to such volatility in utility stocks, and they typically 
loathe policy ambiguity. But they may need to accept more of both, especially 
with Newsom set to leave office in January.

The legislative uncertainty has prompted speculation about whether the 
governor will call lawmakers back into a special session before exiting. Such a 
move, which he hasn’t ruled out, could prove promising for PG&E and Edison.

“Conversations remain fluid and nothing is certain at this point,” said Ryan 
Levine, an analyst at Citigroup Inc. Newsom’s imminent departure leaves the 
“clock ticking.”

—Brian Eckhouse, Bloomberg News
