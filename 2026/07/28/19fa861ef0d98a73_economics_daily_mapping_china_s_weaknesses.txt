Mapping China’s Weaknesses

Germany is preparing for a potential economic showdown with China.

Officials in Berlin are privately mapping Beijing’s choke points — the 
technologies, suppliers and industrial capabilities Europe could use as 
leverage if trade tensions were to escalate.

Read More: Germany Maps China’s Weaknesses in Preparation for Trade War 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/germany-maps-china-s-weaknesses-in-preparation-for-a-trade-war?cmpid=BBD072826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7024>

After years of prioritizing commercial ties, the German government is 
increasingly viewing trade through the lens of economic security.

The thinking is that strategic reliance runs both ways: If China can exploit 
the dependencies of its big trading partners — as it did last year in the
Nexperia dispute 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-09/china-resumes-nexperia-chip-export-urges-eu-to-make-progress?cmpid=BBD072826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7024>
 and withcritical minerals 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/china-is-exporting-20-fewer-magnets-to-us-despite-trade-truce?cmpid=BBD072826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7024>
 — Europe also holds leverage in highly specialized technologies that Chinese 
companies still struggle to replicate.

Semiconductors are among the key sectors under scrutiny. Companies including 
Trumpf, Carl Zeiss, Siltronic, Aixtron and SUSS MicroTec occupy critical 
positions across the global chip supply chain. Germany is also assessing 
whether maintenance and servicing of equipment already installed in China could 
become a pressure point.
Chinese President Xi Jinping welcomes German Chancellor Friedrich Merz prior a 
meeting at the Diaoyutai State Guesthouse in Beijing in February.Photographer: 
Michael Kappeler/AFP/Getty Images
The review extends beyond chips to sectors including chemicals, medical 
technology and other specialized industrial supply chains.

The assessment is part of a broader shift 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/merz-is-inching-germany-toward-backing-eu-push-against-china?cmpid=BBD072826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7024>
 in Germany’s China policy under Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who has adopted a 
markedly tougher stance on economic security. Officials insist the goal is not 
confrontation but to preserve leverage — ensuring Germany and the European 
Union have credible options should Beijing resort to economic coercion again.

Berlin may have been late to recognize 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/trump-xi-summit-to-take-stock-of-china-commitments-greer-says?cmpid=BBD072826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7024>
 that globalization had become an arena for geopolitical competition. It’s now 
trying to catch up, embracing the idea that resilience means not only 
protecting its own supply chains, but also understanding where China’s 
vulnerabilities lie.

Need-to-Know Research

The Iran war has disrupted oil supply much more severely than Russia’s 
invasion of Ukraine, yet prices have risen significantly less. European Central 
Bank researchers attribute this to expectations for a swift end to the 
conflict, stronger pre-crisis market fundamentals and greater flexibility in 
Asian demand.

The oil market entered the current crisis with a supply surplus of about 2.5 
million barrels a day — underpinned by record US shale output and China’s shift 
to electric vehicles, Lea Demuth, Ana-Simona Manu and Arthur Stalla-Bourdillon 
wrote in ablog post 
<https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/blog/date/2026/html/ecb.blog20260727~1212bdb8f9.en.html>
 on the ECB’s website. Inventories were substantially larger than in 2022, fuel 
demand in Asia weaker, and policymakers reacted more forcefully in releasing 
strategic reserves.

“Together, these factors help explain why a much larger supply shock has 
translated into a comparatively muted” price rise, the researchers wrote.

They caution that “conditions in the Strait of Hormuz 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/strait-of-hormuz-fees-how-suez-panama-canal-and-other-waterways-charge-ships?cmpid=BBD072826_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260728&utm_content=7024>
 — and, by extension, on global energy markets — remain highly volatile.” A 
prolonged closure could bolster inflation, while a sustained reopening “could 
exert significant downward pressure on prices,” they said.
