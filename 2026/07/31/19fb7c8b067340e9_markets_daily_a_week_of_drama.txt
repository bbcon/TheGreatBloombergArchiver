Forced Unwinds

Whether you’re a stock, bond or currency trader, it’s been an extraordinary 
week of market drama.

Chief among them, the blowup of Situational Awareness goes down as yet another 
story about the perils of highly leveraged bets in highly volatile markets. As 
we explain in thisdeep dive on the 24-hour race 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/the-24-hour-race-to-salvage-situational-awareness-souring-bets?cmpid=073126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7221>
 to stitch together a deal, the hedge fund was facing margin calls on its 
investments before Citadel’s Ken Griffin swooped in.

Then in Korea, this week took the swings to a whole new level 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/sk-hynix-shares-surge-in-seoul-on-us-peer-rally-chey-purchase?cmpid=073126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7221>
. While markets are surging today, the Kospi had previously tumbled 17% over 
three days in a market stampede out of chipmakers. One individual trader 
succinctly said, “I’ve just been to hell.”

“In a market where everyone is a momentum investor until they become a forced 
seller, those unwinds are never pretty,” wrote Stefan Koopman, a strategist at 
Rabobank. “You take the escalator up, but the elevator down.”

In Treasuries, long bonds took a beating, with 30-year yields hitting the 
highest level since 2007, asinvestors questioned the Fed’s resolve 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/-talk-is-cheap-wall-street-delivers-harsh-verdict-on-warsh-fed?cmpid=073126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7221>
 to tackle inflation. On top of that, Japan decided to wade back into the
currency markets 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/yen-surges-after-japan-intervened-in-market-again-ahead-of-boj?cmpid=073126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7221>
, triggering a more than 2% pop in the yen yesterday.

All told, it adds up to a sense that markets swings are turning more severe in 
every corner of the world.

Interestingly, now that the people who wanted (or needed) to sell have done 
so, themood is starting to calm 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/griffin-s-move-offers-ai-reprieve-as-traders-eye-more-blowouts?cmpid=073126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7221>
. As Rabobank explains it: “Once the position clearing became public, the 
selloff looked less like a systemic event and more like a straightforward 
deleveraging episode. That was enough to help put a floor under markets, at 
least for now.”

Calvin Yeoh, who helps manage the Merlion Fund at Blue Edge Advisors, echoed 
that sense of a reprieve starting to take shape.

“In Korea, all the retail guys got blown out, and no more sellers are there,” 
he said. “It doesn’t mean we rally back. Just means that for now the leverage 
unwind is mostly done.”

For now is the key word there. It’s anyone’s guess what other things might 
break next. (And for a reminder about how weird investing has become, check out 
this story about acompany called Corgi Funds 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/ai-fueled-corgi-chases-blackrock-s-etf-crown-with-500-fund-push?cmpid=073126_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260731&utm_content=7221>
 that’s on pace to launch roughly 500 ETFs.)

“If there is one Situational Awareness, how many of them are out there?” said 
Cusson Leung, chief investment officer of international wealth management at 
KGI. —Lynn Thomasson and Abhishek Vishnoi
