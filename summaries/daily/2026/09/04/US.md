• August payrolls data due today; equity futures held steady ahead of the print, with traders assessing whether the report would give the Fed sufficient cause to hold rates.
• Fed Governor Waller signaled his next rate decision will be "heavily influenced" by August CPI data due next week; remarks pared market bets on an imminent hike.
• US construction spending fell for the 12th consecutive month on a year-on-year basis, down nearly 5% from its February 2025 peak — a soft-side surprise that challenges the AI stimulus narrative.
• High-grade corporate bond yields rose above 5.5%, a two-year high; 30-year mortgage rates approaching 7% as Treasury selloff extends.

**Macro Data**
July construction spending marked the 12th straight year-on-year decline, with the pace of contraction the steepest since the end of the 2006 housing boom when data-center activity is excluded. Data centers account for only 3.5% of total construction spending and carry significantly lower labor intensity than residential building, undermining the administration's argument that AI infrastructure investment will serve as a broad blue-collar stimulus. Manufacturing construction is down roughly 21% year-on-year. Thirty-year mortgage rates are creeping back toward 7%, perpetuating the residential downturn.

**Monetary Policy**
Fed Governor Waller struck a cautiously hawkish tone in prepared remarks before tempering his language in Q&A, saying he expects next week's August CPI print to come in at a "reasonable" level. Markets interpreted the comments as reducing — though not eliminating — the probability of a September rate increase. The signal reinforced a broader dynamic in which Fed communication is becoming a key near-term volatility driver.

**Markets**
S&P 500 futures were modestly firmer ahead of the jobs report. High-grade corporate yields above 5.5% are incentivizing front-loaded debt issuance. Lululemon plunged 18% in premarket trading after cutting its profit outlook for a second consecutive quarter.
