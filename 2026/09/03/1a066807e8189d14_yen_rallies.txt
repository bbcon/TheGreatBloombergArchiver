A rapid gain in Japan’s currency is leaving traders guessing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-02/yen-surges-with-traders-on-high-alert-for-further-intervention?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8485>
 about whether officials were behind the move or whether the risk of support 
measures was enough to buoy the yen.

The yen was up 1.3% at 156.6 per dollar, extending a 0.9% advance. On 
Wednesday a Bank of Japan board member raised the possibility of outsize or 
back-to-back interest-rate hikes. The BOJ is saidto be leaning 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/boj-is-said-to-favor-quarter-point-hike-flexible-on-future-path?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8485>
 toward raising its benchmark interest rate by a quarter point this month, 
while leaving open the possibility of accelerating the pace of hikes thereafter.

The yen move “shows how sensitive positioning has become,” said Nathan Thooft 
at Manulife Investment Management. “The market got the message that the 
authorities want a stronger yen.”

Japan spent a record 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/japan-s-yen-intervention-hits-record-96-billion-in-past-month?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260903&utm_content=8485>
 $96.4 billion over the past month to bolster the yen after it tumbled to a 
four-decade low, according to Finance Ministry data. Japanese officials have 
repeatedly indicated that it is the speed and disorderliness of currency moves, 
rather than any particular exchange-rate level, that is key in assessing the 
need to intervene.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said he expects BOJ Governor Kazuo Ueda to 
“do the right thing” on monetary policy, while describing recent moves in the 
yen as “pretty well contained.”
