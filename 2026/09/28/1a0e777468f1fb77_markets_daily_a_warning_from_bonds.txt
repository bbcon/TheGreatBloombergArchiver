Market warning

The bond market is on the brink of sending a powerful signal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-27/the-bond-market-is-getting-closer-to-sounding-alarm-on-economy?cmpid=092826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9331>
: The US economy is at risk of stalling out.

The extra yield investors demand to hold 10-year Treasuries over two-year 
notes shrank to as little as 0.17 percentage points last week in intraday 
trading. If that small gap disappears, and the 10-year actually yields less 
than shorter maturities, investors will be looking at a so-called curve 
inversion.


An inverted curve historically has offered a powerful signal: It has preceded 
each of the last eight recessions going back to the 1960s, although its 
predictive power proved faulty earlier this decade.

It’s essentially bond investors’ way of showing they see the Fed pushing rates 
high enough to stymie the economy.That would have broad implications for 
financial markets, particularly with stocks near record highs.

“Seeing the two- and 10-year curve invert or flatten dramatically calls into 
question the idea that the economy is very strong and that is part of what’s 
being priced into the bond market,” said Zach Griffiths at research firm 
CreditSights. —Greg Ritchie 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJMWGi29pc/greg-ritchie?cmpid=092826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9331>

The week ahead

Reports on US inflation and jobs could go a long way toward cementing investor 
expectations for another Fed interest rate increase next month.

On Wednesday, the central bank’s preferred inflation gauge, the personal 
consumption expenditures price index, is likely to show price pressures 
quickened in August. Then on Friday, economists see the jobs report showing 
that hiring remained solid in September.

The earnings schedule is light this week, with Jefferies reporting after the 
close today, CarMax and Carnival tomorrow and Micron Technology on Wednesday.

AI whiplash

AI could wipe us all out. Or it might just kill our unwanted subscriptions.

Those are the two extremes the stock market has swung between 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-27/ai-whiplash-jolts-stocks-as-sentiment-lurches-from-fear-to-greed?cmpid=092826_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260928&utm_content=9331>
 over the past two weeks, sending hundreds of billions of dollars sloshing out 
of — and then back into — AI-exposed stocks while fraying investor nerves.

“The moves are staggering in both directions,” said Rhys Williams at Wayve 
Capital Management. “It’s hard to explain from a fundamental point of view.”

Investors will get the latest read on the outlook for AI spending this week 
when memory chip maker Micron Technology reports earnings. One thing everyone 
seems to agree on is that rapid sentiment swings aren’t going away. —Carmen 
Reinicke
