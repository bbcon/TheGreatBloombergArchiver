The US and China are eyeing tariff cuts on goods including American energy and 
agricultural products ahead of the leaders’ summit next week.

The moves are expected to be carried out under an earlier plan for reciprocal 
tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion in trade, with most-favored-nation 
rates applied to some items from China. Next week’s meetings are also likely to 
result in an agreement to cut duties on Chinese inputs for manufacturers, 
Bloomberg News is reporting Wednesday.

That all lines up with comments earlier this month from US Trade 
Representative Jamieson Greer, whotold Fox News 
<https://www.foxnews.com/video/6404564891112> this month that he expected to 
see “announcements on agriculture and non-tariff barriers related to 
agriculture” during Xi Jinping’s visit.

To negotiate a the framework for any agreement for the two leaders to 
announce, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said he’s planning to meet this 
weekend with his Chinese counterpart, He Lifeng.

Extending the nearly year-old trade truce between the world’s largest 
economies would remove a potential headwind for a world economy already facing 
several. Companies, consumers and governments are grappling with wars in Iran 
and Ukraine, elevated oil prices and rekindlinginflation concerns 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/fed-seen-hiking-rates-in-defiance-of-trump-decision-day-guide?cmpid=BBD091626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260916&utm_content=8850>
 that are pushing up global borrowing costs.
