The US is two weeks away from an Aug. 19 deadline to decide whether to impose a
50% tariff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/us-sets-50-tariff-on-some-canadian-goods-over-retaliation-claim?cmpid=BBD080626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7244>
 on some $20 billion worth of Canadian imports.

If you’re basing sentiment between the two countries on President Donald 
Trump’s rhetoric, then thingsare still tense <bbg://news/stories/TIOUFJT9NJLU> 
— as they have been for much of his second term.

Trump lobbed his latest insult northward 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/canada-economy-tracking-3-4-second-quarter-annualized-growth?cmpid=BBD080626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7244>
 on Wednesday night at a rally in Las Vegas, calling Canada’s leadership 
“nasty” on trade. Over the weekend, he said he had decided on a punishment for 
Canada in response to wildfire smoke that migrated south for a few days in July.

The comments come as the two countries negotiate to try and reach a deal 
before thetariff deadline 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/netflix-tax-will-soon-be-scrapped-canada-hints-in-court-filing?cmpid=BBD080626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7244>
. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada’s goal is still for the US to reduce 
the sectoral-specific tariffs that it put oncritical industries 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/canada-posts-fourth-straight-trade-surplus-on-gold-shipments?cmpid=BBD080626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7244>
, including steel and aluminum. The US has instead pushed Canada to walk back 
its retaliatory actions, like a ban on American liquor in certain provinces and 
tariffs on US autos.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/carney-wants-broader-tariff-relief-as-trump-s-new-50-levy-looms?cmpid=BBD080626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7244>

That raises the broader question: What’s to come of the USMCA review? If the 
two countries reach a deal ahead of Aug. 19, it could open the door to formally 
bring Canada to the negotiating table.

The US has remained in bilateral talks with Mexico 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/us-presses-mexico-to-mirror-its-steel-tariff-wall-aimed-at-china?cmpid=BBD080626_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260806&utm_content=7244>
 up until this point, with another round expected in September. US Trade 
Representative Jamieson Greer said he was optimistic for progress in the review 
process by the end of the year, with comprehensive talks continuing into 2027.

But without a US-Canada deal, the US stands to test a never-before-used trade 
authority and how far it can push its relationship with Canada.

GOP Support

Congress is closely watching the administration’s talks with the US’s biggest 
trading partners. On Thursday, 168 House Republicans are releasing a letter to 
Greer voicing support for USMCA, adding that the agreement needed several key 
changes as part of the joint review process.

“While our desired result is an extension agreed to by the United States, 
Mexico and Canada, we support the decision to prioritize outcomes over a 
deadline,” the lawmakers wrote.

The letter calls for securing market access for US industries, addressing new 
actions taken by Mexico and Canada that harm American industries, confronting 
unfair trade and investment practices from third countries and rationalizing 
tariff structures to strengthen regional competitiveness.

“We want to see the predictability and the stability while at the same time 
taking the time to get it right,” said Representative Rudy Yakym, an Indiana 
Republican, in an interview.
