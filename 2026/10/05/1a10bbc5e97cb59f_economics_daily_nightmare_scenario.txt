Nightmare Scenario

For anyone with a long enough memory, headlines on big budget deficits, 
spiking bond yields and a sinking currency are normally enough to drum up 
recollections of the euro-zone debt crisis of more than a decade ago.

The past week has seen plenty of that, with the difference now that instead of 
Greece 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/greece-s-credit-rating-upgraded-to-bbb-from-bbb-at-scope?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
 at the center of turmoil,it’s France 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/europe-s-bond-spread-blowout-prompts-bets-on-fewer-ecb-hikes?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
.

With the Middle East war stoking inflation fears and global bond yields 
surging, a lot is combining to put the spotlight on Paris.

Amid bitter street protests, another bloated budget 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-01/france-to-lay-out-budget-in-latest-test-of-investor-nerves?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
 is grinding through the fractured National Assembly, keeping the country on a 
firm trajectory to pile on debt. Next year, elections to succeed President 
Emmanuel Macron may pit far-right leader Marine Le Pen against afar-left rival 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/melenchon-fumes-at-ecb-rate-hikes-after-calling-for-debt-wipeout?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
, with more quagmire in store.

A Le Pen presidency “would raise questions about the place of France in 
Europe, it would probably raise questions about the place of Europe in the 
world, and I think would destroy value,” Finance Minister Roland Lescure saidin 
an interview 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/how-macron-s-decade-in-power-left-france-europe-s-weakest-link?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
.

Turmoil intensified on Monday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/euro-falls-to-17-month-low-on-region-s-fiscal-political-risks?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
, with the euro falling to its weakest since May 2025. France’s 10-year bond 
yield has risen to about 5%, the highest since 2002, and contagion has spread to
indebted countries such as Italy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/meloni-s-budget-dials-down-on-defense-to-narrow-italy-s-deficit?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
.

Much as with the Greek crisis, the question increasingly asked by investors 
is, how much needs to happen before the ECB acts?

Back in 2012, one driver of turbulence was a lack of firefighting tools 
available to policymakers. The-then ECB president, Mario Draghi, ultimately 
stemmed trouble by stating the institution would do “whatever it takes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-11-27/3-words-and-3-trillion-the-inside-story-of-how-mario-draghi-saved-the-euro?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
” to keep the euro together, and officials got to work designing something to 
let them do that.

Under Lagarde, the institution has another tool ready 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-06-26/lagarde-s-moment-of-ecb-crisis-reckoning-in-a-london-basement?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
, called the Transmission Protection Instrument. But it’s a moot point if 
officials could justify activating that to help France, given the fiscal 
misfeasance behind the current situation.

In truth, the ECB is approaching just the sort of nightmare scenario 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/ecb-s-nightmare-scenario-gets-closer-as-french-bond-yields-surge?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
 it wanted to avoid. Once again, it finds itself drawn into the vortex of a 
crisis that should be fixed through political means. But rather than Greece, 
which was much smaller, the country involved is the euro region’s 
second-biggest economy.

With that in mind, a lot may need to play out before the ECB 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/ecb-must-be-flexible-due-to-uncertain-inflation-path-nagel-says?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
 tackles the turmoil.

“This is just the beginning,” said Hideo Shimomura 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/french-bonds-at-risk-of-more-japanese-selling-on-outsized-stake?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
 at Fivestart Asset Management. “If the European Central Bank just leaves 
things alone, there are concerns that, judging from the European debt crisis, 
France’s 10-year government bond yield could rise as high as 7%.”

 
<https://sli.bloomberg.com/click?s=868592&stpe=default&li=20209395&m=11b724c487f61753eb1a7a4a5f3ef261&p=10052026110413&lctg=6689f0ec-2ca1-4687-9972-bdcb0c2dfbdf>

Need-to-Know Research

Japanese and US policymakers agree, the yen is undervalued 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/takaichi-told-during-trump-talks-that-us-trade-hurt-by-weak-yen?cmpid=BBD100526_NEF&utm_campaign=nef&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261005&utm_content=9663>
. Longtime currency-market participant Fatih Yilmaz at Eurizon SLJ Capital has 
deployed 16 different “long-term fair-value models” to examine just how much.

The average current “misalignment” for dollar-yen that these models found is 
33%, Yilmaz wrote in a recent note. The scale “is close to the level seen in 
1985, before thePlaza Accord 
<https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/plaza-accord-30-years-later-0>,” a 
US-led deal that led to dramatic yen appreciation. Yilmaz also looked at what 
might be driving the deviation over a shorter timeframe, focusing on three 
potential causes.
Source: Datastream and Eurizon SLJ Asset Management Estimates. 
He lists: interest-rate differentials, fiscal policy and equity valuations. 
Yilmaz argues that while the first has gotten more attention, the other two 
have become more important for the misalignment. The US ran bigger budget 
deficits than Japan after Covid, boosting the greenback. And the yen “has 
tended to weaken during prolonged upswings in the global equity cycle,” he 
wrote. “This channel has become particularly powerful in recent years.”
