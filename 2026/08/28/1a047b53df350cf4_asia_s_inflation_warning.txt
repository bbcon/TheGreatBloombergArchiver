The Southeast Asian nation of about 118 million is facing challenges a number 
of countries are — how to combat rising inflation anda weak currency 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-28/php-usd-philippine-peso-falls-past-62-per-dollar-on-elevated-oil-prices?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8278>
 without crushing growth. Its failure to do so may serve as a warning sign, 
particularly to India and Indonesia, which are also dependent on oil imports.

In mid-August the Philippine central bank Governor Eli Remolona said the bank 
would be “less aggressive” on rate hikes, only tohike yesterday 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/philippine-central-bank-hikes-key-rate-a-third-time-as-inflation-runs-hot?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8278>
 for a third consecutive time — saying “we will tighten as much as we need.” 
The central bank projects inflation to remain above target until 2028.

Growth is rapidly slowing 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/philippine-gdp-growth-unexpectedly-slows-to-2-3-on-oil-shock?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260828&utm_content=8278>
. The economy expanded just 2.3% in the second quarter, less than the estimated 
2.9% and down from 5.4% a year earlier, with consumers squeezed. The currency 
is sliding to new record lows, while the stock benchmark is the 
worst-performing global index this week.

Brent crude prices hovering around $90 a barrel and elevated US borrowing 
costs are compounding the Philippines’ woes. Yet it’s unlikely thatanything 
Warsh says

 will alleviate these issues. US inflation is stubbornly high and the market is 
pricing a roughly 34% chance the Fed hikes next month. This means the 
Philippine central bank will need to keep choosing higher rates over growth.

Bathla Group, a major real estate company in Sydney, earlier this week fell 
into insolvency, leaving firms who had provided it with billions of dollars 
exposed to substantial losses. Bathla’s distress heightens concerns across 
Australia’s private lending market, which is heavily skewed toward property 
developers.

Energy was the driver of inflation in each country, sparked by the continued 
fighting in the Middle East. Data for the 21-nation euro area as a whole is due 
next week. Analysts predict an advance beyond 3%.

Investors are fully pricing a quarter-point hike by the ECB in September. That 
would add to June’s initial increase and bring the deposit rate to 2.5%. 
Markets envisage another move by spring 2027. Underscoring the challenges
