Coal controversy

By Iain Boekhoff 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AX_AxKJaJuI/iain-boekhoff?cmpid=BBD101326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10029>
,Michael McKee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ABoevEwlk2U/michael-mckee?cmpid=BBD101326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10029>
, andArielle Schwartz 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXCf_mG_6jg/arielle-schwartz?cmpid=BBD101326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10029>

At the top of Grassy Mountain, some 6,700 feet above sea level, seams of coal 
are baked into the mountainside, visible through mining in this stretch of the 
Rocky Mountains in southwestern Alberta from the early to mid-1900s. This and 
other former mines dot the picturesque mountain towns on the eastern side of 
the continental divide, the industry having moved out of the region decades ago 
after plummeting prices and demand made mining here unprofitable.

But Grassy still has more coal to give — much more. It’s now the center of a 
yearslong battle pitting nearby towns’ economic interests against environmental 
worries from downstream communities and ranchers. And this time, it’s being 
driven not by demand to heat homes in Canada, but to feed steelmakers in China, 
Japan, South Korea, India and other Asian countries.
Exposed coal seams near the top of Grassy Mountain, where mining took place in 
Crowsnest Pass, Alberta.Photographer: Jennifer Gauthier/Bloomberg via Vancouver 
Freelance
Nearly 60 years after this site closed, locals are once again pinning their 
hopes for an economic revival on this windswept mountainside. Northback 
Holdings Corp., a division of Australian billionaire Gina Rinehart’s Hancock 
Prospecting Pty., is working to reopen the former mine.

This is Hancock’s second attempt after an earlier application was rejected by 
a joint federal and provincial review panel in 2021 over concerns about the 
impact on the nearby water ecosystem. Northback Chief Executive Officer Brad 
Johnston believes the company can address the deficiencies in its first 
application and go further in restoring the mountain, including much of the 
unreclaimed former mine site.

Hancock has spent more than a decade and over C$150 million ($105 million) on 
a project that faces stiff opposition and is still at least four years from 
opening. It’s promising to scale back its original plans by around 40% to 
produce 2.5 million metric tons a year instead of 4 million, boost reclamation 
efforts during the course of mining, build a water treatment plant and stick 
around for years after production ceases.

The company is persistent because its forecasts show an undersupply in the 
global market within the next decade and pricing levels far beyond what it 
costs to extract the coal.

The proposal and others like it have struck a nerve in Alberta, a region 
traditionally friendly toward resource development, both in terms of public 
support and government policy. Country music star Corb Lund led a petition 
campaign this year to stop coal mining in the eastern slopes of the Rockies, 
attempting to force a provincial vote on the issue this month, unsuccessfully. 
A recentsurvey found a majority of the public 
<https://www.ctvnews.ca/calgary/article/majority-of-albertans-would-support-eastern-slopes-coal-mining-ban-poll/>
 would have supported his position in a referendum.
Country music singer Corb Lund has led a movement opposing new coal mining in 
the eastern slopes of the Rocky Mountains in Alberta, Canada.Photographer: 
Jennifer Gauthier/Bloomberg via Photographer: JENNIFER GAUTHIER/Vancouver 
Freelance
The fight over the project also reveals a little-known fact about Canadian 
coal. Within the country, it has been on a long decline, as governments moved 
to reduce coal used for power production. But at the country’s western ports, 
coal is still king of commodity volumes. About 39.9 million metric tons were
exported from Vancouver 
<https://www.portvancouver.com/media/documents/2023-2025-statistics-overview> 
last year, compared to 30 million tons of petroleum products.

While the province of British Columbia now accounts for the majority of coal 
production, Alberta has seen a number of proposals in recent years and the 
province recently approved an expansion of theVista Coal Mine in Hinton 
<https://majorprojects.alberta.ca/details/Vista-Coal-Mine-Expansion/12203>, 
near Jasper National Park, that will make it the largest thermal coal mine in 
North America.

About 70% of the world’s steel supply depends on metallurgical coal and Canada 
is the fifth-largest global supplier, accounting forroughly C$9 billion in 
exports 
<https://natural-resources.canada.ca/minerals-mining/mining-data-statistics-analysis/minerals-metals-facts/coal-facts>
.

Hindered by haze

By Chandra Asmara 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWGBWKbKUm0/chandra-asmara?cmpid=BBD101326_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261013&utm_content=10029>

Indonesia’s aerial firefighting efforts have been hampered for more than a 
week by poor visibility from the forest-fire haze, as the smoke continues to 
spread across Southeast Asia.

Dense smog has prevented water-bombing and cloud-seeding operations in Central 
Kalimantan on Borneo, forcing authorities to rely on ground crews, the 
country’s disaster management agency said during a cabinet meeting convened 
Monday by President Prabowo Subianto.

Prabowo said rainfall may be the only way to clear the smog and allow aerial 
operations to resume.
TOPSHOT - A firefighter sprays water to extinguish a raging wildfire in the

(Photo by REINA / AFP via Getty Images)

the haze in avideo <https://youtu.be/wlo-d5Q1lmw?si=rkJQ0hF49FILR3nG> of the 
meeting posted on YouTube. He praised ongoing operations and called for more 
comprehensive prevention measures at the village level. Authorities expect 
rains from mid-October to help contain the fires.
