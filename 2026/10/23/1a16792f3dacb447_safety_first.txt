Deep Dive: Prague’s $10 Billion Transformation
People walk past new apartment houses and apartment buildings under 
construction in Prague on April 29, 2026.Photographer: MARTIN 
DIVISEK/EPA/Shutterstock
Prague’s historic center of baroque churches, gothic spires, and red-tiled 
roofs is ringed by modernist concrete apartment blocks. Between the two,new 
neighborhoods are taking shape 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-23/prague-s-10-billion-redevelopment-boom-transforms-the-city?cmpid=102326_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10455>
 with long boulevards, parks and contemporary mixed-use buildings largely 
funded by Czech and Slovakian billionaires.

 * More than $10 billion of transformations across two decades has seen 
disused rail yards, factories and freight depots converted in the city’s push 
to reshape the Czech capital since communism ended nearly 40 years ago.
 * For developers, it’s as much about leaving a mark on the city of 1.4 
million people as it is about financial returns. Czech’s economy is growing 
faster than the European average, and its housing market is one of the region’s 
hottest.
 * “Prague is an attractive destination for investors,” said Pavel Hnilicka 
<https://www.resite.org/speakers/pavel-hnilicka>, founder of Pavel Hnilicka 
Architects and Planners. “We’re slowly starting to see somewhat larger and more 
significant developments emerge, of the kind we can see elsewhere in Europe.”
