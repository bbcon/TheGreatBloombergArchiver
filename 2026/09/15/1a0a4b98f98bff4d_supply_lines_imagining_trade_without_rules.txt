The World Trade Organization is kicking off a public symposium in Geneva on 
Tuesday with a nearly200-page report 
<https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/publications_e/world-trade-report-2026_e.htm>
 that tries to make the case for its continued relevance in a world were the 
rules of commerce between 166 member countries are giving way to power politics.

The three-decade-old trade body is clinging to the fact that 72% of global 
goods trade still happens under one of its guiding principles — 
non-discrimination, under most-favored nation tariff terms.

But it’s also a running simulations on the economic costs if that number 
declines further and imagining cross-border commerce without rules.

WTO Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala acknowledges that the rules-based 
system faces its most severe disruption in 80 years.

“We have seen trade rules challenged on a scale unseen since multilateral 
institutions were created to underpin open, stable and predictable global trade 
in the wake of the Great Depression and the Second World War,” she wrote in the 
report.

Consider the trade news of just in the past few weeks:

 * The US president says he doesn’t care about trade with Canada 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/mark-carney-seeks-c-1-trillion-of-investment-amid-us-trade-strains?cmpid=BBD091526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8851>
, sparking a small-scale volley of tariffs this month that threatens to 
fracture one of the world’s tightest bilateral relationships.
 * Put off by the US’s heavy-handedness, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney is
stepping up overtures 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/eu-and-canada-plan-far-reaching-alliance-to-strengthen-partnership?cmpid=BBD091526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8851>
 to the European Union about closer transatlantic economic bonds.
 * The EU is nearing a crossroads 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/europe-is-trying-to-gauge-how-much-pain-it-can-take-from-china?cmpid=BBD091526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8851>
 in its response to the China Shock 2.0, with a key speech Wednesday by 
European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, after which she’s supposed 
to meet with Carney.
 * Hardly beacons of free trade themselves, the BRICs nations just finished 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-12/xi-takes-center-stage-at-modi-s-brics-summit-as-iran-war-deepens?cmpid=BBD091526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8851>
 a weekend meetingraising 
<https://d2jiw2zrmmyqt8.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/12185124/New-Delhi-Declaration-2026-1.pdf>
 “serious concerns about the rise of unilateral tariff and non-tariff measures 
which distort trade and are inconsistent with WTO rules.”
 * All this comes just over a week before US President Donald Trump is set to 
meet with Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Washington, where extending afragile 
trade truce 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-13/trump-says-he-s-not-worried-about-xi-calling-off-summit?cmpid=BBD091526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8851>
 is on the agenda and key issues like access to critical minerals hang in the 
balance.
In the report, WTO economists plot out three scenarios through the year 2050 
and offer estimates on the costs and gains of each.

In a world fragmented along geopolitical blocs, real gross domestic product 
drops 5.1% and exports sink 18.6%.

In scenario two, free trade areas form where tariffs are set in 
non-cooperative ways. As a result, real GDP declines 6.9% and exports plunge 
almost 27%.

In the rosy scenario, where the WTO reigns and integration widens, real GDP 
increases 2.9% and exports swell 17.9%

Cost of Inaction

That’s a big gap between worst- and best-case scenarios, according to WTO 
Chief Economist Robert Staiger, who emphasized that these are simulations not a 
range of forecasts.

“If the status quo is unsustainable, as many members believe, this illustrates 
the potential cost of inaction on reform,” Staiger, a Dartmouth College
economics professor <https://sites.dartmouth.edu/rstaiger/>, told reporters on 
Monday.

“These numbers tell a clear story,” he said. “The WTO remains an important 
force for shaping world trade, but the economy it governs is now far more 
integrated, digital, multipolar and complex than the one its rules were 
designed for — and the stakes of reform are high.”

Charted Territory

Two speeds 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-15/china-s-consumption-and-investment-wilt-as-factories-power-ahead?cmpid=BBD091526_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260915&utm_content=8851>
 | China’s consumer spending and investment languished in August even as 
industrial output improved more than expected, in a sign of a widening divide 
within the economy. Retail sales rose 0.4% in August from a year earlier, down 
from 0.6% in July. In contrast, industrial output climbed 5.2% in August, 
exceeding expectations and accelerating from July’s 4.5% gain. Double-digit 
growth in exports is largely behind the uplift, as hundreds of billions of 
dollars flow into the global buildout of data centers and drive up demand for 
artificial intelligence-related products such as integrated circuits.
