Pakistan scored a major diplomatic win over India this week, further rankling 
New Delhi.

On Monday, an international arbitration panel ruled that the decades-old Indus 
Waters Treaty 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-05-27/what-to-know-about-indus-water-treaty-and-its-role-in-india-pakistan-conflict?cmpid=BBD090226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8435>
 governing the respective use of the river resources remains in force and can’t 
be unilaterally suspended, as India did last year following a terrorist attack 
it blamed on Pakistan.
A dry path of the Indus River in Hyderabad, Pakistan, in May 2025.
Photographer: Jan Ali Laghari/AFP/Getty Images
New Delhi promptly rejected the ruling 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/india-ordered-to-uphold-pakistan-water-treaty-curb-kashmir-dam?cmpid=BBD090226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8435>
, saying the court doesn’t have jurisdiction over the matter.

Over in Islamabad, officials were hailing the decision as a slap in the face 
for their long-time rival. Pakistan’s information and broadcasting minister, 
Attaullah Tarar, says his country had been “vindicated.”

While it’s unclear whether the ruling can be enforced, it still represents 
another win for Pakistan on the diplomatic stage. In Tarar’s far-from-impartial 
view, India is facing “embarrassment after embarrassment.”

Pakistan’s prime minister, Shehbaz Sharif, and powerful army chief, Asim 
Munir, have struck up a close bond with US President Donald Trump since last 
year. The Pakistani leaders have leveraged that relationship and close ties 
with Tehran to emerge asan unlikely mediator 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-01/pakistan-backs-us-iran-peace-framework-as-best-hope-for-region?cmpid=BBD090226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8435>
 in the US-Iran war.
Sharif, left, and Munir on June 21.Photographer: Fabrice Coffrini/AFP/Getty 
Images
Pakistan has also been slowly strengthening ties with Muslim nations, notably 
Saudi Arabia. The two countries recently signed a mutual defense agreement, 
which now includes Turkey in what’s called the Mecca Defense Alliance.

The pact stipulates that an armed attack on one member would be regarded as an 
attack on all. This week, the three countries agreed toformalize the structure 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-31/pakistan-says-more-muslim-nations-keen-to-join-defense-pact?cmpid=BBD090226_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260902&utm_content=8435>
 with a secretariat based in Saudi Arabia, with Pakistan appointing the first 
secretary general to lead it.

In New Delhi, there’s wariness about Pakistan’s moment in the international 
arena.

While India continues to struggle to reach a trade deal with Trump — something 
Islamabad achieved more than a year ago — Pakistan is on a diplomatic roll.—
