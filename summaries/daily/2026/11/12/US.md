**Corporate Governance and AI Markets**
Nvidia's succession crisis dominated US market activity. The board engaged an outside search firm to identify potential replacements for Jensen Huang, with CFO Colette Kress cited as a possible interim pick. Nvidia's forward earnings multiple had already compressed to 66% of the S&P 500's — down from a 200% premium three years ago — suggesting the valuation reset predates the scandal. AMD, Broadcom, and Alphabet gained on speculation they could capture displaced business. Analysts at Bernstein and Oppenheimer cut ratings this week; Baird reduced its price target to $200 from $500. The stock's distress is testing whether the AI trade rests on one man or on structural demand that any successor could serve.

**Rates and Monetary Policy**
The 10-year Treasury yield at 5.00% and gold's continued retreat below $4,000/oz reflect market pricing for additional Fed tightening. Persistent energy-driven inflation — with signs the Iran war shock is spreading to core categories — appears to be the primary driver of hawkish Fed rhetoric. The Fed's next policy move remains data-dependent, but market positioning has shifted materially toward further hikes.
