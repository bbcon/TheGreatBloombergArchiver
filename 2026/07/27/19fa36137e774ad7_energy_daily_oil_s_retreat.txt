Welcome to our guide to the commodities driving the global economy. Today, 
Asia Oil Trading Team Leader Serene Cheong takes stock of the latest lull in 
fighting in the Middle East and what that means for the market.

This weekend’s abrupt cessation of hostilities 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/middle-east-tensions-ease-as-the-us-and-iran-pause-hostilities?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 in the Middle East has triggered a swift retreat in crude prices, with Brent 
slumping below$90 a barrel 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-july-27?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
.

But any relief felt by the market is likely to be short-lived, with shippers 
already committed tocostly diversions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/asia-bound-tanker-exits-red-sea-via-suez-as-others-risk-strait?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 that will ultimately result in supply gaps.

After a 13-day run of attacks, US President Donald Trump is apparently giving 
diplomacy“some space.” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/us-pauses-iran-strikes-for-second-night-as-red-sea-tensions-rise?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 Yet as the war hits the five-month mark, there’s no evidence of progress 
toward a lasting agreement.

Last month’s interim peace deal pulled prices back toward $70 before 
collapsing in a flurry of tit-for-tat attacks. Tensions between the warring 
sides remain high and Tehran has said there’s no change to the status of the 
Strait of Hormuz.

The Iran-backed Houthis, meanwhile, have shown in strikes 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/houthi-claim-missile-strikes-on-southern-saudi-arabia?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 on Saudi Arabian ports that they’re able to widen the conflict from Hormuz to 
the Red Sea, one of the region’s crucial workarounds for moving oil to global 
markets.
Vessels transit the Bab el-Mandeb Strait off the coast of southern Yemen on 
July 25.Photographer: Khaled Ziad/AFP/Getty Images
Aware that the US and Iran are still polarized, shipowners with sufficient 
risk appetite may seek to seize this fleeting moment of calm to slip tankers 
through Hormuz or across the Bab el-Mandeb Strait into the Gulf of Aden.

But for the rest of the market, expensive and lengthy 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-25/saudi-oil-can-still-get-out-but-it-won-t-be-cheap-or-easy?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 cargo diversions are already happening. Some Asian buyers are abandoning the 
southern Red Sea route and instead sending Saudi oil through the Suez Canal and 
around Africa, adding weeks to the journey.

These decisions — to accept delays and higher costs in exchange for safe 
passage — are being made by individual companies rather than across the board. 
And elsewhere in the world, news that Kazakh oil isset to return 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/kazakh-oil-port-cpc-set-to-load-tankers-again-after-drone-pause?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 is helping to ease some supply concerns.

Yet for refiners that rely on Middle East crude, a loss of feedstock is 
inevitable. That, in turn, will mean a slower ramp-up in refinery runs across 
Asia at a time when the Northern Hemisphere is supposed to be preparing for 
winter, when diesel and kerosene demand increases.

Add lower global inventories to the mix, and the market — short of a lasting 
settlement in the Middle East — may well soon return to itsnervous state 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-07-24/oil-prices-volatile-as-supply-disruptions-hit-four-global-fronts?cmpid=BBBXT072726_ENERGY&utm_campaign=energy&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260727&utm_content=7016>
 of recent weeks.

—Serene Cheong, Bloomberg News

BNEF today

US liquefied natural gas supply projects could face mounting pressure to lower 
liquefaction plant utilization by the end of the decade, according to 
BloombergNEF. If an expected market surplus pushes Asian spot prices below the 
short-run marginal cost of US LNG, uncontracted capacity could face voluntary 
cargo cancellations, BNEF says.
