**China: Financial System and Property**
Beijing's $45 billion capital injection into major banks and insurers reflects the cumulative pressure from a prolonged property downturn. Macquarie estimates housing starts have fallen 78% from their 2021 peak, returning to 2003 levels — a contraction comparable in magnitude to the US housing bust of 2005–2009 and exceeding Japan's multi-decade adjustment in speed. Home prices have reverted to 2016 levels and rents to 2015 levels, with self-reinforcing expectations of further declines extending the bottoming process. The recapitalization diluted existing shareholders; PICC fell 3.1%, China Reinsurance 3%, and China Life 1.1% in Hong Kong trading.

**Japan and Korea**
Japan's Finance Ministry data confirm the country liquidated approximately $87.8 billion in foreign securities — likely short-dated Treasuries — to fund its record yen intervention. The yen has since recovered to 154.06, surpassing the post-intervention peak. In Korea, the Kospi's 4.6% gain reflects renewed conviction in the AI memory cycle; Goldman's Timothy Moe argues investors are underestimating the duration of AI-driven chip demand, maintaining a 12,000 Kospi target against a backdrop of sharp recent volatility.
