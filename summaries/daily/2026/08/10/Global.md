**Hormuz and Energy Markets**
The Strait of Hormuz remains in a state of strategic limbo. Iran has ruled out direct US talks, promoted a hardline security official, and remains short of a deal with Oman on shipping passage. President Trump signaled a shift toward patience, describing the US as "semi-negotiating" and betting that economic pressure will eventually soften Tehran's position. The practical consequence for energy markets is sustained uncertainty: oil moves through the strait via a fragile network of shuttle tankers, while UAE vessels continue to be targeted. Global oil inventories are drawing at pace, America's Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest since the 1980s, and diesel — critically important ahead of winter — is trading above $160/barrel in Europe, compounded by Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refining infrastructure. The market remains vulnerable to sharp price spikes even if the extreme tail scenarios of the war's early phase have receded.

**Gold and Monetary Policy Divergence**
Gold's 7.3% weekly gain — its largest since late January — reflects a confluence of the payrolls miss reducing Fed rate-hike probability and resumed ETF buying at the fastest pace since April. At roughly $4,340/ounce, bullion remains well below its pre-war peak above $5,400, but the structural case — heavy sovereign debt loads, large deficits, and substantial non-bank leverage — remains intact. Monetary policy divergence persists: the BOJ is flagging accelerating tightening while the Fed's next move is genuinely uncertain pending Wednesday's CPI print. Emerging Asian central banks are increasingly seeking to defend currencies through intervention rather than reserve drawdown as higher-for-longer US rates sustain pressure on the region's exchange rates.
