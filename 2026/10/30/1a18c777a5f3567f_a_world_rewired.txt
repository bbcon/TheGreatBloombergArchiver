If Donald Trump’s disdain for US partners is going to rewire global alliances, 
today’s Canada-EU summit in Montreal is perhaps the clearest example ofwhat it 
might look like 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-30/eu-canada-summit-tests-macron-and-carney-s-new-alliance?cmpid=BBD103026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10747>
.

Prime Minister Mark Carney has made this his flagship effort in lessening 
Canada’s overwhelming reliance 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-24/canada-s-mark-carney-runs-the-country-like-a-ceo?cmpid=BBD103026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10747>
 on the US, with the goal of developing a far deeper relationship with Europe 
on defense, critical minerals, advanced manufacturing and more.

The recent controversy over Starlink’s throttling in Ukraine has only added 
urgency for both European and Canadian leaders to ensure they have alternatives 
to American technology.
EU-Canada summit signage in Montreal yesterday.Photographer: Nasuna 
Stuart-Ulin/Bloomberg
Still, the summit has made it obvious that the two sides are very much at the 
beginning of this process 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/eu-proposes-canada-become-the-bloc-s-first-associate-member?cmpid=BBD103026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10747>
, not the end.

For one thing, they can’t agree on what to call this budding new partnership 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-29/eu-executive-seeks-canadian-associate-membership-despite-dissent?cmpid=BBD103026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10747>
. EU chief Ursula von der Leyen is using the phrase “associate membership,” 
despite dissent from the bloc’s member states concerned about setting 
precedents for other countries.

“The name doesn’t matter,” Carney told reporters in response.

Yet it’s a sign of how internal politics will make it difficult to formalize 
the pact, whatever form it takes.

Canada itself isn’t immune: The newly elected leader of Quebec — who helms a 
separatist party — has protested that he doesn’t havehis own seat at the table 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-28/carney-feels-backlash-in-quebec-after-declining-premier-s-speech-at-eu-summit?cmpid=BBD103026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10747>
, and refused to attend.
Von der Leyen and Carney at the European Parliament in Strasbourg, France, on 
Sept. 17.Photographer: Jean-Christophe Verhaegen/AFP/Getty Images
The summit is taking place less than an hour’s drive from the US border, a 
reminder that Canada can’t escape its geography.

Despite many prior attempts and a recent resumption of talks, Carney has so 
far been unable tostrike a trade deal with Trump 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-07/canada-set-to-hit-back-at-trump-tariffs-risking-wider-trade-war?cmpid=BBD103026_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261030&utm_content=10747>
.

The Montreal meeting’s timing on the eve of the US midterm elections is 
unlikely to endear the Canadian leader any further to the president.

Even as Carney works to build a new alliance across the Atlantic, he has to 
make sure he isn’t provoking too much backlash from his southern neighbor.—
