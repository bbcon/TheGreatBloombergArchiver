**Fiscal and Trade Policy**
The principal US development of the day is a strategic one rather than a data-driven one. Treasury Secretary Bessent, who had previously articulated a vision of coordinating allied pressure on China's trade and currency practices, appears to have allowed a meaningful diplomatic opportunity to pass. Germany and France have in recent months moved toward precisely the China-centric multilateral framework Bessent once described — including Chancellor Merz's call for a currency dialogue modeled loosely on the 1985 Plaza Accord. Instead of engaging this opening, the administration has pursued its own bilateral track with Beijing.

**Currency and Markets**
The Treasury Department's semi-annual currency report, released July 23, drew particular attention for what it omitted: the January description of the yuan as "substantially undervalued." Goldman Sachs analysts interpreted this as a softening of stance, though a Trump administration official pushed back, noting yuan appreciation since the prior report and confirming no joint G-7 yuan pressure effort is underway. The IMF has separately estimated yuan undervaluation at as much as 21%. Whether the administration's posture hardens ahead of the next report — following an October EU-China trade deadline — remains to be seen.
