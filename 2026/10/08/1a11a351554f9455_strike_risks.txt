Deep Dive: The Story of Spain’s Richest Woman
Sandra Ortega Mera, daughter of Amancio Ortega and Rosalia Mera, attends her 
mother funeral on August 17, 2013 in Oleiros, Spain.Photographer: Xurxo 
Lobato/Getty Images
Sandra Ortega, Spain’s richest woman, has spent most of her life in the small 
northwestern town of Oleiros, a windy place overlooking the Atlantic Ocean. The 
58-year-old lives a quiet, private life with little pointing to her immense 
wealth. Ortega is thesecond-largest shareholder of Zara 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-10-08/zara-billioniare-sandra-ortega-mera-set-to-get-even-richer?cmpid=100826_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9861>
 owner Inditex SA after her father, who founded the company, Amancio Ortega. 
Sandra’s 5.05% stake is currently valued at around €8.5 billion.

 * When 90-year-old Amancio — whose net worth sits at $127 billion —eventually 
passes on, Sandra is set to grow her wealth by at least $10.6 billion as local 
laws outline that at least a quarter of his estate’s value will go to his 
descendants.
 * Sandra’s only direct connection with Inditex has been through her husband, 
who has held low-profile jobs at the company. With Sandra and Amancio’s other 
descendants having almost no relationship for decades, investors are 
questioning how Inditex’s family shareholders will deal with one another.
 * “The vulnerability is not today; it is the day Amancio Ortega’s shares 
move,” said Martin Roll, a global family business strategist and senior adviser 
at McKinsey & Co. “A passive shareholder with a meaningfully larger stake, no 
board seat and no track record of how she would vote when it matters becomes a 
strategic variable for everyone around the table, including outside investors.”
