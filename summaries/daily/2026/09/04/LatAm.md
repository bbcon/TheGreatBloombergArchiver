**Brazil Political Economy**
The Senate delay on Lula's workweek bill removes a potential pre-election boost for the incumbent and signals difficulty in converting populist policy proposals into legislative wins. Brazil's Planning Minister has argued that Lula can improve the fiscal position without deep spending cuts if reelected, though that framing requires sustained growth and revenue performance. Polling showing a virtual tie with Bolsonaro raises the probability of a contested second round.

**Argentina and Regional Dynamics**
Milei's Falklands posturing appears partly driven by domestic political calculation — his approval ratings have declined — rather than strategic foreign policy. Accelerating sanctions against energy companies in the disputed zone introduces new uncertainty for any potential hydrocarbon development in the region. Venezuela's trajectory as a Trump administration priority — with US Energy Secretary Wright explicitly stating China will be excluded from Venezuelan oil revenue — adds a great-power dimension to what had been a primarily regional political story.
