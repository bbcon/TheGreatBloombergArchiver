As Donald Trump waits for Brazil to join Latin America’s shift to the right, 
one spot on the US president’shemispheric game board 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/mapping-how-south-america-s-right-wing-shift-has-reached-brazil?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 remains stubbornly resistant.

Cuba’s communist leadership is still firmly in charge 90 miles off the coast 
of Florida, despite nine months ofever-tighter sanctions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/us-sanctions-on-cuba-could-they-force-regime-change?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 and a de facto oil blockade that followed the capture of its principal ally in 
Venezuela.

In an ironic twist, it’s the island’s budding capitalists that are keeping the 
economy afloat 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-05-15/trump-s-cuba-fuel-cuts-highlight-private-market-boom-in-crumbling-economy?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 amid chronic blackouts and geopolitical hostility.

While starving the government of fuel and financing, the Trump administration 
is allowing an increasingflow of US goods 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-trump-us-cuba-trade-boom/?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 to reach Cuba’s nascent private sector — includingsolar panels 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-06/cuba-energy-crisis-solar-exports-from-us-china-help-counter-blackouts?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 to help keep the lights on.
Solar panels on a residential building in Matanzas, Cuba.Source: AFP/Getty 
Images
By chasing off foreign investors, including a Canadian nickel and cobalt 
producer as well as an Australian gold miner, Trump has created space for US 
players to potentially tap strategic mineral assets. And US shipments to 
entrepreneurs might end up making Cuba asdependent on Washington 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-13/trump-s-plan-for-cuba-would-make-the-us-the-island-s-patron?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 as it was previously on Caracas and before that Moscow.

A military operation 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-military-option-for-cuba-fades-as-trump-focuses-on-sanctions?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 against Havana has moved to the back burner as the Iran conflict festers and 
US elections approach, though Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio continue 
to talk tough.

Perhaps as a sop to Florida voters who say the island’s private sector is just 
a front for the regime, they’ve slappedbanking restrictions 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/us-tightens-cuba-travel-rules-restricts-private-sector-banking?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
 on entrepreneurs and are cracking down on fuel shipments to sanctioned 
companies.

But for now, the stalemate persists.

Asked about the possibility of pressure easing after the midterms, Cuba’s 
deputy foreign minister says there’s no sense engaging in “wishful thinking 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/us-sanctions-are-hobbling-cuban-economic-reform-minister-says?cmpid=BBD100826_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261008&utm_content=9870>
.”

The chances of rolling back sanctions seem close to zero.

Given Trump’s propensity for unpredictability, a surprise of some kind — even 
a narrative-altering big bang after an electoral drubbing — can’t be ruled out.—
