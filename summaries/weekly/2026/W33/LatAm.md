**Colombia's Earthquake: Fiscal and Political Stress**
The earthquake that struck Colombia's coffee region represents the most consequential near-term development for Latin American political economy this week. With the death toll approaching 170 and thousands of buildings destroyed, the fiscal implications are severe: a budget deficit potentially reaching 8% of GDP leaves President de la Espriella almost no room to fund reconstruction while honoring the spending discipline that defined his campaign. His response drew early scrutiny — a roughly five-hour delay before addressing the country directly allowed local officials to become the public face of the crisis — and the government's decision to accept aid from the US, Israel, El Salvador, and Ecuador while rejecting Mexico's offer of search-and-rescue assistance has raised questions about the politicization of disaster relief. Whether de la Espriella's political alignment with Washington translates into meaningful US reconstruction support remains to be seen, and will shape the government's fiscal trajectory in coming months.

**Copper and Regional Growth**
The copper rally reflects a structural shift in demand drivers, with AI infrastructure and the energy transition increasingly displacing the market's historical reliance on Chinese growth as the primary price signal. For Chile and Peru — the region's largest producers — sustained price strength supports export earnings and growth: Bank of America's above-$16,000 forecast, roughly 10% above current levels, could lift Peru's real GDP growth to 4.4%. Antofagasta's production cut, however, serves as a reminder that weather-related supply disruptions are becoming a recurring operational constraint in Chile, with extreme rainfall and snow joining labor relations and ore-grade deterioration as systematic risks. Brazil's beef shipments to China reached 90% of the country-specific quota as of August 10, pointing to continued strong Chinese agricultural demand and supporting farm-sector revenues.
