Beyond AI

By Ishika Mookerjee 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJMYcOXODs/ishika-mookerjee?cmpid=BBD092126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9137>

US solar engineering companies and wildfire-mitigation technologies are 
emerging as favored climate-related investments ahead of Climate Week in New 
York City.

According to five managers of environment-focused funds interviewed by 
Bloomberg News, rooftop solar, battery storage and European utilities are other 
areas of interest as power demand surges, despite overhanging market concerns 
about inflation and the future of artificial intelligence. The money managers 
collectively oversee about $7.5 billion.

“Power has become a major bottleneck, not only for our digital growth, but for 
the decarbonization of it as well, and so demand for expertise and solutions is 
also stepping up,” saidKatie Self <https://www.linkedin.com/in/katieself/>, 
investment manager at Pictet Asset Management.

Their recommendations come during a turbulent stretch for stock prices. While 
the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index remains about 63% higher than a 
five-year low set in April 2025, it has lost more than a fifth of its value in 
the past four months.

Reality check

By Alastair Marsh 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQvVwWuhxcQ/alastair-marsh?cmpid=BBD092126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9137>

Of all the ways in which artificial intelligence might destroy humanity, its 
carbon footprint is far down the list for some of Wall Street’s most prominent 
climate experts.

Aniket Shah, the Jefferies 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1621134D:US?cmpid=BBD092126_GREENDAILY&utm_campaign=greendaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260921&utm_content=9137>
 executive who heads the bank’s strategy on sustainability and the energy 
transition, says he’s asking the global finance industry not to let AI 
overshadow talks about reducing planet-warming pollution. His comments, which 
feed into an already polarized debate, come just ahead of Climate Week NYC, 
where more than100,000 
<https://www.climateweeknyc.org/host-a-climateweeknyc-event-2026> people are 
expected to gather over the coming days.

“Climate change is about 50 billion tons of greenhouse gases going to zero, 
and so we actually have to have a right framing of what is driving that,” said 
Shah. “Data center demand growth is actually not in the top five.”

The concern for Shah and those who share his point of view is that angst about 
the greenhouse gas emissions of AI data centers will drag attention away from 
areas with substantially higher emissions, like transport and heavy industry.
An Equinix data center under construction, center, in Slough, UK.Photographer: 
Chris Ratcliffe/Bloomberg
It’s a distinction that others are also starting to make. A recent report by 
the investment firm of former vice president turned climate campaigner, Al 
Gore, notes that the power needed to run air conditioning has far greater 
implications for energy consumption than AI.
