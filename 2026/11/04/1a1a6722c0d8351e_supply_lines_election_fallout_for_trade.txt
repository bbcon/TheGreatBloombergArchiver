For the first time since President Donald Trump returned to the White House in 
January 2025, trade officials in far-flung capitals might feel they have what 
he prizes the most in deal-making: leverage.


Washington is waking up to wider-than-expected election wins 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/inflation-backlash-trump-fatigue-powers-huge-wins-for-democrats?cmpid=BBD110426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10817>
 for Democrats in Congress who saw the midterms as a referendum on Trump’s 
policies ranging from tariffs and energy prices to the boom in artificial

“Crushing blow to Trump and his policies. Major victory for American 
democracy,” Bernd Lange, the European Parliament’s chair of the trade committee 
who played an important role during EU-US trade talks,posted 
<https://x.com/berndlange/status/2117878660367785987> on X. “Voters have 
spoken: Trump only cares about Trump. Now he’s paying the price for it.”

Big Take: Trump Has to Govern Through Meltdown of His Own Making 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-04/midterm-elections-2026-trump-must-now-contend-with-democrats-in-control?cmpid=BBD110426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10817>

Before the scale of the Republican losses was clear, Canadian Prime Minister 
Mark Carney said the US is “asking less” and “offering more” in trade 
negotiations, as the neighboring nations once again work on a potential deal 
after a previous round of talks collapsed.

“Some of our red lines have clearly shifted, around language, around culture, 
around sovereignty, our ability to have trade deals. In other words, those are 
now off the table. That’s very welcome,” Carney told reporters Tuesday during a 
trip to Antigua and Barbuda, where he’s attending a meeting of Commonwealth 
leaders.

Canadian stakeholders didn’t wait long to troll the US administration. “Ten 
weeks ago is calling,”posted 
<https://x.com/Lanampayne/status/2117811594545746390> Lana Payne, the national 
president of union group Unifor, referring to the period when talks between the 
North American trading partners broke down.

‘Major Drubbing’

Flavio Volpe, president of Canada’s Automotive Parts Manufacturers’ 
Association, cautioned against assuming Trump will alter course on trade.

“This is a major drubbing — a message from the electorate that its patience 
has run out,” Volpeposted 
<https://x.com/FlavioVolpe1/status/2117856057485594770>. “It will be 
interesting to see how the dynamic shifts in DC. Of note, the president is 
still the president. I would caution against saying nothing will change or also 
saying everything will.”

According to a story on the Terminal <> from Bloomberg Law, the Democratic 
surge opens the door for opponents of Trump’s tariffs to intensify efforts to 
roll back the levies, or at least prevent future ones.

One wild card for trade, though, in the shift in power. The AI boom has given 
a big lift not just to US economy, but also toglobal goods trade 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-26/kuehne-nagel-ceo-expects-to-ride-ai-driven-cargo-wave-into-2029?cmpid=BBD110426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10817>
 which is on track for record year. Any efforts by Democrats to slow 
data-center construction may hurtsurging exports 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-03/asia-exports-and-the-ai-boom?cmpid=BBD110426_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261104&utm_content=10817>
 from Asia, GDP in the US and the primary driver of international commerce at 
the moment.
