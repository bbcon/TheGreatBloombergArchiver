Almost a quarter of the $19 trillion in global imports faces significant risks 
of supply shocks and the boom in artificial intelligence only amplifies the 
vulnerabilities.

Those are among the key takeaways from a new McKinsey report <> which maps out 
three areas it describes as the Achilles heel of the world economy — product 
criticality, supplier concentration and geopolitical distance.

The research from the McKinsey Global Institute research showed:

 * More than 60% of the vulnerabilities is focused on electronics, basic 
manufacturing, and metals and minerals
 * Within those sectors, about 10 products account for one-third of the 
exposure
 * Five products, including smartphones, memory chips and laptops, combine for 
about 20 percent of the global Achilles’ heel.
The report notes several ways to increase resilience: reconfiguring trade to 
diversify suppliers; boosting domestic production; building strategic 
inventories; and changing demand through substitution, efficiencies or 
different technologies.

Making matters worse, about four-fifths of maritime trade passes through major 
chokepoints, McKinsey’s report said.

“Four of the 24 major maritime passages examined have no viable seaborne 
alternative, leaving little room to absorb a shock when one of them closes,” 
the report states. “The 2026 Hormuz disruptions showed what this means in 
practice: Conflict-driven closures cut transits by as much as 95 percent at 
their peak, and prices of commodities ranging from oil to urea spiked.”
