For all his pro-Israel flourishes over two terms in office, Trump stopped 
short of embracing the settlements.

He has ruled out the West Bank annexations mooted by Prime Minister Benjamin 
Netanyahu. With the Israel-Hamas ceasefire in Gaza teetering — a deal brokered 
by Trump — the Americans are loathe to risk a conflagration in the second 
Palestinian territory.

A former construction magnate, Trump is given to hear out arguments that 
Netanyahu’s settlements boom leaves Palestinians little land for their 
hoped-for state.

That Palestinian civilians, among them American dual citizens, have suffered 
nightly harassment by extremist settlers has moved even US Ambassador to Israel 
Mike Huckabee, a pro-Zionist pastor, to condemn such actions as terrorism.

Yesterday’s sanctions by a dozen Western countries, and the diplomatic 
backlash from Israel, may be viewed in Washington as a chance to watch where 
the chips fall.

The Trump administration knows Netanyahu will be hard-put to retain power in 
next month’s Israeli election. If toppled, his successor will likelytackle the 
West Bank impasse 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-08/israel-s-leftist-leader-yair-golan-pledges-to-end-wars-after-october-election?cmpid=BBD090926_politics&utm_campaign=bop&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=260909&utm_content=8686>
 afresh.
