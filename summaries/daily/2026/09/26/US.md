**Consumer Demand and the Fuel Cost Channel**
US minivan sales reached 281,000 units through August, up 8% from the same period a year earlier, even as total new vehicle sales declined — a dynamic that appears to reflect consumers actively optimizing around elevated fuel costs rather than broad spending strength. Four of six van models on the market are hybrid or fully electric, and the category now matches large SUV volumes for the first time in nearly a decade. The shift may signal that sustained high gasoline prices, amplified by the US-Iran conflict, are exerting a measurable effect on durable goods purchasing decisions. Toyota has responded by expanding production at its Indiana facility. The data point sits in tension with the 6% YoY gain in US retail sales reported last month — suggesting spending resilience may be increasingly concentrated in value-oriented categories rather than reflecting uniform consumer strength.

**Energy and Infrastructure**
The nor'easter disruption adds near-term downside risk to Northeast regional economic activity heading into the final week of Q3. Separately, the pending NRC approval for TVA's small nuclear project would represent a meaningful step forward for domestic baseload capacity, though commercialization timelines remain measured in years rather than months.
