**US-Iran Conflict and Energy Markets**

The conflict, now six months old, shows no near-term resolution. US and Iranian forces exchanged strikes twice this week, with Brent rising more than 2% above $90 on Sunday before advancing further as tanker attacks on Monday targeted vessels operated by Saudi Arabia's Bahri and South Korea's Sinokor. Saudi crude exports in August fell to their lowest level in at least nine years. The oil-gas divergence remains structural: LNG relies on roughly 850 specialized vessels globally versus more than 7,000 crude carriers, making rerouting materially harder, and Qatari LNG traffic has been almost entirely halted since early July. Goldman estimates Gulf oil exports have recovered to 15–16 million barrels per day, still 7–8 million below pre-war levels. US retail diesel prices hit their highest since mid-2022 by Friday, and gasoline averaged $4.15 per gallon — a record for September — feeding directly into logistics costs and headline inflation globally.

**Monetary Policy Convergence**

The more notable global macro story this week may be convergence rather than divergence: the Fed, ECB, BOJ, RBNZ, and Bank of England are simultaneously moving toward or signaling tightening, driven by a common energy and food price impulse against divergent fiscal and growth starting points. The RBNZ hiked 25 basis points on Wednesday; BOJ signaled Thursday; ECB confirmed Friday for next week. Traders are pricing greater-than-50% hike probability across three major central banks for September simultaneously — a configuration not seen since 2022. Norway's $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund proposed cutting government bond allocations to 50% of its fixed-income portfolio from 70%, implying roughly $75 billion in reduced Treasury exposure, the latest signal of accumulating but individually modest pressure on foreign demand for US debt.
