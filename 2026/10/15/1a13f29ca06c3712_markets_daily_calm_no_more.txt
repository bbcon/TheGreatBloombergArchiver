The fastest US inflation in five months set in motion a tumultuous 24 hours in 
global markets, with tech stocks facing heavy losses. The biggest moves:

 * The Nasdaq shed 2% on Wednesday, and futures point to more losses 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates?cmpid=101526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=10131>

 9% on Thursday, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix taking heavy losses.
 * The yield on benchmark 10-year Treasuries zoomed up to 5.33% after the hot 
CPI print, before a sharp reversal to 5.08%. 
Stocks are under pressure as investors question whether billions poured into 
AI will pay off, prompting a shift toward safer assets. Meanwhile, a broad 
upswing in US prices suggests the Fed may be forced to hike borrowing costs.

The odds of an October rate increase surged above 50% in futures markets 
before pulling back. Jason Schenker, a top forecaster from Prestige Economics, 
thinks the Fed will hold this monthbefore considering 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/top-forecaster-says-fed-may-hike-rates-half-a-point-in-december?cmpid=101526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=10131>
 a bigger, half-percentage-point rate increase in December.

In the near-term, the question is whether the stocks selloff is a rotation or 
a fundamental break in the AI trade.

“My view is that these moves are unlikely to be sustained. Little has 
fundamentally changed, and much of yesterday’s price action went against 
stretched positioning,” said Skylar Montgomery Koning, a macro strategist at 
Bloomberg. —Charles Riley

CoreWeave’s ‘yikes’ moment

CoreWeave’s sudden crash illustrates the anxiety over the AI borrowing boom.

In an interview with the Financial Times, co-founder Brannin McBee’s said the 
company wants togain the privilege 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-14/coreweave-s-brazen-dream-crashes-into-reality-as-yields-top-15?cmpid=101526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=10131>
 of borrowing “unlimited” amounts of money.

The remarks weren’t well received. CoreWeave shares plunged as much as 17% on 
Wednesday. Its debt due in 2032, which investors bought at par four months ago, 
traded at less than 78 cents on the dollar — a precipitous drop that Elon Musk 
flagged with a “yikes.”

CoreWeave is in the business of renting out computing power to AI model 
developers and tech leaders like Microsoft and Google. The firm borrows 
billions to finance the buildout, and is reliant on the high-yield debt market.

“If CoreWeave is borrowing at 9% and the best return they can get off of that 
is a 5% return, it doesn’t matter how much they scale: They’re destroying value 
at every turn,” said Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson.

As its stock and bonds sold off, CoreWeave Chief Executive Officer Mike 
Intrator said the market is acting “irrationally,” according to a memo seen by 
Bloomberg News. He said he was confident in the company’s business model.

Luria, however, sees a bigger risk.

“The more leverage we use to build data centers, the more risk we’re bringing 
into the system,” said Luria. —Rachel Graf 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWoaAJWtc5E/rachel-graf?cmpid=101526_marketsdaily&utm_campaign=marketsdaily&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261015&utm_content=10131>
