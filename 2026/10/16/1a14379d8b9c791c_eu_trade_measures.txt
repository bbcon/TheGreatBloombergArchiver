Models present creations for Chanel Women’s Spring/Summer 2027 collection 
fashion show as part of the Paris Fashion Week, in Paris on October 5.
Photographer: Julien De Rosa / AFP via Getty Images
Earlier this month, shoppers on Yoox could snap up suede Brunello Cucinelli 
sneakers for €617 ($693), aroundhalf the price 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-16/destruction-ban-reveals-luxury-brands-problem-with-unsold-stock?cmpid=101626_morningemea&utm_campaign=morningemea&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261016&utm_content=10185>
 on the brand’s official website. If they felt like pairing them with a beige 
Gucci mini-skirt, that was available for €800, close to 50% off as well. 
Overall, the e-commerce website had around 2,500 products from Brunello 
Cucinelli, Burberry and Gucci available at a heavy discount.

 * Yoox represents a rare sign of the challenges that the luxury market faces, 
with even wealthy customers looking to cut back spending
 * High-end retailers actively avoid slashing prices and have historically 
burned or shredded their unsold goods to keep the aura of exclusivity that they 
have: a controversial practice that is now banned in the European Union
 * The EU’s drive for greater sustainability in the fashion industry is about 
to create a serious challenge for luxury companies. From next March, the bloc 
will require them to report how many unsold products they’ve destroyed, and why.
