**China: Stimulus Credibility and Market Stress**
Beijing deployed an array of market-stabilizing measures — reviving equity-support facilities used in 2024 and directing the PBOC to cool the yuan's longest winning streak in three decades — but markets were unimpressed. The CSI 300 has now declined for 12 consecutive weeks and fallen 13% year-to-date, even as most global indices have rallied. The October monthly loss of 8.5% was the index's worst in over a decade. Analysts note that durable stabilization requires demand-side fiscal stimulus reaching households and evidence that AI and tech earnings can justify current valuations — neither of which was delivered Tuesday.

**Australia and Regional Central Banks**
The RBA's decision to hike in a split vote signals continued concern about inflation even as growth moderates. Bullock's guidance toward a pause was interpreted as dovish by markets, with rate expectations pared back and the Australian dollar weakening. The divergence between a hiking RBA and the market's interpretation of a dovish signal illustrates the difficulty central banks face in managing expectations during a late-cycle tightening phase.

**Trade and Export Momentum**
Vietnam's export data and Taiwan's fourth consecutive quarter of double-digit GDP growth — the longest such run in nearly 39 years — underscore that AI-driven demand continues to channel significant economic benefit through supply chain hubs outside China. Taiwan's September exports surged 61% year-on-year to a record, beating all Bloomberg survey estimates.
