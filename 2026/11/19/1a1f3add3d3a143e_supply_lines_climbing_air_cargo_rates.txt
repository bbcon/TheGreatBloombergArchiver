Air Cargo Rates Hit Four-Year High Despite Lower Fuel Costs
Despite declining fuel costs, air cargo rates have reached a four-year high 
and may rise further ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌

Air cargo rates to fly goods eastward across the Pacific have hit a more than 
four-year high and may not peak until mid-December as solid demand and tight 
capacity outweighdeclining fuel costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-18/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-nov-19?cmpid=BBD111926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11459>
.

That’s the assessment of Judah Levine, head of research at Freightos, an 
online cargo booking platform. Air freight rates for goods to North America 
from China reached $9.25 a kilogram last week, up 65% since the start of 
October and the highest level since June 2022. They may keep rising past $10 a 
kilo in the next few weeks, he said.

Demand should stay healthy if the US-China trading relationship stays stable. 
All signs pointed in that direction after a meeting Thursday between President 
Xi Jinping and Donald Trump, who repeated that each side agreed to cut tariffs 
on $30 billion in trade and is aiming for a “fair relationship.”

Like ocean shipping rates 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-09-22/container-shipping-rates?cmpid=BBD111926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11459>
, the air market is seeing a sustained surge in equipment used in artificial 
intelligence. “Tech volumes from the data center buildout have become a major 
growth engine,” Levine said for goods flown by plane, which are usually 
smaller, higher-value components, hardware and consumer electronics.

While carriers have added capacity to the main routes, the 2026 average rate 
is still 20% higher year to date, with Southeast Asia to North America prices 
in particular 30% steeper, according to Freightos.

A big factor in the direction of rates are energy costs 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/china-oil-imports-rebound-as-beijing-turns-back-to-stockpiling?cmpid=BBD111926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11459>
. Levine cited IATA data showing jet fuel prices are down 11% so far in 
November and oil prices have retreated about 18%. Generally, he said, there’s a 
lag between crude price moves and the cost of refined products.

<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-19/trump-suggests-us-can-sell-blackwell-chips-to-china-in-future?cmpid=BBD111926_TRADE&utm_campaign=trade&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261119&utm_content=11459>

For now, though, the AI demand stacked on top of holiday season shipments are 
keeping rates aloft.

“Lower fuel costs should bring some relief to air cargo rates across the 
market including the transpacific,” he said. “But the significant demand-side 
pressure for these lanes may keep rates from easing as significantly as they 
might on many other trades when jet fuel prices do fall.”

According to IATA’s analysis 
<https://www.iata.org/en/publications/economics/reports/air-cargo-market-analysis-september-2026/>
 through September, all regions reported growth and Asia-North America routes 
led the bunch with eight consecutive months of gains and a 17% increase in 
September. Middle East volumes continued to struggle, IATA said.
