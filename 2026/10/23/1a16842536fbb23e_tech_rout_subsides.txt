 as global equity markets returned to calm after a week of wild swings amid 
concern over the sustainability of the global AI buildup. Lingering doubts 
about AI’s eventual payoff spurred some to hedge their bets by flocking to 
bonds.

A regional gauge for Asian stocks erased a decline of as much as 1.1%, while 
Nasdaq 100 futures steadied after the index suffered its worst day since June. 
China’s CSI 300, which entered a bear market on Thursday, staged alate-day 
reversal 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/china-stocks-sink-deeper-into-bear-market-despite-state-buying?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10469>
 as turnover in a basket of exchange-traded funds favored by the so-called 
national team picked up pace and Bridgewater Associates’ China arm said it is
“prudently optimistic” 
<https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-23/bridgewater-china-cut-gold-and-is-optimistic-on-local-stocks?cmpid=eveapac&utm_campaign=eveapac&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_term=261023&utm_content=10469>
 on local shares. Oil rose and gold fell. Treasuries gave up some of the gains 
from the previous session’s rush for safety.

“When you have these big corrections like the one we saw over the last few 
days, particularly overnight, in semiconductor stocks, I think it is a good 
opportunity to buy them,” Mark Matthews, the head of Asia research at Julius 
Baer, told Bloomberg TV. “Demand still massively exceeds supply.”
